## Introduction

The province matters within Spanish UFO history because its best-known reports occurred around places expected to recognise ordinary aircraft: Girona airport, a heavily travelled Mediterranean coastline and an Air Force surveillance installation. That gives the testimony added interest, but not automatic reliability. In every case, the decisive questions are whether instruments confirmed the sighting, whether investigators tested ordinary explanations and whether the surviving documents contain enough information for later researchers to reconstruct the event.

## The airport case that was identified

On 26 September 1969, staff at Girona airport reported two luminous objects at an estimated altitude of about 8,000 metres. The incident entered the Spanish Air Force’s UFO documentation system, but airport personnel concluded that the objects were French meteorological balloons. Their size, appearance, brightness and movement matched balloons they had seen before. This is one of the most useful Girona cases precisely because it did not remain mysterious. An unfamiliar object was observed, reported through official channels and identified by people with relevant local experience. Weather balloons can appear unusually bright when illuminated against a darker sky, drift at heights where their true distance is difficult to judge and seem almost stationary when viewed from the ground.

The 1969 report also provides a warning against treating the existence of a military file as evidence of something extraordinary. Spanish Air Force files collected reports of unusual aerial phenomena so that potential hazards, foreign aircraft, balloons and other unknown objects could be assessed. A document’s classification or later declassification records how information was handled; it does not authenticate an extraterrestrial interpretation. Spain’s defence archive now makes this material publicly searchable as part of its digitised UFO collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

## Blanes in 1982: detailed testimony, little investigation

At about 22:30 on 19 August 1982, a resident of Blanes said he saw a large disc-like object moving along the coast. He watched from the top-floor terrace of a seafront building and called seven other people outside. Using 16×50 binoculars, he described a rotating cylindrical disc with several illuminated panels or “windows”, dark gaps and an open triangular section. He estimated its diameter at between 50 and 100 metres and said the observation lasted roughly three minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1982-08-19_avistamiento_en_blanes_gerona.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Planeta BenitezAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI)AVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO DE...</span></span></span>

The report is memorable because it contains drawings and an unusually elaborate physical description. The principal witness also speculated about propulsion, construction, energy collection and possible occupants. Those additions make the account vivid, but they do not strengthen its evidential value. They were interpretations made after the observation rather than independently measured features.

The most important passage in the Air Force summary is not the description of the object. It is the admission that no subsequent investigation is recorded and that there was apparently no further correspondence with the witness. The document therefore contains no confirmed aircraft checks, meteorological analysis, astronomical comparison, radar data or separate signed statements from the other seven observers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1982-08-19_avistamiento_en_blanes_gerona.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Planeta BenitezAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI)AVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO DE...</span></span></span>

That leaves the Blanes event in a weakly unresolved category. The principal witness may have sincerely seen something unfamiliar, and the presence of other people is potentially significant. Yet their individual perceptions cannot be compared because the file preserves only one developed narrative. Estimates of size and distance made against a dark sea or sky are particularly uncertain: a small nearby object and a much larger distant one can occupy the same apparent angle.

Possible conventional explanations include an aircraft seen at an unusual angle, illuminated advertising or recreational equipment, a balloon, or several lights visually combined into one structure. None can be demonstrated from the surviving record. Equally, the file does not justify the stronger claim that a structured craft of extraordinary origin flew past Blanes. Later local coverage has largely repeated the official summary rather than adding new primary evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lloretgaceta.com/avui-es-cumpleixen-40-anys-dun-avistament-dovnis-a-blanes/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lloretgaceta.com">[Lloret Gaceta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lloretgaceta.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lloret Gaceta Avui es compleixen 40 anys d&#x27;un albirament d&#x27;ovnis a Blanes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lloret GacetaAvui es compleixen 40 anys d&#x27;un albirament d&#x27;ovnis a Blanes - Lloret GacetaAvui es compleixen 40 anys d&#x27;un albirament d&#x27;ovni...</span></span></span>

## The 1991 EVA-4 sighting at Roses

Girona’s strongest documented UFO case occurred at 04:12 on 13 September 1991 at the Air Force’s EVA-4 station on Mount Paní, between Roses and Cadaqués. The commander of the security guard reported a bright white circular object with a more luminous centre moving in repeated right-hand circles above the installation. He estimated its altitude at between 100 and 200 metres and said it completed approximately one circuit every six seconds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-eva-4-rosas-1991-1991-09-13-avistamiento-en-eva-4-rosas-gerona" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en EVA-4 Rosas, Gerona (13 September 1991), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1991 · U...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1991</span></span></span>

The commander called another guard, who reportedly saw the same phenomenon. A further soldier stationed at the access control later said that he too had observed a white circle moving around the site. The event lasted about five or six minutes. According to the commander, the light disappeared abruptly as another serviceman approached the exterior to look at it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-eva-4-rosas-1991-1991-09-13-avistamiento-en-eva-4-rosas-gerona" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en EVA-4 Rosas, Gerona (13 September 1991), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1991 · U...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1991</span></span></span>

These details make the Roses incident more substantial than a solitary light report. It involved military personnel, more than one viewing position and a duration long enough for witnesses to communicate. The location is also notable. EVA-4 is part of Spain’s air-surveillance network and is responsible for obtaining and transmitting radar information and supporting ground-to-air communications. The installation was established during the development of the Spanish-American early-warning network and has long covered a large area of the western Mediterranean.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ejercitodelaireydelespacio.defensa.gob.es/EA/ejercitodelaire/es/Unidades/Unidad/e9b448f6-7fc7-11ee-99f3-005056bf91c5/?path=%2Fsites%2Finternet.es%2F.content%2Funidad%2Funidad_00011.xml&amp;resourceId=e9b448f6-7fc7-11ee-99f3-005056bf91c5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ejercitodelaireydelespacio.defensa.gob.es">[Ejército del Aire]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ejercitodelaireydelespacio.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ejército del Aire- Unidades</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ejército del Aire- Unidades</span></span></span>

Yet the station’s radar did not confirm the object. The duty operator reported no strange echo and no abnormal equipment behaviour during the sighting. That does not prove that nothing physical was present: very small, low, poorly reflective or clutter-obscured targets may escape detection. Nevertheless, the absence of an instrument return weakens claims that a large solid craft was circling directly above an operational radar site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-eva-4-rosas-1991-1991-09-13-avistamiento-en-eva-4-rosas-gerona" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en EVA-4 Rosas, Gerona (13 September 1991), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1991 · U...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1991</span></span></span>

The estimates recorded in the summary also deserve caution. The object was described as roughly ten metres across, around 150 metres high and following circles about 400 metres in diameter. None of those dimensions came from triangulation or instrument measurement. A luminous object against a dark sky provides few reliable cues to distance, making calculated size and flight path highly dependent on the witness’s initial distance assumption.

No conventional identification is recorded in the file. Plausible categories include an illuminated balloon, airborne material caught in local wind, a celestial object distorted by cloud movement, or a perception effect involving a fixed or slowly moving light. The rapid circular motion described by the witnesses makes each simple explanation imperfect, but the documentation lacks the weather records, precise bearings, angular measurements and independent interview transcripts required to test them properly.

The file was formally declassified on 20 February 1996 because the Air Force intelligence officer found no reason to preserve its classified status. Declassification did not mean the event had been solved, nor did it certify the witnesses’ distance and size estimates. It meant that the documents no longer required secrecy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-eva-4-rosas-1991-1991-09-13-avistamiento-en-eva-4-rosas-gerona" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en EVA-4 Rosas, Gerona (13 September 1991), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1991 · U...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1991</span></span></span>

## Why the radar setting can mislead

The phrase “UFO over a radar base” sounds stronger than the underlying evidence. Readers may reasonably assume that a surveillance station would automatically produce a complete electronic record of everything visible nearby. In practice, military air-surveillance radar is designed primarily to detect and track aircraft across a wide operational area, not necessarily every small or low object close to the antenna.

Even so, the lack of a radar contact remains a central fact. A visual observation supported by radar would establish that an instrument detected something at a measurable position and range. In the Roses case, the evidence consists instead of several human observations from the same military environment. Multiple witnesses reduce the likelihood of a purely private hallucination, but they can still share the same misidentification, viewing geometry or expectation.

The base’s location also complicates eyewitness assessment. Mount Paní overlooks the Gulf of Roses, Cape Creus and the Gulf of Lion, an area containing aircraft routes, maritime traffic, powerful coastal lights and strong winds. The regional tramontane can produce rapid cloud movement and carry lightweight objects in unusual ways. The setting offers broad visibility, but it also supplies many potential sources of unfamiliar lights. The extreme local winds are well documented and have even damaged the protective dome of the radar installation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ejercitodelaireydelespacio.defensa.gob.es/EA/ejercitodelaire/es/Unidades/Unidad/e9b448f6-7fc7-11ee-99f3-005056bf91c5/?path=%2Fsites%2Finternet.es%2F.content%2Funidad%2Funidad_00011.xml&amp;resourceId=e9b448f6-7fc7-11ee-99f3-005056bf91c5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ejercitodelaireydelespacio.defensa.gob.es">[Ejército del Aire]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ejercitodelaireydelespacio.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ejército del Aire- Unidades</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ejército del Aire- Unidades</span></span></span>

## A province of separate incidents, not a proven flap

The surviving evidence does not establish a sustained Girona UFO “flap” in which closely connected reports describe the same phenomenon over days or weeks. The principal official cases are separated by years and differ markedly in quality and likely cause:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Girona airport, 1969:** officially reported and plausibly identified as French weather balloons.
* **Blanes, 1982:** a detailed civilian account with several reported witnesses, but no documented follow-up investigation.
* **Roses EVA-4, 1991:** a multiple-witness military sighting that remained unidentified, without radar confirmation.

</div>

These incidents should therefore be treated as separate case studies rather than parts of one continuous pattern. Their concentration around aviation and coastal observation points may partly reflect reporting opportunity: airport employees, soldiers and people watching an open sea horizon are more likely than the general population to notice and report unfamiliar aerial lights.

Across Catalonia, the Ministry of Defence files contain cases ranging from identified balloons to events left without a firm conclusion. Contemporary reporting on the release of the records noted that 16 of the documented Spanish Air Force cases occurred in Catalonia. That regional total provides useful context, but it does not indicate 16 unexplained craft. The collection includes sightings with differing evidence, investigation quality and final assessments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://english.elpais.com/cat/2016/11/11/catalunya/1478881679_067169.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: english.elpais.com">[EL PAÍS English]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">english.elpais.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on elpais.com.</span></span></span>

## Local archives and the growth of the story

Girona’s UFO history extends beyond military paperwork into local publications, private collections and specialist groups. The Centre for Interplanetary Studies, founded in Barcelona in the late 1950s, accumulated reports and correspondence relating to Catalan sightings. Its surviving archive is now being catalogued by Archives for the Unexplained in Sweden, offering researchers access to material that may not appear in government files.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afu.se/afu2/?page_id=8732" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afu.se">[AFU]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afu.se</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on afu.se.</span></span></span>

Girona was also associated with the privately produced bulletin *Búsqueda*, published between 1988 and 1995. Specialist archival summaries describe it as containing Catalan UFO material alongside contactee claims. Such publications are valuable for locating forgotten witnesses and contemporary rumours, but they require careful source criticism because reporting, personal belief and speculation were often printed together.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.el-cei.org/2023/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: el-cei.org">[El CEI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">el-cei.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on el-cei.org.</span></span></span>

Local newspaper collections provide another route for checking whether a supposed mass sighting was actually reported at the time. The Girona provincial archive maintains historical press holdings, while Olot’s digital newspaper library covers local titles extending back into the nineteenth century. These collections are especially useful for testing later claims that “many people” saw an event: researchers can look for dated reports, named witnesses, weather information and descriptions written before the story became part of UFO folklore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bibgirona.cat/regira/municipis/olot/colleccions/hemeroteca-dolot" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibgirona.cat">[bibgirona.cat]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibgirona.cat</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on bibgirona.cat.</span></span></span>

This distinction matters in the case of sensational stories linked to the Roses base. Later internet accounts have described an alleged 1971 encounter involving an enormous humanoid and soldiers opening fire. The readily available versions are poorly sourced, heavily embellished and unsupported by a corresponding declassified Air Force case file found in the official index. They should not be placed on the same evidential level as the documented 1991 observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://forocoches.com/foro/showthread.php?t=8729886" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: forocoches.com">[Forocoches]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">forocoches.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Base Militar EVA-4 de Rosas – Caso OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Base Militar EVA-4 de Rosas – Caso OVNI</span></span></span>

## What remains unexplained

The 1991 EVA-4 sighting is Girona’s clearest genuinely unresolved official case. It has named institutional provenance, several reported observers and a contemporaneous military paper trail. What it lacks is equally important: radar confirmation, photographs, physical effects, measured positions, detailed meteorological analysis and a recorded attempt to reproduce or identify the observation.

Blanes remains unresolved in a looser sense. The testimony is descriptive, but the absence of a proper investigation means that “unresolved” largely reflects missing work rather than the failure of strong competing explanations. An untested report is not necessarily a difficult anomaly.

The 1969 airport event belongs in a different category because relevant observers identified it as a familiar type of balloon. Including such resolved cases is essential to understanding the province’s record. They show that strange appearance, official interest and sincere testimony can coexist with an ordinary cause.

Girona’s UFO history therefore supports neither blanket dismissal nor claims of confirmed extraordinary craft. It shows a more complicated and useful reality: observers sometimes reported genuinely puzzling lights; official documentation was uneven; some cases were resolved through local knowledge; and others survived mainly because the information needed to settle them was never collected.
