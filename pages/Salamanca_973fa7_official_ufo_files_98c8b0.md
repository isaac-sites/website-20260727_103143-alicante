## Introduction

Spain’s released UFO files provide an important documentary record for understanding how unusual sightings were reported, investigated and classified by official authorities. For [Salamanca]({{ 'salamancas-ufo-stories-between-claims/' | relative_url }}), however, the archives are more revealing for what they do **not** show: they preserve the wider Spanish process of recording aerial anomalies, but they do not provide evidence that a Salamanca sighting was a confirmed unknown craft or an extraordinary event. The province’s UFO history remains based mainly on witness reports, local investigations and later discussion rather than official confirmation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Salamanca_973fa7_official_ufo_files_98c8b0-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Official Files" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The value of Spain’s UFO archive is therefore not that it proves unexplained origins. Its importance is that it shows the procedures behind these cases: dates, locations, witness statements, military assessments, weather information and final classifications. For Salamanca researchers, these records offer a way to separate documented investigation from later storytelling and to understand where the evidence is strong, limited or missing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

## Military UFO archive background

Spain’s main official collection comes from the former Spanish Air Force UFO investigation files, now available through the Virtual Defence Library of the Spanish Ministry of Defence. The archive contains 80 declassified files covering approximately 1,900 pages of reports on unusual aerial phenomena involving Spanish airspace and, in different ways, military personnel or equipment. The declassification process began in the early 1990s, with documents later made available digitally to the public.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

These files were not created as proof of extraterrestrial activity. They were administrative and military records designed to assess reports that could potentially relate to aviation safety, airspace monitoring or unidentified observations. Individual files commonly include a summary of the event, location, date, witness information, investigative notes, meteorological details and a final assessment or classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

This distinction matters when looking at Salamanca. An entry in an official archive means that authorities received and examined a report. It does not mean investigators concluded that the object was unknown in a scientific sense, nor that the case supported an unusual explanation.

## Salamanca reports in official records

The public archive allows searches by location and province, providing a way to examine whether a reported event generated an official investigation. Its search system includes geographical fields for places connected with the files, although the available public catalogue does not establish Salamanca as a major concentration of documented military UFO cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/en/consulta/busqueda.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

This fits the broader pattern of Salamanca’s UFO history. The province is mainly associated with a small number of widely circulated reports, particularly the 1974 Horcajo de Montemayor case involving lorry driver Maximiliano Iglesias. That incident became important within Spanish UFO literature, but its surviving evidence remains primarily testimonial rather than a military record demonstrating an unidentified object. The official-file perspective helps place such reports in context: a famous sighting in UFO culture is not automatically the same thing as a case with extensive government documentation.

The absence of a major Salamanca archive record should not be misunderstood as evidence that nothing was reported. Many UFO accounts historically remained within local newspapers, private investigation groups or informal collections rather than becoming military files. Spain’s Defence archive was focused on reports that reached official channels, especially those involving possible aviation relevance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

### The role of aviation connections

Salamanca’s relationship with aviation is one reason official records are relevant to the province. The area includes military aviation infrastructure, including Matacán air facilities, which has contributed to public interest in possible aircraft-related explanations for unusual sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-B-2018-41232" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: boe.es">[BOE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">boe.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Objeto: Servicio de control de fauna en la Base Aérea de Matacán (Salamanca). Expediente: 2018/EA46/00000751.August 24, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 24, 2018</span></span></span>

However, an aviation connection does not automatically strengthen a UFO claim. Military or airport-related locations can increase the number of observations because more aircraft, lights and technical activity are present. At the same time, aviation personnel and monitoring systems can provide better opportunities for identifying conventional explanations.

The Spanish archive shows this wider investigative principle: officials generally treated unusual observations as events requiring assessment, not as evidence of a particular origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

## Why files do not prove origins

Official UFO records are often misunderstood because the phrase “unidentified” describes an information problem rather than an explanation. A case may remain unidentified because investigators lack enough data, because witnesses disagree, because records are incomplete or because a conventional explanation cannot be confirmed.

Spain’s released files demonstrate this process clearly. They preserve investigations into reports, but the existence of a file only confirms that something was reported and examined. It does not confirm that the object was an advanced aircraft, an unknown technology or a non-human craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

For Salamanca, this is especially important because the province’s best-known stories are based on personal testimony. A witness may accurately describe what they experienced while still being mistaken about what caused it. Distance, darkness, unfamiliar aircraft, atmospheric effects and expectations can all influence interpretation.

The official record therefore provides a useful evidence framework:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Documented:** a report was made, investigated or recorded.
* **Unresolved:** available information did not allow a firm identification.
* **Confirmed:** independent evidence demonstrated the nature of the event.
