## Introduction

[Huelva]({{ 'huelvas-ufo-stories-and-the-evidence/' | relative_url }})’s published UFO history begins not with a well-documented official investigation, but with a brief close-range story from Cartaya in 1954. Fourteen years later, reports of coloured and slowly moving lights appeared across the Condado, the western coast and roads inland. Together they are often described as Huelva’s first UFO wave.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Huelva_bc0cde_early_sightings_1968_ed9e3f-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Early Sightings" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The word “wave”, however, needs qualification. The surviving accounts do not establish that one object crossed the province or that every witness saw the same phenomenon. They describe several incidents on different dates, mostly preserved through later summaries rather than contemporary case files. Their historical importance lies in showing how Huelva entered Spain’s developing UFO culture: isolated observations became linked into a regional pattern, even though the information needed to test that pattern was rarely recorded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.huelvainformacion.es/huelva/avistamientos-OVNI-provincia-Huelva_0_1614138790.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huelvainformacion.es">[Huelva Información]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huelvainformacion.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span></span></span>

## The 1954 Cartaya close encounter

The earliest incident normally included in Huelva UFO chronologies is dated 19 December 1954. Manuel Moral Bernal and Riquelme Camacho were reportedly at La Tavirona, near Cartaya, when they saw a circular object approximately 50 metres away. According to the surviving account, it made considerable noise and flew westwards in the direction of Portugal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.huelvainformacion.es/huelva/avistamientos-OVNI-provincia-Huelva_0_1614138790.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huelvainformacion.es">[Huelva Información]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huelvainformacion.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span></span></span>

That description is memorable because it sounds more substantial than a distant light in the sky. At a claimed range of 50 metres, witnesses might reasonably have been expected to notice the object’s apparent size, structure, height, duration and effect on the surroundings. A noisy object at close range might also have attracted other observers or left some trace in local police, municipal or press records.

None of that supporting material has been made publicly available with the story. The commonly repeated version comes from a short retrospective chronology published decades later. It does not reproduce a statement made in 1954, identify when the witnesses were first interviewed or explain how the estimated distance was calculated. No photograph, drawing, weather report, physical trace or independently dated document accompanies it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.huelvainformacion.es/huelva/avistamientos-OVNI-provincia-Huelva_0_1614138790.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huelvainformacion.es">[Huelva Información]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huelvainformacion.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span></span></span>

This does not prove that the witnesses invented the experience. It means that the case cannot now be tested with confidence. The reported noise could point towards an aircraft, engine or unfamiliar machine rather than an astronomical object, but the lack of information about altitude, speed and visibility prevents a meaningful comparison. Even the phrase “circular object” is ambiguous: it could describe a disc seen face-on, a rounded light, or simply the witness’s later memory of an indistinct shape.

The Cartaya story therefore matters chiefly as a historical marker. It is the starting point of Huelva’s published chronology, but it is not a strong foundation for claims that an extraordinary craft visited the province.

## Coloured lights across Huelva in 1968

The character of the evidence changes in September 1968. Instead of one alleged close encounter, Huelva acquired a loose sequence of observations involving luminous objects, changing colours and apparent movement. These reports coincided with wider interest in unusual aerial phenomena across Spain.

On 4 September, lights were reportedly seen from La Palma del Condado and Bollullos del Condado on the same day that several objects were reported over parts of Málaga. José Romera Cárdenas, travelling towards Valverde del Camino, described a circular object crossing the sky while flashing alternating red and green light. Another local resident, Manuel Reyes Hernández, was also named as a witness, although the available summary gives little detail about what he personally observed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.huelvainformacion.es/huelva/avistamientos-OVNI-provincia-Huelva_0_1614138790.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huelvainformacion.es">[Huelva Información]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huelvainformacion.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span></span></span>

The timing is potentially significant. Official Spanish files show that unusual high-altitude objects were reported and investigated elsewhere in the country on 5 and 6 September. Radar stations, military pilots, civil aviation crews and ground observers reported bright or apparently structured objects over areas including Madrid, Toledo, Cuenca and Pamplona. The military record considered at least some of those observations compatible with high-altitude meteorological research, noting that one slowly moving object could have been a sounding balloon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-09-05-06_avistamiento_en_madrid-toledo-cuenca-pamplona.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI)AVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO D...</span></span></span>

Those official incidents do not authenticate the Huelva reports, and there is no demonstrated connection between them. They do show that the provincial stories emerged during a real period of heightened reporting rather than being assigned to a “wave” solely by later writers. They also illustrate why the term is difficult to interpret: a reporting wave may contain genuinely puzzling observations, balloons and other unrelated events that become associated because they occur close together.

### The Ayamonte observation

A more detailed Huelva account is dated 14 September 1968. A witness watching from an apartment terrace near the coast at Ayamonte described an oval object above the southern horizon, in the general direction beyond the lighthouse. It appeared orange, produced intermittent red flashes and moved slowly from north to south and back again with what the observer regarded as a pendulum-like motion.

The witness called his wife and the occupants of a neighbouring apartment. According to the published testimony, the group watched for about three minutes before the light gradually faded as though being switched off.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.huelvainformacion.es/huelva/avistamientos-OVNI-provincia-Huelva_0_1614138790.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huelvainformacion.es">[Huelva Información]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huelvainformacion.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span></span></span>

Several features make this report more useful than the Cartaya story. It includes an approximate direction, duration, colour, pattern of movement and multiple observers. The reference to a lighthouse also provides a fixed visual comparison.

Yet the same details raise conventional possibilities. A low light over the sea can be difficult to judge because the horizon supplies little reliable information about distance. Atmospheric turbulence can cause a bright astronomical object to shimmer, change colour or seem to move slightly. Autokinesis—the apparent wandering of a stationary light when viewed against a dark, featureless background—can exaggerate this impression. Alternatively, an aircraft seen partly head-on can appear nearly stationary before changing direction or fading as its angle changes.

The account says the light flashed at intervals resembling those of a lighthouse, but does not provide exact timings that would allow the two signals to be compared. Nor does it give the observation time, elevation above the horizon or weather conditions. Without those facts, it is impossible to reconstruct the sky or check aircraft and maritime activity.

### Gibraleón, San Juan del Puerto and the road reports

Other observations attributed to the same period broadened the apparent geographical spread. Reddish and green lights were reportedly seen rising and falling near Gibraleón before moving towards San Juan del Puerto and disappearing near the cellulose factory. The named witnesses were José Rodríguez González, his wife Concepción Sánchez and their nephew José María Morales.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.huelvainformacion.es/huelva/avistamientos-OVNI-provincia-Huelva_0_1614138790.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huelvainformacion.es">[Huelva Información]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huelvainformacion.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span></span></span>

The surviving narrative contains a geographical inconsistency: it says the witnesses were at the castle of Gibralfaro, a famous site in Málaga, while describing lights around Huelva. That may be a simple error in the later article, but it matters because observation position is essential when reconstructing direction and distance. A mistaken place name can radically alter the possible interpretation.

A final report, placed near the end of October, concerns a motorist who saw a light apparently suspended above olive trees near a road junction. The chronology refers both to the Cortegana–Aracena junction and to proximity to San Juan del Puerto, locations too widely separated to describe the same junction without further clarification. The light was said to sway gently at an estimated distance of 80 metres, appear roughly as large as a car headlamp and cast no noticeable light on the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.huelvainformacion.es/huelva/avistamientos-OVNI-provincia-Huelva_0_1614138790.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huelvainformacion.es">[Huelva Información]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huelvainformacion.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span></span></span>

Again, the uncertainty may reflect imperfect transcription rather than a defect in the original testimony. Nevertheless, it prevents the report from functioning as reliable geographical evidence. A light seen in line with nearby trees can look close even when it is a distant aircraft, planet or lamp. The claim that it did not illuminate the ground may suggest that the source was farther away than the witness believed.

## Was this really a provincial wave?

The 1968 incidents justify speaking of a rise in reported sightings, but not necessarily a single physical event. The observations differ in date, setting and behaviour:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The Condado report involved a circular light flashing red and green while crossing the sky.
* The Ayamonte object was orange, intermittently red and apparently oscillated above the southern horizon.
* The Gibraleón lights reportedly moved vertically before heading towards San Juan del Puerto.
* The late-October road light seemed to hang above trees and sway gently.

</div>

These similarities are broad rather than distinctive. Red and green are standard aircraft navigation colours, while orange or reddish light is common when bright objects are viewed through a long stretch of atmosphere near the horizon. Apparent hovering, oscillation and sudden fading can arise from viewing geometry, haze, thin cloud or the lack of a stable visual reference.

At the same time, it would be too simple to label every account a mistaken aircraft or star. The necessary observational details were not preserved. No single explanation can responsibly be assigned to all the reports, and the absence of evidence is not evidence that every witness saw the same mundane source.

The most defensible conclusion is that Huelva experienced a reporting cluster. People in several parts of the province noticed lights they could not identify, while unusual-object stories were circulating more widely across Spain. The cluster is historically real as a body of testimony, but its interpretation remains uncertain.

## Rockets, balloons and the Huelva sky

One local factor deserves particular attention. The [El Arenosillo]({{ 'el-arenosillo/' | relative_url }}) testing centre near Mazagón became operational in 1966, two years before the reported wave. Its early work included meteorological sounding rockets and balloons used to measure winds and atmospheric conditions at high altitude. Contemporary histories describe metallic reflectors and other balloon-borne equipment being used before and during the first launch campaigns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.diariodehuelva.es/articulo/area-metropolitana/inta-arenosillo-espacial/20220627111435207089.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: diariodehuelva.es">[Diario de Huelva]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">diariodehuelva.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Diario de Huelva El INTA pone a Huelva en la lanzadera aeroespacial</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Diario de HuelvaEl INTA pone a Huelva en la lanzadera aeroespacialJuly 30, 2022 — El INTA pone a Huelva en la lanzadera aeroespacial El I...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2022</span></span></span>

High-altitude balloons can remain visible over long distances, appear nearly stationary and reflect sunlight after the ground below has entered twilight. Depending on their payload and viewing angle, they may look rounded, elongated or unusually bright. Sounding-rocket launches can produce brief luminous trails or clouds, although such displays would not automatically fit reports lasting several minutes on different dates.

There is no publicly demonstrated launch or balloon record matching each Huelva sighting. El Arenosillo should therefore be treated as a plausible source category, not a universal solution. Its importance is that it adds a documented stream of unfamiliar aerial activity to the province at precisely the time its UFO chronology began to expand.

The official Spanish investigation from 5–6 September 1968 reinforces that caution. Military observers elsewhere reported objects that appeared highly unusual, yet the corresponding file explicitly considered a meteorological probe as a possible explanation. ## Why the early reports remain difficult to test

The fundamental weakness of Huelva’s early cases is not that the witnesses necessarily lacked sincerity. It is that the surviving record lacks the information needed to distinguish between competing explanations.

Most accounts do not preserve exact observation times, compass bearings, angular elevations, weather conditions or independent statements from each witness. Estimated distances such as 50 or 80 metres are presented without explaining what visual cues made those estimates possible. In several instances, confused or contradictory place names further obstruct reconstruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.huelvainformacion.es/huelva/avistamientos-OVNI-provincia-Huelva_0_1614138790.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huelvainformacion.es">[Huelva Información]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huelvainformacion.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Huelva Información Los avistamientos ovni en la provincia de Huelva</span></span></span>

There is also no clear evidence that the Cartaya or 1968 Huelva reports entered the formal military investigation system. Spain began declassifying its official files in 1991, and digital copies are now available through the Ministry of Defence’s virtual library. Those records can contain witness questionnaires, maps, aviation communications and technical assessments. The publicly cited Huelva stories, by contrast, remain largely press and investigator narratives rather than comparable official dossiers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/indice_campo.do?campo=idtitulo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

Later retellings have preserved names, dates and memorable descriptions, but they have not materially strengthened the original evidence. No newly released photograph, radar trace or contemporary Cartaya document has transformed the assessment. Some retellings have instead introduced geographical ambiguities that make the cases harder to analyse.

Huelva’s first wave is therefore best understood as the formation of a local UFO tradition. The 1954 Cartaya report supplied an apparent starting point; the coloured lights of 1968 supplied a cluster; and later chronologies joined them into a provincial history. What survives is valuable evidence of what witnesses reported and how those reports were remembered. It is much weaker evidence for determining exactly what appeared in Huelva’s skies.
