## Introduction

On 24 October 1978, an AVIACO DC-9 approaching Menorca Airport reported two fast-moving lights at very low apparent altitude where no declared aircraft were supposed to be. After the airliner landed, the duty controller went outside with binoculars and saw a luminous object north of the island. Menorca, Palma and Barcelona air-traffic services could not identify corresponding traffic, and the Spanish Air Force opened a formal inquiry.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Balearic_Islands_d9fdd5_menorca_airport_enco_73bf65-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Menorca Case" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The case remains important because it combines professional aviation witnesses, real-time radio exchanges and a surviving military file. Yet “unresolved” needs careful qualification. The inquiry’s investigator judged that the lights were probably two jet fighters flying in formation. What the file lacks is independent confirmation that such fighters were actually present. There is no preserved radar track, aircraft identification, photograph or physical evidence that settles the question. ## What the DC-9 crew and controller reported

At about 20:30 local time, AVIACO flight A0203 was on final approach to Menorca. The captain contacted the airport tower because he could see two apparent aircraft to his left, approximately eight nautical miles away and, by his estimate, below 500 feet. The surviving summary identifies the aircraft as a DC-9 arriving from Barcelona and records its approach speed as about 150 knots.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-menorca-1978-1978-10-24-avistamiento-en-menorca" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl es expediente menorca 1978 1978 10 24 avistamiento en menorca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">luminous object, two objects, aviation encounter, DC-9 visual, tower binocular observation, Menorca, Balearic Islands, Spain, 1978, Spani...</span></span></span>

The captain did not initially describe structured discs, impossible manoeuvres or an obviously non-human machine. He described two points of white light that looked sufficiently aircraft-like for him to ask whether other traffic had been reported. In his later statement, he compared the lights with small white lamps used on aircraft to illuminate the leading edges of their wings. He believed the objects were moving at a speed comparable with the DC-9’s maximum speed and formed the opinion that they might be two fighters travelling together.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-menorca-1978-1978-10-24-avistamiento-en-menorca" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl es expediente menorca 1978 1978 10 24 avistamiento en menorca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">luminous object, two objects, aviation encounter, DC-9 visual, tower binocular observation, Menorca, Balearic Islands, Spain, 1978, Spani...</span></span></span>

That aircraft-like description is central to the case. It makes an unconventional vehicle less necessary as an explanation, but it also sharpens the flight-safety problem: two fast jets operating low near an active approach path without appearing in declared traffic information would themselves have been serious.

The tower controller checked the situation rather than merely accepting the pilot’s impression. Menorca tower had no reported traffic, Palma Area Control said it knew of none, and Barcelona Control likewise reported no corresponding aircraft near Menorca. The transcript preserved in the file records the captain openly raising the possibility that the lights were either two aircraft or two unidentified objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-menorca-1978-1978-10-24-avistamiento-en-menorca" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl es expediente menorca 1978 1978 10 24 avistamiento en menorca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">luminous object, two objects, aviation encounter, DC-9 visual, tower binocular observation, Menorca, Balearic Islands, Spain, 1978, Spani...</span></span></span>

After A0203 landed, the controller switched off the tower lights, took binoculars onto the terrace and looked north. He reported one luminous object rather than two. He estimated it to be 10–15 nautical miles away and between 2,000 and 3,000 feet high, apparently moving from east to west. The sighting lasted about 30 seconds, in good weather, and he heard no noise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1978-10-24_avistamiento_en_menorca.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[J.J. The controller’s observation is often described as corroboration, but it is more accurately a second observation made shortly afterwards. The number of lights differed, as did the estimated altitude and distance. The file does not establish that the controller saw the same objects reported from the DC-9. One light may have disappeared, the formation may have changed aspect, or the controller may have seen an entirely separate source.

## How air-traffic services and the Air Force responded

The airport authorities treated the report as an aviation-security matter. On 25 October, the day after the encounter, Menorca’s air commander reported the incident through the military chain of command, stressing that unidentified high-speed traffic near the airport could endanger arriving and departing aircraft. This practical concern—not an assumption about extraterrestrial visitors—explains why the case entered the Air Force system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-menorca-1978-1978-10-24-avistamiento-en-menorca" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl es expediente menorca 1978 1978 10 24 avistamiento en menorca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">luminous object, two objects, aviation encounter, DC-9 visual, tower binocular observation, Menorca, Balearic Islands, Spain, 1978, Spani...</span></span></span>

A lieutenant colonel was appointed to conduct a formal inquiry, with proceedings beginning on 2 December 1978. The file includes administrative correspondence, witness interviews, the reported communications between control centres and the investigator’s assessment. Higher commands forwarded the findings without claiming that they possessed additional information capable of overturning the investigator’s opinion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-menorca-1978-1978-10-24-avistamiento-en-menorca" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl es expediente menorca 1978 1978 10 24 avistamiento en menorca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">luminous object, two objects, aviation encounter, DC-9 visual, tower binocular observation, Menorca, Balearic Islands, Spain, 1978, Spani...</span></span></span>

The inquiry’s conclusion was conventional but qualified. The investigator considered that the lights seen by the captain and controller were probably two high-speed jet fighters in formation. This was not a confirmed identification based on a callsign, military exercise record or radar correlation. It was an interpretation drawn largely from the captain’s own description of their speed, formation and lighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-menorca-1978-1978-10-24-avistamiento-en-menorca" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl es expediente menorca 1978 1978 10 24 avistamiento en menorca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">luminous object, two objects, aviation encounter, DC-9 visual, tower binocular observation, Menorca, Balearic Islands, Spain, 1978, Spani...</span></span></span>

That distinction is sometimes lost in later retellings. The Air Force did not simply leave the file marked “unknown”; nor did it prove that fighters had been found. It proposed fighters as the most plausible answer while acknowledging, through the wording “apparently” or “seemingly”, that their identity had not been established. Local retrospective reporting has likewise noted that the file contains no supporting data demonstrating that two military aircraft were actually over the island.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.menorca.info/menorca/local/2016/10/23/1527532/ovni-avisto-1978-alcance-todos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: menorca.info">[Menorca - Es diari]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">menorca.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ovni avisto 1978 alcance todos</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Es diariEl OVNI que se avistó en 1978, al alcance de todos...</span></span></span>

The file was reviewed during Spain’s later UFO-document declassification programme. An intelligence officer recommended declassification on 10 May 1995, the formal declassification reference is dated 17 May, and the copy bears a library-entry stamp of 24 May. These different dates describe stages of the same administrative process rather than conflicting versions of the encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-menorca-1978-1978-10-24-avistamiento-en-menorca" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl es expediente menorca 1978 1978 10 24 avistamiento en menorca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">luminous object, two objects, aviation encounter, DC-9 visual, tower binocular observation, Menorca, Balearic Islands, Spain, 1978, Spani...</span></span></span>

The broader Ministry of Defence collection contains 80 files and roughly 1,900 pages covering unusual aerial observations from 1962 to 1995. According to the ministry, these records commonly include witness interviews, reports, weather information and summary conclusions, although their depth varies considerably. ## Why the evidence is unresolved but limited

The Menorca encounter has several advantages over a typical anecdotal sighting. The first report was made during an operational flight, the tower sought information from two control centres, a controller conducted a separate visual search, and the authorities preserved contemporary documentation. The witnesses were also accustomed to judging aircraft activity, even though professional experience does not eliminate errors of distance or perception.

Its evidential weaknesses are equally important.

**No positively identified aircraft emerged.** The fighter explanation fits the pilot’s description, but the surviving file does not name a unit, aircraft type, exercise, route or crew. If the objects were military jets flying without coordination, the record does not explain whose they were or why they entered the airport environment.

**The observations were visual, not measured.** Estimates of altitude, range and speed were based on appearance. At night, a light with no visible background or known size provides poor information about distance. An object judged to be low and fast can instead be farther away, larger or moving differently from the observer’s estimate.

**There is no preserved instrumental confirmation.** Palma reportedly had no relevant traffic information and, in the controller’s account, saw nothing useful on its display. However, the public file does not contain radar plots, raw returns or a technical analysis showing what radar coverage should have detected at the reported altitude and location. “Not shown as traffic” is not necessarily the same as “physically absent from radar”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1978-10-24_avistamiento_en_menorca.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[J.J. Benítez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1978 10 24 avistamiento en menorca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BenítezAVI STAMI ENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDI ENTES OVNI)AVI STAMI ENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDI ENTES OVNI) AVI STAMI ENTO...</span></span></span>

**The witnesses did not give matching descriptions.** The pilot saw two apparent aircraft; the controller later saw one luminous object. The file’s conclusion treats the reports as parts of the same event, but it supplies no triangulation or continuous tracking connecting them.

**The duration was short.** The controller watched the light for only about 30 seconds. There was no time to obtain a second ground viewpoint, photograph it or establish a reliable angular path.

These limitations mean that several ordinary possibilities remain open. Unreported military aircraft fit much of the testimony. Misjudged conventional traffic, distant aircraft lights or separate objects seen from different angles cannot be excluded. Astronomical explanations are less comfortable for the captain’s report of two formation-like lights apparently moving near his aircraft, but the file does not contain enough positional detail to test every celestial or atmospheric possibility rigorously.

Conversely, the case offers no strong support for an extraterrestrial interpretation. No solid body was described, no extraordinary acceleration was documented, no radar performance was preserved, and no physical trace or later technical evidence appeared. The unexplained element is the failure to identify the lights, not demonstrated behaviour beyond known aviation.

## The case’s place in Balearic UFO history

Within the [Balearic Islands]({{ 'balearic-islands/' | relative_url }}), the Menorca Airport incident stands out because the story does not depend mainly on folklore, a retrospective interview or a newspaper headline. Its central claims can be traced to a contemporary Air Force file and to communications made during an actual approach. That gives it greater historical and evidential value than the islands’ many loosely sourced accounts from the late-1970s UFO craze.

It also illustrates why official documentation can strengthen and weaken a case at the same time. The documents confirm that something genuinely concerned an airline captain and airport controller, that traffic checks were conducted, and that military officials took the safety implications seriously. They also show that the original witnesses themselves considered conventional jets plausible and that the inquiry found no evidence requiring an exotic conclusion.

Later digitisation made the case easier to examine but did not add decisive evidence. No subsequently published radar record, military flight log or independent observation has conclusively identified the lights or established unusual technology. The encounter therefore remains unresolved in a narrow, defensible sense: the exact aircraft or light sources were never proved. Its enduring value lies not in demonstrating a visitation, but in preserving a rare Balearic example of unidentified traffic reported by aviation professionals, investigated officially and left with a plausible explanation that was never independently verified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[gob.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>
