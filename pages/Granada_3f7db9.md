## The Motril interception

The incident formed part of a wider military file covering reports from Valencia, Motril and Madrid on 11, 17 and 28 November 1979. The Granada episode occurred in controlled military circumstances rather than beginning as a civilian report. According to the official account, a fighter launched from Los Llanos Air Base in Albacete after an unidentified radar contact had been reported. ## Why the case carries more weight than most sightings

The Motril report has several features that raise it above a typical anecdotal UFO claim.

**Trained witnesses were involved.** The principal observer was a military pilot operating in contact with an air-defence controller. Professional training does not make a witness infallible, but it can improve the reporting of headings, relative positions, radio conditions and aircraft behaviour.

**The observation occurred during an official mission.** The event was not reconstructed only from a newspaper interview years later. It entered the records of the Air Force’s Operational Air Command and Intelligence Section and was retained in the military archive. The surviving catalogue describes a substantial 147-page collection covering the linked November incidents, alongside a later 12-page file concerning their review and declassification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/busqueda_referencia.do?campo=idtitulo&amp;idValor=396020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

**More than one kind of anomaly was reported.** The case combined an unidentified radar indication, visual lights and radio interference. Multiple channels of observation can strengthen a case, although only when the records show that they referred to the same object. Here, that connection is not completely secure: the initial radar trace had become intermittent or had vanished before the pilot’s most dramatic visual observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0apQH3ZzFL4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: youtube.com">[YouTube]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">youtube.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">You Tube OVNI EN MOTRIL GRANADA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OVNI EN MOTRIL GRANADA - YouTube...</span></span></span>

**The official assessment did not dismiss the witnesses.** The report assigned them a high degree of reliability and recorded good visibility under a clear sky. ## What the official investigation did—and did not—conclude

The Air Force did not confirm the presence of an unknown aircraft. Its conclusions were noticeably cautious. The files stated that the identification of the lights was doubtful, that they had not been shown to be solid objects and that conventional aircraft had not been established as the source. Atmospheric temperature inversions were raised as a possible cause of refractive effects in at least some of the three November reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.granadahoy.com/vivir/ovni-visito-motril-1979-tres_0_2002289169.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: granadahoy.com">[Granada Hoy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">granadahoy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ovni visito motril 1979 tres 0 2002289169</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Granada HoyEl ovni que &#x27;visitó&#x27; Motril en 1979: tres luces y un &quot;hola, hola, cómo estás&quot; por radio...</span></span></span>

A temperature inversion occurs when warmer air lies above cooler air near the surface. Under certain conditions, layers of air with different temperatures can bend light or radio waves. Distant lights may appear displaced, enlarged, duplicated or apparently suspended. Radar propagation can also behave abnormally, creating misleading returns or extending detection beyond normal ranges.

That possibility fits several elements of Motril: bright points rather than a clearly described structure, an apparent inability to reduce the distance, and a location over or near the sea, where strong atmospheric layering can develop. It does not neatly explain every part of the story, however. Refraction alone would not account for intelligible voices on the radio, and the official material available to the public does not provide enough meteorological measurements, radar plots or technical radio analysis to demonstrate that an inversion caused the event.

The result is best described as unresolved rather than unexplained in the strongest sense. The file contains plausible conventional mechanisms, but not a complete reconstruction that accounts for the radar, lights and radio transmission together.

## The electronic-warfare explanation

A more specific sceptical interpretation emerged decades later through an investigation published by *El Ojo Crítico*. Its authors interviewed a retired senior Air Force officer with experience in electronic warfare. On this reading, the “UFO” story could have grown from a real air-defence incident combined with radar countermeasures, communications interference and distant lights associated with military activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elojocritico.info/manises-1978-el-scramble-ovni-que-no-existio/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elojocritico.info">[elojocritico.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elojocritico.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">manises 1978 el scramble ovni que no existio</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MANISES 1978: EL SCRAMBLE OVNI QUE NO EXISTIO – El Ojo Critico...</span></span></span>

## Motril and the November 1979 cluster

The Granada incident is often associated with the famous Manises case, which occurred six days earlier. On 11 November, a commercial aircraft diverted to Valencia after its crew reported unusual lights, and a Mirage F1 was subsequently sent to investigate. Because the Motril mission also involved a fighter from Los Llanos and occurred within the same week, later UFO literature frequently treats the two events as part of a single “flap”, or concentrated period of sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.andaluciainformacion.es/opinion/jose-manuel-garcia-bautista/verdad-detras-expedientes-ovni-espana-que-defensa-revelo-que-nunca-aclaro/202605271430003392937.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: andaluciainformacion.es">[Andalucía Información]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">andaluciainformacion.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">expedientes desclasi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">expedientes desclasi</span></span></span>

The official archive itself grouped reports from Valencia, Motril and Madrid. That administrative grouping shows that investigators considered possible similarities in timing and description. It does not prove that one object or cause linked all three locations. The reports occurred on separate dates, under different circumstances, and the published summaries do not establish a continuous track moving between them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/busqueda_referencia.do?campo=idtitulo&amp;idValor=396020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

This distinction is important for Granada’s provincial history. Motril should not simply be treated as a minor sequel to Manises. It had its own radar alert, fighter mission, triangular light formation and radio anomaly. Equally, the national attention surrounding Manises may have encouraged later writers to combine separate November events into a more coherent mystery than the documentation supports.

## Declassification and later reporting

Spain began declassifying its Air Force UFO records in the early 1990s. Physical copies were deposited at the Air Force’s central library, and the collection was later digitised through the Ministry of Defence’s virtual library. The catalogue now lists more than 80 titles covering reported phenomena across Spanish airspace from the 1960s to the mid-1990s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

The Motril material was released in stages. One large file was declassified in August 1994, while a related 12-page record was declassified in November 1996. The archive identifies the Operational Air Command and its Intelligence Section as the originating bodies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/busqueda_referencia.do?campo=idtitulo&amp;idValor=396020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

Digitisation in 2016 gave the Granada case renewed publicity. Local reports accurately highlighted the triangular lights, the unsuccessful attempt to approach them and the children’s voices. More recent coverage has generally repeated those same core details rather than uncovering new contemporary documents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.granadahoy.com/granada/Defensa-publica-informe-avistamiento-Motril_0_1075392706.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: granadahoy.com">[Granada Hoy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">granadahoy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Granada Hoy Defensa publica un informe del avistamiento de un ovni en Motril</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Granada Hoy Defensa publica un informe del avistamiento de un ovni en Motril</span></span></span>

Later storytelling has not always been as restrained. Some versions identify alleged participants, describe a giant structured object, claim another aircraft entered it, or report damage and unusual effects after landing. These additions may come from later interviews or UFO literature, but they are not supported by the short official account most readily available to the public. They should therefore be treated as secondary claims rather than established parts of the Motril record.

## Granada beyond the landmark case

The Defence archive’s title index does not reveal a comparable series of formally investigated Granada cases. Contemporary local coverage has consequently described Motril as the province’s only recorded military “strange phenomenon” in the released collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.granadahoy.com/vivir/ovni-visito-motril-1979-tres_0_2002289169.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: granadahoy.com">[Granada Hoy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">granadahoy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ovni visito motril 1979 tres 0 2002289169</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Granada HoyEl ovni que &#x27;visitó&#x27; Motril en 1979: tres luces y un &quot;hola, hola, cómo estás&quot; por radio...</span></span></span>

That does not mean nobody else in Granada has reported unexplained lights. As in most Spanish provinces, local newspapers, enthusiasts and residents have circulated informal accounts. The difference is evidential quality. Civilian reports without dates, original testimony, photographs, police records, flight data or astronomical checks cannot be weighed on the same level as an Air Force file.

Granada’s geography can also encourage honest misidentification. The province combines clear mountain skies, extensive views from high ground, busy civil and military air routes, coastal atmospheric effects and exceptionally prominent celestial objects. Aircraft approaching airports, satellites, meteors, planets low on the horizon and distant lights distorted over the sea can all appear unusual. These are general possibilities, not retrospective solutions to every local claim.

The available record therefore does not support the idea of Granada as a sustained UFO hotspot. Its importance comes from the quality and strangeness of one aviation incident, not from a large body of well-documented encounters.

## The fairest assessment

The [Motril case]({{ 'motril-case/' | relative_url }}) remains one of the more interesting episodes in Andalusia’s official UFO archive because something operationally significant appears to have occurred. A radar alert was taken seriously enough for a fighter mission; the pilot then reported a persistent triangular arrangement of lights; and a highly unusual radio transmission entered the account. The event was documented within the military system, and the witnesses were regarded as credible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/busqueda_referencia.do?campo=idtitulo&amp;idValor=396020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

Nevertheless, the strongest extraordinary interpretation is not supported. There is no released photograph, cockpit recording, physical trace, recovered material or complete radar dataset demonstrating an unknown craft. The official investigation itself questioned whether the lights represented solid objects. Atmospheric refraction offers a plausible partial explanation, while later electronic-warfare testimony offers a potentially stronger explanation for the radio interference and possibly the radar behaviour. Neither has been proven in enough detail to close the file.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elojocritico.info/manises-1978-el-scramble-ovni-que-no-existio/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elojocritico.info">[elojocritico.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elojocritico.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">manises 1978 el scramble ovni que no existio</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MANISES 1978: EL SCRAMBLE OVNI QUE NO EXISTIO – El Ojo Critico...</span></span></span>

For Granada’s UFO history, the most defensible verdict is therefore **credible incident, incomplete evidence, unresolved cause**. The Motril interception deserves attention because it exposes the real difficulty of investigating aerial anomalies: a witness may be trustworthy, an official response may be genuine and the experience may remain striking, while the evidence still falls short of showing what was actually present.
