## Introduction

The San José de Valderas photographs of 1 June 1967 made the Ummo story look like something more than anonymous letters and imaginative claims. The pictures appeared to show a disc-shaped craft over the western edge of [Madrid]({{ 'madrids-ufo-history-between-hoax-and/' | relative_url }}), bearing the same distinctive emblem used on documents supposedly sent by visitors from another world. For believers, the match between photograph and correspondence seemed too precise to be coincidence.

That apparent confirmation did not survive later investigation. Photographic studies concluded that the object behaved like a small nearby model rather than a large craft in the sky. In 1993, José Luis Jordán Peña, a Madrid UFO enthusiast closely involved in the case, stated that he had created the Ummo deception. Later reconstructions showed how easily the Valderas images could be reproduced with a model and a suspension line. Although arguments continue over individual negatives and witness memories, the evidence that made the incident famous has been severely discredited.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ummo-sciences.org/fr/data-E/E50.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ummo-sciences.org">[ummo-sciences.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ummo-sciences.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TRÈS CONFIDENTIEL Madrid le 11 avril 1993... ## From Aluche to the Valderas photographs

The Valderas episode did not appear in isolation. It followed a supposed UFO incident at Aluche, then an outer district of Madrid, on 6 February 1966. Reports described a disc-like object associated with an unusual emblem, while marks and material allegedly found at the site added the suggestion of a landing. Jordán Peña took an active role in investigating the event, giving him access both to the emerging story and to the people most likely to publicise it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.guiadealuche.net/2025/02/los-avistamientos-de-ovnis-en-1966-en.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guiadealuche.net">[guiadealuche.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guiadealuche.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">los avistamientos de ovnis en 1966 en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">los avistamientos de ovnis en 1966 en</span></span></span>

At the same time, members of Madrid’s small but lively contactee community were receiving typed communications attributed to beings from Ummo, a supposed planet orbiting the red-dwarf star Wolf 424. Meetings connected with Fernando Sesma’s UFO discussion circle at the Café Lyon helped provide a ready audience. The letters offered far more than a simple claim of visitation: they contained diagrams, invented terminology and long discussions of science, society, religion and alien technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uaplogbook.com/ummo-ufo-letters-spain/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uaplogbook.com">[UAP Logbook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uaplogbook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP LogbookUmmo UFO letters: the Spanish hoax that lived on paperUmmo UFO letters: the Spanish hoax that lived on paper Short Note / Jul...</span></span></span>

The decisive event came on the evening of 1 June 1967 at San José de Valderas, close to Alcorcón and the well-known Valderas castles. A sequence of photographs showed a dark disc against a pale sky. On its underside was the emblem already associated with the Ummo correspondence: a symbol often described as resembling a capital H with an extra central stroke.

The Madrid evening newspaper *Informaciones* gave the incident prominent coverage. The combination of recognisable suburban scenery, apparently ordinary witnesses and striking photographs made the story far more persuasive than the letters alone. Recent Madrid reporting still identifies the publicity surrounding these images as the moment when Ummo became one of the capital’s best-known UFO narratives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telemadrid.es/programas/cuarto-de-maravillas/El-dia-que-Madrid-creyo-ver-un-ovni-el-caso-de-Aluche-y-San-Jose-de-Valderas-2-2868033230--20260306101300.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telemadrid.es">[Telemadrid]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telemadrid.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de Valderas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de Valderas</span></span></span>

The pictures were particularly effective because they seemed spontaneous. Their uneven framing and changing position suggested that a photographer had hurriedly followed an object moving across the sky. Yet investigators later argued that this appearance of spontaneity was misleading. Analysis of the sequence indicated a camera position and pattern of movement more controlled than the published account implied, while the apparent craft remained consistent with a small object placed close to the camera.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ummo-sciences.org/activ/art/art11-2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ummo-sciences.org">[ummo-sciences.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ummo-sciences.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ummo-sciences.org.</span></span></span>

## How the symbol and letters reinforced belief

The Valderas photographs gained their force from the way different pieces of the story appeared to confirm one another. The letters established the symbol and predicted an extraterrestrial presence; the Aluche and Valderas accounts then supplied physical-looking events; newspaper publication gave the material public legitimacy.

This created a closed chain of confirmation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The correspondence explained who the visitors supposedly were.
* The reported sightings appeared to demonstrate that the letter writers existed.
* The emblem on the photographed object linked the craft directly to the correspondence.
* Investigators and recipients then treated that link as evidence that the documents contained privileged information.

</div>

The weakness in this reasoning was that the apparently independent strands were not demonstrably independent. Jordán Peña moved within the same Madrid circles that received, discussed and investigated the claims. Someone controlling both the written mythology and the staged visual evidence could make each element support the other without supplying any genuinely external confirmation.

The distribution of the documents also encouraged belief. Different recipients were sent different portions of a much larger supposed archive, meaning that followers had to compare, copy and assemble the material. That collaborative work helped create a community of researchers personally invested in understanding the case. The enormous volume and technical tone of the documents could then be mistaken for evidence of an origin beyond a single author, even though quantity and complexity do not establish authenticity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uaplogbook.com/ummo-ufo-letters-spain/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uaplogbook.com">[UAP Logbook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uaplogbook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP LogbookUmmo UFO letters: the Spanish hoax that lived on paperUmmo UFO letters: the Spanish hoax that lived on paper Short Note / Jul...</span></span></span>

The letters also mixed verifiable scientific vocabulary with speculative or invented assertions. To a general reader, technical detail can signal expertise; within the Ummo affair, it often functioned as atmosphere. Precise-looking measurements, unfamiliar terms and elaborate diagrams gave the impression of a coherent extraterrestrial civilisation, but they did not provide independently testable information capable of proving that such a civilisation existed.

Most importantly, the Valderas photographs did not merely illustrate the story. They transformed how it was judged. A postal deception could be dismissed as literary role-playing, whereas a photographed craft seemed to show that something had physically appeared over Madrid. Once the photographs were accepted, readers became more inclined to interpret the letters generously. The visual evidence therefore acted as the bridge between an invented mythology and a claimed real-world event.

## Why the photographs failed technical scrutiny

No single photographic argument settled every dispute, but several investigations converged on the conclusion that the pictures were compatible with a small suspended model.

French space researcher Claude Poher examined available negatives and the geometry of the sequence during the 1970s. His analysis considered the lighting, apparent dimensions, camera movement and changes in the object’s orientation. He concluded that the scene could be reproduced by photographing a lightweight model suspended close to the camera. A later discussion in the *Journal of Scientific Exploration* summarised Poher’s assessment as that of a small model hung from a line.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ipaco.fr/JSE-08-04_Guerin.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ipaco.fr">[ipaco.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ipaco.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scienti Jic Exploration, Vol</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">8, NO. 4, pp. 447-469, 1994utive images convinced Poher that it was, in fact, a small model hung from one... Ground Saucer Watch, an American group that applied early computer enhancement to alleged UFO photographs, also reported a linear feature above the object and interpreted it as a supporting thread. Its analysis became influential among investigators who had previously regarded Valderas as unusually strong photographic evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ignaciodarnaude.es/ficha/analisis-tecnico-de-las-fotografias-de-san-jose-de-valderas-1-junio-1967/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ignaciodarnaude.es">[Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ignaciodarnaude.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">analisis tecnico de las fotografias de san jose de valderas 1 junio 1967</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">analisis tecnico de las fotografias de san jose de valderas 1 junio 1967</span></span></span>

There are legitimate qualifications. The Ground Saucer Watch team apparently did not work from the best surviving original material, and a later Guardia Civil laboratory examination concluded that one line identified as a possible thread was a scratch on the negative. The same examination found no evidence that the photographic emulsion itself had been altered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ignaciodarnaude.es/ficha/analisis-tecnico-de-las-fotografias-de-san-jose-de-valderas-1-junio-1967/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ignaciodarnaude.es">[Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ignaciodarnaude.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">analisis tecnico de las fotografias de san jose de valderas 1 junio 1967</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">analisis tecnico de las fotografias de san jose de valderas 1 junio 1967</span></span></span>

Those findings are sometimes presented as authentication, but they do not establish that the photographed object was a full-sized craft. A photograph of a physical model suspended in front of the camera would produce an ordinary, unaltered negative. Demonstrating that an image was not retouched is therefore different from demonstrating that its subject was distant, large or extraordinary.

The strongest case against the photographs does not rest solely on whether one disputed line was a thread. It rests on the combination of factors: the object’s apparent scale, the controlled geometry of the sequence, the ability to reproduce its appearance with simple materials, and the later confession of a participant positioned to organise the deception. The model explanation accounts for the images without requiring either photographic alteration or an unknown aircraft.

In 1997, journalist and investigator Manuel Carballal recreated the Valderas effect using a model and fishing equipment, illustrating how the supposed craft could be staged with straightforward means. A reconstruction cannot by itself prove how an earlier photograph was produced, but it removes the argument that the Valderas images required technology or circumstances beyond an ordinary hoax.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elpais.com/elpais/2019/08/29/icon/1567087063_903049.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elpais.com">[El País]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elpais.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La gravedad del caso Edelweiss fue tal que conmocionó a la sociedad española e hizo aflorar en el mundillo ufológico las sospechas de que...</span></span></span>

## Jordán Peña’s confession

The case changed decisively in April 1993, when Jordán Peña circulated a written statement accepting responsibility for the Ummo deception. In the document, dated 11 April, he declared that he was the author of the fraud. A further letter the following month defended that admission against people who refused to accept that he had written the supposed extraterrestrial reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ummo-sciences.org/fr/data-E/E50.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ummo-sciences.org">[ummo-sciences.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ummo-sciences.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TRÈS CONFIDENTIEL Madrid le 11 avril 1993... Je n’en peux plus, alors j’ai décidé de dire MA vérité: c’est moi-même qui suis l’auteur d...</span></span></span>

His account described Ummo as an experiment in credulity and social suggestion. He said that letters, telephone calls, reports and supporting incidents had been created to observe how intelligent and educated people could accept an elaborate falsehood when it was presented through apparently converging evidence.

Accounts of his admission also describe the Valderas craft as a small model suspended on a very fine thread. Local Madrid histories repeat this explanation and identify Jordán Peña as the photographer or organiser behind the famous images.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.guiadealuche.net/2025/02/los-avistamientos-de-ovnis-en-1966-en.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guiadealuche.net">[guiadealuche.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guiadealuche.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">los avistamientos de ovnis en 1966 en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">los avistamientos de ovnis en 1966 en</span></span></span>

The confession is not a perfect source. Jordán Peña gave changing or eccentric accounts of certain details, and some researchers doubt that one individual produced every document attributed to Ummo over several decades. His suggestion that outside organisations had assisted parts of the operation has not been established by convincing independent evidence. Later material bearing the Ummo name may also have been created by imitators or additional participants.

These uncertainties matter when reconstructing the full history of the letter network, but they do much less to rescue Valderas. A confession does not stand alone when it agrees with an already existing model hypothesis, with photographic concerns raised years earlier and with successful practical reconstructions. The cumulative case is substantially stronger than any one of its components.

It is also significant that Jordán Peña’s admission came after sceptics had already challenged the photographs. The model explanation was not invented merely to fit his later statement. Technical investigators had independently reached broadly similar conclusions before he publicly accepted responsibility.

## What remains disputed

Supporters of the case commonly argue that fraudulent photographs would not disprove every report of an unusual object over San José de Valderas. Some witnesses were said to have described a disc bearing the Ummo emblem, and defenders maintain that a real sighting could have occurred alongside staged photographs.

That possibility cannot be excluded as a matter of pure logic. It is possible for false evidence to become attached to an otherwise unexplained observation. The evidential question, however, is whether the surviving testimony can establish that this happened.

The witness record has serious limitations. Many accounts were collected or publicised within circles already familiar with the Ummo story. Reports changed in detail, and some testimony became widely known only after the photographs and emblem had appeared in the press. This makes it difficult to determine which descriptions were genuinely independent and which were influenced by publicity, discussion or repeated retelling.

The emblem is especially problematic. If a witness clearly recorded the unusual symbol before anyone involved in the correspondence or photography could have disclosed it, that would be notable. In practice, the symbol circulated through the same network as the letters and photographs. Its appearance in several parts of the story is therefore more readily explained as consistent design than as independent corroboration.

Some investigators who accepted that the photographs might show a model nevertheless continued to defend the broader sighting. J. J. Benítez, for example, acknowledged that technical reports had damaged his earlier confidence in the images but argued that the testimony of people who claimed to see a disc remained unexplained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.guiadealuche.net/2025/02/los-avistamientos-de-ovnis-en-1966-en.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guiadealuche.net">[guiadealuche.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guiadealuche.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">los avistamientos de ovnis en 1966 en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">los avistamientos de ovnis en 1966 en</span></span></span>

That position preserves a residual mystery but not the original claim. Once the photographs are removed, the case becomes a collection of disputed recollections from a highly publicised event rather than a photographed extraterrestrial craft. The burden of proof cannot be shifted from discredited physical evidence to testimony whose independence is uncertain.

## How the hoax convinced Madrid

The Ummo affair succeeded because it did not rely on one dramatic claim. It combined several modest forms of persuasion: technical-looking correspondence, advance expectation, a repeatable emblem, sympathetic investigators, newspaper exposure and photographs that appeared to connect everything.

Madrid itself was central to that mechanism. The meetings, recipients, investigators, suburban locations and press coverage were close enough to form a social network. Information moved rapidly between enthusiasts and journalists, while each new report seemed to validate material already circulating inside the same community.

The case also exploited an important difference between evidence and narrative consistency. The letters, symbol and photographs fitted together extremely well. That consistency was impressive only while the elements were treated as independent. Once a common author or organising group became plausible, their close agreement became evidence of planning rather than extraterrestrial contact.

This is why Valderas remains one of the most instructive episodes in Madrid’s UFO history. It shows that photographs can be physically genuine yet misleading: the camera may record a real object while giving no reliable indication of its size or distance. It also shows that multiple forms of evidence do not provide multiple confirmation when they originate within the same controlled chain.

## The present assessment

Later reporting has weakened, not strengthened, the original Valderas claim. The pictures are now widely treated as staged photographs of a model, and the Ummo correspondence is regarded by mainstream journalistic and sceptical accounts as an elaborate human deception. Contemporary Madrid coverage presents Aluche and Valderas chiefly as historic examples of how the capital came to believe a manufactured UFO story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telemadrid.es/programas/cuarto-de-maravillas/El-dia-que-Madrid-creyo-ver-un-ovni-el-caso-de-Aluche-y-San-Jose-de-Valderas-2-2868033230--20260306101300.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telemadrid.es">[telemadrid.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telemadrid.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de Valderas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de Valderas</span></span></span>

Questions remain about the precise division of labour, whether Jordán Peña had collaborators and who created later additions to the Ummo archive. Those are questions about the construction and continuation of the hoax, not strong evidence that the Valderas craft was genuine.

The fairest classification is therefore not unresolved but debunked at the centre, with peripheral disputes surviving around witness memories and authorship details. The photographs once appeared to provide the physical proof that made Ummo famous. Technical analysis, practical reconstruction and confession instead turned them into the clearest evidence of how the deception worked.
