## Introduction

Flight 502 is remembered as [Biscay]({{ 'how-strong-is-biscays-ufo-evidence/' | relative_url }})’s most dramatic aviation mystery: an Aviaco Caravelle approaching Bilbao on 31 January 1978 allegedly entered a brilliant cloud, suffered widespread instrument and radio failures, diverted to Santander and arrived with seventeen minutes that nobody aboard could account for.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Biscay_fc6f6b_flight_502_missing_t_53d46a-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Flight 502" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The basic diversion is plausible, and the story is consistently associated with an experienced captain named Carlos García Bermúdez. The famous time discrepancy is much harder to establish. Public retellings say that the crew experienced seven minutes inside the cloud while air-traffic controllers recorded a 24-minute loss of contact. Yet no accessible contemporary flight log, controller transcript, crew report or official investigation file has been produced to demonstrate that those two timings were recorded independently in 1978.

That distinction is central to the case. Flight 502 may preserve the memory of a genuinely unsettling encounter with severe weather or electrical disturbance. The claim that the aircraft literally “lost” seventeen minutes is a later, much more weakly supported conclusion.

## The flight, diversion and luminous cloud

The accepted version begins with an Aviaco flight from Valencia to Bilbao, operated by a Sud Aviation Caravelle 10R. Later accounts identify García Bermúdez as the captain and describe him as a veteran airline pilot with about 11,500 flying hours. As the aircraft approached Sondika, Bilbao’s airport at the time, poor visibility reportedly prevented a landing and the flight continued towards Santander, the normal sort of operational decision expected when conditions at a destination fall below safe limits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.espaciomisterio.com/enigmas-y-anomalia/vuelo-502-el-avion-que-viajo-en-el-tiempo_38124" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: espaciomisterio.com">[Espacio Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">espaciomisterio.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Espacio Misterio Vuelo 502 el avión que viajó en el tiempo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El 31 de enero de 1978 le sucedió algo parecido al comandante Carlos García Bermudez.Este piloto de líneas aéreas con una experiencia de...</span></span></span>

What supposedly happened during that diversion is far less routine. The crew reportedly encountered a large, intensely luminous cloud, often described as lenticular or lens-shaped. Its brightness was said to have forced the pilots to use sunglasses. Once inside it, radio contact allegedly failed and several navigation instruments behaved erratically: compasses rotated or gave contradictory headings, the artificial horizon produced implausible indications, and the aircraft appeared briefly to be travelling in the wrong direction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.espaciomisterio.com/enigmas-y-anomalia/vuelo-502-nube-devoro-tiempo_59367" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: espaciomisterio.com">[espaciomisterio.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">espaciomisterio.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Espacio Misterio El vuelo 502 y la nube que devoró el tiempo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Espacio Misterio El vuelo 502 y la nube que devoró el tiempo</span></span></span>

The accounts then say that normal indications returned after about seven minutes. The Caravelle landed safely at Santander, with no reported injuries or damage. This uneventful outcome matters because it helps explain why any real operational occurrence might have left a lighter documentary trail than an accident. Aviation accident databases concentrate principally on accidents and serious safety events; Flight 502 does not appear in the Aviation Safety Network’s 1978 listings as an accident involving an Aviaco Caravelle. That absence does not disprove an instrument or weather incident, but it does mean the case cannot be verified through the usual public accident record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://aviation-safety.net/database/year/1978" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aviation-safety.net">[Aviation Safety Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aviation-safety.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">18 Jan 1978 &#124; Sud-Aviation SE-210 Caravelle &#124; &#124; SAETA &#124; 0 &#124; Havana-José Martí International Airport (HAV) &#124; Image &#124; non &#124;... 31 Jan 1978...</span></span></span>

Even within the repeated narrative, some technical details are unstable. Different versions place the aircraft at markedly different altitudes, disagree over whether it climbed or descended after the diversion, and variously describe the cloud as appearing suddenly, surrounding the aircraft or lying directly across its route. One account says the mileage indicator remained frozen; another says it ran backwards. Such variations are warning signs that the familiar story has been copied and embellished rather than reconstructed from a common set of flight documents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elpensante.com/el-avion-que-viajo-en-el-tiempo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elpensante.com">[elpensante.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elpensante.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El Pensante El avión que viajó en el tiempo &#124; El Pensante</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El PensanteEl avión que viajó en el tiempo &#124; El PensanteJanuary 16, 2020 — El avión que viajó en el tiempo &#124; El Pensante ◄ El insecto más...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 16, 2020</span></span></span>

A luminous cloud and temporary instrument problems are not, by themselves, evidence of a UFO in the sense of a structured craft. Clouds near mountainous northern Spain can take striking forms, while static electricity, icing, turbulence and electrical interference can disturb aircraft systems. None of those possibilities has been demonstrated as the explanation here, because the aircraft’s weather observations, maintenance records and instrument readings are not publicly available. They nevertheless remain more conservative starting points than a break in time.

## Where the seventeen-minute claim comes from

The famous calculation is simple: seven minutes supposedly elapsed aboard the aircraft, whereas ground controllers were allegedly unable to contact it for 24 minutes. Subtracting seven from 24 produces seventeen “missing” minutes.

That arithmetic is repeated almost word for word across mystery websites and popular-media treatments. The full journey after the diversion is commonly said to have lasted 32 minutes rather than an expected fifteen. Some versions go further, stating that every clock aboard showed the shorter interval, that the distance counter did not advance and that aviation authorities confirmed the discrepancy after landing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.espaciomisterio.com/enigmas-y-anomalia/vuelo-502-nube-devoro-tiempo_59367" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: espaciomisterio.com">[espaciomisterio.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">espaciomisterio.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Espacio Misterio El vuelo 502 y la nube que devoró el tiempo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Espacio Misterio El vuelo 502 y la nube que devoró el tiempo</span></span></span>

The problem is not the subtraction. It is the provenance of the numbers.

For the claim to be strong, researchers would need to establish at least four separate times:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the precise moment the aircraft entered the reported cloud;
* the moment radio communication stopped;
* the moment it resumed;
* and the aircraft’s actual arrival or landing time at Santander.

</div>

Ideally, those times would come from independent records: cockpit notes, radio logs from Bilbao and Santander, flight-plan amendments, airline movement sheets or a captain’s written report. No such primary record is reproduced by the accessible articles that popularised the case. Instead, they present the seven-, 24- and 32-minute figures as settled facts while citing one another, television treatments or general paranormal literature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.espaciomisterio.com/enigmas-y-anomalia/vuelo-502-nube-devoro-tiempo_59367" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: espaciomisterio.com">[Espacio Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">espaciomisterio.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Espacio Misterio El vuelo 502 y la nube que devoró el tiempo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Espacio Misterio El vuelo 502 y la nube que devoró el tiempo</span></span></span>

This creates a circular-source problem. Numerous websites agreeing on the same numbers may appear to provide corroboration, but agreement has little evidential value when the accounts descend from the same earlier narrative. Several later articles use almost identical sequences: bad weather at Bilbao, diversion to Santander, brilliant lenticular cloud, total instrument failure, seven minutes aboard, 24 minutes on the ground and a seventeen-minute gap. Repetition has made the chronology precise without showing where that precision originated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elpensante.com/el-avion-que-viajo-en-el-tiempo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elpensante.com">[elpensante.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elpensante.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El Pensante El avión que viajó en el tiempo &#124; El Pensante</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El PensanteEl avión que viajó en el tiempo &#124; El PensanteJanuary 16, 2020 — El avión que viajó en el tiempo &#124; El Pensante ◄ El insecto más...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 16, 2020</span></span></span>

The phrase “missing time” also encourages a stronger interpretation than the evidence warrants. A communications gap is not automatically a time anomaly. Radio contact can be delayed by terrain, frequency changes, workload in the cockpit, incomplete logging or unsuccessful calls that were not heard or acknowledged. Likewise, an expected fifteen-minute diversion is only an estimate. Heading changes, altitude adjustments, holding, navigation around weather and the geometry of the route could extend it. Before interpreting a difference as physically missing time, investigators would have to show that all ordinary additions to the flight time had been removed from the calculation.

The story’s precision is therefore misleading. “Seventeen minutes” sounds like an instrumentally measured quantity, but in the available public record it is better understood as a number reported by later narrators rather than a verified finding from two surviving clock records.

## Missing records and the limits of the legend

Spain does possess a substantial official archive of reports about unidentified aerial phenomena. The Ministry of Defence’s virtual library hosts declassified Air Force files covering cases investigated between 1962 and 1995. The digitised collection contains 80 files and roughly 1,900 pages, including military correspondence, witness material and technical assessments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[gob.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

Flight 502, however, has not been located in the accessible catalogue under its flight number, captain’s name or the Bilbao–Santander account. That does not prove that no report was ever written. An Aviaco document could have remained in company files, passed through civil rather than military channels, been discarded under routine retention rules or been catalogued under wording that current searches do not reveal. What it does mean is that statements claiming an official file “remains open” cannot presently be checked against a publicly identifiable file.

The same caution applies to the alleged investigation. Recent mystery reporting says authorities ruled out conventional mechanical failure, lightning and static electricity before closing a technical investigation without a conclusion. Yet the article does not reproduce the investigating authority’s report, its reference number, its tests or the names of the engineers involved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.espaciomisterio.com/enigmas-y-anomalia/vuelo-502-nube-devoro-tiempo_59367" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: espaciomisterio.com">[Espacio Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">espaciomisterio.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Espacio Misterio El vuelo 502 y la nube que devoró el tiempo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Espacio Misterio El vuelo 502 y la nube que devoró el tiempo</span></span></span> Such claims should therefore be attributed to the later narrative, not treated as independently established findings.

There is also little accessible contemporary press support. Searches of Spanish newspaper archives and official sources do not readily produce a dated report from February 1978 describing an Aviaco aircraft arriving at Santander after unexplained instrument failures. This silence is not decisive: local newspaper collections are incomplete, search recognition can fail on scanned pages, and an incident without casualties might have received little attention. Nevertheless, the lack of a traceable report published close to the event weakens confidence in its later, highly detailed form.

The named captain gives the story a firmer human anchor than an anonymous legend. ## Did Flight 502 really lose seventeen minutes?

On the evidence currently available to the public, the answer is no: a literal loss of seventeen minutes has not been demonstrated.

The case is best divided into three levels of confidence.

**Reasonably plausible:** An Aviaco flight associated with García Bermúdez was diverted from Bilbao towards Santander in poor weather and the crew later described an alarming encounter with an unusual cloud or atmospheric condition. The persistence of the captain’s name, aircraft type and route across accounts suggests that the story may rest on a real flight experience rather than being wholly invented.

**Possible but unverified:** The crew suffered radio or instrument abnormalities. These details are aviation-specific and may derive from pilot testimony, but no cockpit report, maintenance entry or controller record is publicly available to establish their number, duration or severity.

**Weakly supported:** Ground clocks recorded 24 minutes while every clock aboard recorded seven, producing an exact seventeen-minute discontinuity. This is the central legend, yet it is also the claim most dependent on missing primary records.

Later reporting has expanded rather than strengthened the evidence. Newer versions add dramatic technical conclusions, assertions that the investigation remains open and comparisons with supposed space-time disturbances, but they do not supply the original logs required to test the arithmetic. A 2022 article even frames the story as an urban legend while repeating the same unsupported timing sequence and placing it alongside demonstrably fictional “lost flight” tales.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://larepublica.pe/datos-lr/respuestas/2022/10/09/leyenda-urbana-la-historia-del-vuelo-502-que-se-detuvo-en-el-tiempo-por-mas-de-15-minutos-evat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: larepublica.pe">[La República]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">larepublica.pe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on larepublica.pe.</span></span></span> That context does not disprove Flight 502, but it illustrates how easily an unresolved aviation anecdote can migrate into folklore.

For Biscay’s UFO history, the case remains significant because it shows how a local aviation incident can become the province’s defining mystery despite a thin archive. Its memorable elements — Bilbao airport, a veteran pilot, a glowing cloud, failing instruments and a precise gap in time — are ideal for retelling. The evidential weakness lies precisely where the story appears strongest: in the exact numbers.

Until a dated captain’s report, airline movement record, controller log or other contemporary documentation emerges, Flight 502 should be classified as a witness-led aviation legend with an unconfirmed missing-time claim. Something unusual may have happened during the diversion. The public evidence does not show that seventeen minutes disappeared.
