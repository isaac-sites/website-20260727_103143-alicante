## Introduction

On 22 June 1976, witnesses across the Canary Islands and on ships at sea watched a silent luminous display develop over the Atlantic. The clearest official chronology begins at 21:27 Zulu time, when the Spanish Navy corvette *Atrevida*, sailing off Fuerteventura, reported a white light rising, turning blue and forming a large circular halo. Similar descriptions followed from Gran Canaria and elsewhere in the archipelago. The halo remained visible until about 22:10, making this a sustained mass sighting rather than a fleeting meteor or an isolated mistake.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brief On 22 June 1976 at 21:27Z, the Spanish Navy corvette Atrevida, sailing parallel to Fuerteventura, watched a white...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

The event became known as the Gáldar case because its most dramatic testimony came from the north of Gran Canaria. Yet the geographical spread is also the strongest reason not to reconstruct the main phenomenon as a craft hovering near Gáldar. A display seen from separated islands and vessels was more probably extremely distant, very high, or both. ## The sighting from 21:27 to 22:10

The official Spanish Air Force file, numbered 760622, provides the firmest framework for reconstructing the evening. Its opening summary records that at 21:27 Z the crew of the *Atrevida* saw a white light apparently emerge from the interior of Fuerteventura and move slowly towards the island’s airport. That apparent origin should not be read as a measured location: from a ship at sea, a distant light low above the horizon could easily have appeared to rise from the island itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brief On 22 June 1976 at 21:27Z, the Spanish Navy corvette Atrevida, sailing parallel to Fuerteventura, watched a white...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

After reaching an elevation estimated at 15 to 18 degrees, the light appeared to stop. Its intensity increased, its colour became bluish and a circular halo formed around it. Roughly two minutes later, the central focus divided. A smaller portion remained lower within a bluish cloud, while an upper portion climbed rapidly in an irregular spiral before disappearing. The original halo, however, stayed approximately fixed and remained visible until 22:10 Z. The ship’s surface radar showed no corresponding target.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brief On 22 June 1976 at 21:27Z, the Spanish Navy corvette Atrevida, sailing parallel to Fuerteventura, watched a white...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

The reported duration of more than 40 minutes applied chiefly to the lingering luminous cloud, not necessarily to a solid-looking object performing manoeuvres for the entire period. That distinction is central to the later missile interpretation. Rocket exhaust can expand and remain sunlit at high altitude long after the vehicle or brightest exhaust source has moved beyond naked-eye visibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bibliotecapleyades.net/ciencia/ufo_briefingdocument/1976a.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecapleyades.net">[Biblioteca Pleyades]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecapleyades.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Biblioteca Pleyades UFO Briefing Doc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It remained like this for approximately two minutes. The published times can appear confusing because the military file uses Zulu time, equivalent to UTC, whereas newspapers and witnesses sometimes reported civil clock time. Accounts that place the display at around 22:27 or later may therefore be describing the same opening stage using local summer time rather than contradicting the naval record. The more reliable reconstruction follows the internal sequence and intervals in the file rather than forcing every later recollection into a single clock convention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brief On 22 June 1976 at 21:27Z, the Spanish Navy corvette Atrevida, sailing parallel to Fuerteventura, watched a white...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

## Reports from Gran Canaria, other islands and ships

Three minutes after the *Atrevida* observation began, the Air Force summary records reports of a comparable phenomenon from several points on Gran Canaria. Witnesses around Gáldar, Las Rosas, Boca Barranco and other northern locations described red, blue or transparent luminous forms, expanding circles and lights that seemed to move towards Tenerife or remain suspended above the islands. A municipal policeman in Gáldar, for example, reported a reddish arrow-like object followed by an intense blue circle that enlarged before dissolving.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/avistamiento-de-fenomenos-extranos-en-canarias-22-de-junio-de-1976.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on planetabenitez.com.</span></span></span>

At Pozo de las Nieves, in Gran Canaria’s mountainous interior, a military sergeant reported seeing a circular object emerge from behind Tenerife at about 21:45 Z. He estimated an apparent diameter of three full moons and watched it rise to roughly 45 degrees before disappearing after about ten minutes. This observation is particularly useful because it illustrates how location estimates varied. What appeared to rise from Fuerteventura to the crew of the *Atrevida* could appear behind Tenerife from a different line of sight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brief On 22 June 1976 at 21:27Z, the Spanish Navy corvette Atrevida, sailing parallel to Fuerteventura, watched a white...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

Reports were not confined to [Las Palmas]({{ 'las-palmas/' | relative_url }}) province. Later summaries of the file and contemporary press material place observers on Tenerife, La Palma and La Gomera, with additional claims from aviation personnel, communications staff and vessels in Atlantic waters. Although the reliability and detail of individual reports differ, the broad agreement concerns a huge, silent, coloured halo rather than a sharply defined machine passing close to each observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/canary-islands-1976/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uapcodex.org.</span></span></span>

The maritime evidence gives the reconstruction particular weight. A naval crew could provide a timed observation from a known moving platform, and its report was transmitted through official channels rather than assembled solely from later newspaper interviews. Accounts associated with merchant shipping also described a luminous disc or cloud large enough for stars to remain visible through it, a characteristic more compatible with translucent gas than with an opaque physical body.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bibliotecapleyades.net/ciencia/ufo_briefingdocument/1976a.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecapleyades.net">[Biblioteca Pleyades]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecapleyades.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Biblioteca Pleyades UFO Briefing Doc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It remained like this for approximately two minutes. At the same time, professional status should not be confused with immunity from visual error. Sailors, soldiers and air-traffic personnel can report direction, timing and apparent motion carefully, but a single distant light offers no direct measure of range. The lack of a radar return on the *Atrevida* therefore matters: it does not prove that nothing physical was present, but it weakens the idea of a large solid craft moving close to the ship or islands. A tenuous exhaust cloud at high altitude would not be expected to produce the kind of surface-radar echo associated with an aircraft or vessel.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brief On 22 June 1976 at 21:27Z, the Spanish Navy corvette Atrevida, sailing parallel to Fuerteventura, watched a white...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

## Why Gáldar became the name of an archipelago-wide event

The label “Gáldar incident” survives largely because the most memorable account came from a taxi journey near Las Rosas. A doctor and the driver said they saw a transparent sphere close to the ground, approximately 25 to 30 metres across, with two tall reddish figures inside. The object was said to be only 15 to 20 metres away. A third person in the taxi did not provide equivalent confirmation, while another local witness reported a large blue ball and possible figures but expressed uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/avistamiento-de-fenomenos-extranos-en-canarias-22-de-junio-de-1976.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on planetabenitez.com.</span></span></span>

This close-range story was far more dramatic than the distant halo and consequently came to dominate popular retellings. It supplied a specific place, recognisable witnesses and apparent occupants, whereas the wider display was an immense but impersonal light in the sky. Local and national media, followed by UFO writers, increasingly joined the two levels of testimony into a single narrative: a craft seen across the islands had supposedly descended at Gáldar and revealed its crew.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eldia.es/islas/2023/09/14/ovni-galdar-92066515.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eldia.es">[El Día]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eldia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El Día OVNI CANARIAS &#124; La verdad sobre el ‘ovni de Gáldar’</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El Día OVNI CANARIAS &#124; La verdad sobre el ‘ovni de Gáldar’</span></span></span>

## The viewing area changes the interpretation

The apparent simultaneity across the archipelago creates a simple geometrical problem for the near-craft interpretation. Gran Canaria, Tenerife, La Palma, La Gomera and Fuerteventura are separated by substantial distances. An object tens of metres above fields near Gáldar could not produce a huge, similarly shaped halo in the sky for observers spread across those islands and on ships far offshore.

A remote luminous cloud, by contrast, could be viewed from all of them. Each witness would project it against a different foreground: one observer might place it over Fuerteventura, another behind Tenerife and another above open ocean. Without reliable triangulation, these apparent positions do not identify the source’s true location. They instead indicate that observers were seeing the same distant display along different lines of sight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/canary-islands-1976/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uapcodex.org.</span></span></span>

The scale also explains why witnesses could honestly describe the light as enormous or nearby. People estimate the size of an unfamiliar object by unconsciously assuming a distance. If a luminous cloud hundreds of kilometres away is interpreted as something just beyond a hillside, its apparent angular width translates mentally into a gigantic nearby sphere. Descriptions of the glow illuminating sea or land are likewise impressions of brightness, not range measurements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapglobe.com/cases/canary-islands-1976" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapglobe.com">[UAP Globe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapglobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">canary islands 1976</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP GlobeThe Canary Islands Sphere (1976) (22 June 1976) &#124; UFO Case File &#124; UAP GlobeIslands, Spain, on the night of 22 June 1976 the sky...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

Meteors normally cross the visible sky within seconds, and conventional aircraft lights would not expand into a translucent halo that remained almost stationary for roughly 40 minutes. ## The Poseidon reconstruction

The leading later explanation is that the witnesses observed exhaust and staging effects from one or more Poseidon C-3 submarine-launched ballistic missiles fired over the Atlantic test range west of the Canary Islands. Missile chronology records place Poseidon activity on 22 June 1976, the exact date of the sighting. Spanish researchers Vicente-Juan Ballester Olmos and Ricardo Campo later argued in the Spanish Air Force’s professional journal that a series of spectacular Canary Islands sightings between 1974 and 1979 matched Poseidon tests rather than unknown craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuclearcompanion.com/data/poseidon-c3-missile-chronology/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuclearcompanion.com">[Nuclear Companion]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuclearcompanion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nuclear Companion Poseidon (C3) Missile Chronology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nuclear Companion Poseidon (C3) Missile Chronology</span></span></span>

The proposed mechanism does not require the missile itself to look like a vast sphere. After launch, hot exhaust and released gases expand rapidly as atmospheric pressure falls. At high altitude, the cloud can grow to an enormous physical size. Because it is above the Earth’s shadow, it may still receive direct sunlight even though observers below have passed into evening darkness. The result is a brilliant translucent bubble, fan or halo, sometimes accompanied by a moving central light and spiral-like trails.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/our-collections/photography/numerical-list-of-images/nhhc-series/naval-subjects-collection/l55-weapons/l55-15/poseidon-missiles/l55-15-04-01-poseidon-missile-launch.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on navy.mil.</span></span></span>

This model accounts for the main features unusually well:

* **The western Atlantic setting:** submarine missile testing occurred far offshore, allowing a plume to be seen from several islands.
* **The expanding sphere:** exhaust dispersal produces a growing translucent cloud rather than a rigid outline.
* **The blue, white and reddish colours:** twilight illumination and atmospheric scattering alter the colour of the plume.
* **The split and spiral motion:** staging, venting or separate missile components can create multiple moving lights and curved trails.
* **The long persistence:** exhaust remains visible and spreads after the bright vehicle has disappeared.
* **The silence and radar absence:** a very distant high-altitude event would neither sound nearby nor necessarily register on a ship’s surface-search radar.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapedia.ai/wiki/the-canary-islands-uap-incident-1976/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapedia.ai">[UAPedia - Unlocking New Realities]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapedia.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Later cross-disciplinary work has associated the display with U.S. Navy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unlocking New RealitiesThe Canary Islands UAP Incident (1976) - UAPediaThe case illustrates how mass sightings, lighting geomet...</span></span></span>

## What the reconstruction leaves unresolved

The main mass sighting is no longer best described as wholly mysterious. Its timing, form, geographical reach and duration fit a distant aerospace event far better than a craft operating locally over Gáldar. The wide witness base strengthens confidence that an extraordinary display genuinely occurred, but it simultaneously weakens the claim that the source was a nearby vehicle. Mass testimony establishes the light; it does not establish the witnesses’ estimates of its distance or nature.

The alleged transparent sphere and two figures remain less securely explained because they are not simply another angle on the common island-wide description. They require a second event very close to the ground, witnessed clearly by only a small number of people, at almost exactly the time when the sky contained a spectacular unfamiliar stimulus. No radar trace, photograph, physical residue or broad independent witness cluster confirms that separate object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brief On 22 June 1976 at 21:27Z, the Spanish Navy corvette Atrevida, sailing parallel to Fuerteventura, watched a white...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

Several possibilities remain: the doctor and driver may have misjudged the distant plume as a nearby transparent body; foreground objects, reflections or irregular bright regions may have supplied the impression of figures; or later interviews may have sharpened an initially ambiguous experience. A genuinely separate close-range event cannot be disproved from the surviving paperwork, but it is much less well supported than the mass sighting and should not determine the classification of the entire case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bibliotecapleyades.net/ciencia/ufo_briefingdocument/1976a.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecapleyades.net">[Biblioteca Pleyades]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecapleyades.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Biblioteca Pleyades UFO Briefing Doc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It remained like this for approximately two minutes. Reconstructed carefully, the Gáldar sighting is therefore two stories with different evidential strengths. The first is a well-documented, multi-island observation of a huge luminous atmospheric display, probably caused by Poseidon missile testing. The second is a striking but weakly corroborated close encounter that became attached to it. Keeping those stories separate makes the case more intelligible and preserves what is genuinely important in Las Palmas UFO history: hundreds of witnesses may accurately report an extraordinary sky event while still being profoundly mistaken about its distance, size and source.
