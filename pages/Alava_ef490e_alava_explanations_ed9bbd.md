## Introduction

Many UFO reports from Álava remain debated not because they provide strong evidence of extraordinary craft, but because ordinary explanations are not always immediately obvious to witnesses. The province’s UFO history is largely a record of lights, shapes and movements reported by residents, later examined through the possibilities of aircraft, astronomical objects, atmospheric effects, military activity and human perception. Local investigators such as José Luis Guillerna collected many Álava testimonies over decades, helping preserve the history of these reports while also illustrating the challenge: a genuine observation does not automatically mean a correct identification of what was seen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eitb.eus/es/audios/detalle/2316244/avistamientos-ovnis-alava-jose-luis-guillerna-35-anos-investigando/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eitb.eus">[EITB]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eitb.eus</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Avistamientos OVNIS en Alava. ## Common explanations for Álava sightings

The most common sceptical interpretations of Álava UFO reports fall into a few broad categories. These explanations do not claim that witnesses invented events; instead, they suggest that normal objects or natural phenomena can appear unusual when seen briefly, at a distance, or under poor viewing conditions.

### Aircraft and aviation misunderstandings

Aircraft are among the most frequent explanations considered in UFO investigations. At night, a distant plane approaching head-on can appear almost stationary because its movement is mostly towards the observer rather than across their field of view. Navigation lights, landing lights and changes in altitude can also create the impression of sudden acceleration, hovering or unusual manoeuvres.

This possibility is particularly relevant in a province like Álava, where observations may occur near routes used by civilian and military aviation. A witness who sees a bright light without knowing the aircraft’s distance, direction or speed may describe movements that seem impossible but are consistent with normal flight behaviour.

Official investigations in other countries have repeatedly found that aircraft, along with astronomical objects and balloons, account for a large share of identifiable UFO reports. The former US Air Force Project Blue Book, which examined thousands of reports between 1947 and 1969, concluded that no investigated case provided evidence of extraterrestrial technology and that many reports had conventional explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.secretsdeclassified.af.mil/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/459832/project-blue-book/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: secretsdeclassified.af.mil">[secretsdeclassified.af.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">secretsdeclassified.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book &gt; Air Force Declassification Office &gt; Display</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book &gt; Air Force Declassification Office &gt; Display</span></span></span>

### Stars, planets and astronomical objects

Bright celestial objects are another recurring explanation. Venus is especially important because it can appear as an extremely bright, steady light close to the horizon. Under changing atmospheric conditions, it may appear to shimmer, change colour or move slightly due to the movement of the observer’s eyes or the effects of haze.

Other astronomical events, including meteors and satellite re-entries, can produce brief but striking observations. A meteor may appear to travel at exceptional speed, while a satellite catching sunlight can appear as a moving light that disappears suddenly when it enters the Earth’s shadow.

For Álava reports based mainly on descriptions of lights rather than detailed measurements, astronomy is often difficult to exclude after the event. Without exact times, viewing directions and independent observations, investigators may be unable to distinguish a rare atmospheric appearance from an unusual object. Studies of UFO identification have found that bright stars and planets, aircraft and other ordinary sources explain a large proportion of investigated cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identification_studies_of_UFOs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Identification studies of UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Identification studies of UFOs</span></span></span>

### Balloons, atmospheric effects and distant lights

Balloons and atmospheric phenomena can also create misleading impressions. Weather balloons, research balloons and other airborne objects may move differently from aircraft because they are carried by wind currents. From the ground, their speed and direction can be difficult to judge.

Atmospheric conditions add another layer of uncertainty. Reflections, haze, temperature differences in the air and distorted views across long distances can change how lights and objects appear. A distant source may seem to move when the observer is moving, or appear to change shape as visibility conditions shift.

These explanations are especially relevant for historical UFO reports because many were made before widespread smartphone recording and modern tracking tools. Older accounts often depended on memory, sketches and written descriptions rather than multiple independent recordings.

## How investigators test UFO claims

A careful investigation begins by separating the observation from the interpretation. A witness may accurately report “a bright object moved across the sky” while the conclusion that it was an unknown craft requires additional evidence.

A stronger investigation normally asks several questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* **When did the observation occur?** Exact time allows comparison with aircraft movements, astronomical events and satellite passes.
* **Where was the object seen?** Direction, elevation and location help estimate distance and possible sources.
* **Were there independent witnesses?** Multiple observers can strengthen a report, especially if they describe the same features.
* **Was there physical or technical evidence?** Radar records, photographs, video, flight data or official documents provide more reliable information than memory alone.
* **Did the object behave in a way that rules out ordinary causes?** Claims of extreme speed or unusual manoeuvres require particularly strong evidence.

</div>

Spain’s Ministry of Defence preserved and later released UFO investigation files, making many historical cases available for review. These archives are useful because they show how investigators approached reports, but the existence of a file does not itself confirm that the reported object was extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

For Álava, the main difficulty is that many reports collected by local researchers were based primarily on testimony. ## Why unresolved does not mean extraordinary

One of the most important distinctions in UFO analysis is the difference between “unidentified” and “unexplainable”. A sighting may remain unresolved because there is not enough information, not because the remaining explanation must be extraordinary.

This issue appears repeatedly in official UFO investigations. The Spanish military archive contains reports of unusual aerial observations, but the purpose of these investigations was to analyse unidentified events, not to confirm alien technology. The archive itself reflects a process of evaluation rather than proof of extraordinary origins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

A sceptical interpretation of Álava’s UFO history therefore does not require dismissing every report. Instead, it ranks explanations according to evidence:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Well explained cases:** Reports where aircraft, astronomical objects or other known causes match the details.
* **Weakly supported claims:** Reports based mainly on memory, with little independent evidence.
* **Genuinely unresolved cases:** Reports where available information is insufficient to determine the cause.

</div>

The last category is where many UFO stories remain. An unresolved report can be interesting because it shows the limits of available evidence, but it does not by itself demonstrate an extraordinary event.

## The role of perception in Álava’s UFO debates

Human perception is a major factor in interpreting unusual [sightings]({{ 'sightings-86791c/' | relative_url }}). People are generally good at recognising familiar objects under normal conditions, but judging distance, speed and size in the open sky is difficult. A small object nearby can appear large and distant, while a large object far away can appear small and stationary.

Memory also changes over time. A dramatic observation may become more detailed as it is discussed, especially when later interpretations influence how the original event is remembered. This does not mean witnesses are dishonest; it means that human observation has limits, particularly for short-lived events without instruments.

For Álava’s UFO history, this helps explain why some reports continue to attract interest decades later. The mystery often comes from the gap between what someone experienced and what can be demonstrated afterwards.
