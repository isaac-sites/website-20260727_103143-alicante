## Introduction

Atmospheric refraction is one of the strongest conventional explanations for the Motril lights of 17 November 1979, but it is not a complete solution to the case. A temperature inversion above the [Granada]({{ 'granadas-ufo-history-beyond-the-motril/' | relative_url }}) coast or the Alboran Sea could have bent light from distant aircraft, ships or coastal installations, making three separate sources appear unusually bright, displaced or almost impossible to approach. The same layered atmosphere could also have disturbed radar propagation, creating intermittent or misleading returns.

This matters because the most striking visual detail—the fighter pilot’s failure to reduce the apparent distance after flying towards the lights for about ten minutes—is exactly the sort of behaviour expected when the observer is pursuing refracted light rather than a nearby object. Yet the official record contains no detailed atmospheric profile for the route, no confirmed source for the three lights and no convincing link between refraction and the children’s voices reportedly heard on the military radio. ## How temperature inversions distort light and radar

Under ordinary conditions, air temperature generally falls with height. During a temperature inversion, a layer of warmer air sits above cooler air, producing a sharper-than-normal change in air density. Light and radio waves crossing that boundary can be bent away from their usual paths. In coastal areas, where cool, moist air near the sea can lie beneath warmer and drier air, the atmosphere may become strongly layered even while the sky appears clear.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://prod-01-alb-www-noaa.woc.noaa.gov/jetstream/ocean/marine-layer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prod-01-alb-www-noaa.woc.noaa.gov">[NOAA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prod-01-alb-www-noaa.woc.noaa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Marine Layer &#124; National Oceanic and Atmospheric AdministrationThe Marine Layer &#124; National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration...</span></span></span>

For visible light, this can create superior mirages and related effects. A distant light below or close to the normal horizon may be lifted into view, vertically displaced, stretched, duplicated or made unstable. Because the source may be tens or hundreds of kilometres away, its position in the sky does not behave like that of a nearby aircraft. An observer can fly towards the apparent image while seeing little obvious change in its angular position or estimated distance.

That distinction is important in Motril. The pilot did not report closing on a clearly outlined vehicle. He reported three strong points of similar colour and intensity, arranged as an isosceles triangle. Points of light offer very few distance cues at night or twilight: without a visible body, known dimensions, background detail or measurable parallax, the brain can easily interpret separated lights as one formation. The official narrative says that the aircraft headed towards the centre of the triangle but that the relative distance did not decrease. It also states that the pilot obtained no radar lock on the lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.granadahoy.com/granada/Defensa-publica-informe-avistamiento-Motril_0_1075392706.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: granadahoy.com">[Granada Hoy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">granadahoy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Granada Hoy Defensa publica un informe del avistamiento de un ovni en Motril</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">En el documento se explica que los testigos, un controlador y un piloto de caza que despegó desde Albacete, vieron &quot;tres luces fuertes de...</span></span></span>

Radio and radar can be affected by a related process. The refractive index of air depends on temperature, pressure and water vapour. A steep vertical gradient can produce “super-refraction”, in which radio waves bend more strongly towards the Earth, or ducting, in which part of the signal becomes trapped within an atmospheric layer. International Telecommunication Union guidance notes that ducts can carry signals beyond their normal line-of-sight range, alter apparent arrival angles and cause multipath interference. Marine research likewise shows that temperature inversions and sharp humidity changes above the sea can significantly change microwave propagation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-r/opb/hdb/R-HDB-26-2013-TOC-HTM-E.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: itu.int">[itu.int]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">itu.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">R HDB 26 2013 TOC HTM E</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

For air-defence radar, anomalous propagation can create several kinds of confusion. A beam may reach distant terrain, ships or weather layers that would normally lie below the radar horizon. Reflected energy can then appear as an unexpected contact. The return may be weak, elongated, intermittent or located at a misleading range or height. Atmospheric ducting does not manufacture a perfect radar image of a structured aircraft, but it can produce transient echoes and apparent tracks that disappear as the propagation conditions change.

The sea is especially relevant. Evaporation continuously produces a sharp humidity gradient immediately above the water, and evaporation ducts are common over oceans. Larger surface-based or elevated ducts can form when warm, dry air overlies cooler marine air. These effects are well recognised in naval radar and communications because they can extend detection ranges in some directions while creating blind zones or false impressions in others.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.617-6-202509-I%21%21TOC-HTM-E.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: itu.int">[ITU]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">itu.int</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on itu.int.</span></span></span>

## Which Motril details fit the theory

The refraction explanation works best when applied to the visual geometry and the uncertain radar context, rather than to every element of the story.

**The lights did not become appreciably closer.** This is the clearest point in its favour. If the three lights were refracted images of distant sources, flying towards their apparent position would not necessarily bring the fighter nearer to the real emitters. The apparent image could remain close to the same bearing, retreat as the aircraft moved or disappear when the viewing angle left the refracting layer. That is a more economical explanation than assuming that three objects matched the fighter’s motion precisely for ten minutes while maintaining an unchanged formation.

**Only lights were seen.** The surviving summary describes three intense lights but no fuselage, outline, surface, exhaust or other physical structure. The absence of a visible body is compatible with distant navigation lights, ship lights or fixed coastal lighting seen under distorted conditions. It does not prove that any of these were the source, but it leaves the mirage hypothesis open.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.granadahoy.com/granada/Defensa-publica-informe-avistamiento-Motril_0_1075392706.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: granadahoy.com">[Granada Hoy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">granadahoy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Granada Hoy Defensa publica un informe del avistamiento de un ovni en Motril</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">En el documento se explica que los testigos, un controlador y un piloto de caza que despegó desde Albacete, vieron &quot;tres luces fuertes de...</span></span></span>

**The triangular arrangement need not imply a triangular craft.** Three unrelated lights can appear connected when they share colour, brightness and apparent motion. If refraction displaced several sources by similar amounts, they might retain a stable geometric relationship for part of the observation. A triangle is also a particularly easy pattern for the eye to impose because any three non-collinear points define one.

There is, however, an important limitation. A refracting layer normally alters light that already exists; it does not explain where the three original sources were. A strong case would identify plausible lights in the correct direction—aircraft, ships, offshore activity, lighthouses or coastal installations—and then show that the geometry and atmospheric profile could reproduce the observation. The public record does not provide that reconstruction.

**The lack of radar lock supports caution about a solid target.** The fighter reportedly failed to lock its own radar on the three lights. That is consistent with distant optical sources or mirage images, which would have no reflecting body at their apparent position. It is less compatible with the simplest interpretation of three nearby, solid aircraft-sized objects, although radar performance also depends on range, aspect, altitude, equipment settings and target reflectivity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publicaciones.defensa.gob.es/media/downloadable/files/links/d/e/de-w-9-motril-a-eva-n-9-1955-2023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publicaciones.defensa.gob.es">[publicaciones.defensa.gob.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publicaciones.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Informe desclasificado sobre el incidente ovni de Motril, 17 de noviembre de 1979 (composición de los autores)...</span></span></span>

**The earlier ground-radar contact may have been anomalous propagation.** The Motril sequence reportedly began when the Pegaso control system detected an unidentified trace moving towards Air Surveillance Squadron No. 9. The contact became intermittent and disappeared while the fighter was searching the area. Such instability is compatible with propagation effects, particularly when a radar beam is being bent towards remote ground or sea targets. It is not diagnostic: a real aircraft can also be lost because of terrain, altitude, manoeuvre or weak reflection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publicaciones.defensa.gob.es/media/downloadable/files/links/d/e/de-w-9-motril-a-eva-n-9-1955-2023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publicaciones.defensa.gob.es">[publicaciones.defensa.gob.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publicaciones.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Informe desclasificado sobre el incidente ovni de Motril, 17 de noviembre de 1979 (composición de los autores)...</span></span></span>

The later Madrid material in the same group of files shows why radar wording must be treated carefully. In that episode, echoes were described as fleeting, elongated and diffuse, sometimes disappearing on the next sweep, while the intercepting crew saw no corresponding object. Those are characteristics that can be consistent with anomalous propagation, although the Madrid incident should not be treated as proof of the atmospheric conditions over Motril eleven days earlier.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1979-11-11-17-28_avistamiento_en_valencia-motril-madrid-ii.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive</span></span></span>

**Clear visibility does not rule out an inversion.** Reports that the weather was excellent or the sky clear are sometimes presented as arguments against an atmospheric explanation. In fact, an inversion describes the vertical temperature structure of the air, not necessarily cloud, rain or poor visibility. High pressure and stable air can produce both clear conditions and pronounced layering. ## What the theory does not explain well

Refraction is often discussed as though it must explain the entire Motril narrative or none of it. A more realistic assessment is that different parts of the incident may have had different causes.

The visual lights and the radar trace were not necessarily manifestations of the same thing. The radar contact had reportedly disappeared before or during the fighter’s search, while the three lights were noticed later as the pilot was returning at about 35,000 feet. Without continuous tracking that tied the radar position to the visual bearings, treating them as a single object is an inference rather than an established fact. The atmospheric hypothesis may explain both independently, but the evidence does not require one unified cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publicaciones.defensa.gob.es/media/downloadable/files/links/d/e/de-w-9-motril-a-eva-n-9-1955-2023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publicaciones.defensa.gob.es">[publicaciones.defensa.gob.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publicaciones.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Informe desclasificado sobre el incidente ovni de Motril, 17 de noviembre de 1979 (composición de los autores)...</span></span></span>

Nor does refraction readily explain why the lights reportedly had similar colour and intensity. It could preserve or alter the appearance of real sources, but a detailed explanation would need to specify those sources. Suggestions involving ships, aircraft or coastal lights remain possibilities, not identifications.

The duration is another complication. A stable inversion can persist long enough for a ten-minute observation, particularly over the sea, but mirage images are sensitive to altitude, viewing angle and changes in the refractive layer. A fighter travelling at high speed would be moving through a rapidly changing geometry. The continued visibility of a neatly arranged formation is therefore possible but not automatically expected. A quantitative reconstruction would need the aircraft’s precise course, altitude, bearings to each light and the vertical refractivity profile along the line of sight.

There is also no indication in the public summary that the pilot described classic visible mirage features such as vertical stretching, duplication, shimmering or inversion of an image. That absence does not disprove refraction, since point lights may simply appear displaced or unstable, but it means the report lacks the most recognisable optical clues.

## The radio problem and missing weather data

The children’s voices are the hardest part of the Motril account to connect with atmospheric refraction. According to the published official summary, the pilot heard laughter and a repeated greeting on ultrahigh-frequency channel 11 for about 30 seconds. He reportedly removed the guard channel and found that the voices continued to enter through channel 11 with similar clarity. The aircraft then returned to Los Llanos and landed without incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.granadahoy.com/granada/Defensa-publica-informe-avistamiento-Motril_0_1075392706.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: granadahoy.com">[Granada Hoy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">granadahoy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Granada Hoy Defensa publica un informe del avistamiento de un ovni en Motril</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">En el documento se explica que los testigos, un controlador y un piloto de caza que despegó desde Albacete, vieron &quot;tres luces fuertes de...</span></span></span>

Atmospheric ducting can carry radio transmissions farther than expected. In principle, it might allow a distant transmitter to be heard outside its normal service area, particularly over a long marine path. Ducts are known to extend line-of-sight communications and to produce interference between geographically separated systems using the same or nearby frequencies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.617-6-202509-I%21%21TOC-HTM-E.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: itu.int">[itu.int]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">itu.int</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on itu.int.</span></span></span>

That general mechanism is not enough to solve the radio episode. Several details would be needed:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* the exact operating frequency assigned to the channel;
* the receiver and radio configuration in the fighter;
* whether the audio was amplitude-, frequency- or otherwise modulated;
* the possibility of intermodulation, equipment leakage or cross-channel coupling;
* recordings or controller logs confirming that the transmission was heard by anyone else;
* information about civilian, military, maritime or broadcast transmitters using a compatible signal at the time.

</div>

None of those technical elements is established in the short public account. The phrase “voices through the radio” is therefore evidence of an unexplained reception, not evidence that the light source transmitted a message. The timing may encourage readers to join the two events, but coincidence, distant interference, accidental transmission or an onboard radio fault remain at least as plausible as a direct connection.

The greatest weakness in the refraction theory is the absence of contemporaneous vertical weather measurements in the accessible case summary. Surface observations saying that the weather was clear cannot show whether there was an inversion at several hundred or several thousand metres, nor whether a strong humidity gradient existed over the Alboran Sea. To test the explanation properly, investigators would need radiosonde data, upper-air charts, sea-surface temperatures and pressure patterns for the afternoon of 17 November 1979, preferably from stations around southern Spain and North Africa.

The official archive confirms that the main Valencia–Motril–Madrid file contains 147 pages and was declassified in 1994, while a later supplementary file contains 12 pages. Yet the Motril narrative available in press accounts and later official historical writing remains concise. It records the observation and the Air Force’s deductions but does not present a detailed optical ray trace, a reconstructed radar propagation model or a source identification for the lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160070576" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

This evidential gap cuts both ways. It prevents sceptics from claiming that a documented inversion conclusively caused the sighting. It also prevents proponents from treating the lack of an immediate identification as evidence of an extraordinary vehicle. The appropriate conclusion is conditional: the coastal atmosphere supplied a physically credible mechanism, but the surviving public evidence does not show that the required conditions were actually measured over Motril at the relevant time.

## A plausible explanation, not a demonstrated one

Atmospheric refraction explains several of the [Motril case]({{ 'motril-case/' | relative_url }})’s most important features with relatively few assumptions. It can account for distant lights appearing above or away from their true position, for the pilot’s inability to close the apparent distance, for the absence of a radar lock at the visual location and, potentially, for an earlier intermittent ground-radar trace. The maritime setting makes such effects more credible because strong temperature and humidity gradients commonly develop above the sea.

Its limits are equally clear. No source for the three lights has been identified. No surviving public reconstruction matches their bearings against ships, aircraft or fixed installations. No upper-air profile has been produced to demonstrate a suitable inversion along the viewing path. The theory also does not, by itself, explain the brief reception of children’s voices.

Within Granada’s UFO history, Motril is therefore best described as a partly explainable military observation rather than either a solved mirage or compelling evidence of an unknown craft. Refraction substantially lowers the need for an exotic interpretation, especially because the report concerns lights without a visible body. But without the missing weather, radar and radio data, it remains a strong hypothesis rather than a verified solution.
