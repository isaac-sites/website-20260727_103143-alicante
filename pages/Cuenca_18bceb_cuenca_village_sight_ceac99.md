## Introduction

[Cuenca]({{ 'cuencas-ufo-stories-under-the-evidence/' | relative_url }})’s later village sightings are memorable but difficult to judge. In Vellisca, local investigator Antonio Pastor recalls a silent greenish light seen in his youth and a second object in 1997 that traced a curved path before vanishing in a flash. Reports associated with Landete and Campillo de Altobuey have also survived through local radio retrospectives, while a photograph from a pilgrimage at Santa Cruz de Moya has been presented as showing unexplained luminous spheres. Yet none of these examples has the documentary depth of Cuenca’s officially recorded 1968 aerial case.

The central problem is not that village witnesses must have been mistaken. It is that the surviving material usually lacks exact timings, viewing directions, original photographic files, radar confirmation, weather reconstruction and a documented investigation conducted soon after the event. ## Accounts from Vellisca and nearby villages

Vellisca occupies a distinctive place in Cuenca’s UFO culture largely because of Antonio Pastor, a resident who has collected unusual-sky reports and discussed them publicly for more than half a century. His interest began, he says, when he was about 15 or 16. Returning home after a journey connected with his studies in Cuenca, he saw a very large light moving silently across the sky and leaving a greenish trail. Another resident reportedly approached him during the observation and confirmed that he, too, had noticed it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cadenaser.com/castillalamancha/2026/06/10/un-ovni-en-las-pinturas-rupestres-y-otros-avistamientos-en-los-pueblos-de-cuenca-ser-cuenca/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cadenaser.com">[Cadena SER]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cadenaser.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cadenaser.com.</span></span></span>

That account has two features that make it more interesting than an entirely solitary recollection: a second person apparently saw the phenomenon, and Pastor described both colour and motion rather than merely reporting a stationary point of light. Even so, the published version leaves crucial questions unanswered. It gives no precise date, duration, compass direction or elevation, and does not identify the second observer or preserve an independent statement from him. A green trail could accompany several ordinary phenomena, including a bright meteor, but the available details are too limited to test that possibility properly.

Pastor’s better-dated Vellisca report occurred on 9 October 1997. He said that he and another witness watched an object make a movement resembling the letter “D”, after which it produced a powerful flash and disappeared. The unusual path is the feature that most clearly resists an immediate identification: ordinary aircraft, stars and planets do not normally appear to draw a neat curved figure unless observer movement, cloud interference or changing reference points distort the apparent track.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cadenaser.com/castillalamancha/2026/06/10/un-ovni-en-las-pinturas-rupestres-y-otros-avistamientos-en-los-pueblos-de-cuenca-ser-cuenca/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cadenaser.com">[Cadena SER]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cadenaser.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cadenaser.com.</span></span></span>

However, the report survives primarily as Pastor’s retrospective description. The accessible account does not supply the observation’s start time, total duration, direction, angular size, colour or relationship to the horizon. Nor does it include a contemporary police report, aviation inquiry, meteorological record or statement from the accompanying witness. Without those controls, the “D” is a vivid description rather than a measurable flight path.

This distinction matters because Pastor is both a witness and a committed UFO investigator. Long experience collecting reports can make someone attentive to unusual details, but a firm prior belief can also shape how an ambiguous light is remembered and interpreted. In his 2026 interview, Pastor openly connected the sightings with his belief in non-human intelligence and a continuing presence throughout human history. That conviction does not invalidate what he saw, but it means the observation and the later explanation should be treated as separate claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cadenaser.com/castillalamancha/2026/06/10/un-ovni-en-las-pinturas-rupestres-y-otros-avistamientos-en-los-pueblos-de-cuenca-ser-cuenca/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cadenaser.com">[Cadena SER]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cadenaser.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cadenaser.com.</span></span></span>

## Landete and Campillo remain fragmentary cases

Landete and Campillo de Altobuey appear in Cuenca’s modern UFO storytelling through a local radio feature broadcast in January 2020. The programme described two incidents from earlier decades and suggested that one might have been connected with the famous Manises aviation case of November 1979. The surviving programme page, however, offers no full chronology, witness list or supporting documents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cadenaser.com/audio/1579610868_060543/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cadenaser.com">[Cadena SER]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cadenaser.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cadenaser.com.</span></span></span>

That suggested Manises connection should therefore be approached cautiously. Temporal proximity does not by itself establish that lights seen over different parts of Spain had the same cause. To make such a link persuasive, investigators would need matching observation times, directions, altitudes and behaviour, ideally supported by air-traffic or meteorological information. The brief published summary does not provide those elements.

The limited record also illustrates how a local sighting can remain culturally alive while becoming harder to investigate. A radio programme may preserve the names of villages and the broad outline of an event, but it cannot replace original testimony. Once dates, locations and viewing geometry are lost, later researchers cannot reliably check aircraft routes, astronomical objects, balloons, military activity or weather conditions.

Landete and Campillo are therefore best classified as reported incidents with inadequate surviving documentation. They are part of Cuenca’s UFO tradition because people continued to remember and retell them, not because the available evidence establishes an extraordinary aerial event.

## Flashes, flight paths and personal testimony

The repeated vocabulary of village sightings—silent lights, sudden flashes, rapid disappearance and apparently irregular movement—sounds dramatic, but each feature has more than one possible cause.

**Silence is weak evidence at long range.** A distant aircraft can be inaudible, particularly when wind carries its sound away from the observer. A light may also appear much closer than it actually is because a dark sky provides no reliable scale.

**A flash does not necessarily indicate instantaneous acceleration.** An aircraft can turn and briefly expose a bright landing light; sunlight can reflect from a high object; a meteor can flare before fading; and thin cloud can hide a light abruptly. Without a measured duration and direction, “it flashed and disappeared” cannot distinguish among these possibilities.

**Curved or zigzag movement can be perceptual.** A small light viewed without a fixed foreground reference may appear to wander because of involuntary eye movement, a well-known effect when observing isolated points in darkness. Handheld zoom video can amplify the same impression, making camera shake look like object manoeuvring.

**Personal testimony still has value.** Witness recollections establish that an unusual experience occurred and may preserve details unavailable elsewhere. But testimony becomes much stronger when collected independently, promptly and with structured questions. In the Vellisca cases, the reported presence of additional witnesses is potentially important, yet their separate accounts have not been published alongside Pastor’s version.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cadenaser.com/castillalamancha/2026/06/10/un-ovni-en-las-pinturas-rupestres-y-otros-avistamientos-en-los-pueblos-de-cuenca-ser-cuenca/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cadenaser.com">[Cadena SER]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cadenaser.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cadenaser.com.</span></span></span>

The contrast with Cuenca’s 1968 case is instructive. That event generated reports from an Iberia crew and military meteorological personnel, entered the Spanish Air Force’s documentary system and was later included in the Ministry of Defence’s digitised UFO files. Investigators could compare observers, altitude estimates and possible balloon activity. The later village stories, by contrast, generally reach the public through recollection and local media rather than a preserved official inquiry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cadenaser.com/emisora/2019/01/15/ser_cuenca/1547556298_743888.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cadenaser.com">[Cadena SER]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cadenaser.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cadena SEREl día que un ovni surcó los cielos de Cuenca en 1968 &#124; Actualidad &#124; Cadena SER...</span></span></span>

## Why photographs and orbs require caution

The best-known photographic claim in this village group concerns a picture taken during a pilgrimage at Santa Cruz de Moya. Pastor has drawn attention to luminous round forms visible in the image. He acknowledged that older cameras could produce defects, but argued that the negative was not damaged and that the bright spheres therefore deserved attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cadenaser.com/castillalamancha/2026/06/10/un-ovni-en-las-pinturas-rupestres-y-otros-avistamientos-en-los-pueblos-de-cuenca-ser-cuenca/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cadenaser.com">[Cadena SER]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cadenaser.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cadenaser.com.</span></span></span>

An undamaged negative does not, by itself, show that a luminous form was a distant object in the sky. A photograph can faithfully record an optical effect created at the moment of exposure. Dust, pollen, moisture or insects close to the lens can reflect flash light and appear as large, soft-edged circles. Camera manufacturers describe these spots as “orbs” and note that they are especially common in low-light photographs when a built-in flash sits close to the lens.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sony.co.uk/electronics/support/compact-cameras-dsc-wx-series/dsc-wx350/articles/00028753" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sony.co.uk">[Sony UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sony.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">bs sometimes appear in a recorded image when using the flash &#124; Sony UK Sony Support DSC-WX350 Article ID: 00057547 / Last Modified: 14/...</span></span></span>

This explanation is particularly relevant to photographs taken at outdoor celebrations or pilgrimages, where dust, insects and moisture may be present. A nearby particle can look large because it is badly out of focus, even though it is tiny. Its round appearance comes from the camera’s optics rather than from the physical shape of a distant object.

The available Santa Cruz de Moya report does not establish whether flash was used, what camera and lens produced the image, how many frames were taken or whether the spheres appeared in adjacent photographs. Nor is there a published high-resolution examination of the original negative. Those omissions prevent a firm diagnosis, but they also mean that the photograph cannot safely be treated as proof of an unknown aerial presence.

A robust photographic investigation would require more than enlarging the mysterious area. Analysts would want the original negative or uncompressed digital file, the camera settings, exact location, date and time, the sequence of surrounding frames and statements from the photographer and other people present. The image would then be compared with stars, aircraft, balloons, atmospheric conditions, internal reflections, motion blur and near-lens particles. Modern scientific proposals for investigating unidentified aerial phenomena likewise stress calibrated instruments, triangulation and multiple sensor types because a single image can rarely establish distance, size or speed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://shura.shu.ac.uk/32102/1/calvine_ufo_photographic_analysis_v4.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shura.shu.ac.uk">[SHURA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shura.shu.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The overcast sky visible in the photograph is consistent with that recorded by the UK Met Off...</span></span></span>

A compressed clip or isolated enlargement, however, cannot show whether the movement belongs to the light, the camera, autofocus, digital stabilisation or the person filming. ## How village stories sustained Cuenca’s UFO tradition

The importance of these reports is partly cultural. Cuenca’s official UFO archive is small, and the province did not experience a heavily documented sequence comparable with the best-known Spanish aviation cases. Village testimony filled that gap. Residents and local investigators linked separate lights, photographs and remembered episodes into a continuing provincial tradition.

Antonio Pastor has been central to that process. From Vellisca, he collected press cuttings, sought historical material and connected modern sightings with older stories and images. Local radio programmes later brought reports from Vellisca, Landete, Campillo de Altobuey and Santa Cruz de Moya back into public circulation. This work preserved memories that might otherwise have disappeared, but it also blended several kinds of evidence: first-hand observation, second-hand testimony, photographs, folklore and personal interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cadenaser.com/castillalamancha/2026/06/10/un-ovni-en-las-pinturas-rupestres-y-otros-avistamientos-en-los-pueblos-de-cuenca-ser-cuenca/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cadenaser.com">[Cadena SER]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cadenaser.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cadenaser.com.</span></span></span>

Readers should therefore distinguish between preservation and verification. A report may be worth recording even when it cannot be solved. Preservation tells us what witnesses said and how a community understood an unusual event. Verification asks a different question: whether the available evidence can exclude aircraft, astronomical bodies, meteors, balloons, atmospheric effects, camera artefacts or mistaken recollection.

On that stricter scale, the later village cases remain weakly documented. The Vellisca accounts contain named testimony and one precise date, making them more useful than anonymous rumours, but they lack the measurements and independent records needed for reconstruction. The Landete and Campillo stories are even more fragmentary in their publicly accessible form. The Santa Cruz de Moya photograph is tangible evidence, yet its luminous circles have not been shown to be distant aerial objects rather than photographic artefacts.

Their most defensible status is therefore **unresolved through lack of information**, not **confirmed as extraordinary**. They matter because they show how Cuenca’s UFO tradition continued outside official files—in village streets, family photographs, local newspapers and radio recollections. But later reporting has preserved the claims more successfully than it has strengthened their evidential basis.
