## Introduction

Castellón’s history of strange lights did not begin with flying saucers. In January 1770, an observer in the province watched dark-red bands and cloud-like forms occupy the northern sky for hours. In October 2021, motorists between Castelló and Benicàssim photographed a vivid green point that appeared for only seconds near sunset. The first display is now recognised as an exceptionally low-latitude aurora; the second was probably a green flash produced by atmospheric refraction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hgss.copernicus.org/articles/9/133/2018/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hgss.copernicus.org">[copernicus.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hgss.copernicus.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The records indicate that the phenomenon was observed in both continental and insular territori...</span></span></span>

These cases matter to Castellón’s UFO history because they demonstrate a recurring problem: a sincere description can be accurate while the witness’s first interpretation remains uncertain. What changes over time is not necessarily the sky, but the explanatory tools available. Eighteenth-century written observations can now be compared across countries and reconstructed using solar records, while modern reports may be checked against photographs, automated cameras and specialist monitoring networks.

## The great red aurora of January 1770

On the evening of 18 January 1770, an anonymous observer in Castellón was alerted at about 7.30 pm to what appeared to be red clouds. After climbing a tower for a clearer view, the observer saw red auroral forms stretching across the northern part of the sky. By around 11 pm, four or five separate formations were visible between the north-east and north-west, described as taller than they were wide. Other people reportedly confirmed that the display remained in a similar state at midnight. A further red display was seen in Castellón at about 8 pm on 19 January.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hgss.copernicus.org/articles/9/133/2018/hgss-9-133-2018.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hgss.copernicus.org">[hgss.copernicus.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hgss.copernicus.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The description provided by an anonymous observer in Castellon indicated that the aurora was sighted in the north-ern sector of the sky...</span></span></span>

Seen in isolation, the account might sound like a local curiosity: stationary red clouds, repeated luminous structures and an ominous colour dominating an otherwise dark sky. Its evidential strength comes from the wider geographical pattern. Records of the same event survive from Valencia, Madrid, Barcelona, Córdoba, Cádiz, Badajoz, the Canary Islands and other locations, as well as from elsewhere in Europe. The Spanish descriptions consistently placed the light mainly in the north and overwhelmingly described it as red, although white or ash-coloured features appeared in a few reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hgss.copernicus.org/articles/9/133/2018/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hgss.copernicus.org">[hgss.copernicus.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hgss.copernicus.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The records indicate that the phenomenon was observed in both continental and insular territori...</span></span></span>

Researchers studying these records concluded that the display was an aurora observed at abnormally low geomagnetic latitudes. Its reach was remarkable: within Spain it was recorded as far south as San Cristóbal de la Laguna in the Canary Islands. Contemporary sunspot observations also indicate strong solar activity around 18 January, including ten sunspot groups recorded that day by the Danish astronomer Christian Horrebow. These independent details make an auroral explanation much stronger than any interpretation based solely on the Castellón wording.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08685" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Great Aurora of January 1770 observed in Spain...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1770</span></span></span>

The Castellón account is especially valuable because it records duration, direction, colour and change over time rather than merely declaring that something unusual appeared. Those details are exactly what allow modern historians of astronomy to distinguish a likely aurora from fire, ordinary cloud, a comet or a short-lived meteor. They also show why historical witnesses should not be dismissed as ignorant. The observer correctly recognised the phenomenon as an aurora, even though eighteenth-century science could not yet explain the full chain from solar activity to disturbances in the Earth’s magnetic environment.

## The 2021 green light near Benicàssim

Shortly before sunset on 31 October 2021, people travelling between Castelló and Benicàssim noticed a small green light near the horizon. According to the local press, it appeared for only a few seconds, was photographed on smartphones and seemed to one witness to move rapidly towards the mountains of the Desert de les Palmes. The timing, colour and brevity encouraged immediate speculation about a laser, an unusual aircraft or something more mysterious.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.elperiodicomediterraneo.com/castello-provincia/2021/11/03/avistamiento-paranormal-castellon-benicassim-luz-verde-halloween-59036711.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elperiodicomediterraneo.com">[Mediterráneo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elperiodicomediterraneo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on elperiodicomediterraneo.com.</span></span></span>

The report was framed playfully as a Halloween mystery, but the same article proposed a conventional explanation: a green flash, sometimes called a green ray. This is a recognised atmospheric-optical effect in which a green feature appears at the upper edge of the Sun, or just above its apparent position, as it sets or rises. The World Meteorological Organization describes the most common forms as lasting roughly one or two seconds and notes that they are more frequently seen over the sea, where observers can have a clear, distant horizon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.elperiodicomediterraneo.com/castello-provincia/2021/11/03/avistamiento-paranormal-castellon-benicassim-luz-verde-halloween-59036711.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elperiodicomediterraneo.com">[Mediterráneo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elperiodicomediterraneo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on elperiodicomediterraneo.com.</span></span></span>

The mechanism involves refraction and dispersion. Near the horizon, sunlight travels through a much greater thickness of atmosphere than it does when the Sun is high. Different wavelengths are bent by slightly different amounts, while atmospheric layers and temperature inversions can magnify or separate the coloured images of the Sun. For a brief moment, green light may remain visible after the redder part of the solar image has dropped from view. In rarer cases, the green feature can appear detached or elevated, making it look more like an isolated point than a coloured rim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/green-flash.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cloudatlas.wmo.int">[International Cloud Atlas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cloudatlas.wmo.int</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Cloud AtlasGreen Flash &#124; International Cloud AtlasGreen Flash &#124; International Cloud Atlas Home &gt; Other Meteors &gt; Definition...</span></span></span>

The published material does not amount to a full scientific investigation. There is no cited triangulation, calibrated video analysis or meteorological reconstruction proving that the Benicàssim light was a green flash. The identification is therefore best described as highly plausible rather than formally demonstrated. Even so, several reported features support it: the light occurred at sunset, remained visible for only seconds, appeared against a coastal horizon and was green. Those characteristics fit the recognised phenomenon more closely than they fit an enduring physical object moving through local airspace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.elperiodicomediterraneo.com/castello-provincia/2021/11/03/avistamiento-paranormal-castellon-benicassim-luz-verde-halloween-59036711.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elperiodicomediterraneo.com">[Mediterráneo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elperiodicomediterraneo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on elperiodicomediterraneo.com.</span></span></span>

Apparent motion is not fatal to that interpretation. A fleeting light viewed from a moving vehicle, against clouds and distant mountains, offers few reliable reference points. Changes in the observer’s position, breaks in cloud and the rapid evolution of a refracted solar image can create an impression of lateral movement. The testimony remains useful as a record of what the event looked like, but the witness’s description of “abnormal” speed cannot by itself establish the movement of a solid object.

## Green does not identify a single phenomenon

The Benicàssim episode also illustrates why colour alone is a poor guide to identity. Castellón recorded another striking greenish event in February 2021, but that display belonged to a completely different category. At about 9.30 pm on 23 February, automated cameras in Benicàssim, Ciudad Real and Ibiza recorded a bright fireball crossing the sky. It travelled for several seconds, produced an intense flash and was captured from widely separated locations by equipment associated with Spain’s fireball-monitoring community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.antena3.com/noticias/el-tiempo/actualidad/las-camaras-de-ciudad-real-benicassim-e-ibiza-registran-una-brillante-bola-de-fuego-atravesando-sus-cielos_202102246036843726e3b80001c41e2f.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antena3.com">[antena3.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antena3.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Las cámaras de Ciudad Real, Benicàssim e Ibiza registran una brillante bola de fuego atravesando sus cielos...</span></span></span>

A fireball is an exceptionally bright meteor produced when a meteoroid enters the atmosphere and emits light while heating, fragmenting and losing material. Its colours may vary according to composition, speed, atmospheric conditions and camera response. Witnesses to the February event described a blue interior, a greenish outer layer and a visible trail. This contrast is useful when assessing modern UFO reports:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A **green flash** is tied closely to sunrise or sunset, normally lasts only a second or two and appears at or just above the horizon.
* A **fireball** moves rapidly across a substantial part of the sky, may leave a trail or fragment, and can be detected by cameras many kilometres apart.
* A **laser, drone or aircraft light** should be considered when a source persists, repeats, changes direction locally or can be linked to activity on the ground or in controlled airspace.

The February fireball also demonstrates how modern documentation can prevent an unusual light from becoming a lasting UFO legend. Spain’s Fireball and Meteorite Recovery Network uses multiple observing stations to reconstruct atmospheric trajectories. When cameras at separate sites record the same event, researchers can calculate a three-dimensional path and investigate its likely origin. This is qualitatively stronger evidence than a single photograph with no accurate time, direction or location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ice.csic.es/news/citizen-science?amp%3Bcatid=2&amp;amp%3Bid=319&amp;view=article" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ice.csic.es">[Ice CSIC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ice.csic.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on csic.es.</span></span></span>

## How explanations change without changing the witness

The gap between 1770 and 2021 is not a simple journey from superstition to knowledge. The Castellón aurora account was a careful observation, and its author already used the correct broad label. The modern green-light report, despite cameras and instant news coverage, was less securely documented in scientific terms. Technology increases the opportunity to collect evidence, but it does not guarantee that the right information will be recorded.

What has changed most is the ability to compare reports. The 1770 observer could not immediately know that people across Spain and Europe were watching the same red sky. Modern researchers can assemble those scattered records, calculate the sites’ historical geomagnetic positions and compare the event with surviving sunspot data. The result is a persuasive retrospective identification built from many independent observations rather than from one dramatic description.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hgss.copernicus.org/articles/9/133/2018/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hgss.copernicus.org">[hgss.copernicus.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hgss.copernicus.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The records indicate that the phenomenon was observed in both continental and insular territori...</span></span></span>

In 2021, comparison could happen almost immediately. A supposed local mystery might be checked against sunset time, camera direction, weather conditions and images taken by other witnesses. A moving fireball could be matched across distant monitoring stations. Reports of satellite trains, rocket exhaust or atmospheric re-entry can likewise be compared with orbital predictions. This means that later evidence often weakens the most exotic interpretation while strengthening the basic witness claim that an unusual-looking light was genuinely present.

Media language still shapes the first impression. The Benicàssim green flash was introduced through words such as “paranormal” and “mystery”, even though the same report then supplied a standard optical explanation. Such framing does not falsify the photographs, but it can make an ordinary atmospheric event feel more anomalous than the available evidence warrants.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.elperiodicomediterraneo.com/castello-provincia/2021/11/03/avistamiento-paranormal-castellon-benicassim-luz-verde-halloween-59036711.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elperiodicomediterraneo.com">[Mediterráneo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elperiodicomediterraneo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on elperiodicomediterraneo.com.</span></span></span>

## What these cases add to Castellón’s UFO history

The 1770 aurora and the 2021 sky reports are not UFO incidents in the narrow sense of alleged structured craft. Their importance is interpretive. They reveal how several phenomena capable of producing sincere strange-light reports occur over the province: rare aurorae, refracted sunlight and bright meteors among them.

They also provide a useful standard for judging more disputed Castellón cases. The great aurora is strongly identified because it has long-duration observations from many places, consistent directions and colours, and supporting solar evidence. The February 2021 fireball is strongly identified because multiple cameras recorded a fast atmospheric trajectory. The October green light has a persuasive conventional explanation, but the published evidence is thinner and does not demonstrate the identification with the same precision.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hgss.copernicus.org/articles/9/133/2018/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hgss.copernicus.org">[copernicus.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hgss.copernicus.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The records indicate that the phenomenon was observed in both continental and insular territori...</span></span></span>

That distinction—between **identified**, **plausibly explained** and **unresolved**—is more useful than dividing every report into “UFO” or “not UFO”. An unexplained observation may remain unexplained only because the record is incomplete. Conversely, identifying a natural mechanism does not mean witnesses invented what they saw. Castellón’s strange-sky history repeatedly shows that the sighting and its interpretation must be assessed separately.

From the red northern sky of 1770 to a green point above the Benicàssim horizon, unfamiliar light has been translated through the concepts available at the moment: coloured clouds, auroral bands, paranormal lights, aircraft or unidentified objects. Later knowledge does not erase those experiences. It changes what can responsibly be claimed about them.

      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
