## Introduction

Ceclavín’s reputation for mysterious lights rests on two different bodies of material that have recently been joined into one story. The first is a small group of poorly documented reports attributed to local witnesses in 2023. The second is an established tradition about strange lights near the “Olive Tree of the Souls”, recorded in twentieth-century folklore collections and still performed in local cultural events. Modern paranormal retellings present the two as evidence that the same unexplained phenomenon has persisted for centuries.

The available evidence does not support that continuous timeline. The old account is a legend shaped by religious ideas about restless souls, ritual obligations and a particular tree beside the road to Alcántara. The 2023 claims lack the dates, original recordings, named witness statements and investigation records needed for comparison. Ceclavín therefore matters within Cáceres UFO history not as a proven long-running hotspot, but as a clear example of how recent lights can inherit the meaning of much older folklore.

## What was reported around 2023?

The public account of Ceclavín’s recent activity is surprisingly thin. A later news item says residents reported unusual lights during 2023, sometimes interpreted as appearing to “mark” something, and that the claims attracted interest from the television presenter and paranormal investigator Iker Jiménez. It also names a local researcher, Francisco Vázquez, who connected the reports with the village’s traditional stories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elcongresista.mx/politica/nacional/avistamientos-ovnis-caceres" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elcongresista.mx">[El Congresista]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elcongresista.mx</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El Congresista Avistamientos de ovnis generan misterio en Cáceres, España — El Congresista</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El CongresistaAvistamientos de ovnis generan misterio en Cáceres, España — El CongresistaFebruary 8, 2026 — Avistamientos de ovnis genera...</span></span></span>

Nor has a contemporary police report, aviation record, astronomical analysis or official investigation been identified in the accessible material. The phrase “reports during 2023” should therefore not be treated as a single documented incident. It may refer to separate observations later gathered under one label.

This distinction matters because descriptions such as “a light moved strangely” or “it seemed to indicate a place” are interpretations, not measurements. A point of light offers few visual clues to distance. Movement by the observer, drifting cloud, atmospheric distortion and the small involuntary movements of the eye can make a stationary or slowly moving source appear erratic. Without a stable horizon and timed footage, apparent changes of direction are particularly difficult to judge.

The later article also places the Ceclavín lights beside disc-shaped objects and vehicle encounters reported elsewhere in Cáceres. That creates a broader provincial mystery narrative, but it does not strengthen the Ceclavín evidence. A light above one municipality cannot be treated as corroboration for a differently described event at another location and date merely because both occurred within the same province.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elcongresista.mx/politica/nacional/avistamientos-ovnis-caceres" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elcongresista.mx">[El Congresista]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elcongresista.mx</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El Congresista Avistamientos de ovnis generan misterio en Cáceres, España — El Congresista</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El CongresistaAvistamientos de ovnis generan misterio en Cáceres, España — El CongresistaFebruary 8, 2026 — Avistamientos de ovnis genera...</span></span></span>

## The Olive Tree of the Souls is older than the UFO framing

Ceclavín genuinely possesses a well-established light legend. A 1973 book by Severiano Rosado Vidal, *Ceclavín: Its Life and Folklore*, includes the Olive Tree of the Souls among a much wider collection of local customs, apparitions, witches, ghosts and historical tales. The book’s contents confirm that the story was part of the town’s recorded folklore decades before the alleged 2023 sightings. They do not, however, turn the legend into a dated eyewitness record from the seventeenth century.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.frontespo.org/es/fichas-bibliograficas/ceclavin-su-vida-y-su-folklore-costumbres-leyendas-casos-y-sucesos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: frontespo.org">[frontespo.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">frontespo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on frontespo.org.</span></span></span>

A folklore study held by the Joaquín Díaz Foundation locates the tradition more precisely. It says that lights identified locally as souls in torment were believed to appear over an olive tree near the Zapatera stream on the road to Alcántara. It also records an old obligation to give the tree’s oil for the benefit of the souls in Purgatory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://funjdiaz.net/folklore/07ficha.php?ID=4324&amp;NUM=432" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: funjdiaz.net">[Fundación Joaquín Díaz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">funjdiaz.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fundación Joaquín DíazFundación Joaquín Díaz - Revista de FolkloreEn Ceclavín las luces que el pueblo identifica como ánimas en pena se m...</span></span></span>

That religious element is central. In the traditional version, the lights are not machines entering the atmosphere. They are manifestations associated with the dead, Purgatory and the duty of the living to assist unsettled souls. The tree, the donated oil and the road provide the legend with a physical setting and a repeated social practice. This is exactly the kind of material anchor that helps a local legend survive: an ordinary place becomes evidence of the story because people can point to it, visit it and repeat what was said to have happened there. Research on the persistence of European legends similarly emphasises that stories acquire credibility when collective memory attaches them to apparently historical people, objects and locations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/transactions-of-the-royal-historical-society/article/abs/of-living-legends-and-authentic-tales-how-to-get-remembered-in-early-modern-europe/8FF984D9AFA1F71841B5EDEE929CA393" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Folklore experts have shown that for a legend to be remembered it is important that it is historicised.Focusing on three case-studies fro...</span></span></span>

Three dim lights were said to remain motionless over it. When the rider returned the next day, according to the tale, the lights appeared again, and the tree thereafter became known by its supernatural name. ## How recent lights became part of an ancient mystery

The UFO interpretation changes the story’s vocabulary while retaining its structure. In the traditional account, unexplained lights indicate souls or a sacred obligation. In a modern paranormal programme or online article, they may become evidence of an unidentified aerial presence. In both versions, a light is assumed to carry intention: it watches, warns, marks a location or returns for a purpose.

Three forms of compression help create the appearance of continuity.

**Different kinds of testimony are placed on the same level.** A recent personal observation, a legend collected in 1973 and a story said to concern a remote past can all be presented as equivalent “cases”. Yet they are different types of evidence. A dated witness statement might be tested against weather and astronomical records; a folklore text tells us what a community narrated and remembered, not necessarily what physically occurred.

**Uncertain ages become precise-sounding history.** The accessible folklore sources establish that the Olive Tree of the Souls tradition was recorded by the twentieth century and regarded as old. They do not provide a contemporary document proving that luminous events were observed at the tree exactly four centuries ago. Calling it a “400-year-old sighting” converts the claimed age of a legend into the date of an incident.

**Geographically separate stories are merged into a regional pattern.** Recent summaries connect Ceclavín with accounts from Valencia de Alcántara and other parts of Cáceres. Such comparisons can be useful, but only when dates, descriptions and sources are kept separate. Otherwise, a light, a supposed disc and an alleged close encounter become episodes in one continuing phenomenon without independent evidence that they share a cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elcongresista.mx/politica/nacional/avistamientos-ovnis-caceres" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elcongresista.mx">[El Congresista]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elcongresista.mx</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El Congresista Avistamientos de ovnis generan misterio en Cáceres, España — El Congresista</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El CongresistaAvistamientos de ovnis generan misterio en Cáceres, España — El CongresistaFebruary 8, 2026 — Avistamientos de ovnis genera...</span></span></span>

Folklore scholarship describes comparable distortions in oral chronologies, including the telescoping or lengthening of the past as stories are transmitted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-african-history/article/abs/oral-tradition-and-chronology/A5492CFB8683BF2F43D99AA55F81C825" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cambridge.org.</span></span></span> In Ceclavín, chronological compression does not mean that anyone deliberately invented the tradition. It means that repeated retelling can remove the gaps between “people once told this story”, “someone recently saw a light” and “the same phenomenon has been active here for centuries”.

## Could aurorae or meteors explain the reports?

Natural explanations cannot be matched conclusively to the 2023 claims because their exact dates are missing. Nevertheless, 2023 produced several conspicuous astronomical events that demonstrate why dates and directions are indispensable.

A severe geomagnetic storm on 23 April allowed auroral light to be photographed unusually far south, including from Spain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tiempo.com/ram/auroras-de-finales-de-abril-de-2023-vistas-espana.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tiempo.com">[Meteored España]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tiempo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">auroras de finales de abril de 2023 vistas espana</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">auroras de finales de abril de 2023 vistas espana</span></span></span> Another strong storm on 5–6 November produced aurorae across Europe and at unusually low latitudes elsewhere. Auroral displays can appear as diffuse red or coloured glows rather than the familiar green curtains associated with northern regions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scitechdaily.com/solar-storm-spectacle-colorful-auroras-light-up-earths-atmosphere/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scitechdaily.com">[SciTechDaily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scitechdaily.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on scitechdaily.com.</span></span></span> If any Ceclavín report coincided with such an event and concerned a broad, slowly changing glow low in the northern sky, an auroral explanation would deserve serious consideration. Without a date, however, the comparison remains only a possibility.

Meteors are another recurring source of brief luminous reports. Spain’s National Geographic Institute explains that meteor showers produce increased numbers of streaks appearing to radiate from a common area of sky. Very bright meteors, or fireballs, can outshine planets and may fragment, change colour or leave a temporary trail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://astronomia.ign.es/lluvias-de-meteoros" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomia.ign.es">[Astronomía]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomia.ign.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomía Lluvia de meteoros</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomía Lluvia de meteoros</span></span></span> A meteor would fit an observation lasting seconds, but not a light reported as stationary for a prolonged period.

Other ordinary candidates would depend on the missing details. A planet or bright star near the horizon can flicker and change colour through turbulent air. Aircraft can appear almost motionless when approaching along the observer’s line of sight, then seem to turn sharply once their angle changes. Satellites may travel steadily before fading as they enter the Earth’s shadow. Drones can hover, reverse direction and display changing lights. None should be declared the answer without reconstructing an individual sighting, but all are more testable than the suggestion that the lights were deliberately “marking” a place.

The traditional olive-tree lights require a different kind of caution. Even if an atmospheric or astronomical phenomenon inspired an early version, the surviving accounts are too altered and insufficiently dated for a retrospective identification. The legend is evidence of local belief and storytelling; it is not precise enough to function as a centuries-old observation log.

## What remains after the retellings are separated?

Ceclavín has a meaningful place in the cultural history of unusual lights in Cáceres. Its folklore is unusually well rooted in a named landscape feature, a road, a religious custom and repeated local retellings. The Olive Tree of the Souls was documented in a substantial 1973 collection and continues to appear in municipal heritage activities and regional folklore writing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.frontespo.org/es/fichas-bibliograficas/ceclavin-su-vida-y-su-folklore-costumbres-leyendas-casos-y-sucesos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: frontespo.org">[frontespo.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">frontespo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on frontespo.org.</span></span></span>

The modern UFO claim is much less secure. Public retellings refer to lights reported in 2023, but the accessible record does not yet provide the basic case material needed to establish what was seen or whether multiple witnesses observed the same phenomenon. Later articles have strengthened the narrative connection to the old legend, not the physical evidence for an unidentified aerial object.

A balanced classification is therefore:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **The folklore tradition:** well evidenced as folklore, though not as a literal record of repeated physical events.
* **The 2023 light reports:** possible witness experiences, but weakly documented and presently unverified.
* **The claim of centuries-long continuity:** unsupported by the available chronology.
* **An extraordinary aerial explanation:** neither demonstrated nor required by the evidence currently public.

</div>

The most revealing feature of Ceclavín is not that one phenomenon has necessarily returned for hundreds of years. It is that lights with little measurable information can be absorbed into a story already waiting for them. The UFO legend emerged when recent reports were placed beside an old supernatural landscape, allowing two separate histories—modern observation and traditional belief—to appear as one continuous mystery.
