## Introduction

The strongest conventional explanation for the extraordinary lights seen from Gran Canaria and neighbouring islands on 22 June 1976 is not a hovering craft but the high-altitude exhaust from two United States Navy Poseidon C-3 missile launches far out in the Atlantic. At great height, rocket gases expand into an enormous, thin cloud while remaining illuminated by the Sun after observers below are already in darkness. That combination can produce a glowing halo, translucent sphere, changing colours and apparently separate lights moving within or away from the cloud.

This explanation matters because it accounts for the most widely reported features of the [Las Palmas]({{ 'las-palmas/' | relative_url }}) sighting with one known physical mechanism. Records independently confirm that the submarine USS *Von Steuben* successfully launched two Poseidon missiles on the same date. The Spanish investigation, meanwhile, recorded a bright source, a circular blue halo, splitting lights, spiral-like movement, prolonged visibility and no corresponding radar target. ## How a missile creates a luminous “sphere”

A ballistic missile does not normally resemble the compact streak associated with a firework or a low-flying rocket. After rising into the upper atmosphere, its exhaust enters air that is far thinner than at ground level. The gases and fine particles can therefore spread rapidly sideways, forming a vast diffuse cloud rather than a narrow trail. Changes in thrust, stage separation and the release of residual fuel may create several bright patches, rings or expanding shells.

Twilight makes these structures especially conspicuous. The ground may already be dark while the upper portion of the missile’s path remains in direct sunlight because it lies beyond the Earth’s shadow. The plume then scatters sunlight back towards observers, much as a very high cloud can remain bright after sunset. The United States Space Force describes this “twilight phenomenon” as a product of missile or rocket material freezing or dispersing in the less dense upper atmosphere while the vehicle rises from darkness into sunlight. NASA photographs and explanations show comparable exhaust clouds appearing as broad, brightly illuminated and sometimes coloured formations against a darker sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vandenberg.spaceforce.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/338354/general-information/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vandenberg.spaceforce.mil">[spaceforce.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vandenberg.spaceforce.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The phenomenon most often occurs with launches 30 to 60 minutes after sunset or before sunrise as boosters rise out of the darkness into...</span></span></span>

This geometry helps explain why witnesses could describe the Canary display as transparent. An illuminated exhaust cloud is not a solid surface. Stars or darker sky may remain visible through its thinner portions, while denser regions appear as bright rims, arcs or internal concentrations. From a distance, a roughly symmetrical cloud can resemble a hollow globe or dome, particularly when observers have no reliable scale against which to judge it.

Colour changes are also expected rather than anomalous. Sunlight passing through a long path in the lower atmosphere is reddened, while different particle sizes, densities and viewing angles can produce white, blue, yellow or reddish regions. High-altitude ice crystals and exhaust particles may also generate iridescent colouring. NASA has documented twilight rocket trails with bright diffuse clouds, rainbow-like tints and sharply different illumination between the sunlit upper plume and the darker lower trail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/smoke-plume-1221/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Smoke Plume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science Smoke Plume</span></span></span>

## Why the Poseidon launches fit the Canary reports

The date match is unusually specific. Missile chronologies record that USS *Von Steuben*, a United States Navy ballistic-missile submarine, successfully fired two Poseidon C-3 missiles during an operational test on 22 June 1976. Independent launch compilations also list a Poseidon test from the submarine on that date. These records do not, by themselves, prove that the missiles produced the Canary display, but they establish that the proposed source was real and active on the correct day.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuclearcompanion.com/data/poseidon-c3-missile-chronology/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuclearcompanion.com">[Nuclear Companion]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuclearcompanion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nuclear Companion Poseidon (C3) Missile Chronology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nuclear CompanionPoseidon (C3) Missile Chronology - Nuclear Companion: A nuclear guide to the cold warMarch 24, 2024 — #### 22 JUNE 1976...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2024</span></span></span>

The official Spanish file contains several details that fit a sequence involving more than one missile or more than one active phase of a missile flight. At 21:27 universal time, the crew of the Spanish Navy corvette *Atrevida* reported a white light that rose, became bluish and developed a circular halo. About two minutes later, the bright source appeared to divide. A smaller portion remained below while an upper light climbed rapidly in an irregular, spiral-like movement. The halo persisted until approximately 22:10.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brief On 22 June 1976 at 21:27Z, the Spanish Navy corvette Atrevida, sailing parallel to Fuerteventura, wa...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

A rocket-plume reconstruction makes sense of that description without requiring a stationary vehicle. The initial bright point may correspond to an active missile stage seen almost along its direction of travel. The expanding halo could be exhaust spreading in the upper atmosphere. A stage, warhead bus or second missile moving away from the illuminated cloud could appear as a separate light. Disturbance of the plume by winds, vehicle rotation or a curved line of sight may account for the apparent spiral movement.

The two-launch record is particularly relevant because witnesses did not always describe one simple object. Reports referred to splitting lights, a secondary source and changing formations. Two missiles launched within a short operational sequence could generate overlapping clouds or successive bright centres, making the display look as though one object had divided or released another. Later Spanish researchers, notably Vicente-Juan Ballester Olmos and Ricardo Campo Pérez, developed this interpretation by comparing the witness chronology with known missile-launch effects and other Canary Island displays.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/16027101/Identificados_Los_OVNIS_de_Canarias_fueron_misiles_Poseidon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[academia.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF) Identificados: Los OVNIS de Canarias fueron misiles Poseidon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PDF) Identificados: Los OVNIS de Canarias fueron misiles Poseidon</span></span></span>

## The details the plume model explains well

### A huge halo without a huge nearby object

Witness estimates of physical size are unreliable when distance is unknown. A cloud hundreds of kilometres away and tens or hundreds of kilometres above the Earth can occupy a large angle in the sky while appearing to hang over a nearby island or coastline. Observers may therefore describe something as being above Gáldar, Fuerteventura or a particular stretch of sea even though they are looking towards the same remote plume from different positions.

The broad geographical spread of the reports favours a distant, high-altitude source. The phenomenon was observed from multiple islands and from vessels at sea. A genuinely local object low over northern Gran Canaria would not naturally produce similar views across such a wide area, whereas a large sunlit cloud above the Atlantic could. The different apparent placements given by witnesses are therefore not fatal contradictions; they are a predictable result of trying to locate a distant light against a dark, largely featureless sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/canary-islands-1976/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[uapcodex.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uapcodex.org.</span></span></span>

### Long duration and apparent hovering

The halo remained visible long after the most active lights had changed or disappeared. That is difficult to reconcile with a small object simply flying past, but it is normal for an exhaust cloud. Once deposited at high altitude, the plume can continue expanding and reflecting sunlight even after the missile itself is no longer visible. To an observer, the cloud’s centre may appear almost stationary while its edges slowly enlarge or fade.

The word “hovering” can consequently describe an appearance rather than powered stationary flight. A very distant object moving rapidly along a path with little sideways component may show little apparent motion. The residual cloud then remains in roughly the same part of the sky, reinforcing the impression that something has stopped. Modern twilight launches repeatedly produce similar public reports of motionless domes, glowing jellyfish or expanding bubbles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap260319.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apod.nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apod.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nasa.gov.</span></span></span>

### Changing shapes and transparency

The Spanish naval account did not describe a rigid outline. It recorded a circular halo, a bluish cloud, divided light sources and an irregular rising movement. Those are dynamic optical features, not clear observations of a solid airframe. Exhaust plumes naturally deform as they expand, as different sections receive sunlight and as upper-atmospheric winds act at different heights.

Transparency is similarly more compatible with a cloud than with a manufactured shell. An illuminated plume can have a bright border and a faint interior, making it look like a glassy sphere. Denser knots may be interpreted as objects or figures within it, especially when seen through haze, vehicle windows or intervening terrain. The mechanism does not prove that every witness detail arose in this way, but it explains why a distant aerospace event could be perceived as a structured, semi-transparent object.

### No radar return

The *Atrevida* reported no radar detection associated with the visual phenomenon. That absence is not decisive, because radar performance depends on range, elevation, target geometry and equipment settings. It nevertheless fits the plume explanation better than the idea of a large metallic craft nearby. A diffuse cloud of gas and fine particles is visually bright when sunlit but need not produce a strong conventional radar return. ## Why the original inquiry did not identify the missiles

The Spanish investigation was conducted soon after the incident, but its investigators did not have the same information later researchers could assemble. A submarine-launched ballistic-missile test was a sensitive Cold War activity, and the precise position, trajectory and launch schedule of an American strategic submarine would not normally have been announced to Spanish witnesses or local military investigators in real time.

The [official file]({{ 'official-file-fb8c33/' | relative_url }}) considered missiles among several possibilities but ultimately retained the event as unidentified. That conclusion accurately reflected the evidence available to the inquiry rather than establishing that no missile had been launched. The later Poseidon theory became stronger when researchers connected the witness descriptions with external missile records and with the recognised optical behaviour of twilight rocket exhaust. In other words, “unidentified” described the investigation’s information gap, not necessarily the phenomenon’s permanent status.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[ufotransparency.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brief On 22 June 1976 at 21:27Z, the Spanish Navy corvette Atrevida, sailing parallel to Fuerteventura, wa...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

Cold War secrecy also helps explain why the incident acquired a more mysterious reputation. Witnesses had observed something genuinely spectacular, yet there was no immediate public announcement of a launch. Without an ordinary cause available, journalists and UFO writers interpreted the halo as a craft, and later accounts placed increasing emphasis on its seemingly controlled movements and immense dimensions.

## What remains uncertain

The Poseidon explanation is persuasive, but the surviving public evidence does not provide a perfect second-by-second reconstruction. The available launch chronologies confirm two successful firings on 22 June, yet commonly accessible records do not give a complete public package of exact launch times, submarine coordinates, ascent azimuths and stage events. Those details would be needed to calculate precisely where each bright feature should have appeared from every witness location.

Some reported timings are also approximate or were written down after the event. Clock differences, local-time conversions and memory can shift individual observations by several minutes. The theory is therefore strongest at the level of overall correspondence: correct date, suitable Atlantic test activity, expected twilight conditions, widespread visibility and a morphology characteristic of high-altitude exhaust. It is weaker as a fully verified trajectory reconstruction.

The plume model also should not be stretched to authenticate the separate story of a sphere close to the ground containing two tall figures. That account came from a much narrower group near Gáldar and conflicts with the distant scale implied by the multi-island observations. The official investigator himself expressed reservations about the close-range interpretation, noting differences between those present. A missile plume may have supplied the brilliant light that prompted the experience, but perception, memory, mutual influence or later embellishment would be needed to explain the alleged occupants.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Brief On 22 June 1976 at 21:27Z, the Spanish Navy corvette Atrevida, sailing parallel to Fuerteventura, wa...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

## How strong is the explanation?

For the principal aerial display, the Poseidon hypothesis is substantially stronger than the idea of a craft hovering over Gran Canaria. It rests on a documented pair of missile launches, a well-understood optical mechanism and witness features that follow naturally from an expanding high-altitude plume. It also explains several points that otherwise appear contradictory: the phenomenon looked enormous yet gave no radar return; it seemed close to different islands at once; it changed shape but left a persistent halo; and its bright components appeared to divide or climb away.

Its remaining weakness is not an alternative body of equally strong evidence for an unknown vehicle. It is the absence, in open records, of all the classified operational data needed to close every geometric and chronological gap. That justifies wording such as “probably explained” rather than “proved beyond doubt”.

Within the UFO history of Las Palmas, the most defensible assessment is therefore divided. The [mass sighting]({{ 'mass-sighting/' | relative_url }}) was real and well documented. The broad halo and moving lights were very probably produced by one or two Poseidon missile launches viewed under favourable twilight conditions. The supposed nearby sphere and occupants are weakly supported additions that the missile reconstruction neither confirms nor needs. The case remains historically important not because conventional explanation failed, but because a secret military event created exactly the kind of immense, unfamiliar spectacle that ordinary observers could reasonably mistake for something hovering over the Canary Islands.

    var template = String(cfg.url_template || 'https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw={query}');
    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
