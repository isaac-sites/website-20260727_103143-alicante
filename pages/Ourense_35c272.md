## Introduction

Three episodes stand out. In 1950, motorists reported a brilliant moving object above the Alto do Rodicio during the first international wave of saucer publicity. In 1963, an anonymous farmer near the former [Antela]({{ 'antela/' | relative_url }}) lagoon claimed that a landed craft and three strange occupants had approached him. In 1997, an elderly resident of [Paradaseca]({{ 'paradaseca/' | relative_url }}) described two enormous luminous figures near a tree. Each case became memorable because of its human detail, yet none produced photographs, physical traces, radar confirmation or a surviving official investigation strong enough to establish an extraordinary event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/amp/noticia/ourense/2023/09/24/campesino-narraba-encuentro-extraterrestres-limia/0003_202309O24C4991.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[lavozdegalicia.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">0003 202309O24C4991</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Voz de GaliciaEn 1963 un campesino de Ourense deca haber visto aterrizar un OVNI: Habl con los tres tripulantes...</span></span></span>

## The provinces main cases

### The Alto do Rodicio light, 1950

One of Ourenses earliest recognisable flying-saucer reports appeared in March 1950, when Francisco Castelao and his driver, Artemio Gmez, were travelling towards the city in the early hours. While climbing the Alto do Rodicio, they noticed a very bright object moving steadily at great height. They initially considered a star, but rejected that explanation because of its apparent size, brightness and movement. They watched until the road began to descend and the object passed out of view.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.laregion.es/provincia/historia-4-tiempos-dia-hoy_1_20250326-3463597.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: laregion.es">[La Regin]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">laregion.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on laregion.es.</span></span></span>

The report is historically useful because it shows how quickly the international saucer craze reached inland Galicia. The local newspaper itself framed the incident against the worldwide fever or psychosis surrounding flying saucers. That context does not prove that the witnesses imagined the light, but it does warn against reading a brief newspaper item as a technically investigated case.

The original local reporting contains one modest form of corroboration: another resident apparently saw Nez running and shouting after the encounter. Neither observation independently confirms the reported figures, however. Later retellings made the figures sound increasingly theatrical, comparing them to joined giants or beings wearing luminous mitre-shaped headgear. This illustrates a recurrent problem in UFO history: memorable language survives, while basic investigative measurements do not. ## Galicia-wide sightings seen from Ourense

Not every object reported over Ourense originated within the province. Its inland viewpoints have often formed part of wider Galician sighting events, and these multi-location observations are especially useful because they can be compared across large distances.

A notable example occurred in December 1989, when a bright object remained visible over parts of Galicia for hours and was reportedly observed from Vigo, Ourense and other locations. A photograph taken in Noia became associated with the event, while the Guardia Civil contacted the Air Surveillance Squadron at the Barbanza installation after receiving reports of a lenticular object with lights following an ascending course.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/noticia/barbanza/2020/07/04/foto-paz-profesional-noies-cazo-supuesto-ovni/0003_202007B4C10991.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[La Voz de Galicia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El objeto permaneció sobre el cielo desde primera hora de la tarde hasta pasadas las once de la noche y fue avistado también desde Vigo...</span></span></span>

The military connection makes the event sound stronger than a purely local rumour, but it must be interpreted carefully. The relevant Air Force documentation is associated with the surveillance station at Noia in A Corua province, not with an Ourense military facility. Ourense was one of several observation areas, and there is no clear public evidence that radar independently tracked an anomalous craft over the province.

Spains Ministry of Defence says its digitised UFO collection contains 80 files and around 1,900 pages covering incidents from 1962 to 1995 in which Air Force personnel or equipment were involved. The catalogue includes cases at the Noia surveillance station in 1989 and 1993, but no file is clearly titled as a dedicated Ourense incident. That does not prove that the province appears nowhere inside supporting papers, but it means claims of a substantial classified Ourense UFO dossier are not supported by the public catalogue.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

This distinction matters. A light seen from Ourense may belong to the provinces sighting history, yet the strongest records, instruments and investigation may be located elsewhere. Treating every Galicia-wide incident as a separate Ourense case can inflate the local record and create the false impression of a provincial concentration.

## Press, memory and exaggeration

Local newspapers are the backbone of Ourenses UFO history. They preserve names, dates and witness language that might otherwise have vanished. They also reflect the assumptions and entertainment culture of their period.

In 1950 the Alto do Rodicio report was explicitly connected to the worldwide saucer craze. By 1963, the Antela article presented a highly developed occupant narrative complete with reflective clothing and telepathic communication. In 1997, Paradaseca became a rural mystery centred on an elderly witness whose fear was easier to document than the object of that fear.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/amp/noticia/ourense/2023/09/24/campesino-narraba-encuentro-extraterrestres-limia/0003_202309O24C4991.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[lavozdegalicia.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">0003 202309O24C4991</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Voz de GaliciaEn 1963 un campesino de Ourense deca haber visto aterrizar un OVNI: Habl con los tres tripulantes...</span></span></span>

Ourenses press history also contains deliberate UFO imagery. La Regin later recalled that its traditional 28 December practical-joke editions had included fabricated flying saucers and extraterrestrials: formations of supposed visitors near the railway station in 1956 and a saucer above the cathedral in 1961 were photomontages, not sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.laregion.es/comarca-de-ourense/inocentadas-ayer_1_20161228-2949180.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: laregion.es">[La Regin]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">laregion.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">La Regin Los vestigios fotogrficos de los Santos Inocentes en Ourense</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Regin Los vestigios fotogrficos de los Santos Inocentes en Ourense</span></span></span>

That matters when old clippings circulate without their publication date or original caption. A striking historical UFO photograph from Ourense may be a joke image, an illustration or a later reproduction rather than documentary evidence. Archive research must therefore examine the complete newspaper page and the cultural setting, not simply the isolated picture.

Later anniversary articles present another difficulty. They are invaluable for rediscovering forgotten cases, but often depend on a single original report and may repeat its dramatic details without adding new investigation. In both the Antela and Paradaseca stories, modern retellings have preserved the claims more effectively than they have strengthened them.

## What Ourenses UFO history actually shows

Ourense does not emerge from the available record as a proven UFO hotspot. There is no established sequence of linked encounters, no identified airbase pattern and no publicly documented provincial case combining visual observation, radar, physical evidence and a rigorous official inquiry.

What the province does offer is a revealing cross-section of Spanish UFO culture:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an early moving light reported during the 1950 saucer wave;
* a classic 1960s landed-craft and occupant story;
* a late twentieth-century humanoid report rooted in a remote mountain community;
* participation in wider Galician aerial sightings;
* and recent fireballs whose identification shows the value of cameras and scientific coordination.

</div>

The Antela and Paradaseca incidents remain unresolved only in a limited sense: the original witnesses experiences cannot now be reconstructed conclusively. They should not be presented as confirmed encounters with extraterrestrial beings. Their evidential weaknessesanonymous or solitary testimony, delayed reporting, missing site data and absent physical corroborationare more important than their vivid details.

The strongest lesson from Ourense is therefore methodological rather than sensational. An unidentified sighting is a starting description, not a final explanation. When observations can be timed, photographed and compared across Galicia, meteors and atmospheric effects frequently emerge. When only a decades-old story survives, the honest conclusion is usually not that something extraordinary was proved, but that too little reliable information remains to decide exactly what happened.
