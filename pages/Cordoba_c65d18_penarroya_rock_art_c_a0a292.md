## Introduction

The prehistoric paintings at Peñarroya-Pueblonuevo do not provide credible evidence of an ancient spacecraft. They are genuine rock art, probably created by communities living in northern Córdoba during the Copper Age, but the UFO interpretation arose thousands of years later from a modern resemblance: one symbol was said to look like the “H”-shaped emblem associated with the disputed Ummo extraterrestrial story. No archaeological evidence connects the panel with flying machines, non-human visitors or knowledge beyond that expected in prehistoric Iberia.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Cordoba_c65d18_penarroya_rock_art_c_a0a292-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Rock Art Claim" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The paintings matter within Córdoba’s UFO history because they show how an authentic archaeological site can be recast as a UFO case without any reported object, physical trace or contemporary witness. The underlying heritage is real; the extraordinary interpretation is not supported. Archaeologists instead place the figures within the well-established tradition of schematic rock art found across Sierra Morena, where simplified people, bars, animals, plants and possible ritual groups appear repeatedly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cordopolis.eldiario.es/cultura/literatura/j-j-benitez-dice-pinturas-rupestres-penarroya-representan-llegada-ovni-5-000-anos_1_8403381.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cordopolis.eldiario.es">[eldiario.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cordopolis.eldiario.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on eldiario.es.</span></span></span>

## What the paintings actually depict

The rock shelter lies on El Peñón, the prominent reddish hill overlooking Peñarroya-Pueblonuevo and the Guadiato valley. The hill rises to about 775 metres and contains evidence of prehistoric occupation as well as painted shelters. University of Córdoba heritage material describes six panels or groups of schematic figures at the site, although weathering and mineral patinas now make some much easier to see than others.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uco.es/guadiatvr/penon-de-penarroya/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uco.es">[Universidad de Córdoba]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uco.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Universidad de Córdoba Peñon de Peñarroya &#124; iter guadiat VR</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universidad de Córdoba Peñon de Peñarroya &#124; iter guadiat VR</span></span></span>

The site is generally known in archaeological literature as Abrigo Carmelo. Local heritage material also uses the name Abrigo de la Virgen for the painted shelter on the eastern side of El Peñón. The paintings were formally published in 1966 by José Alfonso Moure Romanillo and Luis Javier Ruiz in the archaeological journal *Empúries*, establishing that they were known to specialists decades before their later appearance in UFO literature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.raco.cat/index.php/Empuries/article/view/103252" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: raco.cat">[Raco]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">raco.cat</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Las pinturas del Abrigo Carmelo (Peñarroya, Córdoba) &#124; Empúries: revista d&#x27;arqueologia del món anticLas pinturas del Abrigo Carmelo (...</span></span></span>

### A Copper Age setting

The surrounding archaeology provides the most important context. El Peñón preserves traces of a settlement attributed to the Copper Age, around the third millennium BC. Finds associated with the hill include pottery, stone tools and evidence of early copper working. Recent excavations in the wider Peñón area have strengthened the picture of an active prehistoric community involved in metallurgy rather than an isolated group of unexplained painters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uco.es/guadiatvr/penon-de-penarroya/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uco.es">[uco.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uco.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Universidad de Córdoba Peñon de Peñarroya &#124; iter guadiat VR</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universidad de Córdoba Peñon de Peñarroya &#124; iter guadiat VR</span></span></span>

One University of Córdoba publication explicitly corrects an earlier description of the paintings as Palaeolithic and identifies the Abrigo de la Virgen or Abrigo Carmelo images as schematic and Copper Age in character. That classification broadly fits the archaeological remains found nearby.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://helvia.uco.es/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10396/4184/SIZIGIA_01_1.pdf?sequence=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: helvia.uco.es">[Helvia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">helvia.uco.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">En su mayor parte, l...</span></span></span>

Exact dating of rock art is often difficult because mineral pigments cannot always be dated directly and panels may have been repainted over time. It is therefore safer to describe the Peñarroya paintings as Copper Age or later prehistoric schematic art than to treat “5,000 years old” as a laboratory-established date for every individual mark. That uncertainty, however, does not favour a spacecraft explanation. It concerns where the paintings fall within prehistory, not whether they represent modern technology.

## How the spacecraft interpretation arose

The ancient-UFO claim became widely visible through the work of Spanish mystery writer J. J. Benítez. In his 2021 book *Mis primos*, Benítez presented the Peñarroya panel as what he considered the earliest documented indication of a UFO in the Iberian Peninsula. His argument relied substantially on information supplied by local mystery investigator José Manuel Frías after a visit to the paintings in 2008.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cordopolis.eldiario.es/cultura/literatura/j-j-benitez-dice-pinturas-rupestres-penarroya-representan-llegada-ovni-5-000-anos_1_8403381.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cordopolis.eldiario.es">[Cordópolis]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cordopolis.eldiario.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on eldiario.es.</span></span></span>

Frías drew attention to a sign positioned above a group of figures interpreted as dancers. He thought the shape resembled the “famous H” associated with Ummo, a supposed extraterrestrial civilisation that featured prominently in Spanish UFO culture from the 1960s onwards. Benítez then treated the resemblance as evidence that the prehistoric scene showed the arrival or presence of a flying object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cordopolis.eldiario.es/cultura/literatura/j-j-benitez-dice-pinturas-rupestres-penarroya-representan-llegada-ovni-5-000-anos_1_8403381.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cordopolis.eldiario.es">[Cordópolis]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cordopolis.eldiario.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on eldiario.es.</span></span></span>

None of those steps is supported by an artefact, inscription, independent dating result or archaeological feature indicating advanced machinery. The argument is visual and retrospective: the viewer begins with knowledge of UFO imagery and then recognises that imagery in an older, ambiguous form.

### The Ummo resemblance is especially weak

The claimed “H” does not point to a historically documented ancient symbol system. It points to Ummo, one of Spain’s most controversial modern UFO narratives. The Ummo material emerged through typed letters, symbols and photographs circulated in the twentieth century, not through archaeology. Linking a prehistoric mark to that emblem therefore does not independently confirm either story. It merely joins two disputed interpretations through a loose resemblance.

Simple lines joined by a crossbar are also not distinctive enough to carry such a conclusion. Similar arrangements can arise when artists schematise people, plants, tools, enclosures or abstract signs. They can also appear because cracks, fading pigment and later image enhancement alter how a form is perceived. To establish a meaningful connection, researchers would need a repeated set of matching symbols, a plausible route of cultural transmission and evidence that the signs carried the same meaning. The Peñarroya claim provides none of these.

## Why archaeologists reject the UFO claim

Archaeologists do not reject the spacecraft reading merely because it sounds extraordinary. They reject it because the conventional explanation accounts for the paintings more successfully and with fewer unsupported assumptions.

### The forms belong to a regional artistic tradition

Peñarroya is not an isolated panel containing a unique machine-like picture. Its bars, simplified bodies and grouped figures occur within schematic rock art across the Iberian Peninsula, particularly in Sierra Morena. Local heritage documentation specifically notes that bar motifs and groups interpreted as possible dances are frequent in this wider tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cordopolis.eldiario.es/cultura/literatura/j-j-benitez-dice-pinturas-rupestres-penarroya-representan-llegada-ovni-5-000-anos_1_8403381.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cordopolis.eldiario.es">[Cordópolis]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cordopolis.eldiario.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on eldiario.es.</span></span></span>

Comparable sites elsewhere in Sierra Morena contain stylised human figures, animals, circular signs, bars and ritual-looking arrangements. At Fuencaliente, north of Peñarroya across the provincial boundary, official heritage material describes an extensive schematic repertoire including anthropomorphic figures, bar motifs, triangular idols, circles and grouped scenes. Such comparisons show that unusual-looking geometry was normal within the artistic language of prehistoric communities in the region.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.turismocastillalamancha.es/es/cultura-y-patrimonio/arte-rupestre/arte-rupestre---esquem-tico-fuencaliente" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: turismocastillalamancha.es">[Turismo Castilla-La Mancha]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">turismocastillalamancha.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on turismocastillalamancha.es.</span></span></span>

A strong interpretation should explain the whole panel and its regional parallels. The archaeological reading does this: the Peñarroya images belong to a shared tradition of highly reduced human, animal, plant and symbolic forms. The UFO reading concentrates on one shape while disregarding the visual vocabulary surrounding it.

### There is no spacecraft anatomy

Claims about ancient vehicles are more persuasive when an image contains a consistent set of technological features: a clearly enclosed body, propulsion system, repeated structural details, interaction with the ground or an unambiguous sequence showing flight. Peñarroya offers none of these.

Descriptions of a disc, windows, landing legs or propulsion are modern additions to the act of viewing. They are not elements securely identified in archaeological recording. Nor is there evidence of unusual metal, heat damage, altered rock, anachronistic tools or materials that would support a physical visitation event.

The apparent scene of people beneath an aerial symbol is similarly uncertain. Rock art panels are not necessarily single moments painted at one time. Individual marks may have different dates, meanings or relationships. Spatial proximity alone does not prove that the lower figures are watching, greeting or worshipping the upper motif.

### The claim is not testable as presented

The spacecraft theory offers no clear method by which it could be proved wrong. A bar becomes a person when that suits the scene, an abstract sign becomes an alien emblem, and uncertainty about prehistoric meaning becomes room for extraterrestrial speculation. This is the reverse of archaeological method, which compares motifs, records pigment and superimposition, studies the landscape and tests interpretations against other sites.

An unusual visual resemblance can justify a question, but it cannot establish identity. A prehistoric mark that resembles a modern logo is no more likely to share its meaning than a natural rock formation resembling a face is likely to be a carved portrait.

## What is known and what remains uncertain

The evidence supports several firm conclusions. The paintings are authentic prehistoric heritage. They form part of a larger archaeological landscape associated with later prehistoric settlement and early metallurgy. Their style resembles schematic rock art found widely in Sierra Morena, and specialist treatment of the site predates the UFO interpretation by decades.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.raco.cat/index.php/Empuries/article/view/103252" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: raco.cat">[raco.cat]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">raco.cat</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Las pinturas del Abrigo Carmelo (Peñarroya, Córdoba) &#124; Empúries: revista d&#x27;arqueologia del món anticLas pinturas del Abrigo Carmelo (...</span></span></span>

Some details remain open. Archaeologists cannot confidently translate every bar or geometric figure into a specific object or ceremony. The grouped forms may represent dancing, ritual action or another social activity, but such readings are probabilities rather than recovered eyewitness accounts. Precise dates for individual figures may also remain uncertain without direct scientific dating.

Those gaps should not be confused with positive evidence for visitors from space. “We do not know exactly what this symbol meant” and “this symbol depicts an extraterrestrial craft” are very different claims. The first reflects the normal limits of prehistoric interpretation. The second requires evidence that has not been produced.

## How later reporting affected the story

Later publicity strengthened awareness of the ancient-UFO claim but did not strengthen its evidential basis. The 2021 reporting around Benítez’s book documented who promoted the idea and how it developed from the 2008 visit. ## Its place in Córdoba’s UFO history

Peñarroya’s rock art is unlike a conventional UFO report. There was no modern observation to investigate, no aircraft crew, radar return, police record or unexplained object moving through Córdoba’s airspace. The supposed incident exists only after a twentieth- or twenty-first-century viewer assigns a technological meaning to a prehistoric image.

That makes the case useful precisely because its weaknesses are so clear. It illustrates three recurring problems in ancient-UFO arguments:

**Present-day projection.** Modern viewers recognise spacecraft, antennae or logos because those images already exist in their culture.

**Selective attention.** One suggestive shape is enlarged while the panel’s ordinary regional features receive less attention.

**Uncertainty turned into evidence.** The inability to translate prehistoric symbolism is presented as support for an extraordinary answer rather than as a limit on what can responsibly be claimed.

The Peñarroya paintings deserve attention as part of Córdoba’s prehistoric landscape, not as proof that a UFO landed there around 2500 BC. Their genuine importance lies in what they reveal about the symbolic life of early communities in the Guadiato valley and their relationship with a prominent hill, a settlement and an emerging metallurgical economy. The ancient-spacecraft story adds a memorable modern layer to that heritage, but it remains an unsupported interpretation rather than an unresolved archaeological mystery.

    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
