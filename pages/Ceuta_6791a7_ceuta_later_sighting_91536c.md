## Introduction

After [Ceuta]({{ 'ceutas-ufo-mystery-beyond-the-radar/' | relative_url }})’s 1954 radar incident, the city’s UFO story continued through a small collection of reported sightings in 1968, 1969, 1974, 1983 and 1999. These later accounts are interesting because they show how an isolated military-radar mystery developed into a local tradition involving residents, journalists, amateur investigators and organised skywatches. They do not, however, provide a second case of comparable evidential weight.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Ceuta_6791a7_ceuta_later_sighting_91536c-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Later Sightings" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Nearly all the details come from specialist catalogues compiled years after the events. Original witness statements, photographs, police records, newspaper pages and investigation files are generally unavailable online. Some catalogue entries openly describe the reports as rumours pending further documentation. The result is a fragmented history: there may have been genuinely puzzling observations over Ceuta, but most cannot now be separated confidently from aircraft, meteors, planets, distorted memories or errors introduced as stories were copied between sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com/2021/06/16/ovnis-en-la-historia-de-ceuta/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com">[La esencia misma del Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wordpress.com.</span></span></span>

## The reported sighting years

The surviving chronology does not reveal a sustained “flap” in the usual sense of numerous closely documented reports over a short period. Instead, it consists of several isolated episodes separated by years, each with a different type of witness and a different evidential problem.

### 1968: a flashing object over the hills

At around midnight on 29 November 1968, several Ceuta residents reportedly watched an object shaped like a rugby ball pass over the city. It was said to emit red and blue flashes before moving rapidly above a hill identified in the catalogue as Monte García Aldave and disappearing after several minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com/2021/06/16/ovnis-en-la-historia-de-ceuta/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com">[La esencia misma del Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wordpress.com.</span></span></span>

The description is vivid but too incomplete for meaningful reconstruction. The number and names of the witnesses are not supplied, nor are their exact positions, the object’s direction, angular size or elevation. There is no known photograph, contemporary press cutting or meteorological assessment. The specialist catalogue preserving the report consequently advises treating it as a rumour until more information appears.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl/Casuistica-ovni-en-Ceuta.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl">[andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Andalucia Misteriosa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Casuistica ovni en Ceuta...</span></span></span>

Red and blue flashes might suggest aircraft navigation or anti-collision lights, particularly in a region with substantial civilian and military traffic. Yet that remains only a possibility. Without a precise route, duration and viewing geometry, neither an aircraft explanation nor an anomalous one can be tested.

### 1969: lights, alleged objects and traces

The most elaborate post-1954 story is dated only to late November 1969. According to the surviving account, Francisco Gil and Cristina Gil were travelling through Ceuta when they noticed a distant luminous object described as two concentric circles. A strong oscillating light supposedly came from its centre, with a weaker glow around its outer portion. The object appeared to descend towards a hilltop and then vanished behind trees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com/2021/06/16/ovnis-en-la-historia-de-ceuta/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com">[La esencia misma del Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wordpress.com.</span></span></span>

The couple later reached Punta Bermeja, where they reportedly saw a second object. This one was estimated to be about three metres across, remained stationary for approximately ten minutes and then departed rapidly. Members of a local investigative group called CEONIX were subsequently said to have found two grooves and other unexplained marks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com/2021/06/16/ovnis-en-la-historia-de-ceuta/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com">[La esencia misma del Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wordpress.com.</span></span></span>

If fully documented, this would be Ceuta’s most important civilian case: named witnesses, an apparently close or low-level observation, a prolonged stationary phase and possible physical traces. Unfortunately, the documentation needed to support those claims has not surfaced. There are no published measurements or photographs of the grooves, no soil analysis, no clear map of the alleged landing area and no surviving CEONIX report available through the cited compilations.

Even the quoted size estimates are difficult to interpret. A witness cannot normally determine that an unfamiliar light is 50 centimetres or three metres wide without knowing its distance. If the distance was assumed incorrectly, the calculated dimensions would be unreliable. The catalogue itself says that former CEONIX members would need to be contacted before the story could be assessed properly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl/Casuistica-ovni-en-Ceuta.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl">[andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Andalucia Misteriosa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Casuistica ovni en Ceuta...</span></span></span>

The 1969 episode is therefore better described as an unverified investigation claim than as a documented landing case. Its intriguing elements come from a narrative summary, not from recoverable primary evidence.

### 1974: journalists see a distant light

Shortly before or around midnight on 28 April 1974, several members of a Ceuta newspaper’s staff reportedly observed a luminous object at considerable height and distance. It appeared to move slowly, shone with a whitish-orange light and disappeared after only a few seconds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com/2021/06/16/ovnis-en-la-historia-de-ceuta/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com">[La esencia misma del Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wordpress.com.</span></span></span>

Journalists might seem unusually useful witnesses because a contemporary newspaper could have recorded the event. Yet the surviving catalogue does not name the publication, identify the observers or reproduce an article. Its own recommendation was to locate and interview the journalists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl/Casuistica-ovni-en-Ceuta.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl">[andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Andalucia Misteriosa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Casuistica ovni en Ceuta...</span></span></span>

The observation itself is also weakly distinctive. A distant light seen briefly and without a known reference point could have been an aircraft, meteor, satellite or bright astronomical object affected by haze. The witnesses’ professional background does not compensate for the lack of directional, temporal and observational detail.

## Skywatches and local UFO interest

The reported event of 7 January 1983 differs from the earlier cases because witnesses were deliberately searching the sky. Several groups around Ceuta and the Campo de Gibraltar reportedly organised an “UFO alert”, a coordinated night watch intended to detect unusual aerial activity. The resulting catalogue lists eleven observations during the night, mostly coloured lights and “fireballs”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com/2021/06/16/ovnis-en-la-historia-de-ceuta/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com">[La esencia misma del Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wordpress.com.</span></span></span>

At about 11.15 pm, observers in Ceuta reportedly saw a brilliant white light with bluish tones travelling rapidly and leaving a pronounced trail. A second observation, recorded at approximately 12.17 am, involved an orange, green and blue light moving from Ceuta towards the north of the Iberian Peninsula.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com/2021/06/16/ovnis-en-la-historia-de-ceuta/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com">[La esencia misma del Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wordpress.com.</span></span></span>

These descriptions sound dramatic, but they also resemble phenomena commonly encountered during prolonged skywatching. A brilliant fast-moving light with a visible trail is consistent with a meteor. Changes of colour can result from atmospheric scintillation, particularly when a bright star or planet is low above the horizon. Aircraft viewed head-on may appear almost stationary before turning, while navigation lights become more obvious as their angle changes.

The number of reports that night should not be treated as eleven independent confirmations of one object. The source describes multiple lights of varying colours and several fireball-like events, suggesting a set of separate observations rather than a single coherent incident. Nor is it clear whether the participating groups recorded precise bearings, compared watches, checked known aircraft movements or obtained independent reports from people who were unaware of the skywatch.

Organised observation can improve evidence when participants use calibrated cameras, agreed reporting forms and accurate time and direction measurements. It can also create selection bias: people expecting unusual objects may interpret ordinary but unfamiliar lights as significant. Modern official investigation methods distinguish between phenomena that remain unidentified after a thorough inquiry and those left unidentified merely because essential information was never collected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Classification &#124; GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN Classification Home \ The Geipan \ Classification A classification methodology conso...</span></span></span>

## The 1999 formation beside an aircraft

Ceuta’s latest commonly listed twentieth-century case concerns a daytime observation above the Strait of Gibraltar. Emilio V. Pomares Martínez said that he was standing in Calle Marina Española when he watched two military jets over the coast. After they had disappeared, he noticed a much larger jet aircraft travelling west to east at relatively low altitude. Four flat, circular objects appeared to flank it symmetrically, with two on each side.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com/2021/06/16/ovnis-en-la-historia-de-ceuta/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com">[La esencia misma del Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wordpress.com.</span></span></span>

This report has more contextual detail than many of the earlier stories: a named witness, a street, a daytime observation, an identifiable large aircraft and nearby military aviation. It also has a plausible conventional interpretation. The specialist publication *Papers d’OVNIS* reportedly concluded that the witness had probably seen four combat aircraft engaged in aerial refuelling with a tanker. Viewed from below or at a shallow angle, aircraft around a larger tanker could appear as dark circular or flattened shapes, particularly if glare obscured their wings and fuselages.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl/Casuistica-ovni-en-Ceuta.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl">[andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">andaluciamisteriosa.es.tl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Andalucia Misteriosa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Casuistica ovni en Ceuta...</span></span></span>

The catalogue contains an important dating inconsistency. Its reference code gives 3 July 1999, while the reproduced witness statement says 3 June. One later retelling also labels the entry as 1999 without resolving the discrepancy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com/2021/06/16/ovnis-en-la-historia-de-ceuta/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com">[La esencia misma del Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wordpress.com.</span></span></span> This matters because confirming a refuelling exercise would require the correct date, time and air-traffic record.

Even so, the presence of a large jet accompanied by four smaller objects in a military operating area makes the refuelling explanation much stronger than an interpretation involving four unknown craft. The case is not conclusively solved because no flight log or photograph accompanies the published account, but later reporting weakened rather than strengthened its value as a UFO mystery.

## Why the later cases are harder to assess

The central difficulty is not that every Ceuta witness must have been mistaken. It is that the records are too thin to establish what they saw. The surviving accounts usually lack the information required to distinguish an unexplained event from an unexplained report.

Several recurring problems stand out:

* **The sources are derivative.** The same short narratives appear on multiple websites, often with nearly identical wording. Repetition demonstrates circulation, not independent confirmation. Both main online compilations rely heavily on earlier material attributed to Ángel Rivero, a catalogue associated with J. G. Blanco and the specialist newsletter *Papers d’OVNIS*.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com/2021/06/16/ovnis-en-la-historia-de-ceuta/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com">[La esencia misma del Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wordpress.com.</span></span></span>

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* **Primary records are missing.** No original interview forms, photographs, newspaper reports or laboratory results accompany the 1968, 1969, 1974 or 1983 entries.
* **Basic data were not preserved.** Bearings, angular measurements, weather conditions, aircraft checks and astronomical comparisons are mostly absent.
* **Some details are internally unstable.** The 1952 duration varies sharply between versions, while the 1999 date changes between June and July. Such inconsistencies show how easily catalogue data can become corrupted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com/2021/06/16/ovnis-en-la-historia-de-ceuta/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com">[La esencia misma del Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">revistavocesdelmisterio.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wordpress.com.</span></span></span>
* **Investigations are asserted rather than demonstrated.** The alleged CEONIX ground traces are potentially important, but their evidential value cannot be assessed without the group’s original notes and measurements.

</div>

Spain’s official declassified UFO archive illustrates the difference between a catalogue entry and an investigation file. The Ministry of Defence collection contains approximately 1,900 pages across 80 case files from 1962 to 1995. Depending on the incident, these files may include summaries, witness interviews, operational reports and meteorological information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span> The publicly indexed collection includes numerous cases from mainland Spain, the Canary Islands and Spanish airspace, but fresh searches do not reveal a comparably developed file for the later Ceuta reports discussed here.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/indice_campo.do?campo=idtitulo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

Absence from that archive does not prove that an event never happened. Many civilian observations would never have reached the Air Force. It does mean that claims of military corroboration or official investigation should not be assumed unless the relevant document can be produced.

## What the later sightings add to Ceuta’s history

The post-1954 reports broaden Ceuta’s UFO history socially more than they strengthen it evidentially. The witnesses shift from an alleged military radar installation to residents, a courting couple, newspaper staff, organised enthusiasts and a daytime observer watching military aircraft. The stories also follow wider changes in Spanish UFO culture: unexplained lights in the 1960s, alleged landing traces, public interest during the 1970s and coordinated skywatch events in the 1980s.

Ceuta’s geography helped sustain that interest. From the city, observers look across a narrow and heavily travelled strait containing aircraft, ships, distant coastlines and lights at different elevations. Atmospheric haze can obscure distance and shape, while military activity naturally encourages speculation when aircraft cannot immediately be identified. These conditions make unusual observations likely, but they also increase the range of ordinary objects capable of appearing strange.

The fairest assessment is therefore case-specific. The 1968 and 1974 lights are too poorly recorded to classify. The 1969 story contains potentially significant claims but lacks the original investigation needed to support them. The 1983 skywatch produced numerous observations, yet its descriptions favour meteors, aircraft and astronomical lights over a single extraordinary event. The 1999 formation has a credible aerial-refuelling explanation and is the clearest example of later analysis reducing a case’s mystery.

Together, the reports created a modest Ceuta sighting tradition after 1954. They show that local curiosity survived long after the radar episode, but they do not establish a continuing pattern of verified unknown craft. In Ceuta’s UFO history, the later cases remain chiefly a record of stories awaiting documentation—and, in several instances, documentation that may no longer exist.
