## Introduction

At 04:12 on 13 September 1991, the commander of the security guard at the EVA-4 air-surveillance station near Roses saw a brilliant white light apparently circling above the installation. A guard corporal saw the same phenomenon, and another soldier later said that he too had noticed a white circle moving around the base. The observation reportedly lasted about five or six minutes and ended with the light disappearing suddenly. Yet the radar-room operator detected no strange echo and reported no equipment fault.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-eva-4-rosas-1991-1991-09-13-avistamiento-en-eva-4-rosas-gerona" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en EVA-4 Rosas, Gerona (13 September 1991), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1991 · U...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1991</span></span></span>

That combination makes the Roses case one of [Girona]({{ 'gironas-ufo-cases-solved-weak-and/' | relative_url }})’s most interesting but easily overstated UFO reports. It is stronger than a solitary civilian memory because military personnel reported it promptly through official channels. It is weaker than a confirmed intrusion because the apparent object’s size, height and flight path were visual estimates, while the station’s principal instrument supplied no supporting detection. The surviving evidence therefore supports an unidentified light sighting, not proof that a large physical craft flew over the radar station.

## The guards’ account of a circling white light

The incident began while the guard commander was outside the main building, near the non-commissioned officers’ car park, waiting for the incoming guard to undergo its weapons inspection. According to his written statement, he looked upwards to judge the night’s weather and suddenly noticed a bright white object overhead. He described it as circular, with a central area brighter than the rest, repeatedly tracing circles above the station.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-eva-4-rosas-1991-1991-09-13-avistamiento-en-eva-4-rosas-gerona" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en EVA-4 Rosas, Gerona (13 September 1991), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1991 · U...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1991</span></span></span>

The commander called the guard corporal from inside the building. The corporal came outside and reportedly saw the same thing. They watched together for roughly five minutes. The commander then summoned another recruit so that more people could witness the event, but the light disappeared before that soldier reached the exterior. After the guard change, a soldier stationed at the access-control point was asked whether he had noticed anything unusual and replied that he had seen a white circle moving around the installation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-eva-4-rosas-1991-1991-09-13-avistamiento-en-eva-4-rosas-gerona" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en EVA-4 Rosas, Gerona (13 September 1991), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1991 · U...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1991</span></span></span>

The Air Force summary reduced this sequence to a primary observation confirmed by two other members of the guard. That is broadly consistent with the commander’s statement, although the documents do not preserve full, separately developed interviews with every observer. The file gives the commander’s detailed narrative, but the supporting witnesses’ accounts are either brief or reported through him. This matters because agreement that a light was present is not necessarily agreement about its distance, physical form or exact motion.

The commander estimated that the phenomenon was between 100 and 200 metres above the station. The later intelligence summary described it more precisely as a sphere with a radius of about five metres, at an altitude of roughly 150 metres, making right-hand turns with a radius of approximately 200 metres. No sound was heard.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-eva-4-rosas-1991-1991-09-13-avistamiento-en-eva-4-rosas-gerona" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en EVA-4 Rosas, Gerona (13 September 1991), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1991 · U...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1991</span></span></span>

Those figures give the story its apparent physical solidity, but the original description offers no indication that the witnesses measured them. There was no recorded triangulation from separated observation points, no photograph and no known range-finding equipment directed at the light. “Five metres in radius” should therefore be read as an impression derived from an assumed distance, not as a measured diameter.

The reported rate of one circuit every six seconds is especially revealing. If an object had genuinely flown around a 200-metre-radius circle at that rate, its speed would have been about 750 kilometres per hour. A ten-metre-wide body travelling so quickly only 100–200 metres above a military compound, making repeated tight turns without noise, would be an extraordinary aircraft claim. But that speed follows entirely from two uncertain visual estimates: the radius of the apparent circle and the belief that the light itself was moving through three-dimensional space.

A projected spot moving across cloud or haze can appear to follow a large, fast circular path even though no object occupies the apparent position. Searchlights and similar beams are a recognised source of reports involving luminous patches sweeping rapidly across clouds, particularly when the beam below the illuminated layer is not obvious to a distant observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-science/things-mistaken-for-ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skyatnightmagazine.com">[Sky at Night Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skyatnightmagazine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Image A Russian bulava missile misfired in December 2009 to produce this strange spectacle.... ## What the station radar did and did not detect

During the observation, the guard commander contacted the operator in the station’s surveillance room and asked whether the equipment was behaving abnormally or displaying any strange echo. The operator replied that it was not. The file therefore records two negative findings: no unusual radar target and no reported malfunction that might have accompanied the visual event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-eva-4-rosas-1991-1991-09-13-avistamiento-en-eva-4-rosas-gerona" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en EVA-4 Rosas, Gerona (13 September 1991), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1991 · U...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1991</span></span></span>

This is the central tension in the case. EVA-4 was not simply a barracks where soldiers happened to see a light. It was an Air Force surveillance unit whose mission included obtaining and transmitting radar information for the monitoring and control of airspace. The station’s location on the mountain above Roses formed part of Spain’s north-eastern air-defence coverage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ejercitodelaireydelespacio.defensa.gob.es/EA/ejercitodelaire/es/Unidades/Unidad/e9b448f6-7fc7-11ee-99f3-005056bf91c5/?path=%2Fsites%2Finternet.es%2F.content%2Funidad%2Funidad_00011.xml&amp;resourceId=e9b448f6-7fc7-11ee-99f3-005056bf91c5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ejercitodelaireydelespacio.defensa.gob.es">[Ejército del Aire]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ejercitodelaireydelespacio.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ejército del Aire- Unidades</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidad- Unidades - Unidad Inicio Unidades Escuadrón de Vigilancia Aérea no4 (EVA 4) y Acuartelamiento Aéreo Rosas Ver Todo Cuartel Genera...</span></span></span>

A large conventional aircraft circling directly over such a station for several minutes would ordinarily be expected to attract instrumental attention. The absence of a return therefore weakens the literal interpretation of the visual estimates. It makes a ten-metre-diameter craft at approximately 150 metres less persuasive than the simpler proposition that the guards saw a light whose true distance and nature they could not determine.

However, “no radar return” does not mean that radar proved there was nothing there. Surveillance radar is not an all-seeing camera. Detection depends on the beam’s coverage, the target’s height and position, terrain, equipment settings, filtering and the amount of radio energy reflected back. Low-altitude targets can be screened by terrain or lie below a radar beam, while ground clutter and processing designed to suppress unwanted returns may complicate the recognition of objects close to a site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aip_html/part2_enr_section_1.1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The characteristics of radio waves are such that they normally travel in a continuous straight line unless they ­are:... Relatively low...</span></span></span>

The EVA-4 documents do not provide the technical information needed to reconstruct those conditions. They do not identify which radar display was checked, its elevation coverage at very short range, the filters in use, whether stored data were reviewed afterwards or whether the operator merely inspected the live display after receiving the call. The record also does not state whether the supposed path lay inside a near-range or overhead coverage gap.

For that reason, the negative radar report has a limited but important meaning:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* It does **not** independently confirm the guards’ estimated height, size or circular flight path.
* It makes an ordinary, radar-reflective aircraft of the described dimensions less likely, assuming it was within effective coverage.
* It remains compatible with a distant light, an optical projection, a small or weakly reflecting target, or a phenomenon outside the relevant radar volume.
* It cannot support claims that the object deliberately evaded radar or possessed “stealth” technology; the file contains no evidence for either proposition.

</div>

The most defensible reading is therefore neither “the radar disproved the witnesses” nor “the absence of an echo makes the object more mysterious”. The visual report and the radar display were observing different kinds of evidence, and the surviving file does not contain enough technical detail to reconcile them conclusively.

## Why the size and flight path remain uncertain

Human vision can establish that a light has a particular direction and an apparent angular size, but it cannot by itself reveal the light’s distance. A small nearby source and a much larger distant source may occupy the same angle in the observer’s field of view. Once an observer assumes a distance, an estimated physical diameter and speed can follow, even when that starting assumption is wrong.

That problem is acute in the Roses report because the witnesses apparently saw brightness rather than a clearly illuminated solid structure. The commander described a white circular form with a brighter centre, while the access-control soldier referred simply to a white circle. The documents mention no visible surface, wings, fuselage, exhaust, shadow or interaction with the station. Nor was there any sound, despite the short distance and high apparent speed claimed in the summary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-eva-4-rosas-1991-1991-09-13-avistamiento-en-eva-4-rosas-gerona" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en EVA-4 Rosas, Gerona (13 September 1991), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1991 · U...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1991</span></span></span>

The distinction between a **luminous body** and an **illuminated patch** is therefore crucial. If the guards saw a self-contained object at 150 metres, the estimated scale and manoeuvres would be remarkable. If they saw light reflected from a layer of cloud or mist, the apparent object could seem circular, silent and capable of rapid repeated turns without possessing any physical dimensions at that location.

A later Cadena SER report quoted veteran Spanish UFO investigator Vicente-Juan Ballester Olmos as favouring the reflection of a moving spotlight or projector beam as the likely explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cadenaser.com/cataluna/2023/03/23/a-trets-amb-un-extraterrestre-el-testimoni-sense-confirmar-de-la-base-militar-de-roses-sercat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cadenaser.com">[Cadena SER]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cadenaser.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cadenaser.com.</span></span></span> This interpretation fits several reported features: whiteness, a brighter central area, circular repetition, silence, the absence of a radar echo and sudden disappearance. A light source being switched off, redirected or blocked could vanish instantly without accelerating away.

Nevertheless, the spotlight explanation was not demonstrated in the original military file. No projector was identified, no source position was traced and no weather record confirming a suitable cloud layer appears in the nine-page dossier. It is best treated as a plausible retrospective explanation rather than a solved identification.

Other ordinary possibilities are also difficult to test after the fact. An astronomical object viewed through broken cloud can appear to shift or circle as the cloud moves, although the witnesses’ repeated circular-motion description would require a specific visual configuration. A helicopter or small aircraft seems less satisfactory because of the reported silence, tight apparent circuit and missing radar return, but the file contains no documented check of local aviation movements. None of these alternatives can now be evaluated confidently without information that investigators did not preserve.

## How the Air Force handled the report

The incident was reported through the military chain rather than emerging years later as folklore. The head of EVA-4 prepared an internal report, which was forwarded through the Air Force command structure on 25 September 1991, less than two weeks after the sighting. The surviving dossier was assigned file number 910913 and originally carried a confidential classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160070873" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

That official provenance gives the case historical value. It establishes that personnel at the station reported an unusual visual event at the stated time and that the Air Force retained paperwork concerning it. It does not, however, show that investigators conducted a comprehensive inquiry. The file contains no recorded site reconstruction, witness-position diagram, angular measurements, photographs, meteorological assessment, astronomical comparison, civilian-air-traffic check or examination of possible ground-based lights.

The declassification review in February 1996 concluded only that there was no reason to keep the material classified. The public catalogue records formal declassification under an Air Force order dated 27 February 1996.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160070873" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span> This administrative conclusion did not identify the phenomenon and should not be mistaken for a finding that something extraordinary had penetrated Spanish airspace.

The distinction is important across Spain’s declassified UFO archive. These files were retained because unusual aerial reports could have implications for aviation and defence, not because the Air Force certified extraterrestrial vehicles. The Ministry of Defence describes the collection as reports reviewed for possible declassification, with summaries, considerations and classification decisions included in each dossier.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

In the Roses case, the process preserved the initial testimony but did little to resolve it. By the time the file was opened to the public, the opportunity to check temporary lights, local activities, detailed weather and the exact operating state of the radar had long passed.

## What the missing radar echo changes

Without the radar result, the Roses account might be presented simply as a multiple-witness military observation of a large, silent sphere. With it, the evidential picture is substantially different. The witnesses probably did see an unusual light, but the strongest objective instrument at the location did not corroborate the interpretation that a sizeable aircraft was circling close above them.

That does not make the testimony worthless. The commander’s report was contemporary, specific and apparently made in the course of official duty. A second guard observed the light with him, and another soldier supplied brief supporting testimony. These features reduce the likelihood that the entire incident was invented after the event.

They do not remove the main weaknesses:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The witnesses were not observing from widely separated positions, so there was no reliable triangulation.
* Estimates of altitude and diameter depended on an unverified assumption about distance.
* The supporting accounts were not developed with equal detail.
* No image, physical trace or independent civilian report is preserved in the file.
* No unusual radar echo was reported.
* The documented inquiry did not systematically test ordinary light sources or environmental conditions.

</div>

The case is therefore most accurately described as **unresolved but weakly instrumented**. “Unresolved” means that the surviving documentation does not establish a definite source. It does not mean that all conventional explanations failed, because most were never properly investigated.

Within Girona’s UFO history, Roses remains important less for what it proves than for the evidential lesson it provides. Trained or disciplined witnesses can report something genuinely puzzling while making uncertain judgements about its scale and movement. A military setting can ensure that a report survives in an official archive, but the presence of radar equipment does not automatically turn a visual sighting into a radar-confirmed event.

## The most balanced assessment

The secure core of the 1991 EVA-4 incident is modest. Shortly after four in the morning, several military personnel at the Roses station saw a bright white light apparently moving in repeated circles. It was silent, remained visible for several minutes and disappeared abruptly. An internal report was filed and eventually declassified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-eva-4-rosas-1991-1991-09-13-avistamiento-en-eva-4-rosas-gerona" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en EVA-4 Rosas, Gerona (13 September 1991), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1991 · U...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1991</span></span></span>

The more dramatic elements—a roughly ten-metre-wide sphere, only about 150 metres overhead, travelling in rapid 200-metre-radius circuits—were estimates rather than measurements. Taken literally, they describe an object performing extreme low-level manoeuvres without sound while remaining invisible to the station radar. Taken as visual impressions of a moving light, they no longer require an extraordinary aircraft.

A projected light on cloud or haze provides a coherent possible explanation and has been endorsed in later sceptical commentary, but it was not proved by tracing an actual projector.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cadenaser.com/cataluna/2023/03/23/a-trets-amb-un-extraterrestre-el-testimoni-sense-confirmar-de-la-base-militar-de-roses-sercat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cadenaser.com">[Cadena SER]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cadenaser.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cadenaser.com.</span></span></span> The case should consequently remain open at the narrow level of source identification, while the claim of a large craft above the base should be regarded as poorly supported.

Roses is thus neither a decisive debunking nor a strong radar-visual UFO case. It is a documented military sighting whose most important evidence is also its principal limitation: the guards saw something, but the radar did not return it.
