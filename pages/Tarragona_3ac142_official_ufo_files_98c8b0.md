## Introduction

Spanish Air Force UFO files provide the most important documentary record for unusual aerial reports connected with [Tarragona]({{ 'tarragonas-ufo-cases-between-mystery-and/' | relative_url }}). The central archive is the 1969 Reus Air Base case, where military personnel and aviation staff reported a strange aerial object and the Spanish Air Force opened an official investigation. The surviving file is significant not because it proves an unknown craft, but because it shows how Spanish authorities recorded, assessed and eventually explained an unusual observation. The document was later declassified and made available through the Ministry of Defence’s digital collections, allowing the public to examine the original investigation material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160068825" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

For Tarragona’s UFO history, these files are valuable as evidence of government procedure rather than confirmation of extraordinary claims. ## Cold War aerial investigations around Tarragona

During the Cold War period, reports of unidentified aerial phenomena were treated by many air forces as potential aviation or security questions. In Spain, the Ejército del Aire (Air Force) maintained a system for receiving and analysing reports involving military personnel, aircraft operations or observations near sensitive locations. The resulting “Expedientes OVNI” collection contains official investigations of unusual aerial observations made across Spanish airspace. The Ministry of Defence later released the material through the Biblioteca Virtual de Defensa, creating a public archive of 80 files and around 1,900 pages of documentation relating to these investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

Tarragona’s strongest connection to this archive is the former Reus Air Base, an important aviation location that gave the 1969 report a level of official attention that many civilian sightings never received. A report from a remote witness describing a light in the sky could be difficult to verify, but an observation involving controllers, aircraft movements and a military installation created a formal chain of reporting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160068825" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

### The Reus Air Base file of 13 May 1969

The [Reus case]({{ 'reus-case/' | relative_url }}) began when personnel at the air base reported an unusual bright object near the path of a departing Boeing 727. The official investigation record, titled *Avistamiento de fenómenos extraños en la base aérea de Reus: 13 de Mayo de 1969*, was prepared by the Air Command’s staff and intelligence section. The archive identifies it as a 1969 document later declassified under JEMA decision 3382 on 24 May 1993.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160068825" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

The importance of the case comes from the combination of circumstances:

* the observation took place at an active airfield;
* several trained observers were involved;
* aircraft operations could be checked against the report;
* the event generated an official military investigation rather than remaining only a newspaper story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160068825" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

These features explain why the Reus incident remains one of Tarragona’s best-known UFO cases. They also demonstrate a key distinction in historical UFO research: reliable witnesses do not automatically mean an extraordinary explanation. A witness can accurately describe an unusual appearance while the cause remains uncertain or later receives a conventional interpretation.

## Declassified documents and archives

The release of Spanish Air Force UFO files changed the way researchers could approach cases such as Reus. Instead of relying only on later accounts, books or newspaper reports, investigators could consult official paperwork containing witness statements, technical notes and conclusions from military analysts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

The archive system itself is important because it shows that the Spanish military did not simply collect stories without evaluation. The files were organised as investigations of “strange phenomena” observed in Spanish airspace, involving Air Force personnel or material. After the declassification process began in the 1990s, copies were preserved and later digitised for public consultation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

For Tarragona researchers, the archive provides several types of evidence:

* **Primary observations:** records of what witnesses reported seeing and when they saw it.
* **Investigation methods:** information on how military officers checked [possible explanations]({{ 'possible-explanations-f4583f/' | relative_url }}).
* **Administrative history:** evidence that authorities considered these reports serious enough to document.
* **Limits of official records:** the files record investigations, not proof that the reported objects were unknown technologies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160068825" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

The availability of these documents also helps separate historical fact from later storytelling. The fact that a case appears in a military archive confirms that a report was investigated. It does not by itself confirm the original interpretation of the event.

## What the Reus investigation concluded

The official handling of the Reus sighting illustrates the cautious approach found in many Spanish Air Force UFO investigations. The investigation examined the observation and considered whether natural or technical explanations could account for the appearance. Later summaries of the case describe the final interpretation as an optical effect involving atmospheric conditions, sunlight and the relationship between the observers, the aircraft and the position of the Sun.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160068825" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

This conclusion does not mean the witnesses imagined the event. The military record treated the observation as a genuine report requiring examination. The unresolved element was not whether people saw something unusual, but what produced that appearance. This distinction is central to understanding official UFO files: the word “unidentified” describes the initial state of an observation, not necessarily the final explanation.

The Reus case therefore occupies a middle position in Tarragona’s UFO history. It is stronger than an unsupported anecdote because it has an official paper trail, but it does not provide evidence of an extraordinary vehicle or unknown technology. Its historical value lies in showing the investigation process itself.

## What official files can prove

Spanish Air Force UFO archives are often misunderstood because the existence of a classified or declassified document can be presented as more dramatic than the contents justify. The Tarragona files are most useful when read as records of decision-making.

They can show:

* that an unusual aerial report occurred;
* that authorities received and investigated it;
* which witnesses and circumstances shaped the inquiry;
* what explanations investigators considered;
* how conclusions changed over time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

This makes the Tarragona archive a useful example of how official UFO records should be interpreted. The most interesting question is often not “what hidden answer did the file reveal?” but “how did trained investigators move from an unusual observation towards an explanation?”

## Why Tarragona’s military files still matter

The Reus documents remain important because they preserve a rare combination of aviation history, Cold War administration and public curiosity. They show a moment when military organisations had to respond to reports that did not immediately fit ordinary expectations, while also applying normal investigative standards.

For Tarragona, the archive adds depth to the province’s UFO history. It moves the subject away from simple mystery narratives and towards a more useful historical question: how institutions handle uncertainty.
