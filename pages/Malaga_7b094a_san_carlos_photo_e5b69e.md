## Introduction

The San Carlos photograph is one of Málaga’s most recognisable UFO images because it appears to show a solid, disc-shaped object photographed in daylight over an urban area. Taken in January 1984 in the San Carlos neighbourhood of Málaga, the image quickly reached national newspapers and became part of the province’s UFO folklore. However, its importance lies less in proving an extraordinary event than in showing the difficulties of using a single photograph as evidence. The original story involved witnesses, a visible object and a physical image, but the public record does not provide the kind of technical verification that would normally allow a photograph to be treated as conclusive evidence.

## Málaga Hoy

The case remains debated because it sits between two categories. It is stronger than a simple anonymous UFO claim because named witnesses reported the event and a photograph exists. At the same time, it is weaker than a scientifically documented aerial anomaly because key information about the image, the camera, the original film and independent analysis has never been fully available in the public domain.

## The 1984 San Carlos sighting account

On 19 January 1984, reports stated that two young men, Antonio Romero and David Romero, were taking photographs of their dogs in the San Carlos area of Málaga when they noticed an unusual object in the sky. Contemporary reporting described the object as visible for only a few seconds before disappearing, with other people in the area also said to have witnessed the event. The photograph was taken with a small 16mm pocket camera and was later published by newspapers including El País.

## El País

The circumstances initially made the case attractive to UFO investigators. A daylight photograph over a built-up area appeared more persuasive than many night-time light reports because it seemed to show a physical object with a defined shape. The witnesses also claimed that the object was not a distant point of light but a structured craft-like form, which encouraged comparisons with classic “flying saucer” reports.
## Málaga Hoy

However, the short duration of the observation created an immediate difficulty. A sighting lasting only seconds leaves little opportunity for accurate estimates of distance, size, speed or movement. Without independent measurements, statements such as “large object” or “fast movement” remain witness impressions rather than precise data.

## Claims about the object's appearance and movement

The reported object was described as a dark, disc-like or saucer-shaped form moving through the sky. Later accounts associated with UFO literature presented it as a classic structured craft, sometimes adding details about its apparent size and behaviour. These descriptions helped establish the image’s reputation, but many of the more detailed interpretations came from later investigations rather than from independently recorded measurements made at the time.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://es.scribd.com/document/517288568/Jimenez-Iker-Encuentros-O-V-N-I-La-Historia-De-Los-Ovni-En-Espana" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: es.scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">es.scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jimenez Iker</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jimenez Iker</span></span></span>

One important issue in evaluating the photograph is that appearance alone does not identify an object. A camera records light, contrast and perspective, not the physical nature of what was photographed. A blurred object, a nearby small item, an unusual reflection or an object at an unknown distance can appear very different depending on the camera, lens and exposure conditions.

The available investigations did not produce a widely accepted technical solution. Some UFO researchers treated the image as an important unexplained photograph, while critics highlighted weaknesses in the investigation process, including limited access to original photographic material and questions about whether the available analysis was sufficient to rule out ordinary explanations.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.ufomagazines.com/1a-epoca-cuadernos-de-ufologia-no-10-11-1985/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufomagazines.com">[UFO Magazine Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufomagazines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

## Why photographic verification matters


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The main credibility question is not simply whether the photograph looks unusual. It is whether the image can be independently tested. A strong photographic case normally requires several forms of supporting evidence:

</div>

The original negative or original digital file: This allows examination of exposure, alterations and image structure.

A complete camera history: Knowing the camera, lens, settings and photographic process helps establish what the image could contain.

Independent technical analysis: Multiple specialists should be able to examine the material without relying only on witness statements or copies.

Environmental information: Measurements of distance, angle, lighting and background features help determine whether the object’s apparent size and movement are realistic.

The San Carlos case has attracted attention precisely because some of these elements are missing or inaccessible. The published image created a strong impression, but an impressive appearance is not the same as a verified identification. This distinction is central to many historical UFO photographs: the image may be genuine as a photograph while the interpretation of what it shows remains uncertain.

## The credibility debate after the initial publicity

The early media attention gave the San Carlos photograph a lasting reputation. Being published in major newspapers helped transform a local Málaga report into a nationally known UFO case. Yet media coverage is not the same as investigation. Newspapers can accurately report that witnesses saw something and that a photograph exists, but they usually do not have the resources to conduct forensic image analysis.
## El País

Later UFO-focused examinations kept the case alive but also exposed the limits of the available evidence. Some investigators argued that the photograph deserved continued attention because no simple explanation had conclusively replaced the original claim. Others argued that an unresolved image should not automatically be treated as evidence of an unknown craft, especially when important technical records are absent.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.ufomagazines.com/1a-epoca-cuadernos-de-ufologia-no-10-11-1985/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufomagazines.com">[UFO Magazine Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufomagazines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

This difference is the key point in assessing credibility. “Unexplained” can mean that available information is insufficient to identify an object; it does not necessarily mean that the most extraordinary explanation is the strongest one.

## Where the San Carlos photograph stands today

Within Málaga’s UFO history, the San Carlos photograph remains notable because it combines three elements that often make cases culturally durable: a local setting, named witnesses and a striking image. It is one of the province’s best-known photographic UFO reports and continues to appear in discussions of Spanish UFO history.
## Málaga Hoy

From an evidence perspective, however, the case remains limited. The photograph has not achieved the level of verification that would be expected for a confirmed unusual aircraft or unknown technology. The strongest conclusion supported by the public record is that a photograph was taken during a reported sighting in San Carlos, but the identity of the object in the image remains unresolved.

Its lasting value is therefore historical rather than definitive: the San Carlos photograph shows how quickly a compelling image can shape a UFO story, while also demonstrating why photographs require careful technical examination before they can establish what was actually seen in the sky.
