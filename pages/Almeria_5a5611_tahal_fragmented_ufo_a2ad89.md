## Introduction

The Tahal sighting is important less because it offers strong evidence of an extraordinary craft than because it exposes the fragile way much of Almería’s UFO history has survived. The clearest accessible account says that four people in Tahal saw an intense light flood a room through its windows for a few seconds during a wider series of luminous reports over southern and eastern Spain in December 1976. It records no direction, shape, speed, altitude, photograph or named testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.afu.se/Downloads/Magazines/Spain%20and%20Catalonia/Stendek%20%28CEI%2C%20eISSN%202604-1383%29/Stendek%20-%20Vol%2009%20No%2032%20-%20June%201978.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.afu.se">[files.afu.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.afu.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1978</span></span></span>

Even the date is unstable. Later Almería writing places the Tahal incident on 21 December, while the specialist source that preserved it carries a confusing mixture of 20 and 29 December.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.albertocerezuela.com/ovnis-sobre-aguadulce-misterios-de-almeria/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: albertocerezuela.com">[albertocerezuela.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">albertocerezuela.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span></span></span> This does not make the report worthless, but it means that each retelling must not be mistaken for another independent confirmation. Tahal is therefore a useful test case for a wider question: can Almería’s fragmented UFO records be trusted? The answer is yes as evidence that reports circulated, but only cautiously as evidence of what was actually in the sky.

## What survives from the Tahal sighting

The most substantial surviving source presently available is issue 32 of *Stendek*, published by Barcelona’s Centro de Estudios Interplanetarios in June 1978. Its article, written by Fermín Sánchez de Medina, attempted to assemble reports of a fast, luminous phenomenon seen from Valencia and Alicante through Murcia, Almería, Granada, Málaga, Cádiz and Melilla. The author acknowledged at the outset that the material was heterogeneous, rarely first-hand and largely extracted from newspapers because local investigators were scarce or their records remained in private collections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.afu.se/Downloads/Magazines/Spain%20and%20Catalonia/Stendek%20%28CEI%2C%20eISSN%202604-1383%29/Stendek%20-%20Vol%2009%20No%2032%20-%20June%201978.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.afu.se">[files.afu.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.afu.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1978</span></span></span>

Those details establish a reported luminous event, but not an observed flying object in any precise sense. The witnesses apparently saw its illumination rather than a clearly described body. Without their positions in the room, the orientation of the windows, the weather, the exact date and time, or statements recorded separately from each person, it is impossible to reconstruct the geometry.

The absence of names is particularly limiting. “Four people” may mean four people who independently looked outside and described the same object, or four occupants who noticed a flash and discussed it together. These are not evidentially equivalent. A group sharing one visual environment can corroborate that a room became bright, but it cannot automatically provide four independent estimates of the source’s movement, colour or distance.

The date problem further weakens reconstruction. A later Almería account calls it a 21 December 1976 sighting and cites page four of *Stendek* number 32. The magazine’s article, however, is headed as a case from 29 December, while its opening summary refers to a phenomenon at about 1.50 on 20 December.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.albertocerezuela.com/ovnis-sobre-aguadulce-misterios-de-almeria/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: albertocerezuela.com">[albertocerezuela.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">albertocerezuela.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span></span></span> Whether this arose from typographical error, confusion between the night and following calendar day, or the merging of different reports cannot now be decided from the published text alone.

That discrepancy matters because checking astronomy, aviation and meteorological records depends on an exact time window. A one-day uncertainty may be manageable; a possible nine-day difference is not. It also demonstrates why a polished later date should not be treated as firmer simply because it is repeated without qualification.

## Tahal was one fragment of a much larger luminous event

The *Stendek* article did not present Tahal as an isolated close encounter. It placed it alongside numerous reports of a brief, fast and brightly coloured phenomenon across southern Spain. Witnesses elsewhere described a red, yellow, violet or blue object, often with a green or blue-green trail. Reported durations were commonly only four to eight seconds, and several locations experienced a strong flash or temporary daylight-like illumination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.afu.se/Downloads/Magazines/Spain%20and%20Catalonia/Stendek%20%28CEI%2C%20eISSN%202604-1383%29/Stendek%20-%20Vol%2009%20No%2032%20-%20June%201978.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.afu.se">[files.afu.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.afu.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1978</span></span></span>

In Almería city, the magazine listed several witnesses near the port who reportedly saw a cylindrical object and said the surrounding area was illuminated green. At Huéscar in neighbouring Granada, four witnesses described an intensely luminous sphere and a glow that grew until it resembled dawn. In Lorca, between 30 and 40 people reportedly saw a red ball with a long green trail for four or five seconds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.afu.se/Downloads/Magazines/Spain%20and%20Catalonia/Stendek%20%28CEI%2C%20eISSN%202604-1383%29/Stendek%20-%20Vol%2009%20No%2032%20-%20June%201978.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.afu.se">[files.afu.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.afu.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1978</span></span></span>

Viewed together, these reports make a conventional atmospheric explanation more plausible than the idea of separate structured craft visiting each location. A bright meteor or fireball can appear over a very large area, move with extraordinary apparent speed, show changing colours, leave a luminous trail and briefly illuminate buildings or landscape. Modern monitored fireballs over Spain have been visible from hundreds of kilometres away and have produced intense flashes as meteoroids fragmented high in the atmosphere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cadenaser.com/andalucia/2025/10/20/espectaculo-cosmico-sobre-andalucia-una-bola-de-fuego-ilumina-el-cielo-a-mas-de-600-kilometros-de-distancia-ser-malaga/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cadenaser.com">[cadenaser.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cadenaser.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A pesar de parecer cercana, el fenómeno ocurrió a más de 600 kilómetros de distancia, sobre el norte de Marruecos. That comparison is not a retrospective identification of the 1976 event. No instrument record, trajectory calculation or recovered contemporary astronomical report has been located that conclusively matches the confused dates. It is instead an evidential judgement: the short duration, wide geographical spread, reported trail and intense illumination fit a fireball better than they fit a low-altitude vehicle manoeuvring independently above Tahal.

The original compiler partly undermined his own more exotic interpretation by admitting that the reported times and directions were mutually incompatible. Only two witnesses in the wider dataset were said to have checked the exact minute, and those timings did not fit the proposed route. He therefore treated the chronology flexibly and inferred a high-altitude, extremely fast path from approximate newspaper accounts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.afu.se/Downloads/Magazines/Spain%20and%20Catalonia/Stendek%20%28CEI%2C%20eISSN%202604-1383%29/Stendek%20-%20Vol%2009%20No%2032%20-%20June%201978.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.afu.se">[files.afu.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.afu.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1978</span></span></span> That was an understandable attempt to find order in scattered material, but it was not a measured trajectory.

## How specialist magazines preserved local cases

Without *Stendek*, Tahal might have disappeared almost completely from Almería’s UFO history. The magazine was the quarterly bulletin of the Centro de Estudios Interplanetarios, an organisation founded in Barcelona in 1958. It ran from 1970 to 1981 and tried to give Spanish UFO study a more organised and analytical form than sensational newspaper coverage alone could provide.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.el-cei.org/2015/02/stendek-internet.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: el-cei.org">[el-cei.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">el-cei.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bloc del Centre d&#x27;Estudis Interplanetaris (CEI): Stendek a Internet</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bloc del Centre d&#x27;Estudis Interplanetaris (CEI): Stendek a Internet</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 25, 2015</span></span></span>

Its value was real. Editors collected provincial press reports that might otherwise have been discarded, converted them into comparable fields and connected observations across administrative boundaries. In issue 32, readers can compare times, witness numbers, durations, directions and visual descriptions from many towns rather than encountering each report as a detached local curiosity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.afu.se/Downloads/Magazines/Spain%20and%20Catalonia/Stendek%20%28CEI%2C%20eISSN%202604-1383%29/Stendek%20-%20Vol%2009%20No%2032%20-%20June%201978.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.afu.se">[files.afu.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.afu.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1978</span></span></span>

The magazine also showed more critical awareness than its subject might suggest. In the same issue, an editorial warned that films and press publicity could produce contaminated reports by introducing images and expectations into later testimony. It anticipated that investigators would have to separate useful cases from a large amount of low-value material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.afu.se/Downloads/Magazines/Spain%20and%20Catalonia/Stendek%20%28CEI%2C%20eISSN%202604-1383%29/Stendek%20-%20Vol%2009%20No%2032%20-%20June%201978.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.afu.se">[files.afu.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.afu.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1978</span></span></span> Elsewhere in its run, *Stendek* published conventional identifications as well as unresolved claims, including a Galician report attributed to a polyethylene balloon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufomagazines.com/stendek-cei-eissn-2604-1383-stendek-vol-04-no-14-september-1973/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufomagazines.com">[UFO Magazine Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufomagazines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">stendek cei eissn 2604 1383 stendek vol 04 no 14 september 1973</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">No, the article &#x27;FALSO OVNI EN GALICIA&#x27; (False UFO in Galicia) explains that a reported sighting was later identified as a polyethylene b...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 1958</span></span></span>

Nevertheless, preservation is not the same as verification. For Tahal, the magazine appears to have condensed an earlier source rather than publishing interview transcripts or a full field report. Readers are not told which newspaper supplied the information, who contacted the witnesses, whether questions were asked promptly, or whether the four accounts were recorded separately. The source chain therefore resembles:

**event or perception → witness discussion → local report or correspondent → newspaper or private notes → magazine summary → later book and website retelling.**

At every stage, details can be lost, simplified or standardised. A witness who said only that the bedroom suddenly became bright may later be described as having witnessed an object. An approximate night may acquire a precise date. A report included in a catalogue of many sightings may gradually become remembered as an investigated “case”.

The surviving magazine collection itself is now far more accessible than many underlying case papers. Archives for UFO Research in Sweden says it received and catalogued the CEI collection, including more than 550 annual magazine volumes, complete runs of *Stendek* and other CEI publications, and extensive Iberian report files. It has digitised hundreds of Spanish newsletters and magazines and supplies documents for reinvestigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afu.se/centro-de-estudios-interplanetarios-cei-collections/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afu.se">[afu.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afu.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro de Estudios Interplanetarios (CEI) archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro de Estudios Interplanetarios (CEI) archives</span></span></span> This is a major preservation success, but it also highlights the imbalance: the published layer may survive even when the original witness layer remains difficult to locate.

## Press cuttings can record history without proving a case

Local newspapers are indispensable for reconstructing Almería’s sighting history. They preserve dates, public reactions, witness quotations and contacts with airports, police or observatories. A later account of a 1998 Almería sighting, for example, reproduced references to *La Voz de Almería* reports from 1969 and 1979 and noted that the latter story involved numerous observers, radio amateurs, alleged radio interference and an inquiry to the airport meteorological observatory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mundoparapsicologico.com/ufologia/ovni-en-el-paseo-maritimo-de-almeria-en-junio-de-1998/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mundoparapsicologico.com">[mundoparapsicologico.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mundoparapsicologico.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ovni en el paseo maritimo de almeria en junio de 1998</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ovni en el paseo maritimo de almeria en junio de 1998</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2006</span></span></span>

Yet a clipping is a record of publication, not necessarily a record of independent investigation. Newspapers often combine several callers into one narrative, use dramatic vocabulary, omit unsuccessful checks and repeat agency copy. If a magazine then summarises that article, and a later writer cites the magazine, three surviving texts may all descend from one original telephone conversation.

The Tahal entry provides a clear warning. Its four witnesses sound like multiple corroboration, but the accessible source does not disclose four statements. Its inclusion alongside reports from many cities suggests a coherent regional event, but the article admits imprecise and conflicting times. Its later repetition gives it a settled date, while the cited magazine does not.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.afu.se/Downloads/Magazines/Spain%20and%20Catalonia/Stendek%20%28CEI%2C%20eISSN%202604-1383%29/Stendek%20-%20Vol%2009%20No%2032%20-%20June%201978.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.afu.se">[files.afu.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.afu.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1978</span></span></span>

The right approach is therefore to distinguish three different claims:

**Secure:** a report associated with Tahal entered Spain’s specialist UFO literature and was published in *Stendek* in 1978.

**Reasonably supported:** four people reportedly experienced a brief, intense nocturnal illumination in December 1976.

**Not established:** that they saw a structured aerial craft, that the source was close to Tahal, or that the incident was unexplained after contemporary technical investigation.

This distinction preserves the historical value of the record without assigning it more physical certainty than its documentation allows.

## Separating independent testimony from repetition

A trustworthy reconstruction should count sources by origin, not by how many times a story has been republished. For Tahal, the strongest next step would be to locate material that predates or underlies the *Stendek* summary: the original newspaper cutting, correspondence sent to the CEI, interview notes, witness names, or a local police record.

Several tests would then materially improve the assessment:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **Establish the date.** The disagreement between 20, 21 and 29 December must be resolved before astronomical or aviation checks can be meaningful.
2. **Trace the first publication.** The wording of the Tahal entry may reveal whether it came from a newspaper, an investigator’s letter or a second-hand catalogue.
3. **Separate the witnesses.** Four independently recorded accounts would carry more weight than one shared group recollection.
4. **Recover the viewing geometry.** Window direction, nearby hills, cloud cover and the witnesses’ exact positions could show whether the source was in the sky, reflected from outside or inferred solely from illumination.
5. **Compare genuinely independent reports.** Similar descriptions from distant locations recorded before media coverage would strengthen the case for one regional atmospheric event. Similar wording published only after newspapers circulated the story might instead show narrative borrowing.
6. **Check conventional records.** Meteor observations, military exercises, aircraft movements, electrical disturbances and weather data would be relevant, but only after the time discrepancy is settled.

</div>

The lack of those materials does not prove that they never existed. The CEI’s surviving collections include report files capable of supporting reinvestigation, while Spain’s national and regional newspaper holdings are not always complete or immediately digitised. The Biblioteca Nacional de España notes that catalogue records for periodicals do not necessarily show the exact issues held and that newspaper reproduction and access can be delayed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afu.se/centro-de-estudios-interplanetarios-cei-collections/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afu.se">[afu.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afu.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro de Estudios Interplanetarios (CEI) archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro de Estudios Interplanetarios (CEI) archives</span></span></span>

## Can Almería’s fragmented UFO records be trusted?

They can be trusted selectively. Specialist magazines, press cuttings and later recollections are good evidence for the development of Almería’s UFO culture: which places became associated with sightings, which stories investigators considered important and how reports were linked into provincial or national waves. They are much weaker when used to establish exact trajectories, physical characteristics or extraordinary causes.

Tahal illustrates both sides. *Stendek* rescued a small inland report from likely obscurity and placed it within a remarkable series of short-lived luminous observations across southern Spain. It also preserved enough detail to suggest that the witnesses experienced a real and striking flash. But the same article exposes unresolved contradictions, reliance on press material and missing first-hand investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.afu.se/Downloads/Magazines/Spain%20and%20Catalonia/Stendek%20%28CEI%2C%20eISSN%202604-1383%29/Stendek%20-%20Vol%2009%20No%2032%20-%20June%201978.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.afu.se">[files.afu.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.afu.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stendek Vol 09 No 32 June 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1978</span></span></span>

On the surviving evidence, the Tahal incident is best classified as **a weakly documented report, probably connected to a widespread luminous atmospheric event and plausibly consistent with a bright fireball**. That explanation remains provisional because the original date and source chain have not been secured. Later retellings have kept the story alive, but they have not added measurements, photographs, named testimony or official findings that strengthen an extraordinary interpretation.

Its lasting importance lies precisely there. Tahal shows that Almería’s UFO archive is not one coherent repository but a patchwork of clippings, private files, specialist publications and memories. Reading that archive responsibly means treating every repeated story as a trail to an earlier source—not as another vote that the story must be true.
