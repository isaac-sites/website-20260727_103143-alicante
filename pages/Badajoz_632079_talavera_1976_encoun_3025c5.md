## Introduction

The Talavera la Real incident of 12 November 1976 became [Badajoz]({{ 'badajoz-ufo-cases-beyond-the-famous/' | relative_url }})’s defining UFO story because armed servicemen reportedly confronted a tall luminous figure inside a restricted air base. The familiar account includes an increasingly painful high-pitched sound, a patrol dog in distress, temporary weakness or paralysis, bursts of automatic gunfire and an unexplained disappearance. It sounds unusually strong because the witnesses were military guards rather than casual passers-by.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Badajoz_632079_talavera_1976_encoun_3025c5-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for 1976 Encounter" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The surviving evidence is much weaker than that reputation suggests. Spain’s declassified file is a 28-page compilation assembled mainly between 1982 and 1994, not the original investigation made immediately after the event. Military archivists repeatedly searched for the supposed contemporary report and judicial findings but could not locate them. Much of the dramatic narrative preserved in the file comes from later magazine articles and correspondence from UFO researchers. The case therefore supports a cautious conclusion: several servicemen probably experienced a frightening alarm, but the surviving record cannot establish what they saw, where their bullets went or whether an external figure was present at all.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160069099" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

## The guards’ account and reported sequence

The best-known version places the beginning of the episode at about 1.45 in the morning. José María Trejo and Juan Carrizosa Luján were reportedly stationed in separate sentry posts near the fuel-storage area, roughly 60 metres apart. They began hearing a faint sound resembling radio interference or a whistle. According to later retellings, it grew sharper and more painful, prompting the guards to call for a mobile patrol accompanied by a dog.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://prensamercosur.org/2026/06/29/el-hombre-verde-de-talavera-la-real-el-expediente-ovni-que-todavia-desconcierta-al-ejercito-del-aire-espanol/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prensamercosur.org">[Prensa Mercosur]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prensamercosur.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Prensa MercosurEl &#x27;Hombre Verde&quot; de Talavera la Real: el expediente OVNI que todavía desconcierta al Ejército del Aire español &#124; Prensa M...</span></span></span>

The number and role of the witnesses are not presented consistently across the surviving accounts. Some summaries refer primarily to two sentries; the fuller popular narrative describes three soldiers together when the alleged figure appeared. This matters because “multiple military witnesses” can sound like independent corroboration, yet the witnesses were in communication, shared the same alarm and apparently observed the crucial scene as a group. Their agreement may be sincere without being fully independent.

The patrol reportedly searched the area without finding an intruder. The sound then returned, accompanied in some versions by radio interference and abnormal behaviour from the dog. The men said they eventually saw a large human-like shape near the perimeter wall, perhaps 15 metres away. It was described as roughly three metres tall, greenish or phosphorescent, with very long arms, poorly defined hands and indistinct or invisible legs. Some accounts portray the body as being composed of many points of light rather than as a solid, clearly illuminated person.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://prensamercosur.org/2026/06/29/el-hombre-verde-de-talavera-la-real-el-expediente-ovni-que-todavia-desconcierta-al-ejercito-del-aire-espanol/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prensamercosur.org">[Prensa Mercosur]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prensamercosur.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Prensa MercosurEl &#x27;Hombre Verde&quot; de Talavera la Real: el expediente OVNI que todavía desconcierta al Ejército del Aire español &#124; Prensa M...</span></span></span>

Trejo later said that he tried to raise or fire his weapon but felt suddenly weak and physically rigid. He fell forward while remaining conscious. The other soldiers were said to have opened fire, after which the figure vanished in a bright flash. The piercing sound was reportedly heard once more before the incident ended. Trejo said that his chest hurt for approximately 15 to 20 minutes afterwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1976-11-12_avistamiento_en_la_base_aerea_de_talavera_%28badajoz%29_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files</span></span></span>

This sequence is memorable, but it should not be mistaken for a verbatim contemporary military statement. The surviving file’s own summary says the story was known through a newspaper or magazine version, while later researchers asked the Ministry of Defence to confirm what had happened. The record is therefore partly an archive of how the story circulated, not simply an operational report describing the night itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1976-11-12_avistamiento_en_la_base_aerea_de_talavera_%28badajoz%29_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files</span></span></span>

## Weapons, symptoms and missing physical traces

Gunfire is often treated as the incident’s strongest physical element. Published versions commonly state that the soldiers fired between 40 and 50 rounds. They also say that an inspection confirmed the weapons had been discharged. If reliably documented, that would establish that the alarm caused a real armed response, though it would not establish what the guards fired at.

The evidential problem is that the most striking details about the ammunition come from reproduced press coverage. According to that narrative, about 50 servicemen searched the area after daylight but found neither cartridge cases nor bullet marks on the nearby wall. The articles presented this absence as an additional mystery: the weapons had supposedly been fired at mid-height towards a figure close to the wall, yet no trace of the shooting remained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1976-11-12_avistamiento_en_la_base_aerea_de_talavera_%28badajoz%29_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files</span></span></span>

That claim raises more questions than it answers. The surviving archive does not provide a contemporaneous ammunition log, armoury record, ballistic examination, marked site plan, photographs of the firing position or signed search report. It does not identify precisely which weapons were fired, how many rounds each held, their direction of fire, whether the cases were expected to remain at the firing point or how thoroughly the ground was examined. Without those details, “missing cases” cannot be treated as evidence that bullets vanished through an anomalous process.

The absence of impacts is similarly difficult to assess. Night firing under fear and confusion can be inaccurate, particularly if soldiers fire in bursts, lose control of their aim or direct shots into open ground rather than squarely at a wall. A later claim that impacts “should” have appeared depends on geometry that the preserved documentation does not reconstruct. Conversely, if almost 50 rounds really were fired towards a nearby solid structure, the lack of reported damage is a serious inconsistency. Either the popular account exaggerates the volume or direction of fire, the search record is incomplete, or important physical evidence was never retained.

The reported health effects are also intriguing but medically inconclusive. Trejo described weakness and chest pain during the encounter. Later magazine coverage said that he subsequently fainted, experienced temporary loss of sight and was admitted to the Air Force hospital in Madrid, where he reportedly remained for about a month. The same account says doctors referred to a nervous disturbance but provided no definite diagnosis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1976-11-12_avistamiento_en_la_base_aerea_de_talavera_%28badajoz%29_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files</span></span></span>

No clinical notes, test results or discharge diagnosis appear in the publicly accessible case material. The symptoms cannot therefore be tied to radiation, sound, electromagnetic exposure or any other unusual external agent. Fainting, transient visual disturbance, headache, muscular weakness and chest discomfort can occur in many neurological, cardiovascular and stress-related conditions. Temporal proximity to the incident makes the illness relevant, but it does not identify its cause.

## The official file is not the original investigation

The most important fact about the declassified Talavera file is what it does not contain. The Ministry of Defence catalogue describes a 28-page file produced between 1982 and 1994 and declassified in November 1996. Its contents include press material, letters from private researchers, official replies to those letters and later administrative efforts to locate information about the alleged event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160069099" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

Correspondence quoted in the file suggests that an investigating officer or judicial examiner may once have been appointed and may have submitted conclusions to a military judicial authority. However, when the Air Force reviewed its UFO holdings in the 1990s, that report could not be found. The documentation transferred to the relevant command in January 1992 contained no record of the incident or of the supposed conclusions. ## Stress, misperception and later retellings

A stress-based explanation does not require the soldiers to have fabricated their experience. Guard duty at night combines darkness, restricted vision, isolation, expectation of intrusion and a strong obligation to react to uncertain sounds. Once one guard reports an alarming noise, later sensations and ambiguous shapes may be interpreted within the same threat frame. A patrol dog’s agitation can intensify that expectation without independently identifying the cause.

The luminous “figure” was also described in visually unstable terms: exceptionally tall, greenish, lacking clear legs or hands and apparently composed of small lights. Those features are compatible with an object or patch of light being organised by perception into a roughly human outline. In low illumination, observers rely heavily on contrast and expectation, while distance and size estimates become unreliable. Fear can further narrow attention and make brief impressions feel more detailed or prolonged than they were.

Spanish UFO researcher Vicente-Juan Ballester Olmos, who was involved in work surrounding the military file-declassification process, has argued that an altered perception by one soldier could have spread through fear and suggestion among the young conscripts. Time has therefore weakened rather than strengthened the case: memories have aged and the story has become more elaborate, while the primary documentation needed to test it remains absent.

## Did something enter Talavera Air Base?

Something almost certainly disturbed the guards. The reported alarm, apparent weapon use and later administrative correspondence make it unreasonable to dismiss the entire episode as a story created from nothing. The servicemen may have sincerely believed that they faced a luminous intruder, and their fear appears to have been real.

What cannot be established is that a physical, three-metre figure entered the base. The central account survives chiefly through retrospective journalism, while the original inquiry, ammunition documentation, medical records and scene evidence are missing. The supposed physical clues are either absent or known only through secondary descriptions. Even the declassified dossier explicitly records repeated failures to locate the underlying report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1976-11-12_avistamiento_en_la_base_aerea_de_talavera_%28badajoz%29_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files</span></span></span>

Within Badajoz’s UFO history, Talavera remains important because it shows how an intense military witness experience can become a landmark case despite a fragile documentary foundation. It is unresolved in the limited sense that the initial stimulus cannot now be identified. It is not unresolved in the stronger sense of leaving well-documented extraordinary evidence that defeats conventional explanation. The fairest classification is a serious but weakly preserved encounter: credible as an episode of fear and confusion, uncertain as an observation, and unsupported as proof that an unknown being entered the air base.
