## Introduction

The electronic-warfare theory offers a plausible way to explain the most peculiar part of the Motril incident of 17 November 1979: the laughter and brief greeting in children’s voices heard over a military radio channel. According to a later account attributed to a retired Spanish Air Force officer, an American electronic-warfare aircraft transmitted a recording of a child’s voice during an exercise, accidentally or deliberately reaching the fighter sent towards the [Granada]({{ 'granadas-ufo-history-beyond-the-motril/' | relative_url }}) coast.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elojocritico.info/manises-1978-el-scramble-ovni-que-no-existio/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elojocritico.info">[elojocritico.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elojocritico.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">manises 1978 el scramble ovni que no existio</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">manises 1978 el scramble ovni que no existio</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2016</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Granada_3f7db9_motril_electronic_wa_748ff9-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Jamming Theory" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That story is technically possible, but it is not established fact. Electronic warfare can inject deceptive signals into radio and radar systems, and the Cold War setting makes such activity conceivable. However, no released exercise order, flight log, transmitter record or contemporary operational report has been found that connects a jamming aircraft to Motril that afternoon. The theory therefore weakens the claim that the voices were inexplicable, without proving exactly who transmitted them or why.

## The retired officer’s account

The jamming explanation entered public discussion decades after the incident. Investigator José Antonio Caravaca reported speaking to a former officer identified only by an initial, who said he had served in the Spanish Air Force’s electronic-warfare section between 1987 and 1995. The officer reportedly claimed that American forces had used a “jammer” to introduce false or disruptive information into communications channels and had played a recording of a child’s voice during an operation near Spanish airspace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elojocritico.info/manises-1978-el-scramble-ovni-que-no-existio/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elojocritico.info">[elojocritico.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elojocritico.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">manises 1978 el scramble ovni que no existio</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">manises 1978 el scramble ovni que no existio</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2016</span></span></span>

The account is striking because it resembles the official description of what the Motril pilot heard. The declassified summary says that, while communicating with the Pegaso control centre on ultrahigh-frequency channel 11, the pilot heard children laughing and saying a short greeting equivalent to “Hello, how are you?” The voices remained audible for roughly 30 seconds after he removed the guard channel from his radio configuration, apparently confirming that they were entering through channel 11 itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.granadahoy.com/vivir/ovni-visito-motril-1979-tres_0_2002289169.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: granadahoy.com">[Granada Hoy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">granadahoy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ovni visito motril 1979 tres 0 2002289169</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Granada HoyEl ovni que &#x27;visitó&#x27; Motril en 1979: tres luces y un &quot;hola, hola, cómo estás&quot; por radioAugust 26, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 26, 2024</span></span></span>

The former officer reportedly added a more personal detail: the transmission was especially unsettling because the intercepted pilot had a child in hospital. That detail has circulated in magazine articles and later retellings, but it does not appear in the accessible official summary of the Motril flight. Nor does the released file identify the speaker, the transmitting aircraft, the unit conducting the alleged operation or the purpose of using a child’s voice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elojocritico.info/manises-1978-el-scramble-ovni-que-no-existio/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elojocritico.info">[elojocritico.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elojocritico.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">manises 1978 el scramble ovni que no existio</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">manises 1978 el scramble ovni que no existio</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2016</span></span></span>

This creates an important distinction between two layers of evidence. The pilot’s report of hearing voices is contained in an official military case file. The explanation involving an American aircraft, a recorded tape and a psychological connection to the pilot’s family rests on retrospective, anonymised testimony published many years later.

## How jamming could affect radar and radio

In ordinary speech, “jamming” often means simply drowning out a signal with noise. Military electronic warfare is broader. It can deny the use of a frequency, interfere with communications, imitate legitimate transmissions or introduce misleading information into a receiver. Modern United States Air Force doctrine describes electromagnetic deception as the manipulation, distortion or falsification of information in order to provoke a mistaken response. It also recognises “imitative” deception, in which transmitted energy resembles signals the target expects to receive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.doctrine.af.mil/Portals/61/documents/AFDP_3-85/AFDP%203-85%20Electromagnetic%20Spectrum%20Ops.pdf?trk=public_post_comment-text" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doctrine.af.mil">[Doctrine AF]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doctrine.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">EW is critical in gaining the necessary degree of control to ensu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 14, 2023</span></span></span>

A recorded voice entering a military radio is therefore not technically far-fetched. A transmitter operating on the correct frequency, with sufficient power and suitable line of sight, could cause a pilot to hear speech that had not come from the expected controller. The transmission would not have needed to seize control of the aircraft’s radio in any science-fiction sense. It would merely have had to reach the receiver more strongly or clearly than competing signals for a short period.

The pilot’s attempt to remove the guard channel does not rule this out. Guard is a separate emergency frequency commonly monitored alongside the selected working channel. By deselecting it and still hearing the voices, the pilot apparently established that the sound was not arriving only through guard. That would still be consistent with a transmission directed at, or spilling into, channel 11.

Electronic warfare could also offer a general mechanism for confusing radar operators. NATO describes the field as including attacks on radar, communications and navigation systems, along with electronic masking, probing and intelligence gathering. Radar deception may generate misleading indications, distort the apparent position of a target or create returns that encourage controllers and interceptors to look in the wrong place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/deterrence-and-defence/electromagnetic-warfare" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nato.int">[NATO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nato.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Electromagnetic warfare &#124; NATO Topic</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electromagnetic warfare &#124; NATO Topic</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 22, 2023</span></span></span>

This does not mean that one device necessarily produced every feature of the [Motril case]({{ 'motril-case/' | relative_url }}). The three lights seen by the pilot could have had a separate optical explanation, while the radio voices came from interference or an unauthorised transmission. The Air Force’s own assessment considered atmospheric [refraction]({{ 'refraction/' | relative_url }}) relevant to the November sightings and stressed that the lights had not been shown to be solid objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.granadahoy.com/vivir/ovni-visito-motril-1979-tres_0_2002289169.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: granadahoy.com">[Granada Hoy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">granadahoy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ovni visito motril 1979 tres 0 2002289169</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Granada HoyEl ovni que &#x27;visitó&#x27; Motril en 1979: tres luces y un &quot;hola, hola, cómo estás&quot; por radioAugust 26, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 26, 2024</span></span></span>

The local military setting makes an electronic explanation worth examining. Motril was associated with Air Surveillance Squadron No. 9, part of Spain’s radar and air-control network. An official history of the unit describes air surveillance, radar sensors and military communications as central to its mission, while also placing Spanish air-defence development within a wider strategic relationship with the United States during the Cold War.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publicaciones.defensa.gob.es/de-w-9-motril-a-eva-n-9-1955-2023-libros-pdf.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publicaciones.defensa.gob.es">[Publicaciones Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publicaciones.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">de w 9 motril a eva n 9 1955 2023 libros pdf</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">de w 9 motril a eva n 9 1955 2023 libros pdf</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 9, 2024</span></span></span>

Yet proximity to military radar infrastructure is not evidence that a particular exercise caused the incident. It merely shows that the Granada coast was not an electronically empty area: radar surveillance, command communications, military aircraft and allied activity all formed part of the operational environment.

## What the voices do and do not prove

The children’s voices are sometimes presented as though they must have come from the luminous formation. The official account does not demonstrate that connection. The pilot saw lights during the outbound phase of the interception and heard the voices during the return journey. Temporal proximity makes an association understandable, but no recorded message identified the lights, referred to the pursuit or showed knowledge that only an extraordinary source could possess.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.granadahoy.com/vivir/ovni-visito-motril-1979-tres_0_2002289169.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: granadahoy.com">[Granada Hoy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">granadahoy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ovni visito motril 1979 tres 0 2002289169</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Granada HoyEl ovni que &#x27;visitó&#x27; Motril en 1979: tres luces y un &quot;hola, hola, cómo estás&quot; por radioAugust 26, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 26, 2024</span></span></span>

The content was also extremely limited. The file describes laughter and a repetitive greeting, not a sustained conversation. Later versions sometimes turn the episode into a direct exchange between the pilot and an unknown intelligence, but the surviving official wording supports only a brief, intrusive transmission.

Several ordinary mechanisms remain possible even without a planned exercise:

* an accidental transmission on the same or a nearby frequency;
* intermodulation or signal mixing within radio equipment;
* deliberate misuse of a transmitter;
* a test signal or recording broadcast by military personnel;
* electronic-warfare training that was not documented in the UFO file;
* misidentification of the apparent age or character of an unclear voice.

The pilot’s professional experience makes his report important, but it cannot by itself identify the transmitter. A trained airman can reliably state that an unexpected voice entered his headset while still lacking the information needed to determine its origin.

## Why the theory remains unproven

The central weakness is the absence of a documentary chain. A convincing electronic-warfare explanation would ideally identify the transmitting platform, its unit, its route, the frequencies used, the exercise authority and the timing of the broadcast. None of those details appears in the publicly accessible Motril material.

The retired officer was not described as a participant in the 1979 operation. His stated posting to the electronic-warfare section began in 1987, eight years after the event. He may have learned of an earlier operation through colleagues or internal knowledge, but the published account does not show how close he was to the original source or whether he had examined contemporary records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elojocritico.info/manises-1978-el-scramble-ovni-que-no-existio/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elojocritico.info">[elojocritico.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elojocritico.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">manises 1978 el scramble ovni que no existio</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">manises 1978 el scramble ovni que no existio</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2016</span></span></span>

There is also some uncertainty about which episode his story describes. The account refers broadly to a pilot near the Balearic Islands and Spanish airspace, a scramble from Albacete and a child’s recorded voice. Those elements resemble Motril, but they also sit within a much larger group of November 1979 incidents involving Valencia, Madrid, radar contacts and military interceptions. A later witness could have combined details from related cases or from stories that had already circulated publicly.

The archive itself is incomplete. Documents assembled in the 1990s note that certain routine surveillance, equipment and operations-room logs could be destroyed six months after the end of the relevant year. That helps explain why later researchers may be unable to reconstruct every radar or communications event from 1979. It does not prove that records of a secret exercise were deliberately concealed, but it means the absence of a log cannot always be treated as evidence that no operation occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1979-11-11-17-28_avistamiento_en_valencia-motril-madrid-ii.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI)AVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO D...</span></span></span>

## The most cautious assessment

Electronic warfare is one of the stronger conventional explanations for the children’s voices because it provides a realistic mechanism rather than merely labelling the event “interference”. Military transmitters can insert deceptive audio into communications channels, and radar deception can create or alter apparent targets. The Cold War air-defence environment around Spain and the Granada coast makes such techniques relevant to the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/deterrence-and-defence/electromagnetic-warfare" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nato.int">[NATO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nato.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Electromagnetic warfare &#124; NATO Topic</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electromagnetic warfare &#124; NATO Topic</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 22, 2023</span></span></span>

The theory also separates two matters that are often fused together in popular retellings. The pilot may have observed distant lights whose apparent geometry and unchanging range arose from optical conditions, while independently receiving an unusual radio transmission. Nothing in the available record requires the lights themselves to have spoken.

However, the evidence stops well short of identifying a confirmed electronic-warfare operation. The voices are documented; the recorded-child explanation is not documented contemporaneously. The alleged American role, the use of a Hercules-type aircraft and the personal reference to the pilot’s sick child all depend on later testimony whose provenance cannot presently be checked against released operational material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://elojocritico.info/manises-1978-el-scramble-ovni-que-no-existio/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: elojocritico.info">[elojocritico.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">elojocritico.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">manises 1978 el scramble ovni que no existio</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">manises 1978 el scramble ovni que no existio</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2016</span></span></span>

For Granada’s UFO history, the result is neither a clean debunking nor an untouched mystery. The jamming account makes the radio episode less extraordinary by showing that known military technology could have produced it. At the same time, the lack of an exercise log prevents the theory from becoming a verified solution. The children’s voices should therefore be treated as a credible report of an unidentified transmission, not as evidence of communication from the lights and not as a proven electronic-warfare experiment.
