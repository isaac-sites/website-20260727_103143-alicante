## Introduction

The province therefore offers a useful lesson in how UFO history should be assessed. Official paperwork proves that reports were made and taken seriously, but it does not prove that extraordinary vehicles were present. Some incidents remain unresolved because the records are incomplete; others were probably meteors, aircraft effects or astronomical objects that looked unfamiliar at the time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[gob.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

## The cases that define A Coruña’s UFO history

### Ferrol, 2 April 1966

The earliest major official case in the province occurred around Ferrol on the night of 2 April 1966. Personnel at the naval radio receiving station at A Carreira described a large, intensely luminous object near Monte Campelo. According to the military file, its apparent shape altered roughly every five minutes. A serviceman in Xuvia reported seeing what appeared to be the same light from another position, and one observer took a long-exposure photograph.

This sounds impressive until the limitations of the record are examined. The photograph preserved in the file was extremely dark, no useful measurement of distance or speed was made, and the object was not reported to move across the sky. Most importantly, the intelligence summary stated that no later investigation was recorded and that the absence of basic data prevented any reliable hypothesis. The official conclusion was therefore not that an unknown craft had been confirmed, but that the report could not be resolved from the available material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gciencia.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/1966-04-02_avistamiento_en_el_ferrol.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gciencia.com">[gciencia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gciencia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1966 04 02 avistamiento en el ferrol</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

Later popular accounts have claimed that an unusual device recovered from the sea near Ferrol was a United States or NASA surveillance prototype. That story may relate to a separate recovered object rather than the stationary light described by the 1966 witnesses, and the surviving military sighting file itself does not identify the observed phenomenon as a prototype. Treating the later recovery story as a complete solution to the original sighting therefore goes beyond what the primary document establishes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/noticia/ferrol/ferrol/2023/03/20/misterio-ovni-ferrol-ejercito-aire-oculto-40-anos/00031679316690770270476.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[La Voz de Galicia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Voz de GaliciaEl misterio del ovni que se avistó en Ferrol: «El Ejército del Aire lo tuvo oculto 40 años»March 21, 2023 — El misterio...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 21, 2023</span></span></span>

The Ferrol case matters because it has several features often associated with stronger UFO reports: military witnesses, more than one viewing position, a contemporary photograph and an official record. Yet almost every potentially decisive detail is missing. It is best classified as genuinely unresolved but evidentially weak, rather than as proof of an extraordinary aircraft.

### The EVA-10 radar episode, 5 December 1989

The most technically interesting incident in A Coruña province began at the EVA-10 air-surveillance station on Monte Iroite, near Noia. At 18:48 UTC on 5 December 1989, military personnel received a report of an unidentified object in the broad area between Santiago de Compostela and A Coruña. Santiago airport was contacted, while the Civil Guard had separately passed on a visual description of a slow-moving, lens-shaped object with conspicuous changing lights near the Sada area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive</span></span></span>

At about 19:00, operators recorded a radar echo on both primary and secondary displays. The indicated altitude was initially about 57,000 feet and was later reported to vary as high as 75,000 feet, while the return changed little in horizontal position. Radio interference and emergency-frequency alarms were also noted during the episode. Operators later detected several additional returns at different flight levels, and the main height display reportedly continued showing an echo until the following morning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive</span></span></span>

These details make the case more substantial than an isolated light in the sky, but they do not create a clean radar-visual confirmation. Civil aircraft sent towards the relevant area did not obtain a positive visual sighting. The military file also does not demonstrate that the civilian description, the radio interference and each radar return came from one physical object. Secondary-radar returns can involve transponders, aircraft identification systems or processing effects, while stationary or erratic high-altitude echoes can arise from equipment, propagation or interpretation problems. The declassified record preserves the observations but contains no technical reconstruction capable of ruling those possibilities out.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive</span></span></span>

The 1989 event is therefore the province’s strongest official UFO case in terms of instrumentation, but not its strongest proof of an unknown vehicle. Its real value is that it shows how several ambiguous signals can coincide: a public sighting, military radar activity, radio problems and heightened attention among controllers. Without raw radar data, weather information, equipment logs and confirmed visual tracking from an aircraft, the incident remains unresolved rather than extraordinary.

### The fast green object of 23 November 1993

Four years later, five members of EVA-10 reported a very different event. At about 08:15 on 23 November 1993, they saw a fast luminous trace consisting of a green core and a white-yellow trail. It followed a straight path towards the Atlantic and was visible for only three or four seconds. Unlike the prolonged 1989 episode, this was a brief, coherent visual event observed by trained personnel.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.20minutos.es/noticia/2869393/0/defensa-documentos-desclasificados-expedientes-ovni-espana/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: 20minutos.es">[www.20minutos.es - Últimas Noticia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">20minutos.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Últimas NoticiaDefensa desclasifica hasta 1.900 páginas de &#x27;Expedientes ovni&#x27; en toda España...</span></span></span>

The resulting file was unusually long because investigators collected detailed witness questionnaires. Nevertheless, the description itself closely matches a bright meteor or fireball: extreme speed, a straight trajectory, a glowing core, an incandescent trail and a duration of only a few seconds. The military assessment accordingly considered that it could have been a meteorite or meteor, although the wording of the file retained the event as unexplained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.20minutos.es/noticia/2869393/0/defensa-documentos-desclasificados-expedientes-ovni-espana/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: 20minutos.es">[www.20minutos.es - Últimas Noticia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">20minutos.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Últimas NoticiaDefensa desclasifica hasta 1.900 páginas de &#x27;Expedientes ovni&#x27; en toda España...</span></span></span>

This distinction is important. “Unexplained” in an administrative file does not necessarily mean that conventional explanations were improbable. It can mean that no fragment was recovered, no precise trajectory was calculated or no independent scientific confirmation was obtained. Of A Coruña’s principal official cases, the 1993 sighting is arguably the easiest to interpret naturally.

## Cando: explosion, fireball and later UFO legend

On the morning of 18 January 1994, witnesses in and around Santiago de Compostela reported a very bright object crossing the sky. An explosion was subsequently associated with disturbed ground near Cando, in the municipality of Outes. Researchers connected with the University of Santiago de Compostela investigated the episode, and the case was eventually described in the peer-reviewed journal *Meteoritics & Planetary Science*.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1945-5100.1998.tb01607.x" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[Wiley Online Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">j.1945 5100.1998.tb01607.x</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

The discovery of a shallow, irregular depression and damaged trees encouraged early talk of an impact. It also made the story attractive to UFO writers, who sometimes portrayed Cando as a Spanish equivalent of an alleged spacecraft crash. The evidence, however, never established the recovery of a vehicle, meteorite fragments or manufactured debris. The scientific problem was whether the luminous atmospheric event and the ground disturbance were directly connected at all.

Later interpretations favoured a terrestrial explanation for the disturbed site, involving a sudden release and ignition of underground gas, possibly associated with ground movement. A small atmospheric body or fireball remained relevant to the sky observation, but the more dramatic suggestion that a craft struck the hillside was not supported by physical remains. Cando is consequently better understood as an unusual natural-event investigation that was absorbed into UFO folklore, rather than as one of the province’s strongest UFO cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evento_de_Cando" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Evento de Cando</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Evento de Cando</span></span></span>

The case still matters within A Coruña’s UFO history because it demonstrates how separate uncertainties can be merged into one narrative. A bright object, a loud explosion, damaged vegetation and an apparent crater formed a compelling story. Yet compelling sequence is not the same as demonstrated causation.

## Military records: valuable, but easy to overread

Spain’s Ministry of Defence began declassifying its historical UFO files in the early 1990s and later made digitised copies available through the Virtual Defence Library. The national collection covers reports from 1962 to 1995 and includes about 1,900 pages across roughly 80 files. Within Galicia, the principal official files concern Ferrol in 1966, a Lugo case in 1969, and the two EVA-10 incidents in Noia in 1989 and 1993.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[gob.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

The documents are valuable because they preserve dates, witness statements, military correspondence, sketches and official assessments. They show that some reports were treated as possible airspace, security or aviation matters. They also allow later readers to separate what witnesses actually reported from details added by books, newspapers or oral retellings.

Their limitations are equally important:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Classification was administrative, not evidential.** A file being secret or confidential may reflect military procedure, radar information or witness identities rather than evidence of an exotic craft.
* **Declassification is not confirmation.** It means the material was judged safe to release, not that the original report was proved accurate.
* **Many files lack follow-up evidence.** In the Ferrol case, investigators explicitly said that the basic information was insufficient.
* **Official language can preserve uncertainty.** Terms such as “unidentified” or “inexplicable” describe the state of the investigation, not the origin of the phenomenon.
* **The archive is selective.** It represents reports that entered military channels, not a complete register of everything seen across the province.

</div>

The presence of the EVA-10 station helps explain why Noia appears prominently in the official record. A military radar site is more likely than an ordinary civilian location to detect, document and forward unusual aerial events. This creates an observation effect: a cluster near a surveillance installation can reflect better reporting and instrumentation rather than a genuine concentration of extraordinary objects.

## Local investigators, press coverage and witness memory

A Coruña also played a role in the development of Spanish civilian UFO interest. Óscar Rey Brea, a researcher associated with the city, began collecting reports in the mid-twentieth century and became an early figure in Spanish ufology. Later accounts say that an unusual light seen over central A Coruña by members of his family in October 1945 helped stimulate his interest, before the modern flying-saucer craze began in 1947.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/noticia/sociedad/2001/04/23/cuatrocientos-casos-galicia/0003_526683.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[lavozdegalicia.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">La Voz de Galicia Más de cuatrocientos casos en Galicia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Voz de Galicia Más de cuatrocientos casos en Galicia</span></span></span>

Claims that this made Rey Brea the world’s first UFO researcher should be treated cautiously. They depend partly on retrospective definitions of “ufology” and on biographical sources written long after the events. His clearer historical importance is that he gathered regional reports early and helped create a local archive culture in which unusual sky observations were recorded rather than simply forgotten.

Later investigators and newspapers continued that work. Local reporting has claimed hundreds of Galician sightings in private collections, including clusters during the 1990s. Such totals are useful as cultural history, but they are not equivalent to hundreds of unexplained cases. A catalogue may include meteors, planets, aircraft, satellites, balloons, re-entering space debris, military exercises, duplicated reports and events that were never investigated beyond a brief witness account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/noticia/sociedad/2001/04/23/cuatrocientos-casos-galicia/0003_526683.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[La Voz de Galicia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">La Voz de Galicia Más de cuatrocientos casos en Galicia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Voz de Galicia Más de cuatrocientos casos en Galicia</span></span></span>

Press coverage has sometimes strengthened legends by revisiting them decades later, when witnesses’ memories have become less precise and documentary gaps invite speculation. At other times, local journalism has helped resolve apparent UFOs quickly. In 2023, for example, several striking lights over A Coruña were attributed by astronomers to Jupiter or the bright star Vega, a meteor, and an aircraft condensation trail illuminated at twilight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/noticia/coruna/vivir-coruna/2023/10/07/destello-luz-vio-viernes-cielo-coruna/00031696675278423396612.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[La Voz de Galicia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on lavozdegalicia.es.</span></span></span>

These recent examples are not trivial. They show how easily familiar objects become puzzling when seen without distance cues, against cloud, near the horizon or in unusual light. They also show why photographs alone often fail: a bright point or glowing trail may look dramatic while containing little information about range, size or motion.

## What is genuinely unresolved?

A balanced assessment places A Coruña’s best-known incidents in three different categories.

**Unresolved because the evidence is incomplete:** The 1966 Ferrol light belongs here. Multiple military witnesses and a contemporary file give it historical weight, but the poor photograph, uncertain geometry and absence of follow-up prevent a confident explanation.

**Technically interesting but insufficiently reconstructed:** The 1989 EVA-10 episode is the province’s most complex case. Radar returns, civilian reports and radio interference deserve attention, but the public file does not prove that all the observations represented a single object. The lack of raw technical data severely limits later analysis.

**Probably explained:** The brief 1993 EVA-10 object is strongly consistent with a meteor or fireball. Recent reports of lights over A Coruña have likewise been traced to ordinary astronomical or aviation sources.

Cando lies partly outside these categories because it was not simply an aerial sighting. Its UFO reputation grew from the attempted linking of a luminous object with an explosion site. Later scientific and geological interpretations weakened the spacecraft-crash version, even though some uncertainty about the precise natural sequence remained.

## The clearest conclusion

A Coruña has a meaningful place in Spanish UFO history, chiefly because military installations, radar systems, local investigators and an active regional press left a better paper trail than exists for many other provinces. That record contains real observations and genuine uncertainty, but no case provides verified evidence of extraterrestrial technology.

The 1989 Noia radar episode remains the most intriguing because it combined several sources of information. Ferrol remains historically notable but too poorly documented for a firm conclusion. The 1993 sighting was probably a meteor, while Cando’s transformation into a crash legend illustrates how an unexplained natural event can acquire a more dramatic identity over time.

The province’s files ultimately support a restrained interpretation: unusual things were reported, some deserved investigation, and a few cannot now be reconstructed. Their unresolved status reflects missing or ambiguous evidence, not confirmation of an extraordinary origin.
