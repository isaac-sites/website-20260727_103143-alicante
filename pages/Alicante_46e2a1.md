## Introduction

The most important location is the Air Surveillance Squadron at Aitana, known as EVA-5. Reports associated with the station include an object apparently passing over the installation in 1975, unexplained radar echoes in 1979 and three incidents reviewed together in 1986. Alicante also produced a civilian report describing a boomerang-shaped object, while Benidorm and Villajoyosa became part of the province’s popular UFO culture through press stories and public sky-watching events. The result is a revealing provincial record: stronger than folklore alone, but too incomplete and uneven to support extraordinary conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alicantepress.com/archive/33209/defensa-investigo-al-menos-8-casos-ovni-en-la-provincia-de-alicante-en-los-anos-70-y-80" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alicantepress.com">[alicantepress.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alicantepress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alicantepress.com.</span></span></span>

## Why Aitana dominates Alicante’s UFO record

EVA-5 sits in the Sierra de Aitana, at a strategically useful height overlooking the western Mediterranean approaches. Its role in monitoring air traffic meant that reports from the station could involve trained personnel, radar equipment or both. That gives the Aitana cases more evidential weight than an isolated light seen by a single person. It also introduces important complications: radar can produce anomalous returns, military airspace contains exercises and classified activity, and witnesses at a surveillance station may see aircraft or atmospheric effects that civilians would never encounter.

The Spanish Ministry of Defence’s online UFO collection lists a dedicated file covering incidents at EVA-5 on 24 and 26 April and 26 July 1986. The eight-page dossier was prepared by the Air Operational Command’s intelligence section in 1994 and later declassified. More broadly, the Defence archive contains 80 files and about 1,900 pages concerning unusual phenomena reported between 1962 and 1995 in cases involving Air Force personnel or equipment. The archive therefore confirms that the incidents entered official channels, but inclusion does not mean that military investigators endorsed an exotic explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/busqueda_referencia.do?campo=idtitulo&amp;idValor=395964" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

That distinction is essential. The files were administrative and intelligence records intended to capture events that could affect air safety or defence. A radar return could be investigated because it was unidentified at the time, not because officials believed it came from another world. The Air Force itself explained that reports were centralised and treated as classified material from 1979 before later being progressively reviewed for release.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20230016381" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

## The 1975 object over EVA-5

The most memorable Alicante military case occurred before dawn on 28 July 1975. According to summaries of the declassified material, EVA-5 detected a stationary radar echo over the sea shortly before several non-commissioned officers saw an object passing at low altitude over the installation at about 5.30 am. The combination of an apparent radar contact and multiple military witnesses is why the episode has remained central to Alicante UFO discussions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alicantepress.com/archive/33209/defensa-investigo-al-menos-8-casos-ovni-en-la-provincia-de-alicante-en-los-anos-70-y-80" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alicantepress.com">[ALICANTE PRESS periódico digital]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alicantepress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alicantepress.com.</span></span></span>

A later account by retired Air Force officer Francisco Miñano presents the incident as a much closer and more dramatic encounter near the station’s radar equipment. His recollection is valuable because it comes from someone who says he was directly involved. It is not, however, equivalent to a contemporary statement preserved unchanged from 1975. The publicly accessible summary is brief, while the fuller personal narrative was promoted decades later in a radio programme devoted to mysteries and UFOs. Memory can remain sincere while becoming less precise over long periods, especially when an event has repeatedly been discussed and retold.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.orm.es/programas/elultimopeldano/testimonio-encuentro-cercano-en-la-base-de-aitana-ovnis-en-instalaciones-militares/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: orm.es">[Onda Regional de Murcia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">orm.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on orm.es.</span></span></span>

The case therefore has several positive features: more than one reported observer, a military setting and an apparent radar context. Its weaknesses are equally important. The available summaries do not provide a full technical radar plot, independently timed records, photographs or enough information to establish that the visual object and the radar return were necessarily the same thing. Nor does the surviving public account rule out an aircraft, helicopter, unusual atmospheric propagation or separate events interpreted as connected.

The fairest assessment is that the 1975 incident remains unresolved in the limited sense that the public record does not identify the object conclusively. “Unresolved” should not be confused with “unexplainable”, and neither term means “extraterrestrial”.

## Radar alerts in 1979

Aitana reappeared in the record during Spain’s major UFO year of 1979. On 6 February, EVA-5 reportedly detected radar echoes over the Mediterranean while the crew of the gas carrier *Tamames* observed multiple lights. The Air Force file suggested that parachuting activity might have accounted for the event. That proposed explanation is significant because it shows investigators were considering ordinary military or aviation causes rather than merely cataloguing a mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alicantepress.com/archive/33209/defensa-investigo-al-menos-8-casos-ovni-en-la-provincia-de-alicante-en-los-anos-70-y-80" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alicantepress.com">[ALICANTE PRESS periódico digital]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alicantepress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alicantepress.com.</span></span></span>

The case is not completely settled by the brief published summary. To test the parachute explanation properly, investigators would need to match the precise time, altitude, exercise area, illumination equipment, weather and observed motion. Nevertheless, an identified activity capable of producing several descending or drifting lights is a substantially stronger explanation than an unspecified unknown craft.

On 13 March 1979, Aitana radar reportedly detected an unidentified echo travelling towards the Spanish mainland. A Mirage III was scrambled from Manises Air Base to investigate. A fighter launch sounds dramatic, but it primarily demonstrates that an unidentified radar contact was taken seriously within an air-defence system. Scrambles are precautionary responses to uncertain tracks; they do not, by themselves, establish unusual performance or an extraordinary origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alicantepress.com/archive/33209/defensa-investigo-al-menos-8-casos-ovni-en-la-provincia-de-alicante-en-los-anos-70-y-80" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alicantepress.com">[ALICANTE PRESS periódico digital]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alicantepress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alicantepress.com.</span></span></span>

These incidents also form part of the regional setting for the famous Manises case of 11 November 1979, when an airliner diverted after its crew reported unusual lights near Valencia and a military aircraft was dispatched. Manises belongs chiefly to Valencia’s UFO history, not Alicante’s, but Aitana’s radar role shows how the provinces were linked within the same Mediterranean surveillance network. Reports over the sea could involve observers, controllers and aircraft across several jurisdictions rather than fitting neatly within provincial borders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alicantepress.com/archive/33209/defensa-investigo-al-menos-8-casos-ovni-en-la-provincia-de-alicante-en-los-anos-70-y-80" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alicantepress.com">[ALICANTE PRESS periódico digital]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alicantepress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alicantepress.com.</span></span></span>

## Three incidents in 1986

The official Aitana dossier groups events on 24 April, 26 April and 26 July 1986. The catalogue confirms the dates and the existence of a formal intelligence file, although the public-facing record contains far less detail than would be required for a complete technical reconstruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/busqueda_referencia.do?campo=idtitulo&amp;idValor=395964" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

The best-known episode occurred at about 6 pm on 26 April. Personnel at EVA-5 reportedly saw an object falling towards the sea off Benidorm. The station notified the Civil Guard, but the military investigation was not continued. That limited response weakens attempts to turn the sighting into a major case. There is no publicly documented recovery, impact site, confirmed radar track or physical evidence. A distant object apparently descending over the sea could have been an aircraft seen in perspective, a flare, debris, a meteor, a balloon or another short-lived phenomenon whose true distance was difficult to judge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alicantepress.com/archive/33209/defensa-investigo-al-menos-8-casos-ovni-en-la-provincia-de-alicante-en-los-anos-70-y-80" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alicantepress.com">[ALICANTE PRESS periódico digital]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alicantepress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alicantepress.com.</span></span></span>

On 26 July, radar at EVA-5 recorded an unknown return on a route described as running between the Balearic Islands and Cádiz. An unidentified radar echo is a legitimate aviation observation, but its value depends on technical information that is not supplied in the short catalogue description: duration, speed, altitude, signal strength, continuity, secondary-radar response and whether other stations detected the same target. Without those details, the return cannot reliably be separated from ordinary traffic, electronic interference or anomalous propagation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alicantepress.com/archive/33209/defensa-investigo-al-menos-8-casos-ovni-en-la-provincia-de-alicante-en-los-anos-70-y-80" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alicantepress.com">[ALICANTE PRESS periódico digital]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alicantepress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alicantepress.com.</span></span></span>

The 1986 file matters mainly because it demonstrates continuity. Aitana was not associated with only one famous night; unusual observations were reported there across more than a decade. Yet repetition alone does not establish a single recurring phenomenon. A busy surveillance station overlooking sea and air routes would naturally encounter many different sources of ambiguity.

## The Alicante boomerang report

Not all of the province’s official material came from radar operators. On 25 June 1981, a resident of Alicante wrote to the Air Force describing an object seen from a terrace and included drawings showing a boomerang-like form. The Defence catalogue separately lists an Alicante sighting of unknown event date within a file dated 1981, supporting the existence of the civilian submission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alicantepress.com/archive/33209/defensa-investigo-al-menos-8-casos-ovni-en-la-provincia-de-alicante-en-los-anos-70-y-80" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alicantepress.com">[ALICANTE PRESS periódico digital]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alicantepress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alicantepress.com.</span></span></span>

The report is interesting because the shape differs from the classic circular “flying saucer” image. Boomerang, triangular and wing-like objects later became common categories in UFO reporting. Shape alone, however, is difficult to interpret without reliable distance and scale. A conventional aircraft seen from below, several lights perceived as one body, a formation of aircraft or an object partly concealed by darkness can all produce a broad angular outline.

The evidential limitations are substantial. The widely available account does not identify a second independent observer, a photograph, radar confirmation or a precise flight path. The drawing documents what the witness believed was seen; it does not independently verify the object’s structure. This is best classified as a sincere but weakly supported civilian report.

## Benidorm’s 1970s UFO fever

Alicante’s history also includes a social story: the transformation of UFO reports into public entertainment and collective sky-watching. Archival material revisited in a 2025 exhibition at Benidorm’s Boca del Calvari museum described a 1973 “UFO festival” on Levante Beach. According to the surviving press-based account, hundreds of people watched the sky and reported luminous spheres moving overhead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cadenaser.com/comunitat-valenciana/2025/10/29/del-bosque-a-las-estrellas-el-boca-del-calvari-revive-los-anos-del-festival-ovni-que-benidorm-celebro-en-la-playa-de-levante-radio-benidorm/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cadenaser.com">[Cadena SER]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cadenaser.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hoy por Hoy Benidorm &#124; Cadena SER...</span></span></span>

The event captures the atmosphere of the period. Space exploration, rapid tourism growth, new mass media and popular fascination with flying saucers all converged in a coastal resort already selling modernity and spectacle. Once a crowd gathers expecting unusual lights, ordinary objects can acquire heightened significance. Stars near the horizon, aircraft landing lights, lanterns, flares and satellites may appear to move because of cloud, perspective or the small involuntary eye movements that make a stationary point of light seem mobile.

That does not mean every participant invented what they saw. Crowd reports can begin with a real stimulus. The problem is that shared expectation can shape descriptions, while newspaper coverage tends to preserve the most striking claims rather than careful observational data. In the Benidorm festival material now publicly discussed, there is no robust measurement, radar record or photographic sequence demonstrating anomalous flight.

The same exhibition research recovered a Villajoyosa story in which municipal workers reportedly saw an unidentified object during their working day. This is locally valuable testimony, but the accessible account is a later summary of old press material rather than a complete case file. ## What the official files do — and do not — prove

The release of Spain’s Air Force files changed the evidential landscape by allowing readers to distinguish documented reports from stories that circulated only through books, magazines or word of mouth. The archive provides dates, institutional provenance and, in some files, witness interviews, weather information, operational reports and investigators’ conclusions. Personal details were removed, which protects witnesses but can make independent follow-up difficult.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

Declassification is sometimes misrepresented as confirmation. In reality, it means that the material was reviewed and made available after its security classification was reduced. The archive includes solved, tentatively explained and unresolved reports. Its existence demonstrates official interest in airspace anomalies, not an official judgement that unconventional craft were present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

Alicante’s record also shows why the number of “cases” can be misleading. Local reporting has described at least eight provincial incidents during the 1970s and 1980s, including five associated with Aitana. The national catalogue, however, sometimes groups several dates or observations inside one dossier. Counting files, reported events, radar contacts and witness episodes therefore produces different totals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alicantepress.com/archive/33209/defensa-investigo-al-menos-8-casos-ovni-en-la-provincia-de-alicante-en-los-anos-70-y-80" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alicantepress.com">[ALICANTE PRESS periódico digital]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alicantepress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alicantepress.com.</span></span></span>

## The strongest doubts and recurring explanations

No single explanation covers every Alicante report. The cases involve different settings, witnesses and instruments. Several recurring possibilities nevertheless account for much of the uncertainty.

**Military and civil aviation:** Aitana monitored a region containing established air routes, military operations and traffic between the mainland and the Balearic Islands. Aircraft viewed head-on may seem stationary, while landing lights can appear exceptionally bright.

**Exercises, parachutes and flares:** The Air Force itself considered parachuting activity in connection with the February 1979 lights. Illuminated parachutists or flares can produce multiple lights that descend slowly, separate or appear to hover.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alicantepress.com/archive/33209/defensa-investigo-al-menos-8-casos-ovni-en-la-provincia-de-alicante-en-los-anos-70-y-80" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alicantepress.com">[ALICANTE PRESS periódico digital]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alicantepress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alicantepress.com.</span></span></span>

**Radar anomalies:** Temperature inversions over the Mediterranean can bend radar energy and generate misleading returns, while interference, side lobes, transponder problems and incomplete tracking can create apparently unidentified echoes. A radar detection becomes compelling only when its movement is well recorded and independently corroborated.

**Astronomical and atmospheric objects:** Bright planets, meteors, re-entering debris and unusual cloud illumination frequently produce brief or confusing reports. Over the sea, a lack of visual reference points makes distance, size and trajectory particularly difficult to estimate.

**Expectation and media reinforcement:** Benidorm’s organised sky-watching illustrates how a real light can be interpreted within an existing cultural story. Repetition among witnesses may reflect a common object, but it can also reflect discussion within the crowd and the influence of prior publicity.

These explanations are possibilities rather than automatic solutions. Good scepticism requires matching an explanation to the reported time, direction and behaviour, not simply naming something ordinary after the fact.

## How to judge the main Alicante cases

The Aitana incidents deserve attention because some involved military personnel, radar or formal reporting. They are more substantial than anonymous internet anecdotes, but the available record remains incomplete.

The 1975 EVA-5 observation is the province’s strongest unresolved case because it reportedly combines multiple military witnesses with a preceding radar echo. Its credibility is limited by sparse technical documentation in the easily accessible record and by the gap between the original summary and later, more dramatic testimony.

The February 1979 event is comparatively weakened by the Air Force’s parachuting explanation. The March radar contact and fighter scramble show an operational response, but publicly available summaries do not establish extraordinary motion or physical characteristics. The 1986 observations are officially documented yet thin: the Benidorm sea descent produced no known recovery, while the July radar return lacks enough published technical detail for confident analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alicantepress.com/archive/33209/defensa-investigo-al-menos-8-casos-ovni-en-la-provincia-de-alicante-en-los-anos-70-y-80" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alicantepress.com">[ALICANTE PRESS periódico digital]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alicantepress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on alicantepress.com.</span></span></span>

The 1981 boomerang report and the Villajoyosa testimony are useful parts of the provincial archive, but neither currently has the independent corroboration needed to compete with the Aitana material. Benidorm’s festival is most significant as cultural history rather than as evidence for an anomalous vehicle.

## From “UFO” to an identified hazard

A modern incident at Alicante-Elche Airport helps clarify what “unidentified” means in practice. On 27 October 2025, a reported drone near the runway caused the airport to suspend traffic for one hour and 54 minutes. Ten incoming flights were diverted while Aena, the police and the Civil Guard responded. The airport reopened once further drone activity had been ruled out.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/comunitat-valenciana1/cerrado-el-aeropuerto-de-alicante-elche-por-la-posible-deteccion-de-drones" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: valenciaplaza.com">[Valencia Plaza]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">valenciaplaza.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on valenciaplaza.com.</span></span></span>

This was not treated as a paranormal event. It was an unidentified object with immediate aviation consequences, investigated through safety procedures and eventually described within a familiar technological category. The episode shows why authorities react before they know exactly what has been seen. Identification may come later; precaution comes first.

The same principle applies to the older Aitana files. A military report, radar alert or fighter launch establishes that uncertainty mattered operationally. It does not settle what caused the observation.

## Alicante’s place in Spanish UFO history

Alicante occupies a distinctive but not dominant position in Spain’s UFO history. It lacks a single nationally famous case on the scale of Manises or the large Canary Islands sightings, yet it has an unusually useful combination of military surveillance records, Mediterranean aviation context, civilian reports and a well-preserved period of popular fascination.

Its most important contribution is the Aitana sequence. Reports in 1975, 1979 and 1986 offer a small case study in how air-defence systems handle unusual observations: detect, record, compare, alert other authorities and sometimes close the matter without a firm identification. The cases also reveal the limits of retrospective UFO research. Official provenance can show that a report is genuine, but missing radar data, incomplete witness information and delayed testimony can prevent a reliable conclusion.

The province’s overall record is therefore mixed. A few incidents remain genuinely unresolved in the available documentation. Others have plausible conventional explanations or are too weakly recorded to assess. None supplies publicly verifiable evidence of extraterrestrial technology. Alicante’s UFO history remains worth studying not because it confirms extraordinary visitors, but because it shows how ambiguous events move through military systems, newspapers, witness memory and local culture — often becoming more certain in the retelling than they ever were in the original record.
