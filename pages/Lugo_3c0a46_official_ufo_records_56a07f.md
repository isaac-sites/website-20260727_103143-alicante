## Introduction

Spain’s official UFO records are best understood as a historical archive of reported aerial phenomena, not as a catalogue of confirmed extraterrestrial events. For Lugo, these archives are important because they show how an unusual sighting moved from a witness account into a formal military process. The clearest example is the 1969 Becerreá case, a report from the Lugo area that entered the Spanish Air Force’s investigation system and was later released through the Ministry of Defence’s digital archives. The existence of the file proves that authorities documented the report; it does not prove that the object had an extraordinary origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?id=38113" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

## Military archives and declassified files

Spain’s main official collection of UFO-related documents comes from the former Spanish Air Force investigation files, now available through the Biblioteca Virtual de Defensa. The collection contains 80 declassified files and around 1,900 pages covering reports of unusual aerial observations in Spanish airspace in which Air Force personnel, equipment or procedures were involved. The Ministry of Defence began the declassification process in the early 1990s, reducing the classification level of selected files and later making digital copies available online.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

For researchers studying Lugo, the most relevant document is the file titled *Avistamiento de fenómenos extraños entre Becerreá y Lugo: 02 de Abril de 1969*. The archive identifies it as a 1969 report produced by the Spanish Air Force’s Operational Command and intelligence section. The record was later declassified and entered into the public digital collection, making it one of the strongest documentary links between Lugo and Spain’s official UFO investigation system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?id=38113" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

The [Becerreá file]({{ 'becerreá-file/' | relative_url }}) matters because it demonstrates the route followed by many Spanish UFO reports during the twentieth century. A civilian observation could be passed to aviation authorities, reviewed by military personnel, and stored as part of an intelligence or aviation-safety archive. This process was not unique to Lugo: military organisations in several countries historically kept similar records because unexplained observations could potentially involve aircraft identification, airspace security or unusual atmospheric events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

### What the Becerreá record actually shows


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The archived material documents an investigation into a reported unusual observation near Becerreá and Lugo on 2 April 1969. The surviving record is significant because it contains an official attempt to examine the event rather than simply a later newspaper retelling or private investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?id=38113" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

</div>

However, an official investigation file should not be confused with official confirmation. Spanish military UFO files generally record observations and the investigative response; they do not represent a declaration that an unknown object was an extraterrestrial spacecraft. The Becerreá case remains historically important because it entered a government archive, but the publicly available evidence does not include independently verified physical material, confirmed imagery or other evidence that would demonstrate an extraordinary explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?id=38113" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

Later discussion of the case has often focused on the quality of the investigation and the interpretation of the final assessment. Some UFO researchers have criticised aspects of the official handling, while sceptical interpretations emphasise that witness descriptions alone can rarely establish the true identity of an unusual sighting. The archive allows both positions to be examined, but it does not remove the central limitation: the event remains a documented report rather than a proven anomaly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.becerrea.net/portal/caso_ovni" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: becerrea.net">[Becerreá]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">becerrea.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">BecerreáCaso Ovni &#124; Becerrea.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BecerreáCaso Ovni &#124; Becerrea.net</span></span></span>

## How investigations were recorded

Spanish UFO files followed a bureaucratic model closer to incident reporting than to scientific experimentation. Investigators typically collected descriptions of the observation, information about location and timing, witness statements, possible explanations and an administrative conclusion. The surviving archive therefore provides insight into how institutions managed uncertainty rather than simply whether a sighting was “real” or “false”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

For Lugo, this distinction is particularly important. A report could be considered worthy of investigation because of the circumstances surrounding it, such as the location, the witness account or possible aviation relevance, while still ending without a definitive explanation. “Unidentified” in an official file generally means that investigators could not confidently assign a cause from the available information, not that the object was proven to be unknown technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

The archives also show the limits of historical investigation. Many files preserve summaries and official assessments but do not provide modern levels of evidence such as high-resolution imagery, multiple independent sensor records or comprehensive environmental data. The Ministry of Defence collection itself notes that although the documents were released, personal details of witnesses and reporting officers were removed. This protects privacy but also limits later researchers’ ability to revisit some cases directly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

## What official records can and cannot explain

Official archives changed the study of Lugo’s UFO reports because they moved some cases from folklore and local memory into a verifiable documentary record. A newspaper article or personal account may show that a story circulated, but a military file shows that authorities received, assessed and stored information about the report. This difference is why the Becerreá case remains central to Lugo’s UFO history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?id=38113" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

At the same time, declassification did not produce the dramatic confirmation sometimes expected by UFO enthusiasts. Instead, the records mostly reveal a more ordinary pattern: people reported unusual sights, officials investigated them, and many cases remained unresolved because the available evidence was insufficient. The archive is valuable precisely because it shows the workings of government investigation rather than providing a simple answer to every mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

The Lugo files therefore sit between two extremes. They challenge the idea that UFO reports were ignored by authorities, because some were formally investigated and preserved. But they also challenge the idea that government attention equals proof of extraordinary events. The strongest conclusion supported by the records is that Lugo contains documented cases of unusual observations whose explanations vary in strength, from plausible conventional interpretations to unresolved historical reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?id=38113" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

## Limits of official explanations

Military archives are strongest when answering questions about process: who reported an event, when it was recorded, what investigators considered and how the file was classified. They are weaker when used to prove what an observer actually saw decades later. The passage of time, incomplete data and the absence of physical evidence create unavoidable limits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

For the Becerreá case and other Lugo reports, the most balanced reading is therefore neither dismissal nor confirmation. The records show that unusual sightings were taken seriously enough to enter official channels, but they do not establish that the cause was beyond conventional explanation. Their lasting importance lies in documenting how Spain’s institutions responded to uncertainty in the skies over places such as Lugo.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?id=38113" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>
