## Introduction

The Manises incident of 11 November 1979 remains [Valencia]({{ 'valencias-ufo-cases-between-mystery-and/' | relative_url }})’s most famous aviation-related UFO case because it involved a passenger aircraft, air traffic procedures, military interception and later official review. A TAE Super Caravelle passenger flight diverted to Manises Airport near Valencia after its crew reported unusual red lights appearing to approach the aircraft. The Spanish Air Force then launched a Mirage F1 fighter to investigate, creating a rare case in which a reported unidentified aerial phenomenon became part of an aviation and military response.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manises_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manises UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manises UFO incident</span></span></span>

## The 1979 flight and reported lights

The event began during TAE flight JK-297, a Super Caravelle travelling from Salzburg to Las Palmas with a stop at Palma de Mallorca. On the night of 11 November 1979, while flying over the western Mediterranean near Ibiza, the crew reported seeing bright red lights that appeared to move close to the aircraft. Captain Francisco Javier Lerdo de Tejada considered the situation serious enough to request information from air traffic authorities and later divert the aircraft to Manises Airport in Valencia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manises_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manises UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manises UFO incident</span></span></span>

The central feature of the case was not simply that lights were seen, but that the crew interpreted their behaviour as a possible aviation hazard. According to accounts of the incident, the lights appeared to maintain a relationship with the aircraft during manoeuvres, creating concern that they might represent another object operating dangerously nearby. No conventional traffic was identified by the available control information at the time, increasing the uncertainty faced by the pilots.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manises_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manises UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manises UFO incident</span></span></span>

After the aircraft landed at Manises, reports of unusual lights continued around the airport area. These later observations contributed to the case’s reputation because they appeared to involve several groups of observers rather than a single cockpit report. However, multiple observations do not automatically mean multiple unexplained objects: different witnesses may interpret the same distant light source in different ways, especially at night and without accurate distance information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manises_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manises UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manises UFO incident</span></span></span>

## Military response and official records

The military involvement is the main reason Manises became a landmark case in Spanish UFO history. The Spanish Air Force dispatched a Mirage F1 interceptor flown by Captain Fernando Cámara from Los Llanos Air Base to investigate the reported phenomenon. Cámara later described seeing an unusual visual target during the mission, although the encounter did not result in the identification of a physical object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manises_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manises UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manises UFO incident</span></span></span>

The interception created two separate questions for investigators. The first was whether the original lights seen by the passenger aircraft represented an unknown object. The second was whether the fighter pilot’s experience had the same cause as the earlier sighting. These questions are important because the incident is often discussed as a single event, while the investigation involved several observations made from different locations and under different conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manises_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manises UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manises UFO incident</span></span></span>

The case also gained political attention. In 1980, questions were raised in Spain’s parliament about what had happened, and the Spanish Air Force later released documentation connected with the investigation after UFO files were declassified in the 1990s. The released material provided an official framework for evaluating the case rather than confirming an extraordinary explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manises_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manises UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manises UFO incident</span></span></span>

## Possible explanations and remaining disputes

The official explanation focused on conventional sources of misidentification. The declassified Air Force investigation suggested that the reported lights could have been caused by a combination of distant industrial lights and astronomical objects, including stars or planets. This interpretation argued that the witnesses were observing real lights but drawing incorrect conclusions about their distance, movement and relationship to the aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manises_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manises UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manises UFO incident</span></span></span>

One later civilian investigation by the Spanish Anomaly Foundation also argued that the main observations could be explained through conventional causes, particularly the relationship between distant lights, atmospheric conditions and human perception at night. Supporters of this interpretation consider Manises an example of how even experienced observers can misjudge distant sources when depth perception is limited.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/791111spanish_anomaly.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">791111spanish anomaly</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Anomaly Foundation Solves the &quot;Manises&quot; UFO CaseAfter a scientific and painstaking investigation of almost two decades, the &#x27;Man...</span></span></span>

Critics of the conventional explanation have argued that it does not fully address every reported detail, particularly the pilot’s account of the Mirage F1 encounter and claims about aircraft systems being affected during the interception. Some researchers and witnesses have therefore continued to regard parts of the case as unresolved. However, an unresolved observation is not the same as evidence for a non-conventional craft; it means that the available information does not allow a complete reconstruction accepted by everyone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manises_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manises UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manises UFO incident</span></span></span>

The military encounter has produced additional debate over possible technical explanations.
