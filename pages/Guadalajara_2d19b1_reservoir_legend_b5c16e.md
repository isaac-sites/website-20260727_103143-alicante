## Introduction

Sacedón became a reservoir UFO legend largely because a later story was laid over a much narrower contemporary report. The Spanish [Air Force file]({{ 'military-file/' | relative_url }}) records a five- or six-second sighting in February 1969: a coloured spherical object crossed the sky and disappeared while apparently above the reservoir. It does not say that the object entered the water, emerged from it or returned on other occasions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1969-02-08_avistamiento_en_sacedon_%28guadalajara%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Guadalajara_2d19b1_reservoir_legend_b5c16e-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Reservoir Legend" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

More than four decades later, investigators searching locally for the original witnesses collected stories about luminous objects entering or leaving nearby reservoirs. One woman was presented as a likely witness to the military case after a relative described a “ball” coming out of the water. Yet the available published material does not establish that she and her husband were the couple interviewed by the Air Force in 1969.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.espaciomisterio.com/ovnis-y-vida-extraterrestre/expediente-sacedon-objetos-submarinos-no-identificados_33998" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: espaciomisterio.com">[Espacio Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">espaciomisterio.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Espacio Misterio«EXPEDIENTE SACEDÓN»: OBJETOS SUBMARINOS NO IDENTIFICADOS</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Espacio Misterio«EXPEDIENTE SACEDÓN»: OBJETOS SUBMARINOS NO IDENTIFICADOS</span></span></span>

The result is a useful caution within [Guadalajara]({{ 'guadalajara/' | relative_url }})’s UFO history: the reservoir element is memorable, but the strongest surviving document supports a brief sky sighting, not a verified submerged-object incident.

## The later submerged-object claims

The reservoir interpretation became prominent through a 2012 article by investigators David Cuevas and Juan José Sánchez-Oro. Their report said that enquiries in Sacedón had uncovered numerous people who remembered unidentified objects submerging in reservoirs near the town. It framed the area as the setting for unidentified submerged objects rather than simply one unexplained aerial observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.espaciomisterio.com/ovnis-y-vida-extraterrestre/expediente-sacedon-objetos-submarinos-no-identificados_33998" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: espaciomisterio.com">[Espacio Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">espaciomisterio.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Espacio Misterio«EXPEDIENTE SACEDÓN»: OBJETOS SUBMARINOS NO IDENTIFICADOS</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Espacio Misterio«EXPEDIENTE SACEDÓN»: OBJETOS SUBMARINOS NO IDENTIFICADOS</span></span></span>

The published online portion, however, provides only a short account of how the supposed link to the original case was made. Residents initially remembered the 1969 incident only in broad terms and could not name its witnesses. Eventually, one man said that his aunt and her husband had seen the event while travelling from celebrations at Bolarque. According to his version, they saw a ball emerge near the reservoir. The investigators then interviewed his aunt, identified as Visitación Notario, although the fuller testimony appeared in a print magazine rather than in the accessible article.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.espaciomisterio.com/ovnis-y-vida-extraterrestre/expediente-sacedon-objetos-submarinos-no-identificados_33998" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: espaciomisterio.com">[Espacio Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">espaciomisterio.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Espacio Misterio«EXPEDIENTE SACEDÓN»: OBJETOS SUBMARINOS NO IDENTIFICADOS</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Espacio Misterio«EXPEDIENTE SACEDÓN»: OBJETOS SUBMARINOS NO IDENTIFICADOS</span></span></span>

That is an interesting piece of local oral history, but it is not independent confirmation of the military report. At this stage, the chain of information runs through several layers:

Each stage can preserve genuine information, but each also creates opportunities for details to shift, merge with other stories or be interpreted through later UFO language. The phrase “came out of the reservoir”, for example, is far more specific than the official witness’s statement that the object disappeared when it was “over the reservoir”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1969-02-08_avistamiento_en_sacedon_%28guadalajara%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive</span></span></span>

The wider claim that many local people saw objects enter the water is similarly difficult to assess from the surviving public material. The article asserts that several witnesses were found, including people who experienced close approaches by luminous objects, but it does not provide contemporary reports, dated police statements, photographs, physical traces or an accessible catalogue separating one incident from another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.espaciomisterio.com/ovnis-y-vida-extraterrestre/expediente-sacedon-objetos-submarinos-no-identificados_33998" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: espaciomisterio.com">[Espacio Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">espaciomisterio.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Espacio Misterio«EXPEDIENTE SACEDÓN»: OBJETOS SUBMARINOS NO IDENTIFICADOS</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Espacio Misterio«EXPEDIENTE SACEDÓN»: OBJETOS SUBMARINOS NO IDENTIFICADOS</span></span></span>

This does not prove that the later witnesses invented their experiences. It means that their accounts belong to a different evidential category. They are retrospective oral testimonies collected after the Sacedón case had become publicly known as an official UFO file and after the investigators had arrived specifically looking for extraordinary incidents around the reservoirs.

## Memory, repetition and witness identity

The most important problem is not simply that the later testimony was old. It is that the identity of the later witnesses does not clearly match the profile recorded in the contemporary file.

The Air Force questionnaire describes the principal witness as a 44-year-old married telecommunications engineer who worked as a company director. He was travelling with his wife from the Las Brisas development towards Sacedón. The officer who interviewed him described him as measured or level-headed. The document records no previous similar sighting and says that the couple observed the object with the naked eye while travelling by car.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1969-02-08_avistamiento_en_sacedon_%28guadalajara%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive</span></span></span>

By contrast, the later lead began with a resident saying that his aunt and uncle were returning from celebrations at Bolarque. The accessible article does not show that the husband was the engineer described in the file, that the route was the same, or that the couple’s ages, residence and occupations matched. It also reports the nephew’s version as an object coming out of the reservoir, a detail absent from the original questionnaire.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.espaciomisterio.com/ovnis-y-vida-extraterrestre/expediente-sacedon-objetos-submarinos-no-identificados_33998" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: espaciomisterio.com">[Espacio Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">espaciomisterio.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Espacio Misterio«EXPEDIENTE SACEDÓN»: OBJETOS SUBMARINOS NO IDENTIFICADOS</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Espacio Misterio«EXPEDIENTE SACEDÓN»: OBJETOS SUBMARINOS NO IDENTIFICADOS</span></span></span>

Those differences may have innocent explanations. Las Brisas and Bolarque could have been points on a longer journey, a relative might have simplified the circumstances, and the published article may have omitted identification work contained in the print version. Nevertheless, without a demonstrated match, the later couple cannot safely be treated as the original Air Force witnesses.

The time gap also matters. Psychological research does not imply that old witnesses are dishonest or that every discrepancy makes a story worthless. Memory is reconstructive rather than a fixed recording. Later conversations, newspaper reports and repeated retellings can become difficult to distinguish from details personally perceived during the event. The National Academies’ review of eyewitness evidence notes that vivid memories can still contain errors and that repeated retelling may incorporate information acquired afterwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nap.nationalacademies.org/skim.php?act=nap&amp;chap=1-8&amp;record_id=18891" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nap.nationalacademies.org">[National Academies Publications]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nap.nationalacademies.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">As this report indicates, however, the malleable nature of human visual perception, memory, and confidence; the imperfect ability to reco...</span></span></span>

Repetition presents a particular risk in a small community. A story told for decades can become socially stable even while its details evolve. Research on misinformation has found that repeated exposure increases the likelihood that later information will influence eyewitness recall. That does not show that this happened in Sacedón, but it explains why a decades-later community narrative should not automatically be treated as several independent observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37691811/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In addition, we reported a meta-analysis (k = 19) for the repeated misinformation effect and a small-scale meta-analysis (k = 8) for the...</span></span></span>

The number of people who “remember the case” is therefore less important than the sources of their memories. A resident may remember seeing a light, hearing about the engineer’s report, reading about the declassified file or listening to a neighbour’s reservoir story. Unless interviews establish which kind of memory is being reported, several apparent witnesses may be repeating one local tradition rather than corroborating one another.

## Where the official record differs

The Air Force file offers a much more restrained narrative than the later reservoir legend. Its central points are specific:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The sighting occurred at about 8.30 pm on 8 February 1969.
* The object appeared spherical, with a fiery red front, a silvery body and a blue trail.
* It moved laterally in a straight line for five or six seconds.
* It disappeared while apparently above the reservoir, without passing behind mountains or another visible obstruction.
* The witnesses reported no sound, photograph, physical mark or repeat appearance.
* The principal witness had no instrument with which to calculate its distance or altitude.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1969-02-08_avistamiento_en_sacedon_%28guadalajara%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive</span></span></span>

</div>

The distinction between “above the reservoir” and “into the reservoir” is fundamental. The witness was observing an unidentified light against a dark sky from a moving car. Because its distance was unknown, its apparent position relative to the water could not establish contact with the surface. A distant object fading, burning out or moving beyond visibility could appear to vanish over a nearby landscape feature.

The document also records that the witness initially estimated an altitude below 300 metres and a speed slower than an aircraft coming in to land. Those judgements sound precise but were explicitly unsupported by measuring equipment. Without known distance, angular movement cannot be converted reliably into physical speed or height.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1969-02-08_avistamiento_en_sacedon_%28guadalajara%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive</span></span></span>

The original investigating officer concluded that no explanation had been found. That phrase records the state of the enquiry in February 1969; it is not a conclusion that the object was a craft, still less an underwater vehicle. When the file was reviewed for declassification in 1993, the intelligence assessment noted broadly similar reports from Daimiel and Ciudad Real and considered a meteor-like phenomenon a possible explanation because of the timing and visual similarities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1969-02-08_avistamiento_en_sacedon_%28guadalajara%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive</span></span></span>

The meteor interpretation is not proved beyond doubt. The reported impression of slow movement and low altitude does not fit an obvious textbook fireball, although both impressions are vulnerable to distance error. More importantly for the reservoir story, neither the original unexplained conclusion nor the later meteor suggestion supplies evidence for water entry. Both treat the event as an aerial observation.

The official file contains other gaps. The relevant radar facility was recorded as inoperative, so there was no track to establish range or trajectory. The wife was named as a second observer but does not appear to have supplied a separate detailed questionnaire in the released file. No photograph was taken, and the drawing was produced after the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1969-02-08_avistamiento_en_sacedon_%28guadalajara%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive</span></span></span>

These weaknesses leave the aerial sighting open to interpretation, but they do not create positive evidence for a submerged object. An absence of radar or a failure to identify a light cannot bridge the gap between something disappearing in the direction of water and something physically entering it.

## How the legend grew stronger than the case

Reservoirs make powerful settings for UFO stories. They offer broad horizons, reflections, isolated roads and a visible surface behind which a light may seem to disappear. Sacedón also sits within the Entrepeñas reservoir landscape, giving the 1969 phrase “over the reservoir” an obvious narrative hook. The area’s association with inundated places, including the former royal spa settlement of La Isabela beneath the neighbouring Buendía reservoir, adds to the atmosphere of submerged history, although it provides no evidence about the UFO claim itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cultura.gob.es/va/cultura/areas/archivos/mc/archivos/nhn/destacados/documentos-destacados/banos-isabela.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cultura.gob.es">[Cultura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cultura.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cultura Los baños del Real Sitio de La Isabela (Sacedón</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Los baños del Real Sitio de La Isabela (Sacedón) - Archivo Histórico de la Nobleza &#124; Ministerio de CulturaLos baños del Real Sitio...</span></span></span>

Once the official file became public, several elements were especially easy to amplify: a coloured sphere, a sudden disappearance, a reservoir and an Air Force conclusion that initially offered no explanation. In retelling, “disappeared over the reservoir” can become “fell into the reservoir”, then “entered the water”, and finally part of a pattern of underwater craft.

The 2012 presentation encouraged that shift by treating the meteor possibility as incompatible with reality before the accessible evidence for the later witnesses had been set out. It also used the language of unidentified submerged objects and described repeated reservoir activity, placing the reader within a larger mystery from the outset.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.espaciomisterio.com/ovnis-y-vida-extraterrestre/expediente-sacedon-objetos-submarinos-no-identificados_33998" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: espaciomisterio.com">[Espacio Misterio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">espaciomisterio.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Espacio Misterio«EXPEDIENTE SACEDÓN»: OBJETOS SUBMARINOS NO IDENTIFICADOS</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Espacio Misterio«EXPEDIENTE SACEDÓN»: OBJETOS SUBMARINOS NO IDENTIFICADOS</span></span></span>

A stronger investigation would have separated three questions that the legend tends to merge:

**Was the later couple definitely the couple in the military file?**

The publicly available account does not demonstrate this through matching biographical details or documentation.

**Did the 1969 witnesses say the object touched the water?**

The official record says only that it disappeared while above the reservoir.

**Were there independent later reservoir incidents?**

They may have been reported, but their value depends on when they were first recorded, whether witnesses were interviewed separately and whether the accounts contain verifiable details not derived from the known Sacedón story.

Until those questions are resolved, the safest description is that a documented aerial sighting later became the centre of a broader reservoir legend.

## A weak expansion of a genuine case

The reservoir testimony did not strengthen the original Sacedón file in the way that a photograph, radar record or matching contemporary statement would have done. Instead, it introduced new claims while making witness identity and narrative continuity less certain.

The 1969 case remains a legitimate part of Guadalajara’s UFO history because an Air Force officer interviewed a named, professionally identifiable witness shortly after the event and preserved a structured account. Its limitations are equally clear: a very brief observation, uncertain distance, no sensor confirmation and no physical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1969-02-08_avistamiento_en_sacedon_%28guadalajara%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet Archive</span></span></span>

The later reservoir stories are best retained as local folklore or unverified testimony rather than folded into the official incident as though they were present from the beginning. They may preserve memories of other unusual lights around Entrepeñas or Bolarque, but the available evidence does not establish a repeated pattern of objects entering the water.

Sacedón therefore illustrates how an unresolved case can acquire a more dramatic second life. The contemporary evidence supports an unidentified light seen above a reservoir. The submerged-object narrative depends on testimony gathered more than forty years later, an uncertain witness match and details that differ materially from the Air Force record. On present evidence, the later reporting weakened rather than strengthened the claim.
