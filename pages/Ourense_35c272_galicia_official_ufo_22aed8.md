## Introduction

Official files add context to [Ourense]({{ 'ourense-ufo-history-between-legend-and/' | relative_url }})’s UFO history, but they do not provide a strong official case from the province itself. Spain’s public Ministry of Defence catalogue contains four well-known Galician files: Ferrol in 1966, the Becerreá–Lugo road in 1969, and two incidents associated with the Noia air-surveillance station in 1989 and 1993. None is catalogued as a dedicated Ourense investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[gob.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Ourense_35c272_galicia_official_ufo_22aed8-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Official Records" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The most relevant file for judging wider regional claims is the Noia incident of 5 December 1989. It involved reports from more than one location, radar displays, air-traffic communications and apparent radio interference. That makes it more substantial than an ordinary newspaper anecdote. Yet the surviving papers do not show that a single extraordinary craft was independently confirmed, and they contain no evidence tying the episode specifically to Ourense. The official record therefore strengthens the history of military interest in unusual Galician air reports, not the case for an unexplained Ourense phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160070859" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[gob.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Avistamiento de fenómenos extraños: expedientes OVNI España</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Biblioteca Virtual DefensaBiblioteca Virtual de Defensa &gt; Expedientes OVNI &gt; Avistamiento de fenómenos extraños en EVA-10, Noya (La...Ima...</span></span></span>

## The multi-location sighting of 1989

Shortly before 19:00 UTC on 5 December 1989, personnel at EVA-10, the Spanish Air Force surveillance unit on Monte Iroite near Noia, received information about a possible unidentified object in the area between Santiago de Compostela and A Coruña. Santiago airport’s controller relayed a Guardia Civil report describing something apparently lenticular, moving slowly and displaying numerous bright, changing lights near the Castro–Sada area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNIAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO DE...</span></span></span>

The case became more interesting when surveillance personnel examined their displays. At about 19:00, the report records an echo on primary and secondary radar screens, initially indicated at approximately 57,000 feet. Its displayed altitude reportedly varied as high as 75,000 feet while its horizontal position changed comparatively little. At around 20:00, emergency-frequency alarms were activated and communications between Santiago, Madrid air-traffic control and another station experienced interference. Direction-finding equipment later placed the apparent source of the alarm on a bearing south-east of Santiago.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNIAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO DE...</span></span></span>

These details are sometimes presented as a straightforward combination of eyewitness and radar evidence. The file is less conclusive than that description suggests. Civil aircraft passing near the relevant position did not report seeing the supposed object. The initial visual information came through a chain of communications rather than from a clearly identified observer standing beside the radar operators. The recorded radar indications, radio alarms and coloured-light report may therefore have been related, partly related or coincidental; the short dossier does not demonstrate that they all originated from one physical object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNIAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO DE...</span></span></span>

There are also reasons to be cautious about treating the displayed heights as measurements of a craft. Radar returns can be affected by equipment behaviour, transponder information, atmospheric propagation, interference or unrelated aircraft. The papers note several objects at normal flight levels later in the evening, while the persistent height-display return reportedly remained until the following morning without significant variation. That unusual persistence may be evidence of an anomalous target, but it may also point towards a technical or propagation effect. The released file contains no preserved radar recording or technical reconstruction capable of deciding between those possibilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNIAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO DE...</span></span></span>

For Ourense, the geographical distinction matters. The documented activity centred on the Santiago–A Coruña corridor, the Barbanza surveillance station and communications involving Santiago airport. Nothing in the released file identifies an Ourense witness, an Ourense radar station or a trajectory crossing the province. It belongs in the wider Galician story because it shows how a regional alert could involve police, aviation and military systems, but it should not be counted as an official Ourense case merely because it occurred within Galicia.

## Noia surveillance records and military limits

The importance of Noia lies less in any extraterrestrial implication than in the type of institution involved. EVA-10 formed part of Spain’s air-surveillance network, so its personnel were responsible for recognising aircraft, monitoring radar information and reporting events that might affect controlled airspace. Their records deserve more weight than an unsupported recollection because they were written within an operational system and preserved in a Defence file.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNIAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO DE...</span></span></span>

Official status, however, does not guarantee a complete investigation. The 1989 dossier is only a few pages long and was assembled for archival and classification purposes several years after the event. Its documentation includes an operational report, but not a full scientific study of weather conditions, astronomical objects, radar performance, aircraft movements and radio-frequency faults. The declassification assessment merely found no reason to keep the material classified; it did not certify the event as unexplained technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_coruna.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[J.J. Benítez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1989 12 05 avistamiento en eva 10 noya coruna</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BenítezAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI)AVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMEN...</span></span></span>

The contrast with the second Noia file is revealing. On 23 November 1993, five military witnesses at EVA-10 saw a green luminous nucleus with a white-yellow trail moving rapidly towards the Atlantic for about three or four seconds. That incident generated roughly 125 pages, including witness questionnaires, maps and individual analysis. The Air Force concluded that the phenomenon could have been a meteor or similar natural event, noting its very high speed and straight trajectory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/noticia/barbanza/2016/10/26/ovnis-barbanza-meteoritos/0003_201610B26C3991.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[lavozdegalicia.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Voz de GaliciaLos ovnis de Barbanza eran meteoritosLos ovnis de Barbanza eran meteoritos Los ovnis de Barbanza eran meteoritos JAVIER...</span></span></span>

Contemporary reporting also indicates that the 1993 light was observed from numerous places in southern Galicia, including the coastal area around Bueu and the island of Ons. That wider distribution is significant because a bright atmospheric object at great altitude can appear over a vast area and seem to be travelling towards a nearby landmark. Multiple witnesses do not automatically imply a large low-flying craft; in this instance, the breadth of the reports, short duration, luminous trail and extreme apparent speed all favour a fireball interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.diariodepontevedra.es/articulo/noticias/el-ovni-de-ons-era-un-meteorito/20161105010000313041.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: diariodepontevedra.es">[Diario de Pontevedra]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">diariodepontevedra.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Diario de Pontevedra El OVNI avistado en Ons era un meteorito</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Diario de Pontevedra El OVNI avistado en Ons era un meteorito</span></span></span>

Modern Galician fireballs provide a useful comparison. Bright meteors have repeatedly been reported from Ourense alongside sightings in Pontevedra, Vigo, Lugo, Portugal and other distant locations. They may create intense green or blue light, persistent trails, delayed sounds and initial uncertainty among witnesses. Later photographs, videos and astronomical assessment often reveal that apparently local “objects” were regional atmospheric events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.laregion.es/comarca-de-ourense/bolido-u-ovni-supuesto-meteorito-cruza-cielo-galicia-deja-ver-ourense_1_20240220-2432766.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: laregion.es">[La Región]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">laregion.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on laregion.es.</span></span></span>

This does not prove that the 1989 Noia episode was a meteor. Its longer duration, changing lights, radar indications and radio interference distinguish it from the brief 1993 fireball. It does show why separate strands of evidence must be tested rather than combined automatically. A visual light, a radar return and a communications fault occurring during the same evening are suggestive, but they become a strong case only when timing, direction, range and movement establish that all three tracked the same source.

## What the Defence archive does not establish

Spain’s digital Defence collection is an archive of reports investigated or retained by the Air Force, not a comprehensive register of everything seen over each province. The published collection contains around 80 files and roughly 1,900 pages covering incidents across Spanish airspace from the early 1960s to the mid-1990s. Some files combine reports from several locations, while others concern a single witness or military installation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.europapress.es/galego/noticia-catro-ovnis-avistaronse-galicia-1966-1993-expedientes-defensa-20161022121758.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: europapress.es">[europapress.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">europapress.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on europapress.es.</span></span></span>

The four files normally identified with Galicia are geographically assigned to Ferrol, the road between Becerreá and Lugo, and Noia. The Ferrol file concerned a blue light reported in 1966, while the 1969 road case involved a driver’s description of a brightly illuminated, projectile-shaped form close to the ground. Investigators tentatively suggested that the latter might have been sunlight reflected from mica-rich granite, although the limited information prevented certainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/noticia/ferrol/ferrol/2016/10/22/defensa-situa-ferrol-primer-avistamiento-ovni-documentado-galicia/00031477132195756887939.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[La Voz de Galicia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Voz de GaliciaLos ovnis que el Ejército investigó en GaliciaLos ovnis que el Ejército investigó en Galicia Opinión &#124; 25 artigos para e...</span></span></span>

No comparable catalogue entry is clearly devoted to Ourense. That absence should be interpreted carefully.

* **It does not prove that nobody in Ourense reported unusual lights.** Local newspapers contain numerous claims that never became Air Force files.
* **It does not prove that records were destroyed or concealed.** A civilian report might never have reached military channels, might have lacked aviation significance or might have been filed under the location of the investigating unit rather than every place from which it was visible.
* **It does mean that familiar Ourense stories lack this particular form of corroboration.** The Alto do Rodicio light, the [Antela]({{ 'antela/' | relative_url }}) encounter and later close-encounter narratives are not supported by a clearly matching public Defence dossier.
* **It prevents regional cases from being treated as provincial evidence without geographical support.** A radar event near Noia or a fireball observed across southern Galicia cannot automatically be added to Ourense’s official case count.

The catalogue also records administrative decisions rather than verdicts on origin. “Unidentified” usually means that the available information did not permit a secure identification at the time. Some files remained inconclusive because essential data were missing; others offered plausible conventional explanations without claiming absolute proof. Declassification means that the documents were judged releasable, not that Defence endorsed the witnesses’ interpretation or confirmed an unknown vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[gob.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

## Do official files strengthen Ourense’s UFO record?

Only indirectly. The Noia material confirms that unusual Galician air reports were sometimes taken seriously enough to enter military channels. It also preserves operational details—times, bearings, displayed altitudes, radio frequencies and witness statements—that are rarely available for Ourense’s best-known stories. The 1989 file remains genuinely unresolved in the limited sense that its published documents provide no definitive explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160070859" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Avistamiento de fenómenos extraños: expedientes OVNI España</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Biblioteca Virtual DefensaBiblioteca Virtual de Defensa &gt; Expedientes OVNI &gt; Avistamiento de fenómenos extraños en EVA-10, Noya (La...Ima...</span></span></span>

But it does not supply missing evidence for an Ourense incident. There is no demonstrated physical connection between the Noia radar episode and sightings in the inland province, and the Defence catalogue contains no dedicated investigation confirming the extraordinary elements of Ourense’s local narratives. The strongest Galicia-wide comparison, the extensively investigated 1993 event, ultimately weakens rather than strengthens an exotic interpretation because its characteristics closely matched a bright meteor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.diariodepontevedra.es/articulo/noticias/el-ovni-de-ons-era-un-meteorito/20161105010000313041.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: diariodepontevedra.es">[diariodepontevedra.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">diariodepontevedra.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Diario de Pontevedra El OVNI avistado en Ons era un meteorito</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Diario de Pontevedra El OVNI avistado en Ons era un meteorito</span></span></span>

The balanced assessment is therefore narrow but useful: official records show that Galicia produced several reports worthy of documentation, including one technically intriguing incident in 1989. They do not establish a recurring unidentified presence over Ourense, confirm any alleged landing or occupant encounter, or demonstrate technology beyond conventional aircraft and natural phenomena. Ourense remains part of Galicia’s broader UFO culture and sighting geography, but its provincial history still rests primarily on press reports and witness testimony rather than a strong body of official aviation evidence.
