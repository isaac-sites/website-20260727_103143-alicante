## Introduction

The Antela encounter is [Ourense]({{ 'ourense-ufo-history-between-legend-and/' | relative_url }})’s most detailed alleged meeting with extraterrestrial visitors, but it is not the province’s strongest UFO case. According to a newspaper report published on 19 September 1963, an unnamed farmer claimed that twelve days earlier he had watched a disc-like craft descend near the former Antela lagoon, approached three unusual occupants and spoken with one of them. The story included almost every memorable feature of a close encounter: a landed object, humanoid figures, telepathy, dialogue and the collection of soil and stones.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/amp/noticia/ourense/2023/09/24/campesino-narraba-encuentro-extraterrestres-limia/0003_202309O24C4991.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[La Voz de Galicia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">0003 202309O24C4991</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Voz de GaliciaEn 1963 un campesino de Ourense decía haber visto aterrizar un OVNI: «Hablé con los tres tripulantes»...</span></span></span>

Its evidential foundation, however, is exceptionally narrow. No identified witness, second observer, photograph, physical trace, contemporary official file or independent investigation has emerged. Later coverage has preserved the original narrative rather than adding corroboration. The fairest assessment is therefore that something may have been reported to a journalist in 1963, but there is insufficient evidence to establish what inspired the account, still less that anything extraordinary landed beside Antela.

## The farmer’s reported encounter

The alleged incident took place on 7 September 1963 in the A Limia district. The precise location was not published, beyond its being close to the Antela lagoon and a nearby river. That vagueness matters because Antela was not a compact landmark. Before its drainage, the wetland had occupied a broad plain touching several municipalities, and extensive drainage works had been under way since the late 1950s. ## What evidence survived

The surviving evidence appears to consist of a single newspaper narrative written by Rubén San Julián. The 1963 article was prominently presented across three columns and accompanied by illustrative flying-saucer imagery. La Voz de Galicia reproduced its contents for the sixtieth anniversary in 2023, but that retrospective did not identify the farmer or uncover additional witnesses, documents or material evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/amp/noticia/ourense/2023/09/24/campesino-narraba-encuentro-extraterrestres-limia/0003_202309O24C4991.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[La Voz de Galicia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">0003 202309O24C4991</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Voz de GaliciaEn 1963 un campesino de Ourense decía haber visto aterrizar un OVNI: «Hablé con los tres tripulantes»...</span></span></span>

This leaves several basic questions unanswered:

* **Who was the witness?** An anonymous person cannot be traced, interviewed again or checked against local records.
* **Where exactly did the incident occur?** There is no sufficiently precise site at which investigators could examine terrain, distances or sight lines.
* **When did it occur?** The day was supplied, but the surviving account does not establish a reliable clock time or detailed duration.
* **Who heard the early version?** The farmer reportedly told his family, yet no relative was named or quoted.

* **Were the collected materials significant?** The visitors supposedly took ordinary stones and soil, but no sample remained for comparison.
* **Was an authority notified?** The story mentions a desire to report the event, not a surviving police, military or civil investigation.

The absence of an official file is especially important because Spain’s declassified military collection provides a useful comparison. The Ministry of Defence archive contains 80 files covering reports from 1962 to 1995 in which Air Force personnel or equipment were involved. Such files may contain witness interviews, maps, weather information, operational reports and analytical conclusions. Antela does not appear among the four Galician incidents identified in summaries of that collection, which begin with Ferrol in 1966 and continue with Lugo and Noia cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

That omission does not prove that the farmer invented his story. A rural civilian report could easily have failed to reach military channels, particularly if the witness concealed his identity. It does mean that descriptions of Antela as an officially investigated or declassified Spanish UFO case are unsupported by the accessible record. ## Why the testimony is difficult to test

Anonymity is understandable in a small rural community, where public ridicule could have serious social consequences. Refusing publicity is sometimes treated as evidence that a witness lacked a financial or attention-seeking motive. Yet anonymity also removes the checks that would allow readers to evaluate credibility. There is no way to establish the farmer’s age, occupation, eyesight, reputation, location on the night, familiarity with aircraft or history of telling unusual stories.

The twelve-day interval between the claimed encounter and publication adds another difficulty. That is not an exceptionally long delay, and the farmer reportedly spent part of it deciding whether to speak. Nevertheless, memory is not a recording. Repeatedly telling an experience, considering its meaning and receiving reactions from relatives can alter how it is later organised and described. Without a written statement made immediately after the event, it is impossible to separate an initial observation from later interpretation.

The account is also unusually elaborate. It does not merely describe an unidentified light or oddly shaped object; it supplies clothing, skin colour, hair, facial anatomy, speech, accent, telepathy, origin and purposeful behaviour. Detail can make a story feel convincing, but evidential value depends on whether the details were recorded independently and can be checked. In Antela, every component comes through the same unnamed narrator and the same newspaper article.

There are internal features that invite caution without decisively disproving the account. The farmer first interpreted the occupants as satellite travellers needing assistance, showing that contemporary spaceflight ideas shaped his understanding. The visitors conveniently spoke Spanish, used a recognisable regional accent and provided answers that were evocative but impossible to verify. Their statement that they came from beyond the stars conveyed distance without naming a location that could be tested.

None of these characteristics demonstrates a hoax. They are equally compatible with several possibilities: a sincere but substantially misinterpreted experience, a dreamlike or altered episode, a tale embellished during retelling, a practical joke, or a story deliberately constructed for the press. The evidence is too limited to select confidently among them.

## How UFO culture shaped the story

The newspaper itself placed Antela within a continuing stream of flying-saucer news. Its retrospective noted reports from Spain and abroad in 1962, including sightings over A Coruña, Buenos Aires and Catania, as well as publicity surrounding engineers who proposed launching a saucer-shaped craft. The Antela claim therefore appeared in an environment where readers were already familiar with discs, space visitors and mysterious aerial machines.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/amp/noticia/ourense/2023/09/24/campesino-narraba-encuentro-extraterrestres-limia/0003_202309O24C4991.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[La Voz de Galicia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">0003 202309O24C4991</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Voz de GaliciaEn 1963 un campesino de Ourense decía haber visto aterrizar un OVNI: «Hablé con los tres tripulantes»...</span></span></span>

That cultural context does not mean the farmer consciously copied another story. People interpret unfamiliar experiences using the concepts available to them. In earlier periods, a strange light or radiant figure might have been described in religious or folkloric terms. By 1963, satellites, astronauts and flying saucers offered a modern vocabulary. The farmer’s reported behaviour still reflected rural Catholic culture—he prayed and crossed himself—while his explanation drew on the new space age. The result was a story combining traditional fear of an apparition with contemporary ideas about technological visitors.

The alleged sample gathering is another recognisable space-age motif. The figures did not deliver a prophecy or ask for worship; they collected stones and soil like members of a scientific expedition. Their metallic clothing resembled aviation equipment, and the witness initially regarded them as travellers from a satellite. These details gave the tale a technological surface even though its central exchange—mind reading and answers about realms beyond the stars—remained closer to folklore or visionary experience than to a verifiable scientific contact.

Antela itself strengthened the story’s atmosphere. The lagoon was undergoing dramatic physical transformation as drainage and agricultural redevelopment altered one of Galicia’s best-known wetlands. It was also a landscape already associated with tales of a drowned city, submerged bells and other legends. Those traditions do not explain the 1963 report, but they show why an extraordinary encounter placed on the Antela plain could acquire a particularly durable local identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_de_Antela" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Laguna de AntelaLa laguna de Antela (Lagoa de Antela en gallego) es una laguna desecada en la década de los años 1950, situada en la coma...</span></span></span>

## Did later reporting strengthen the claim?

The 2023 anniversary article is valuable because it preserves extensive wording from the original report and identifies its publication date, author and presentation. As historical evidence that the story appeared in the press, it is strong. As evidence that the described encounter occurred, it adds almost nothing beyond the 1963 article. No witness name, family testimony, official correspondence, site investigation or forgotten photograph was recovered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/amp/noticia/ourense/2023/09/24/campesino-narraba-encuentro-extraterrestres-limia/0003_202309O24C4991.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[La Voz de Galicia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">0003 202309O24C4991</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Voz de GaliciaEn 1963 un campesino de Ourense decía haber visto aterrizar un OVNI: «Hablé con los tres tripulantes»...</span></span></span>

Later access to Spain’s declassified UFO files has indirectly weakened attempts to treat Antela as a substantial investigated case. Those archives demonstrate what a documented incident can look like: dates and locations, formal interviews, witness sketches, operational records, meteorological material and analytical comments. They also show that even cases with several witnesses or radar involvement may remain inconclusive because crucial information is missing. Antela begins from a much weaker evidential position.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

The story has not been debunked in the strict sense because no specific conventional event has been demonstrated. There is too little information to reconstruct the sky, identify an aircraft, examine the ground or test the witness’s condition. Calling it “unexplained” can therefore be misleading: the lack of an explanation results largely from a lack of data, not from the failure of an extensive investigation to eliminate ordinary possibilities.

## Credibility verdict

The Antela report is credible as a piece of Ourense’s newspaper and cultural history. A detailed story was published in September 1963, and its mixture of rural setting, religious reaction and space-age imagery makes it one of the province’s most memorable UFO narratives. The published account can be dated and attributed to a journalist even though its central witness cannot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/amp/noticia/ourense/2023/09/24/campesino-narraba-encuentro-extraterrestres-limia/0003_202309O24C4991.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[La Voz de Galicia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">0003 202309O24C4991</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Voz de GaliciaEn 1963 un campesino de Ourense decía haber visto aterrizar un OVNI: «Hablé con los tres tripulantes»...</span></span></span>

As an extraterrestrial encounter claim, however, it is weakly sourced. Its credibility is reduced by five combined problems: the witness remained anonymous; the location was imprecise; there was no independent observer; no physical or documentary evidence survived; and later reporting uncovered no new corroboration. None of those defects alone proves fabrication, but together they prevent meaningful verification.

The most defensible classification is **an unresolved anonymous testimony of very low evidential strength**. It should not be presented as a confirmed landing, a documented alien meeting or an official Spanish UFO case. Its lasting importance lies elsewhere: Antela shows how a single vivid newspaper account can become a provincial legend even when almost every route to checking it has disappeared.
