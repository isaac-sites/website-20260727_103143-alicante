## Introduction

The EVA-10 incident of 5 December 1989 is A Coruña’s strongest instrument-based UFO case, but it does not demonstrate that a single extraordinary craft crossed the province. The surviving military report records several real anomalies: civilian descriptions of an illuminated object, radar echoes at unusually high indicated altitudes, interference on emergency radio frequencies and later returns at three different flight levels. What it does not establish is that these observations shared one cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNIAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO DE...</span></span></span>

That distinction is central to the case. A radar return can represent an aircraft, but it can also arise from atmospheric propagation, ground clutter, equipment behaviour or a cooperative transponder signal whose relationship to the primary echo is unclear. A bright object near the horizon may meanwhile be astronomical or airborne without being responsible for anything seen on radar. The most cautious reading is therefore that EVA-10 documented a confusing sequence involving several sensors and witnesses, not a verified track of one unknown vehicle.

## The timeline of radar and visual reports

EVA-10 was not an ordinary observation post. Situated on Monte Iroite in the Serra do Barbanza, it formed part of Spain’s military air-surveillance network, collecting and transmitting radar information for air-defence command and control. The station became operational in 1984 and then used long-range surveillance and height-finding equipment, giving its operators considerably more technical capability than a civilian witness looking at an unexplained light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ejercitodelaireydelespacio.defensa.gob.es/EA/ejercitodelaire/es/Unidades/Unidad/a3a6e62d-7a3a-11ee-99f3-005056bf91c5/?path=%2Fsites%2Finternet.es%2F.content%2Funidad%2Funidad_00007.xml&amp;resourceId=a3a6e62d-7a3a-11ee-99f3-005056bf91c5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ejercitodelaireydelespacio.defensa.gob.es">[Ejército del Aire]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ejercitodelaireydelespacio.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ejército del Aire- Unidades</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidad- Unidades - Unidad Inicio Unidades Escuadrón de Vigilancia Aérea no10 (EVA 10) y Acuartelamiento Aéreo Barbanza MACOM Ver Todo Cua...</span></span></span>

The surviving file, numbered 891205, begins at 18:48 UTC. An air-surveillance officer reported an unidentified flying object somewhere in the broad zone between Santiago de Compostela and A Coruña. Two minutes later, EVA-10 contacted Santiago airport. The controller said that the Civil Guard had received a report of a possible unidentified object in the area between Castro and Sada. It was described second-hand as lens-shaped, moving slowly and displaying prominent lights whose brightness or colour appeared to change.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNIAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO DE...</span></span></span>

This opening account is less precise than it can sound in later retellings. The declassified file does not contain a full signed statement from the original civilian observer, a detailed Civil Guard interview, measured angles or an exact plotted position. The familiar description of a slow, illuminated disc therefore reached the military through a chain of communication rather than through direct observation by the radar crew. Secondary accounts claim that the initial report concerned a white light near the distant horizon, but the original witness documentation is absent from the public file.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.scribd.com/doc/109651105/Desclasificacion-OVNIs-Espana" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: es.scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">es.scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Desclasificacion OVNIs Espana</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Al mismo tiempo -sigue revelando el informe del EVA-10-, se detectan en la UPA-35 (pantalla usada normalmente para interceptaciones areas...</span></span></span>

At about 19:00, operators examining the relevant area saw a radar “blitz”, or echo, on displays identified as RAPPI and UPA-35. The report says it appeared in both primary and secondary radar, at a position recorded as approximately 060 degrees and 32 nautical miles, with an indicated height of 57,000 feet. The echo was repeatedly measured. Its horizontal position changed little, while its indicated altitude reportedly varied and reached 75,000 feet. Civil aircraft in the area did not provide visual confirmation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNIAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO DE...</span></span></span>

At 20:00, Santiago reported alarms or signals on the emergency frequencies 121.5 and 243.0 MHz, accompanied by interference and confused radio communications involving Santiago, Madrid’s area control centre and EVA-10, whose operational call sign was NÉCORA. These frequencies are reserved for civil and military distress or urgency traffic and are normally monitored by aviation and radar facilities. Their activation was operationally significant, but it did not by itself identify the transmitter or connect it to the earlier visual report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aip_html/chap6_section_3.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on faa.gov.</span></span></span>

At 20:40, direction-finding equipment placed the bearing of the alarm signal between 160 and 167 degrees from Santiago. Around the same time, the UPA-35 display showed three objects at flight levels 290, 320 and 420—approximately 29,000, 32,000 and 42,000 feet. The active tracking and telephone coordination ended at 21:02. Yet the height-finder return reportedly remained visible without meaningful variation until 08:30 the following morning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_coruna.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[J.J. That final detail is one of the strongest reasons to question a literal single-craft interpretation. A target that remained almost fixed on a height display for more than thirteen hours would require an extraordinary physical explanation. A persistent reflection, propagation effect, fixed environmental source or equipment-related return is generally more plausible than a vehicle hovering at extreme altitude through the night.

## What primary and secondary returns can mean

The phrase “detected on primary and secondary radar” is often treated as decisive proof that a solid, intelligently controlled object was present. Radar terminology is more complicated.

Primary radar sends out pulses and displays energy reflected from an object. It does not require the target’s cooperation: aircraft, terrain, precipitation and other reflective features can all produce returns under suitable conditions. Secondary surveillance radar works differently. It interrogates a transponder carried by an aircraft, which sends back coded information such as identity and pressure altitude. A normal secondary response therefore points towards functioning aviation equipment, not an unknown object with no conventional systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/technology/asr-11" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on faa.gov.</span></span></span>

The EVA-10 file does not preserve enough technical data to determine exactly what “primary and secondary” meant in this instance. It gives no transponder code, aircraft identity, raw plots, signal strength, scan-by-scan track, interrogation record or explanation of how the two displayed responses were associated. It is consequently impossible to tell from the released pages whether operators had one confidently correlated primary-and-secondary target, two nearby returns, a display-processing association or a transient secondary reply from ordinary traffic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNIAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO DE...</span></span></span>

The reported altitude changes also require caution. EVA-10’s period equipment included a long-range surveillance radar and a separate height-finding radar. A height reading was not equivalent to a modern three-dimensional digital track automatically validated across a network. The fact that the echo’s horizontal position scarcely changed while its indicated altitude shifted between 57,000 and 75,000 feet may describe genuine vertical motion, but it could also indicate difficulty measuring an intermittent or anomalously propagated return.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://aevyca.com.ar/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Ficha-Radar-AN-FPS-113.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aevyca.com.ar">[aevyca.com.ar]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aevyca.com.ar</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ficha Radar AN FPS 113</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DENOMINACION AN/FPS-113/90 TPX-42...</span></span></span>

Atmospheric conditions can bend radar energy away from its expected path. This phenomenon, known as anomalous propagation or ducting, may cause a beam to strike distant ground or other normally invisible surfaces and create unexpected or persistent blips. Official aviation guidance notes that temperature inversions can produce multiple extraneous targets, while precipitation, terrain and dense obstacles can reflect or screen radar energy. Moving-target filters reduce such effects but do not eliminate every anomalous return.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications//atpubs/aim_html/chap4_section_5.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on faa.gov.</span></span></span>

This mechanism fits several unusual features of the EVA-10 record: limited horizontal movement, unstable height measurements, the eventual appearance of several returns and the persistence of an echo until morning. It is not a proved solution because the released file contains no meteorological profile, maintenance log or raw radar recording with which to test it. It is nevertheless a conventional mechanism capable of producing the type of display behaviour described.

## One object or several unrelated events?

The most dramatic interpretation links every part of the evening into a single sequence. In that version, witnesses saw an illuminated craft near Sada; EVA-10 detected it at great altitude; the craft caused radio interference; direction finders located its emissions; and it later divided into three radar targets. Some UFO-oriented accounts present the incident in roughly this form, emphasising the apparent coordination between civilian observers, airport controllers and military radar operators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visionovni.com.ar/archivos/1019" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visionovni.com.ar">[visionovni.com.ar]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visionovni.com.ar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on com.ar.</span></span></span>

The chronology alone does not prove those links. The visual report concerned a slowly moving light or lens-shaped object in one broadly described area. The initial radar echo was plotted 32 nautical miles from EVA-10 at a bearing whose precise reference convention is not explained in the summary. The later radio signal was direction-found south or south-east of Santiago, while the three subsequent radar returns were at ordinary high-altitude flight levels. No released calculation demonstrates that these lines of position intersected, that the echoes moved together or that their timing described one continuous trajectory.

A multi-cause explanation therefore deserves serious consideration:

* **The visual report may have had an astronomical or ordinary aviation cause.** A bright planet low above the horizon can appear to change colour, pulsate or move because of atmospheric distortion and observer movement. Venus has been proposed in sceptical discussion of the case, although the incomplete witness statement prevents a conclusive reconstruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.scribd.com/document/460978495/desclasificacion-archivos-ovni-militares-al-descubierto-el-caso-espanol-11p" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: es.scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">es.scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Expediente 891205: embrollo radar en NÉCORA... 1989 en el EVA-10, el Escuadrón de Vigilancia Aérea denominado NÉCORA en clave y cuyas in...</span></span></span>

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **The high, nearly stationary radar echo may have been anomalous propagation or clutter.** Its changing height but limited horizontal displacement is not the behaviour expected of a normal fast-moving aircraft at 57,000–75,000 feet.
* **The three later targets may have been ordinary aircraft.** Flight levels 290, 320 and 420 fall within altitudes used by conventional high-level traffic. The released summary does not say whether these objects had flight plans, transponder identities or any connection to the first echo.
* **The emergency-frequency activation may have been separate interference or a false distress transmission.** Direction finding established a bearing, but the file supplies no message content, source identity or evidence that the transmitter occupied the radar position.

</div>

This fragmented interpretation is less spectacular, but it requires fewer unsupported assumptions. Complex incidents often acquire a single narrative because reports occurred close together in time and were handled by the same control network. Technical investigation must instead ask whether each dataset independently points to the same position, speed and altitude. The public EVA-10 record does not provide that correlation.

## Why the radio interference does not settle the case

The simultaneous activation of 121.5 and 243.0 MHz is one of the incident’s most intriguing details. The first is the principal civil aviation emergency frequency; the second serves the equivalent military role. Because 243.0 MHz is twice 121.5 MHz, equipment faults, harmonics or a transmitter capable of operating across both aviation bands must be considered alongside deliberate emergency transmission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aip_html/chap6_section_3.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on faa.gov.</span></span></span>

The file describes alarms and confused communications rather than a clear voice message from an unknown pilot. It does not say whether the signal was continuous, modulated, pulsed or recognisable as an emergency locator beacon. Nor does it record whether technicians checked ground transmitters, airborne radios or other installations for faults. The radiogoniometer produced only an azimuth range from Santiago, not a distance or three-dimensional location. One bearing cannot establish that a transmitter was attached to the radar echo.

The interference therefore adds complexity rather than proof. It confirms that air-traffic personnel were dealing with a real communications anomaly during the broader episode. It does not demonstrate that the anomaly was emitted by the object reported near Sada, by the first high-altitude return or by any of the three later targets.

## Gaps in the investigation

The case became public only after the original report was retrieved years later. The file includes correspondence from 1992 in which EVA-10 forwarded a copy of the duty operator’s account following a request from the Air Force’s operational command. It was declassified in 1996. The index and later catalogues show a remarkably small dossier—only a few pages for an episode involving several organisations and more than fourteen hours of reported display activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNIAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO DE...</span></span></span>

Several potentially decisive records are missing from the released material:

* the original civilian witness statement;

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the Civil Guard’s complete call log or report;
* Santiago controller notes in their original form;
* radar photographs, raw plots or magnetic recordings;
* transponder codes and flight-plan comparisons;
* weather soundings relevant to anomalous propagation;
* maintenance and interference checks;
* an exact map correlating the visual, radar and radio bearings;
* interviews with crews of aircraft asked to look for the target.

</div>

Secondary investigators have claimed that fuller controller notes once existed and contained additional detail, but such material has not been incorporated into the official public dossier in a form that allows independent technical checking. This weakens both the extraordinary and sceptical interpretations: neither side can reconstruct the event with the precision expected in a modern radar investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ojo-critico.blogspot.com/2007_08_12_archive.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ojo-critico.blogspot.com">[El Ojo Critico]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ojo-critico.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

The paperwork states that there was no reason to retain the case as classified. It offers no completed causal analysis and no conclusion joining the visual, radar and radio observations into one target.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1989-12-05_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNIAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO DE...</span></span></span>

## Why the incident remains technically unresolved

EVA-10 remains important because it contains more than a solitary light in the sky. Military personnel recorded unusual radar behaviour; civil aviation authorities dealt with interference on protected frequencies; and a separate witness report supplied an apparent visual component. Within A Coruña’s UFO history, no other public case combines these forms of evidence so closely.

Yet the case is unresolved mainly because the surviving dataset is too thin, not because the released evidence defeats ordinary physics. No raw technical record confirms extraordinary acceleration, a coherent flight path or a single object simultaneously observed by radar, radio direction finding and eyewitnesses. The long-lived stationary return and the uncorrelated appearance of three targets make a simple “one craft” account especially difficult to sustain.

The best overall assessment is that the incident probably combined several phenomena. A misidentified light, conventional high-altitude traffic, emergency-band interference and anomalous radar propagation could have overlapped during a confusing evening. That explanation remains provisional because the necessary logs and environmental data are missing. Conversely, describing EVA-10 as proof of an unknown advanced vehicle goes well beyond the evidence.

The mystery therefore lies less in what one object supposedly did than in whether the records describe one object at all. Until correlated radar tracks, transmitter data and original witness documentation emerge, EVA-10 should be classified as a technically unresolved multi-sensor episode whose separate elements were never securely connected.
