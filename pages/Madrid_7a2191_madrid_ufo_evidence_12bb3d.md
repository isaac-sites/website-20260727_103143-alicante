## Introduction

[Madrid]({{ 'madrids-ufo-history-between-hoax-and/' | relative_url }})’s UFO cases should not be judged by how famous, strange or widely reported they became. They should be judged by what information survives, whether that information was recorded at the time, whether independent sources agree, and whether ordinary explanations were properly tested. On that scale, Madrid offers examples at almost every level: the San José de Valderas photographs were bound to a documented deception; a December 1968 light was probably Venus; and the November 1979 Torrejón incident remains genuinely unresolved because military radar and an interceptor recorded anomalous contacts without identifying their cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telemadrid.es/programas/cuarto-de-maravillas/El-dia-que-Madrid-creyo-ver-un-ovni-el-caso-de-Aluche-y-San-Jose-de-Valderas-2-2868033230--20260306101300.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telemadrid.es">[telemadrid.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telemadrid.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de Valderas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de ValderasMarch 12, 2026 — El día que Madrid creyó ver un ov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 12, 2026</span></span></span>

“Unresolved” is therefore not another word for “extraterrestrial”. It normally means that the available evidence is insufficient to choose confidently between several possibilities. The strongest approach is to separate the existence of a report from the explanation of the event: a witness may be sincere, an official file may be authentic and a radar return may be real, while the object’s identity remains uncertain.

## A practical scale from explained to unresolved

Madrid’s cases are easiest to understand on a scale rather than through a simple choice between “true” and “false”. Four broad categories are useful.

**Explained cases** have enough information to match the observation with a familiar object or phenomenon. The Spanish Air Force file for 11 December 1968 records two telephone reports of a very bright light to the south-west or west of Madrid, with variations in brightness but no measurable movement, shape or size. The reviewing officer found no feature strange enough to rule out Venus and noted that no further investigation had been conducted. That is not absolute proof that Venus was responsible, but it is a strong explanation because the reported behaviour fits a bright planet and nothing in the file requires a more unusual cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-11_avistamiento_en_madrid.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI)AVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO D...</span></span></span>

**Probably explained cases** contain some uncertainty but still favour a conventional interpretation. On 15 May 1968, an object seen above Madrid through binoculars was described as three parallel bodies. It was not detected by the relevant radar, and Torrejón’s control tower considered it a sounding balloon. The Air Force’s later assessment gave substantial weight to a stratospheric-balloon explanation, partly because similar observations occurred during a French high-altitude balloon campaign.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-05-15_avistamiento_en_madrid-barcelona.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive AVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI)C</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">G. j-05-ABR. 1993-~ FECHA: -1968 I dia 15 de Mayo ENTRADA N. 0...• •• lf.~L...... **Discredited cases** have evidence of fabrication, manipulation or seriously unreliable sourcing. The 1967 San José de Valderas photographs belong here because their apparent strength came from their connection to the Ummo letters and symbol, yet the supposed physical and documentary evidence was produced within the same deceptive network. Madrid technician José Luis Jordán Peña later claimed authorship of the Ummo material, and recent local reporting presents the photographs and correspondence as a constructed affair rather than an independently documented encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ummo-sciences.org/fr/pdf/E53.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ummo-sciences.org">[Ummo Sciences]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ummo-sciences.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ummo SciencesE53 (FR)E53 (FR) 21/04/25 E53 Traduction AJH Dernière Modification: 09/04/2025 Titre de la lettre: Lettre 6 de Pena à Farr...</span></span></span>

**Unresolved cases** survive reasonable checks but still lack enough evidence for identification. The 28 November 1979 Torrejón event is Madrid’s clearest example. Ground controllers reported radar contacts, an F-4 Phantom was scrambled, and its crew recorded four fleeting radar returns and indications that another pulsed radar was illuminating the aircraft. Yet the crew never saw an object, the contacts were brief and diffuse, and parts of the supporting operational record were no longer available when the file was reconstructed in the 1990s. The case is stronger than a lone report of a light, but it does not establish the presence of an extraordinary craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-manises-1979-1979-11-11-17-28-avistamiento-en-valencia-motril-madrid-ii" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente Manises, Avistamientos Valencia–Motril–Madrid II (November 1979), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa ·...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1979</span></span></span>

This scale prevents two common errors. The first is treating every unidentified report as equally mysterious. The second is assuming that any remaining uncertainty supports the most dramatic interpretation. A case can be historically important yet evidentially weak, or technically interesting while still providing no proof of an exotic origin.

## How photographs, witnesses and radar differ

Different kinds of evidence answer different questions. A photograph may show what reached the camera, a witness describes what seemed to happen, and radar may register a reflecting or electronically detected target. None is automatically decisive.

### Photographs need provenance, not just clarity

A sharp image can still be misleading if investigators do not know who took it, where the original negative or file came from, whether frames are missing and whether the scene could have been staged. This is the central lesson of San José de Valderas.

The photographs looked persuasive because they showed a clearly defined disc above recognisable Madrid-area landmarks. Their connection to the Ummo symbol also appeared to turn a visual sighting into confirmation of an existing contact story. In reality, that narrative connection reduced their independence: the letters, predictions, symbol and photographs reinforced one another because they originated within the same circle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telemadrid.es/programas/cuarto-de-maravillas/El-dia-que-Madrid-creyo-ver-un-ovni-el-caso-de-Aluche-y-San-Jose-de-Valderas-2-2868033230--20260306101300.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telemadrid.es">[Telemadrid]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telemadrid.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de Valderas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de ValderasMarch 12, 2026 — El día que Madrid creyó ver un ov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 12, 2026</span></span></span>

Later technical arguments over possible suspension lines and the generation of the photographic copies illustrate another problem. Analyses of poor copies can produce uncertain or disputed conclusions, while incomplete sequences and unclear custody make reconstruction harder. One published account of a later Guardia Civil examination noted missing frames and uncertainty over the order in which the surviving negatives had been taken. Even without relying on a single claim about a visible thread, the combination of compromised provenance, missing material and Jordán Peña’s admission leaves the photographs with little value as evidence for an unknown aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ignaciodarnaude.es/ficha/analisis-tecnico-de-las-fotografias-de-san-jose-de-valderas-1-junio-1967/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ignaciodarnaude.es">[Ignacio Darnaude Rojas-Marcos.]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ignaciodarnaude.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">analisis tecnico de las fotografias de san jose de valderas 1 junio 1967</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pero trataré de no desviarme del asunto capital. ### Witness numbers do not equal independent confirmation

Several witnesses are generally better than one, but only when their accounts are genuinely separate. Ten people repeating a description after seeing the same newspaper photograph do not provide ten independent observations. Nor does a crowd automatically establish unusual movement, distance or size when everyone is looking at the same distant light without reliable reference points.

Witness evidence becomes stronger when statements were recorded promptly, before extensive press coverage; when observers viewed the event from different locations; and when their times, bearings and descriptions can be compared. It becomes weaker when testimony appears years later, changes after publicity or contains estimates that the witness had no practical means to make.

This matters especially in Madrid, where major incidents unfolded within a dense media environment. The Valderas images reached the evening press and gained prominent coverage, while the Ummo correspondence had already prepared followers to expect a craft bearing a particular symbol. Such advance expectation can shape how ambiguous observations are remembered and reported without implying that witnesses were dishonest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telemadrid.es/programas/cuarto-de-maravillas/El-dia-que-Madrid-creyo-ver-un-ovni-el-caso-de-Aluche-y-San-Jose-de-Valderas-2-2868033230--20260306101300.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telemadrid.es">[Telemadrid]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telemadrid.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de Valderas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de ValderasMarch 12, 2026 — El día que Madrid creyó ver un ov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 12, 2026</span></span></span>

The best question is therefore not simply “How many people saw it?” but “How many independent, contemporaneous and mutually consistent records survive?”

### Radar is technical evidence, not automatic proof

Radar can greatly strengthen a case because it may supply information beyond human vision. It can also produce false, misplaced or misunderstood returns through weather, propagation effects, interference, equipment behaviour and ordinary traffic. A radar case deserves higher confidence only when the original plots or recordings, equipment settings, calibration details, operator notes and comparisons with other sensors are preserved.

The Torrejón event is important because it involved more than a rumour about lights. The official file says that Pegaso received information about two unidentified objects above Madrid, Villatobas radar supplied positional information, and an alert F-4 spent about 50 minutes over the area. The crew reported four fleeting contacts, including elongated, diffuse echoes that disappeared on the next sweep, as well as two indications of illumination by a pulsed radar. The returns were intermittent, the reported altitude changed, and there was no visual or photographic corroboration. ## Why official files must be read carefully

The existence of a military file proves that an incident was reported or investigated. It does not prove that the military endorsed an extraordinary explanation.

Spain’s UFO archive was assembled and declassified so that surviving Air Force material could be consulted publicly. The official collection contains reports ranging from readily interpreted observations to cases that remained unidentified. Its value lies in preserving dates, communications, assessments and operational details—not in providing a certificate of authenticity for every claim within it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

Each document should be separated into three layers:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **The original observation:** what a witness, controller or aircrew reported at the time.
2. **The operational response:** what officials did, such as checking radar, contacting a tower or scrambling an aircraft.
3. **The later assessment:** what reviewers concluded during investigation or declassification.

</div>

These layers can differ sharply. In the December 1968 case, the original callers described a bright object, while the later reviewer considered Venus the likely answer. In the 1979 case, the original military response was substantial, but the surviving review did not identify the source of the contacts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-11_avistamiento_en_madrid.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI)AVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO D...</span></span></span>

Official language must also be interpreted precisely. “Unidentified” means that the available procedure did not produce an identification. “Scramble” means that an aircraft was ordered to investigate. “Radar contact” means that equipment registered a return or signal judged relevant by its operators. None of those expressions, alone or together, means “alien spacecraft”.

Equally, gaps in an official file should not automatically be treated as evidence of concealment. The Torrejón dossier records a routine document-destruction policy that could account for missing operational logs. Suspicion may be understandable when records are incomplete, but a mundane archival explanation is more defensible unless there is positive evidence that specific material was deliberately withheld.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-manises-1979-1979-11-11-17-28-avistamiento-en-valencia-motril-madrid-ii" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente Manises, Avistamientos Valencia–Motril–Madrid II (November 1979), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa ·...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1979</span></span></span>

## Why publicity can outgrow the evidence

Madrid’s most famous cases demonstrate that public reputation and evidence quality can move in opposite directions.

The Valderas photographs became memorable because they offered a dramatic object, a distinctive symbol, a suburban setting and an elaborate extraterrestrial story. Each element made the others more newsworthy. Yet the later exposure of the Ummo deception weakened precisely the material that had made the case famous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telemadrid.es/programas/cuarto-de-maravillas/El-dia-que-Madrid-creyo-ver-un-ovni-el-caso-de-Aluche-y-San-Jose-de-Valderas-2-2868033230--20260306101300.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telemadrid.es">[Telemadrid]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telemadrid.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de Valderas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de ValderasMarch 12, 2026 — El día que Madrid creyó ver un ov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 12, 2026</span></span></span>

By contrast, the December 1968 [Venus case]({{ 'venus-case/' | relative_url }}) is less memorable because it lacks spectacle. Its evidential lesson is nevertheless valuable: witnesses can honestly report an exceptionally bright or pulsating object while providing no movement, shape or range data that separates it from an astronomical source. The 1979 Torrejón case occupies the difficult middle ground. Its military setting and technical language can make it sound conclusive in retellings. A careful reading produces a narrower conclusion: something generated reports and intermittent sensor contacts that the surviving material does not satisfactorily identify. That is meaningful, but it is far less specific than claims about a giant craft, extraordinary manoeuvres or non-human technology.

Later reporting strengthens a historical UFO case only when it supplies new primary material, clearer chronology, independent witnesses or a technically testable explanation. Repetition, television treatment and dramatic summaries can increase familiarity without adding evidence. Fame is therefore best treated as a feature of a case’s cultural history, not as a measure of its truth.

## A Madrid evidence test

Readers can apply a straightforward set of questions to any Madrid sighting, whether it concerns a suburban photograph, a light near an airport or a military radar report.

**What is the earliest surviving record?** A statement, log or image made during the event is more useful than a polished recollection recorded decades later.

**Can time and direction be checked?** Exact times, bearings and observer positions allow comparisons with planets, aircraft, balloons, satellites, weather and other reports. Vague descriptions such as “late at night over Madrid” sharply limit investigation.

**Are the sources independent?** Witnesses who discussed the incident together, read the same press report or belonged to the same organised contact group may not provide separate confirmation.

**Does the evidence establish distance or speed?** A light without a known range can appear to race across the sky because of observer movement, camera motion or mistaken scale. Technical analysis requires geometry, not adjectives such as “huge” or “impossibly fast”.

**Are original images and records available?** Copies, cropped photographs, rewritten transcripts and summaries may omit exactly the information needed to test a claim.

**Were ordinary explanations tested fairly?** A convincing assessment should consider astronomical objects, aircraft, high-altitude balloons, atmospheric effects, radar behaviour and deliberate staging before invoking anything more unusual.

**Do multiple systems agree?** A visual observation supported by calibrated radar, photographs from known positions and independent records is much stronger than several ambiguous signals that cannot be aligned in time and space. NASA’s UAP study and current scientific proposals both emphasise multimodal measurement for this reason.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report-0.pdf?emrc=69d735b96d21b" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nasa.gov.</span></span></span>

**What does “unresolved” actually mean here?** It may mean that an event resisted extensive investigation. It may also mean that investigators lacked enough data to perform one. Those are not the same evidential result.

## Which Madrid cases deserve belief?

The evidence supports belief that Madrid has experienced sincere unusual sightings, mistaken identifications, organised deception and at least one notable military incident that cannot now be fully reconstructed. It does not support treating these categories as interchangeable.

The San José de Valderas photographs should be regarded as discredited evidence because their provenance is inseparable from the Ummo deception. The December 1968 report is best treated as probably astronomical, with Venus fitting the limited description. The November 1979 Torrejón episode deserves continued historical and technical interest because military personnel and equipment recorded something they did not identify, but the absence of a visual target, complete original records and stable sensor data prevents a stronger conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telemadrid.es/programas/cuarto-de-maravillas/El-dia-que-Madrid-creyo-ver-un-ovni-el-caso-de-Aluche-y-San-Jose-de-Valderas-2-2868033230--20260306101300.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telemadrid.es">[telemadrid.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telemadrid.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de Valderas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de ValderasMarch 12, 2026 — El día que Madrid creyó ver un ov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 12, 2026</span></span></span>

That distinction is the most important guide to Madrid’s UFO history. A debunked photograph is not rescued by its fame. A probable planet does not become mysterious because officials opened a file. A radar incident does not become extraterrestrial because it remains unexplained. The fairest judgement is the narrowest one that the surviving evidence can securely support.
