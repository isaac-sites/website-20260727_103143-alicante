## Introduction

The evidence therefore falls into sharply different categories. Some Madrid cases are weak because they consist only of brief observations of lights. Others became famous despite evidence of staged photographs and invented correspondence. The November 1979 incident is more difficult to dismiss because military radar operators and an aircrew recorded anomalous contacts, yet no solid object was seen and the surviving file does not establish what caused the returns. Madrid matters within Spanish UFO history precisely because it shows the full range from probable misidentification to documented but unresolved aviation events.

## Aluche, Valderas and the Ummo deception

Madrid’s best-known UFO story began in the western suburbs during the 1960s. Reports from Aluche in 1966 were followed by a far more publicised episode at San José de Valderas, near Alcorcón, on 1 June 1967. Photographs showed a disc-shaped object marked underneath with a distinctive symbol resembling a capital H crossed by an additional vertical stroke. The photographs appeared to support an already developing contact story known as Ummo. Recipients in Madrid and elsewhere had begun receiving lengthy typed letters supposedly written by visitors from a planet orbiting the star Wolf 424. The documents mixed technical language, philosophy, social theory and predictions of future appearances. Some defenders have argued that fraudulent photographs do not automatically disprove every witness report from the area. They point to people who said they had seen a disc independently of the camera. That is logically possible, but it does not rescue the core claim. The most striking material—the photographs, symbol and letters—was controlled by people involved in the deception, while surviving witness testimony is inconsistent, retrospective and difficult to separate from the publicity surrounding the event. The later admissions substantially weakened rather than strengthened the original case.

The Valderas affair remains important for three reasons. First, it demonstrates how photographs can acquire authority before their chain of custody is properly examined. Secondly, it shows how advance predictions can create an audience primed to interpret an ambiguous object in a particular way. Finally, it reveals the role of Madrid’s newspapers and UFO discussion circles in turning a local spectacle into an internationally known narrative. The case is historically significant, but the evidence for an extraordinary craft is poor.

## The official file that pointed to Venus

A much less dramatic Madrid incident occurred on 11 December 1968. According to the Spanish Air Force file, two callers reported an exceptionally bright light to the south-west or west of the city, one at about 8.05 pm and another around 8.30 pm. They described changes or flashes in brightness but reported no clear shape, size or appreciable movement. No detailed follow-up investigation was recorded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-11_avistamiento_en_madrid.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI)AVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO D...</span></span></span>

When the material was reviewed for declassification, the Air Force intelligence assessment found too little unusual information to justify treating the observation as genuinely anomalous. It proposed Venus as the likely explanation. A bright planet low in the sky can appear to shimmer, pulse or change colour when its light passes through unstable layers of atmosphere, particularly when viewed over a city horizon. The official assessment also suggested that widespread public interest in UFOs during 1968 may have encouraged witnesses to report an otherwise ordinary celestial object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-11_avistamiento_en_madrid.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI)AVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO D...</span></span></span>

This file is valuable not because the sighting was spectacular, but because it illustrates how the Air Force assessed weak reports. The record contains a location, approximate times and brief witness descriptions, yet lacks angular measurements, duration, photographs, radar confirmation or evidence of unusual movement. “Unidentified” at the moment of the telephone call did not mean inexplicable after review.

The case should therefore be regarded as plausibly explained, not unresolved in any strong sense. It also cautions against treating every item in Spain’s declassified UFO archive as evidence that military investigators encountered something extraordinary. The archive preserves reports of varying quality, including cases that officials themselves considered ordinary astronomical misidentifications.

## The 1979 Torrejón radar scramble

The strongest Madrid-centred official case occurred during Spain’s intense wave of UFO reporting in November 1979. It forms part of the same broad sequence as the famous Manises aviation incident, although the Madrid episode was operationally separate. During the early hours of 28 November, the Pegaso air-defence control centre received information that two unidentified objects were being observed over Madrid. Radar information from the Villatobas area led to the launch of an alert F-4 Phantom from Torrejón Air Base at approximately 03.30 UTC.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-manises-1979-1979-11-11-17-28-avistamiento-en-valencia-motril-madrid-ii" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente Manises, Avistamientos Valencia–Motril–Madrid II (November 1979), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa ·...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1979</span></span></span>

The interceptor climbed to 35,000 feet and was directed towards a reported contact initially placed much higher. The crew saw no unknown object despite good visibility, but reported several short-lived radar returns. The aircraft’s radar detected individual and multiple diffuse echoes that disappeared on subsequent sweeps. The crew also recorded indications on its radar-warning equipment suggesting illumination by a pulsed radar source. According to the report, some contacts corresponded broadly with directions supplied by ground control, including positions near the vertical of Madrid and Barajas.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1979-11-11-17-28_avistamiento_en_valencia-motril-madrid-ii.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1979 11 11 17 28 avistamiento en valencia motril madrid ii</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">C.· de-. This is more substantial than an unsupported light report because it involved trained military personnel, ground control, airborne radar and an actual interception. Nevertheless, the evidence has serious limitations:

* No unidentified craft was visually acquired.
* The radar contacts were brief, diffuse and unstable.
* The file does not demonstrate that every ground and airborne return came from the same physical target.
* Radar-warning indications establish exposure to radar energy, not the presence of an exotic vehicle.
* Some operational records were no longer available when the incident was reconstructed for later review. The file explains that routine logs could be destroyed under standing retention rules, leaving gaps in the surviving documentary trail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-manises-1979-1979-11-11-17-28-avistamiento-en-valencia-motril-madrid-ii" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente Manises, Avistamientos Valencia–Motril–Madrid II (November 1979), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa ·...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1979</span></span></span>

Possible conventional causes include anomalous radar propagation, interference, sidelobe effects, equipment artefacts, miscorrelation of separate targets or reflections associated with ordinary aircraft. The surviving documents do not contain enough technical data to select confidently among these possibilities. Equally, it would be too dismissive to claim that nothing happened: the F-4 crew recorded genuine instrument indications during a mission launched in response to air-defence reports.

The most defensible classification is therefore unresolved but evidentially limited. Something produced unusual radar information, yet the documentation does not establish a solid, structured object, let alone an extraterrestrial craft. This distinction is central to interpreting military UFO records: an unexplained sensor event is evidence of uncertainty, not proof of an extraordinary origin.

## Barajas, Torrejón and Madrid’s aviation setting

Madrid naturally generated aviation-related UFO reports because it combined several features in a small area: a major civil airport at Barajas, the military base at Torrejón, busy air corridors and air-defence radar coverage. An unusual light over the capital could therefore be reported simultaneously by residents, pilots, controllers and military observers, increasing the chance that an ambiguous event would generate an official paper trail.

The declassified November 1979 dossier also lists nearby or related Air Force incidents, including earlier Madrid observations and a 25 February 1980 report from a military aircraft carrying Prime Minister Adolfo Suárez on a flight from Germany to Madrid. The surviving summary says that unusual lights were observed by pilots and passengers, but the available material is too sparse to make the episode a strong standalone case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://conexaoufo.com/en/spain-declassifies-top-secret-ufo-files/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: conexaoufo.com">[conexaoufo.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">conexaoufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spain Declassifies Top Secret UFO Files</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Spain Declassifies Top Secret UFO Files</span></span></span>

Aviation involvement can improve the quality of a report because professional observers understand aircraft behaviour and have access to instruments. It does not make a sighting automatically reliable. Pilots can misjudge the distance of lights at night; radar systems can display false or distorted returns; controllers may combine reports that do not refer to the same source. The best cases require independent records with precise times, bearings, altitudes, weather conditions and equipment data. Much of that material is absent from the older Madrid files.

Torrejón also played an administrative role in Spanish UFO history. Air Force intelligence staff there reviewed a number of cases during the declassification process, while copies of the wider collection were deposited in the Air Force’s central library in Madrid in 1992. The digitised archive now allows the public to compare original reports with later summaries rather than relying solely on books or press retellings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

## Press coverage amplified both mystery and error

Madrid’s media environment was crucial to the growth of its UFO stories. The Valderas photographs became influential because they were quickly reproduced in a prominent newspaper, where the image, witness claims and Ummo narrative appeared to reinforce one another. Once the case had acquired a public identity, later doubts had to compete with an already memorable visual story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telemadrid.es/programas/cuarto-de-maravillas/El-dia-que-Madrid-creyo-ver-un-ovni-el-caso-de-Aluche-y-San-Jose-de-Valderas-2-2868033230--20260306101300.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telemadrid.es">[Telemadrid]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telemadrid.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de ValderasEl día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Al...</span></span></span>

This pattern was not unique to Madrid, but the capital’s concentration of newspapers, publishers, broadcasters, investigators and government institutions made it especially powerful. A report made in or near Madrid could move rapidly from local testimony to national coverage. Officials, enthusiasts and sceptics then responded to the media version, which was often more coherent and dramatic than the original evidence.

The historical press remains useful, but it should be read as a record of what was reported rather than as automatic confirmation that an event occurred as described. Madrid’s Municipal Newspaper Library provides searchable access to large collections of digitised periodicals, including material that can help establish publication dates, changing descriptions and whether supposed witness details appeared immediately or only in later retellings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecas.madrid.es/portales/bibliotecas/es/Informacion-y-servicios/Busca-tu-biblioteca/Hemeroteca-Municipal/?idCapitulo=5417693&amp;vgnextchannel=200b8e7a1f1d3510VgnVCM1000008a4a900aRCRD&amp;vgnextfmt=default&amp;vgnextoid=b8c9ba1b6bd7d010VgnVCM1000000b205a0aRCRD" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecas.madrid.es">[bibliotecas.madrid.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecas.madrid.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hemeroteca Municipal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hemeroteca Municipal</span></span></span>

For the Valderas case in particular, chronology matters. Claims that appeared after the photographs became famous deserve less weight than statements documented before publication. The same principle applies to later anniversary articles: they are valuable for tracing the story’s cultural survival but cannot replace original photographs, negatives, witness interviews or contemporary official records.

## What the declassified files do—and do not—prove

Spain’s Air Force compiled reports of unusual aerial phenomena from 1962 to 1995. The resulting collection contains roughly 80 files and about 1,900 pages, covering incidents of very different quality. The records were progressively declassified during the 1990s and later made available through the Ministry of Defence’s virtual library.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/details/SpanishUFOFiles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[archive.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archive.org.</span></span></span>

For Madrid, the archive is valuable because it confirms that certain reports reached military channels. It preserves dates, operational messages, witness summaries and official judgements. In the 1968 case, the judgement favoured Venus. In the 1979 case, the documentation confirms an interceptor launch and anomalous radar contacts but does not identify their cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-11_avistamiento_en_madrid.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI)AVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO D...</span></span></span>

Declassification does not mean that a file contains a verified unknown craft. Documents may be released because they no longer affect national security, not because investigators solved the event or endorsed an extraordinary interpretation. Some files contain little more than forwarded letters or telephone reports. Others preserve operational data but lack the calibration records, weather analysis or full radar logs needed for a modern technical reconstruction.

The archive also reflects the limits of record-keeping. Some incidents were written up long after the event, and routine logs could be destroyed according to normal retention schedules. Gaps of this kind can encourage suspicions of concealment, but missing paperwork is not evidence that extraordinary information was removed. In the 1979 Madrid dossier, the Air Force explicitly discussed incomplete records and the concern that journalists’ access to scattered information had created an impression that the institution was withholding material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-manises-1979-1979-11-11-17-28-avistamiento-en-valencia-motril-madrid-ii" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente Manises, Avistamientos Valencia–Motril–Madrid II (November 1979), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa ·...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1979</span></span></span>

## How Madrid’s main cases should be rated

Madrid’s better-known reports do not all deserve the same label.

**Aluche and San José de Valderas, 1966–67:** historically important but substantially discredited. The staged photographs and fabricated Ummo correspondence undermine the central evidential package. Residual witness claims are too poorly controlled to overturn the fraud finding.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telemadrid.es/programas/cuarto-de-maravillas/El-dia-que-Madrid-creyo-ver-un-ovni-el-caso-de-Aluche-y-San-Jose-de-Valderas-2-2868033230--20260306101300.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telemadrid.es">[telemadrid.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telemadrid.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Aluche y San José de ValderasEl día que Madrid creyó ver un ovni: el caso de Al...</span></span></span>

**Madrid light, 11 December 1968:** probably explained. The observations involved a stationary or nearly stationary bright light in the direction where Venus was considered a plausible source. The report lacks features that would make the sighting strongly anomalous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-11_avistamiento_en_madrid.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI)AVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO D...</span></span></span>

**Torrejón interception, 28 November 1979:** unresolved, but not proof of a craft. The military response and radar contacts make it a serious historical incident, yet the absence of visual confirmation and the fleeting, diffuse nature of the returns leave conventional radar or correlation problems as credible possibilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-manises-1979-1979-11-11-17-28-avistamiento-en-valencia-motril-madrid-ii" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente Manises, Avistamientos Valencia–Motril–Madrid II (November 1979), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa ·...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1979</span></span></span>

**Other Madrid aviation and light reports:** generally weak unless supported by complete contemporary records. Brief summaries mentioning pilots, political passengers or numerous observers may sound impressive, but their value depends on whether independent technical documentation survives.

This ranking avoids two common errors. The first is assuming that every unexplained report is equally mysterious. The second is dismissing every military case because famous civilian photographs were fraudulent. Madrid contains both clear deception and genuine operational uncertainty; they should not be merged into a single verdict.

## Madrid’s place in Spanish UFO history

Madrid did not produce Spain’s strongest photographic or physical evidence of an unknown vehicle. Its importance lies elsewhere. The province became a meeting point for witnesses, newspapers, UFO investigators, military institutions and one of Europe’s most elaborate contact hoaxes. It also generated a rare documented interception in which air-defence operators and an F-4 crew encountered radar information they could not readily resolve.

Later evidence has weakened Madrid’s most celebrated case. The Valderas photographs no longer stand as credible support for the Ummo story, and the supposed extraterrestrial correspondence is best understood as an extended human fabrication. The 1968 file likewise became less mysterious after official reviewers identified Venus as a plausible explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/download/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-11_avistamiento_en_madrid.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveAVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI)AVISTAMIENTO DE FENOMENOS EXTRAÑOS (EXPEDIENTES OVNI) AVISTAMIENTO D...</span></span></span>

The 1979 Torrejón episode remains the province’s most interesting unresolved incident, but its significance should be stated carefully. It documents an air-defence anomaly, not an alien encounter. Without stable radar tracks, visual confirmation, preserved raw data or evidence of a physical object, the case cannot bear the extraordinary conclusions sometimes placed upon it.

Madrid’s UFO history is therefore most useful as a lesson in evidence quality. Famous images may be manufactured; sincere witnesses may misidentify astronomical objects; military systems may produce authentic but ambiguous anomalies. The responsible conclusion is neither that every report was meaningless nor that unexplained cases confirm extraordinary craft. It is that Madrid’s record contains a small number of genuinely interesting uncertainties surrounded by much stronger examples of suggestion, publicity, ordinary misidentification and deliberate deception.

  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
