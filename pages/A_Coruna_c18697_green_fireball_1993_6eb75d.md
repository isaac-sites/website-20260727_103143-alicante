## Introduction

The fast green object seen from the EVA-10 military air-surveillance station in A Corua on 23 November 1993 was almost certainly a bright meteor, or fireball. Five servicemen independently described a green luminous core, a white-yellow trail, an extremely rapid straight flight and a total duration of only three or four seconds. Those features fit a meteoroid burning up in the atmosphere far better than an aircraft or controlled vehicle.

The case still matters because it was not a casual report. It generated a 124-page military file, witness interviews and a formal inquiry. ## The witnesses and three-second flight path

The sighting occurred at about 08:15 local time at EVA-10, the air-surveillance station on Monte Iroite near Noia. The head of the station and four other members of the surveillance unit saw the phenomenon from the entrance-control area and nearby positions. Contemporary summaries describe the witnesses as a lieutenant colonel, a warrant officer, two sergeants and a soldier.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.bluebookfiles.org/pdfs/1993.00%20-%20NARA%20-%20SpanishUFOFiles%20-%201993-11-23_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.bluebookfiles.org">[files.bluebookfiles.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.bluebookfiles.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1993.00 NARA SpanishUFOFiles 1993 11 23 avistamiento en eva 10 noya (coruna</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jefe del EVA 10 remite informe a CJMOA con los datos del... sobre el horizonte de 20 a 30 y velocidad vertiginosa, pudieratratarse de...</span></span></span>

Their central descriptions were notably consistent. They reported a luminous trace with a green core and a white or yellow-white glowing trail. It crossed the sky in a straight line at what they regarded as a vertiginous speed, remained visible for only three or four seconds and appeared roughly 20 to 30 degrees above the horizon. Later press accounts say it travelled towards the Atlantic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.bluebookfiles.org/pdfs/1993.00%20-%20NARA%20-%20SpanishUFOFiles%20-%201993-11-23_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.bluebookfiles.org">[bluebookfiles.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.bluebookfiles.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1993.00 NARA SpanishUFOFiles 1993 11 23 avistamiento en eva 10 noya (coruna</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jefe del EVA 10 remite informe a CJMOA con los datos del... sobre el horizonte de 20 a 30 y velocidad vertiginosa, pudieratratarse de...</span></span></span>

Those details are more important than the witnesses military status. Trained observers may be better than average at recognising familiar aircraft, but a fireball is an uncommon, sudden event that offers almost no time for measurement. In three seconds, even experienced personnel cannot reliably judge an unknown lights distance, size or physical speed. What they can report more securely is its apparent colour, direction, duration and path precisely the features that led the investigating officer towards a meteor explanation.

The event also differed sharply from EVA-10s better-known 1989 episode. The earlier case involved a slow-looking illuminated object and radar returns over a longer period. The 1993 object was a brief visual streak with no reported manoeuvres, hovering or prolonged radar behaviour. Treating both as a single unexplained military flap obscures the substantial difference between them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/amp/noticia/ferrol/ferrol/2016/10/22/defensa-situa-ferrol-primer-avistamiento-ovni-documentado-galicia/00031477132195756887939.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdegalicia.es">[La Voz de Galicia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdegalicia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">La Voz de Galicia Los ovnis que el Ejrcito investig en Galicia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El avistamiento fue de unos 3 o 4 segundos de duracin, con una trayectoria rectilnea, una elevacin sobre el horizonte de 20 a 30 grado...</span></span></span>

## Why the colour and trail suggest a fireball

A fireball is simply an unusually bright meteor: the visible effect produced when a meteoroid enters the atmosphere at high speed, heats the surrounding gas and loses material through ablation. Such objects can cross a large section of sky in seconds and leave a luminous trail. They need not belong to a recognised meteor shower; isolated, or sporadic, fireballs occur throughout the year.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://imo.net/docs/02fireballs.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: imo.net">[International Meteor Organization]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">imo.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Meteor Organization</span></span></span>

The EVA-10 report contains four characteristics that strongly favour this mechanism:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Very short duration.** Three or four seconds is entirely compatible with a bright atmospheric entry but unusually brief for an aircraft sighting.
* **A straight, uninterrupted path.** The witnesses reported no turns, stops, acceleration changes or return movement.
* **Extreme apparent speed.** Meteors enter the atmosphere at many kilometres per second and can appear to shoot across the sky almost instantaneously.
* **A bright core and separate trail.** A concentrated luminous head followed by an incandescent or ionised wake is a familiar fireball appearance.

</div>

The timing strengthens the comparison. The sighting occurred shortly after 08:00 during a clear morning. The International Meteor Organizations technical handbook notes that observers are more likely to encounter faster and potentially brighter meteors in the morning sector because Earth is then turning into the direction of its orbital motion. That does not identify this particular object by itself, but it shows that a rapid morning fireball is not an anomalous proposition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://imo.net/docs/02fireballs.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: imo.net">[International Meteor Organization]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">imo.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Meteor Organization</span></span></span>

Green is not evidence against a meteor. Instrumentally observed fireballs are frequently described as blue-green or green. The colour can result from atmospheric emissions and from elements in the meteoroid, while perceived colour also varies with brightness, viewing conditions and human vision. The American Meteor Society, for example, has documented intense green fireballs and associates such colouring in some events with excited atmospheric oxygen. NASA camera networks have likewise recorded confirmed meteors with a bluish-green appearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.amsmeteors.org/2014/09/major-fireball-over-oregon-washington-and-british-columbia-september-13th-2014/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: amsmeteors.org">[American Meteor Society]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">amsmeteors.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">major fireball over oregon washington and british columbia september 13th 2014</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">major fireball over oregon washington and british columbia september 13th 2014</span></span></span>

It would be too strong to infer the objects chemical composition from the witnesses colour descriptions. Without a spectrum, calibrated photography or recovered material, green is only a visual characteristic. It supports the meteor interpretation because green fireballs are well established, but it cannot reveal precisely what the body contained.

## A long investigation of a very short event

The contrast between the sightings duration and the size of its file is striking. The declassified register lists case 01-MACEN-93 as 124 pages, while many other Spanish military UFO files are far shorter. Much of that bulk arose from administrative correspondence, detailed questionnaires and separate statements from the five witnesses rather than from additional physical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ovniteca.net/sites/default/files/2024-08/listado_de_expedientes.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ovniteca.net">[Ovniteca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ovniteca.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">listado de expedientes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">67 &#124; 29/10/96 &#124; 01-MACEN-93 &#124; 23/11/93 &#124; ADC &#124; EVA 10 &#124; 124 &#124; 168 &#124; 11/11/96 &#124; 01-MAEST-95 &#124; 23-26/02/95 &#124; ADC &#124; MORN &#124; 53 &#124; 2...</span></span></span>

The station commander reported the event the following day. Military authorities then appointed an investigating officer, obtained testimony from all five observers and reviewed the aspects considered relevant. The formal summary concluded that the three-to-four-second, rectilinear, extremely fast object could have been a meteorite. In ordinary astronomical language, meteor or fireball would be more exact, because a meteorite is material that survives to reach the ground; the file contains no confirmed fall or recovered fragment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.bluebookfiles.org/pdfs/1993.00%20-%20NARA%20-%20SpanishUFOFiles%20-%201993-11-23_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.bluebookfiles.org">[files.bluebookfiles.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.bluebookfiles.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1993.00 NARA SpanishUFOFiles 1993 11 23 avistamiento en eva 10 noya (coruna</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jefe del EVA 10 remite informe a CJMOA con los datos del... sobre el horizonte de 20 a 30 y velocidad vertiginosa, pudieratratarse de...</span></span></span>

The inquiry apparently found no feature requiring an exotic vehicle. There was no reported structured body, controlled manoeuvre, sustained presence or interaction with the station. Nor does the surviving summary identify a matching radar track, photograph, video or instrument record. The positive evidence therefore consists almost entirely of five mutually supportive visual accounts.

Multiple witnesses make it highly likely that a real luminous event occurred. They do not, however, establish its distance or nature. People standing close together share nearly the same viewing geometry and environmental conditions, so their agreement confirms the appearance of the event more strongly than it confirms an extraordinary interpretation.

## What officially unexplained actually means

The 1993 case is sometimes grouped loosely with unexplained military UFOs because it appears in Spains declassified collection of reports concerning strange aerial phenomena. That label can be misleading. A UFO file is an administrative category: it records that something was unidentified when reported. It does not mean that investigators ultimately judged the object to be an unknown craft.

In this instance, the military conclusion was explicitly conventional. The investigating officer considered a meteor plausible, and the higher command issued an assessment separating the event from an inexplicable occurrence. It also requested that the file be treated as unclassified because the object was probably a meteor. Later case catalogues have consequently listed its assessment simply as meteor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.bluebookfiles.org/pdfs/1993.00%20-%20NARA%20-%20SpanishUFOFiles%20-%201993-11-23_avistamiento_en_eva-10_noya_%28coruna%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.bluebookfiles.org">[files.bluebookfiles.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.bluebookfiles.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1993.00 NARA SpanishUFOFiles 1993 11 23 avistamiento en eva 10 noya (coruna</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Jefe del EVA 10 remite informe a CJMOA con los datos del... sobre el horizonte de 20 a 30 y velocidad vertiginosa, pudieratratarse de...</span></span></span>

There is still a modest difference between **probable** and **proved**. No multi-station photographs were available to reconstruct the atmospheric trajectory, and no recovered meteorite was tied to the sighting. Fireball specialists normally require observations from separated stations, accurate timing and imaging before calculating a precise path, altitude and orbit. A single-site visual report, however detailed, cannot provide that level of confirmation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://imo.net/docs/02fireballs.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: imo.net">[International Meteor Organization]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">imo.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Meteor Organization</span></span></span>

The defensible classification is therefore **plausibly explained, but not instrumentally identified**. The surviving evidence strongly supports a fireball and supplies no persuasive reason to invoke an artificial vehicle. The residual uncertainty concerns which meteoroid produced the light and its exact trajectory, not whether an alien or secret craft was present.

## The lesson for A Coruas UFO history

The green object is one of the provinces best examples of the difference between credible witnesses and an extraordinary cause. The servicemen appear to have accurately reported a startling event. Their reliability strengthens the case that a brilliant green streak crossed the morning sky; it does not weaken the natural explanation.

It also shows why the word military should not be treated as a shortcut to mystery. Military procedures can preserve dates, statements and sighting geometry that would otherwise be lost. In this case, that documentation made the mundane interpretation stronger by revealing a classic fireball pattern: a luminous head, trailing glow, straight path, exceptional speed and disappearance within seconds.

Within A Coruas wider UFO record, the 1993 report is consequently less mysterious than the incomplete Ferrol sighting of 1966 or the complicated EVA-10 radar episode of 1989. Its chief historical value lies not in remaining unexplained, but in demonstrating how a well-documented military UFO report can survive in an official archive even when the best explanation is an ordinary though spectacular natural event.
