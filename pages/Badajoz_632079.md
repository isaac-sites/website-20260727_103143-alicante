## Introduction

The fairest conclusion is that Badajoz has one genuinely unusual but evidentially weak military encounter, one [radar case]({{ 'radar-case/' | relative_url }}) probably caused by false returns, one late civilian sighting weakened by evidence of fireworks, and a fourth file whose geographical classification is confusing. The documents show that reports were recorded and sometimes investigated; they do not establish extraterrestrial visitation.

This number needs careful interpretation. It represents catalogue entries, not four equally well-supported UFO events. The files vary considerably in witness quality, technical content and the extent to which ordinary explanations were considered.

There is also a geographical complication. Montánchez is in neighbouring Cáceres province, although the official file is titled as an observation over “Montánchez (Badajoz)” and is indexed under both Badajoz and Cáceres. That appears to be an archival or location-label problem rather than evidence that the event occurred within modern Badajoz provincial boundaries. For a strictly province-level history, it is better treated as a cross-border aviation case than as a central Badajoz incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/en/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160068009" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

The remaining three files form a clearer provincial sequence. They begin with unexplained radar returns at the Talavera base, reach their most dramatic point with the alleged luminous figure of 1976, and end with a civilian report from Usagre that investigators could compare with a known local celebration.

## Talavera’s 1975 radar contacts

On the evening of 14 January 1975, a ground-controlled approach radar operator at Talavera la Real reportedly observed seven or eight mobile echoes between approximately 19:15 and 19:40 UTC. The returns appeared to alter speed, overlap and respond in different identification modes. A known aircraft was also present in the wider area, but no accompanying aircraft was visually confirmed. The six-page military file was declassified in October 1993.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160069013" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

At first glance, the report sounds impressive because it involves radar rather than a distant light seen by an untrained observer. Radar evidence, however, is only as reliable as the equipment, operating conditions and supporting observations. In this case there was no confirmed visual sighting, and the system being used could not directly determine the alleged objects’ altitude. The suggestion that they were above 25,000 feet was therefore an inference rather than a measurement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1975-01-14_avistamiento_en_talavera_la_real.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1975 01 14 avistamiento en talavera la real</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">750114 RESUMEN: El Controlador de Servicio de la pantalla de G.C.A. de la B.A. de Talavera observa, desde las 19:15Z hasta las 19:40Z ap...</span></span></span>

The military analysis identified two established causes of misleading radar returns. One was the reception of delayed replies from aircraft beyond the instrument’s normal range, producing so-called second-time-around targets. The other was a temperature inversion, in which layers of air at different temperatures bend radio waves and create anomalous propagation or false echoes. The report noted that such conditions were common during January and that experienced controllers had encountered convincing-looking returns that pilots could not see.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1975-01-14_avistamiento_en_talavera_la_real.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1975 01 14 avistamiento en talavera la real</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">750114 RESUMEN: El Controlador de Servicio de la pantalla de G.C.A. de la B.A. de Talavera observa, desde las 19:15Z hasta las 19:40Z ap...</span></span></span>

That does not prove exactly which electronic or atmospheric effect produced every trace. It does substantially weaken the idea that the radar recorded extraordinary vehicles. Without simultaneous visual confirmation, reliable height data or independent tracking from another station, the 1975 incident is best classified as technically interesting but probably explained by radar artefacts.

## The 1976 Talavera air-base encounter

The event that made Badajoz prominent in Spanish UFO literature occurred before dawn on 12 November 1976. José María Trejo and Juan Carrizosa were guarding the fuel area at Talavera la Real Air Base when they reportedly heard a shrill, intrusive sound, sometimes described as resembling radio interference before becoming painfully high-pitched. Believing someone might have entered the restricted area, they began checking the surroundings and were joined by another serviceman.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://prensamercosur.org/2026/05/25/el-expediente-ovni-de-talavera-la-real-que-todavia-desconcierta-al-ejercito-del-aire-espanol/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prensamercosur.org">[Prensa Mercosur]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prensamercosur.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noticias Una guardia rutinaria que terminó con disparos dentro de la base El expediente desclasificado y las dudas sobre lo que ocurrió...</span></span></span>

The later account states that the men encountered a luminous, roughly human-shaped figure near the perimeter. It was described as greenish, perhaps three metres tall, with long arms, a small or helmet-like head and a surface apparently composed of points of light. Trejo said that he experienced dizziness or an inability to react normally, while his companions fired their submachine guns. The figure allegedly brightened and disappeared, after which the piercing sound returned.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://prensamercosur.org/2026/05/25/el-expediente-ovni-de-talavera-la-real-que-todavia-desconcierta-al-ejercito-del-aire-espanol/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prensamercosur.org">[Prensa Mercosur]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prensamercosur.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noticias Una guardia rutinaria que terminó con disparos dentro de la base El expediente desclasificado y las dudas sobre lo que ocurrió...</span></span></span>

The Ministry of Defence catalogue confirms the existence of a 28-page intelligence file titled after the incident. It records the event date, identifies the Talavera base and notes that the material was declassified by order of the Chief of the Air Staff on 26 November 1996. The surviving archive therefore establishes that the Air Force collected material about the episode. It does not, by itself, authenticate every detail later repeated in books, broadcasts and newspaper retellings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160069099" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

### Why the case seems stronger than an ordinary sighting

Several features give the Talavera story its lasting appeal. The witnesses were servicemen on duty inside a controlled military site. More than one person was reportedly involved, weapons were discharged, and the incident generated official paperwork rather than surviving solely as an anecdote told years later.

The report also contains an unusual combination of elements: a disturbing sound, an apparent intruder, physical symptoms and an attempted armed response. That is far more memorable than a brief light crossing the sky. It explains why Talavera became a landmark of Spanish UFO culture even though it lacks the instrument records that would normally make a military aviation case persuasive.

The official documentation matters most as evidence of institutional response. It shows that military authorities considered the allegations worth preserving and reviewing. It should not be confused with official confirmation that a non-human being entered the base.

### The missing physical evidence

The case becomes much weaker when judged by material evidence. No photograph, film, radar record or uncontested physical trace of the figure is known. Later accounts emphasise that no clear bullet impacts, blood, footprints or remains were found. Reports also say that spent cartridge cases were not recovered from the alleged firing position, an especially awkward detail because a submachine gun normally ejects its cases beside the shooter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://prensamercosur.org/2026/05/25/el-expediente-ovni-de-talavera-la-real-que-todavia-desconcierta-al-ejercito-del-aire-espanol/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prensamercosur.org">[Prensa Mercosur]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prensamercosur.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noticias Una guardia rutinaria que terminó con disparos dentro de la base El expediente desclasificado y las dudas sobre lo que ocurrió...</span></span></span>

Some retellings say that military personnel confirmed the weapons had been fired. Even so, confirmation of discharged ammunition would establish only that frightened guards opened fire, not what they fired at. The absence of recoverable impacts makes it impossible to reconstruct the trajectory, direction or exact number of shots.

Trejo’s later collapse and treatment for a nervous disorder are often cited as evidence that something powerful affected him. They are equally compatible with an acute stress reaction following a frightening night-time episode. Medical distress may support the sincerity of a witness without proving the witness’s interpretation of what caused it.

### Could fear and misperception explain it?

The principal sceptical interpretation is not that the soldiers necessarily invented the entire story. It is that a chain of ambiguous stimuli escalated under difficult conditions. Guards in darkness heard an unexplained noise in a sensitive fuel area, began searching for an intruder and may have interpreted a poorly seen object, person, reflection or illuminated feature as something monstrous. Once one man reacted, the others’ expectations and fear could have reinforced the same perception. Critical commentators have accordingly suggested stress, suggestion and distorted perception rather than an unknown visitor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://prensamercosur.org/2026/06/29/el-hombre-verde-de-talavera-la-real-el-expediente-ovni-que-todavia-desconcierta-al-ejercito-del-aire-espanol/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prensamercosur.org">[Prensa Mercosur]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prensamercosur.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Prensa MercosurEl &#x27;Hombre Verde&quot; de Talavera la Real: el expediente OVNI que todavía desconcierta al Ejército del Aire español &#124; Prensa M...</span></span></span>

This explanation is plausible but not demonstrable. The surviving public record does not provide enough environmental detail to identify a specific light source, animal, aircraft, person or mechanical fault. “Unresolved” is therefore defensible in the narrow sense that no single cause has been proved. It does not mean that an extraordinary explanation is equally well supported.

Talavera is best described as a serious witness incident with very poor physical corroboration. Its official status raises it above folklore, but the archive records a claim and investigation, not a verified humanoid encounter.

## Usagre and the wedding fireworks

The last officially catalogued Badajoz report occurred at Usagre on 11 October 1993. Civilian witnesses described an unusual aerial object or light, and some later versions added aircraft apparently moving in the same area. Because the report came near the end of Spain’s historical UFO-file period, it is sometimes presented as the province’s final unresolved official sighting. The Ministry of Defence catalogue confirms the date and location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/es/consulta/registro.do?id=38329" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

The most important counter-evidence came from the local mayor. According to regional reporting based on the file, a wedding was taking place in Usagre at the relevant time, with numerous firecrackers and aerial rockets being launched. That provides a direct, local source for bright, moving and short-lived lights in the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eldiario.es/extremadura/ovnis-region-espaciales-figura-antropomorfa_1_3767371.amp.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eldiario.es">[elDiario.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eldiario.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on eldiario.es.</span></span></span>

Fireworks do not mimic every claimed UFO characteristic perfectly. Distance, darkness and uncertainty about scale can, however, make an ascending rocket or burning debris appear larger, slower or farther away than it really is. Witnesses who see aircraft shortly afterwards may then connect unrelated events into one continuous episode.

The wedding evidence therefore substantially weakens the Usagre case. Unless reliable timing, direction and duration data show that the reported object behaved incompatibly with the fireworks, the conventional explanation should take priority. This is a useful example of why local investigation matters: a dramatic report may become much less mysterious when compared with activities taking place on the ground.

## What the declassification actually proves

Spain’s historical UFO files became publicly accessible through a declassification programme begun in the early 1990s. Copies were deposited with the Air Force’s central library, and digitisation later made the collection available through the Ministry of Defence’s online library. The Badajoz records sit within that national archive rather than representing a separate provincial investigation programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

Declassification proves that reports existed, were retained and eventually released. It does not certify the accuracy of witnesses or imply that military analysts endorsed an extraterrestrial explanation. Files may contain raw statements, correspondence, retrospective summaries and proposed explanations of very different quality.

This distinction is particularly important at Talavera. The words “military file” can give the impression of radar confirmation, forensic testing and a formally unexplained intrusion. The publicly described case is much less robust. Its core remains human testimony recorded after a frightening event, surrounded by disputed details and an absence of decisive physical evidence.

The archive is nevertheless valuable. It allows readers to separate documented facts—the dates, locations, existence of an inquiry and wording of official assessments—from details that grew through later ufological storytelling. In Badajoz, that separation generally makes the cases less extraordinary but historically more understandable.

## Was there a Badajoz UFO wave?

The available official record does not support the idea of a sustained province-wide UFO wave. The catalogued events are spread across 1967, 1975, 1976 and 1993, with the first of those geographically tied to Montánchez in Cáceres. Two of the clearest Badajoz entries concern the same military installation at Talavera la Real.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/busqueda_referencia.do?campo=idlugar&amp;idValor=659549" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

That concentration is better explained by reporting opportunity than by an established hotspot. An air base contains radar, trained observers, communications systems and procedures for reporting unusual aerial activity. It is therefore more likely to generate a preserved file than a comparable light seen over sparsely populated countryside.

The mid-1970s clustering at Talavera is still worth noting. A radar incident occurred in January 1975, followed by the guards’ encounter in November 1976. But the cases do not form a coherent technical pattern: the first involved unconfirmed radar echoes, while the second centred on sound, a perceived figure and gunfire without known radar support. Treating them as repeated visits by the same phenomenon goes beyond the evidence.

## A balanced verdict on Badajoz

Badajoz has an important place in Spanish UFO history because Talavera la Real produced one of the country’s most memorable military witness stories and because the associated papers are preserved in an official archive. The episode deserves attention as a case study in fear, testimony, military procedure and the later growth of legend.

It does not provide strong evidence of an unknown aircraft or non-human entity. The 1976 incident lacks photographs, instrument confirmation and recoverable physical traces. The 1975 radar observations have credible technical explanations involving false returns and atmospheric propagation. The [Usagre sighting]({{ 'usagre-sighting/' | relative_url }}) has a specific competing explanation in the form of wedding fireworks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1975-01-14_avistamiento_en_talavera_la_real.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[planetabenitez.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1975 01 14 avistamiento en talavera la real</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">750114 RESUMEN: El Controlador de Servicio de la pantalla de G.C.A. de la B.A. de Talavera observa, desde las 19:15Z hasta las 19:40Z ap...</span></span></span>

The most accurate classification is therefore mixed:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Talavera, 1975:** probably explained by radar artefacts or atmospheric conditions.
* **Talavera, 1976:** unresolved as a witness experience, but weakly supported as an extraordinary event.
* **Usagre, 1993:** plausibly explained by fireworks, with insufficient evidence for anything more unusual.
* **Montánchez, 1967:** an aviation file with a confusing provincial label, not a secure Badajoz-centred incident.

</div>

Badajoz’s UFO legacy is not a record of confirmed unexplained craft. It is a compact history of how ambiguous events become official reports, how military surroundings amplify their cultural importance, and how later investigation can either narrow the mystery or reveal that the decisive evidence was never collected.
