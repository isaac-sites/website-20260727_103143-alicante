## Introduction

On the night of 23 March 1977, the newsroom of *La Voz de Almería* reportedly received dozens of calls about a conspicuous point of light over the coast around Aguadulce and Roquetas de Mar. Members of the local Avance association were among the callers. Later accounts describe the light as flashing in different colours, but the surviving public record provides no precise observation times, bearings, photographs, radar data or independent aviation report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.albertocerezuela.com/ovnis-sobre-aguadulce-misterios-de-almeria/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: albertocerezuela.com">[albertocerezuela.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">albertocerezuela.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Almeria_5a5611_aguadulce_roquetas_s_64c081-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Coastal Sightings" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The episode matters because it helped establish Aguadulce and Roquetas as Almería’s most persistent UFO-watching zone. Yet the number of callers should not be confused with the number of unexplained objects: people spread along the same coastline may all have been watching one bright astronomical or airborne light. The most responsible assessment is therefore that a striking display was widely noticed, while its supposedly extraordinary behaviour remains poorly documented and open to ordinary explanations.

## The calls from Aguadulce and Roquetas

The central fact is modest but reasonably consistent across later accounts. During the evening of 23 March, callers contacted *La Voz de Almería* to report a luminous point apparently positioned over, or in the direction of, Roquetas and Aguadulce. The local writer Alberto Cerezuela says the newspaper registered “dozens” of calls and that the light produced flashes of different colours. He identifies Avance, an Almería association interested in UFOs and paranormal subjects, as one of the groups that reported it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.albertocerezuela.com/ovnis-sobre-aguadulce-misterios-de-almeria/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: albertocerezuela.com">[albertocerezuela.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">albertocerezuela.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span></span></span>

That is better evidence for a shared public sighting than for an isolated personal mistake. A bright object was evidently noticeable enough to prompt repeated telephone reports. It also appeared at a time when Almería’s earlier wave of UFO publicity had begun to subside, making the renewed display newsworthy to witnesses already familiar with the subject. Cerezuela places it after the particularly active local period around 1974 and presents the 1977 event as a return of a familiar coastal mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.albertocerezuela.com/ovnis-sobre-aguadulce-misterios-de-almeria/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: albertocerezuela.com">[albertocerezuela.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">albertocerezuela.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span></span></span>

However, the available sources do not preserve the information needed to reconstruct the observation scientifically. There is no accessible list of callers showing where each person stood, when they first saw the light, which direction they faced or whether their descriptions were recorded before they heard what others were reporting. “Over Roquetas and Aguadulce” may represent an estimated location rather than a measured position. A distant object seen above the sea can appear to hang over several different coastal communities depending on the observer’s viewpoint.

Nor is there evidence that the reports led to a formal military or civil-aviation investigation comparable with Spain’s declassified Air Force case files. The official material and later cultural retrospectives confirm that Avance investigated prominent Almería sightings, but they do not identify a surviving technical dossier for the March 1977 coastal event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/cultura/agendaculturaldeandalucia/evento/mesa-redonda-ovnis-en-almeria-en-los-anos-70" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: juntadeandalucia.es">[Junta de Andalucía]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">juntadeandalucia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Junta de Andalucía Mesa redonda. OVNIS en Almería en los años 70</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Junta de Andalucía Mesa redonda. OVNIS en Almería en los años 70</span></span></span>

This distinction is important. Many witnesses can establish that a light attracted attention, but without independent measurements they cannot establish its distance, size or speed. If every caller was looking at the same planet, star or aircraft, the call volume would multiply the testimony without adding new physical evidence.

The local setting helped the event endure. Aguadulce offered open views across the Mediterranean, while the nearby El Cañarete road and coastal cliffs became regular observation points for enthusiasts equipped with binoculars and cameras. Avance and similar groups did not merely wait for reports; they deliberately watched the sky from these locations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.albertocerezuela.com/ovnis-sobre-aguadulce-misterios-de-almeria/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: albertocerezuela.com">[albertocerezuela.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">albertocerezuela.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span></span></span> This increased the chance of noticing genuinely unusual traffic, but it also increased exposure to distant aircraft, stars near the horizon and ambiguous lights viewed for long periods.

The broader culture of the 1970s strengthened that effect. Spanish radio and television treated UFO reports as mass entertainment, and organised “UFO alert” nights encouraged large audiences to look upwards expecting something remarkable. An official Andalusian cultural retrospective describes Avance as part of this national enthusiasm, while a later *La Voz de Almería* account recalls young people gathering on rooftops and listening to late-night broadcasts that framed unexplained lights as possible visiting craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/cultura/agendaculturaldeandalucia/evento/mesa-redonda-ovnis-en-almeria-en-los-anos-70" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: juntadeandalucia.es">[Junta de Andalucía]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">juntadeandalucia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Junta de Andalucía Mesa redonda. OVNIS en Almería en los años 70</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Junta de Andalucía Mesa redonda. OVNIS en Almería en los años 70</span></span></span>

That context does not prove that witnesses imagined the light. It does mean that they already possessed a ready-made interpretation for it.

## Why a bright light can appear to move

Descriptions such as “hovering”, “changing direction” or “moving irregularly” sound impressive, but they are unreliable when applied to an isolated light in a dark sky. Without clouds, hills or other fixed reference points, the eye has difficulty judging whether a distant point is moving at all.

One well-established effect is the **autokinetic illusion**. When a person stares at a single stationary light against a dark, featureless background, small involuntary eye movements can make the light appear to drift, dart or approach. The US Federal Aviation Administration warns pilots that a fixed star or ground light may seem to move when viewed in darkness, sometimes strongly enough to be mistaken for traffic on a collision course.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-01/Spatial%20Disorientation.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationSpatial DisorientationSpatial Disorientation General Aviation FAA Joint Steering Commitee Aviation Safety...</span></span></span>

The coastal geometry around Aguadulce and Roquetas is well suited to this illusion. Looking out over an unlit sea removes many of the visual references normally used to judge position and motion. A bright object low above the horizon may therefore seem to wander even when its astronomical position changes only gradually.

Colour changes are not necessarily more mysterious. A bright star seen through turbulent air can twinkle, vary in intensity and flash red, blue or green, particularly when it is low on the horizon and its light passes through a greater thickness of atmosphere. Atmospheric scintillation can also make a star appear to jump slightly from side to side.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomy.com/observing/why-do-some-stars-appear-to-twinkle-while-others-dont/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomy.com">[Astronomy Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Magazine Why do some stars appear to twinkle while others don’t?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">From the August 2018 issue Why do some stars appear to twinkle while others don’t? Stars twinkle and sometimes appear to move around due...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 2018</span></span></span> This closely resembles the later description of the 1977 light as a point producing flashes of several tones.

Several ordinary candidates consequently remain viable:

* **A bright star or planet:** capable of remaining visible for a long period and appearing stationary or slowly mobile. On 23 March 1977, the Moon was only a slim crescent, leaving much of the night sky relatively dark; Jupiter was also approaching a conjunction with the Moon on 24 March and was therefore a prominent candidate in that region of the sky, although the surviving reports lack the time and direction needed for a firm identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theskylive.com/moon/1977-03-23?lang=es" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theskylive.com">[The Sky Live]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theskylive.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Sky LiveFase lunar del 23 de marzo de 1977: Luna Creciente &#124; TheSkyLiveFase lunar del 23 de marzo de 1977: Luna Creciente &#124; TheSkyLiv...</span></span></span>

* **A distant aircraft:** its landing lights could appear nearly fixed while it approached the observer head-on, then seem to change direction when it turned. Distance would also make engine noise faint or inaudible.
* **More than one aircraft:** callers comparing reports informally might combine separate lights into a single object said to manoeuvre over a wide area.
* **A light affected by haze or broken cloud:** intermittent obscuration can create apparent pulses, colour changes or sudden disappearance.

</div>

None of these explanations has been demonstrated for the Aguadulce event. The necessary weather records, detailed witness statements, flight logs and astronomical bearings have not been published together. The point is narrower: the reported features do not require an exotic vehicle. Each can arise from familiar objects viewed under difficult night-time conditions.

The event also illustrates why telephone clusters require careful handling. Once a newspaper or radio station confirms that other people are calling, later witnesses may watch more intently, reinterpret an ordinary light or report what they think matches the emerging story. Reports are no longer fully independent after information begins circulating. A large response can therefore measure both the visibility of the stimulus and the speed of social reinforcement.

## The missing-time story and its weaknesses

The most dramatic narrative attached to the coastal cluster concerns Paco Barrilado, an Almería actor and film stunt performer. In a story published decades later, Barrilado reportedly told Cerezuela that he and his wife entered their car in Roquetas de Mar and found themselves on the outskirts of Almería less than three minutes later, with neither remembering the journey. The following day, he supposedly saw a newspaper headline reporting UFOs near Roquetas.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.albertocerezuela.com/ovnis-sobre-aguadulce-misterios-de-almeria/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: albertocerezuela.com">[albertocerezuela.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">albertocerezuela.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span></span></span> Barrilado was a real and well-known figure in Almería’s film industry, later honoured by the city for his work as an action performer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://almeriaciudad.es/actualidad/el-ayuntamiento-rinde-homenaje-paco-barrilado-con-una-estrella-en-la-casa-del-cine" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: almeriaciudad.es">[almeriaciudad.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">almeriaciudad.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on almeriaciudad.es.</span></span></span>

The witness’s local standing makes the anecdote culturally memorable, but it does not repair its evidential problems. The public version appears to come from a much later personal recollection rather than a dated statement made immediately after the journey. No contemporary police report, medical examination, written testimony from his wife, fuel record, clock comparison or description of the precise route has surfaced in the accessible sources.

The claimed three-minute journey is also difficult to evaluate because the starting point, destination and method of timing are unspecified. “Roquetas de Mar” and “the outskirts of Almería” cover broad areas. A remembered estimate made years later cannot be treated like a stopwatch measurement. Even if the elapsed time genuinely seemed too short, an incorrectly set car clock, mistaken departure time, incomplete memory or ordinary absorption while driving would have to be excluded before proposing an external cause.

Most importantly, the story contains no direct observation of a strange object during the alleged gap. Its connection to the coastal light rests on the later newspaper report and the assumption that the two events occurred on the same night. Temporal proximity can make incidents feel linked without showing that one caused the other.

“Missing time” became a familiar theme in UFO literature because memory gaps were sometimes interpreted as evidence of concealed encounters. Yet memory is reconstructive rather than a continuous recording. Routine journeys are especially prone to weak recall because familiar actions require little conscious attention. Fatigue, distraction, stress and later questioning can all affect how an episode is remembered. Without contemporary documentation, hypnosis records or independent physical evidence, the Barrilado account cannot support a claim of abduction, time distortion or interference with the vehicle.

There is a further source problem. Cerezuela’s account states that the next day’s newspaper carried a front-page headline saying that several UFOs had been seen near Roquetas. Yet the accessible retelling does not reproduce the page, give an edition number or quote the accompanying report in full.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.albertocerezuela.com/ovnis-sobre-aguadulce-misterios-de-almeria/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: albertocerezuela.com">[albertocerezuela.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">albertocerezuela.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span></span></span> Until the original issue is located and examined, it remains uncertain how closely the later wording matches the 1977 coverage or whether the article itself included Barrilado’s experience.

The anecdote is therefore best classified as **interesting testimony with very low verification value**. Barrilado’s identity is confirmed, but the extraordinary part of the story survives through retrospective narration rather than a contemporary evidence trail.

## What the coastal cluster supports

The Aguadulce and Roquetas episode supports three conclusions with different levels of confidence.

First, a conspicuous light was reported by numerous people on 23 March 1977, including members of a local organisation actively watching the sky. The repeated calls make it unlikely that the entire episode was invented by a single storyteller.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.albertocerezuela.com/ovnis-sobre-aguadulce-misterios-de-almeria/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: albertocerezuela.com">[albertocerezuela.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">albertocerezuela.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ovnis Sobre Aguadulce – Misterios De Almería &#124; Alberto Cerezuela</span></span></span>

Second, the case helped consolidate the coast west of Almería city as a lasting centre of local UFO interest. Enthusiasts continued using Aguadulce’s beaches, cliffs and roadside viewpoints, and Avance became part of the province’s remembered 1970s UFO culture. Recent exhibitions and public discussions have treated these investigators and witnesses as significant figures in Almería’s social history, while stopping short of validating extraterrestrial interpretations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/cultura/agendaculturaldeandalucia/evento/mesa-redonda-ovnis-en-almeria-en-los-anos-70" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: juntadeandalucia.es">[Junta de Andalucía]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">juntadeandalucia.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Junta de Andalucía Mesa redonda. OVNIS en Almería en los años 70</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Junta de Andalucía Mesa redonda. OVNIS en Almería en los años 70</span></span></span>

Third, the surviving evidence is too incomplete to show that the light performed movements beyond the capabilities of an aircraft or astronomical object. The reports available today lack exact times, angular measurements, photographs, radar confirmation and properly separated witness statements. Features that made the sighting seem extraordinary—apparent hovering, movement and coloured flashes—are also characteristic of bright lights viewed against a dark maritime horizon.

Later reporting has therefore preserved the event rather than strengthened it. It has added atmosphere, names and the missing-time anecdote, but not the technical information needed to identify the light. The case remains unresolved in the literal sense that no object has been conclusively matched to it. In evidential terms, however, it is a weak UFO case and a much stronger example of how one ambiguous coastal light, amplified by dozens of calls and an active local UFO culture, could turn Aguadulce and Roquetas into Almería’s best-known sighting cluster.
