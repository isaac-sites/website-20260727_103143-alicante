## Introduction

The Mazarrón incident of 14 July 1978 is [Murcia]({{ 'why-murcia-opens-spains-official-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s most dramatic military UFO report, but not its best-investigated one. During a five-day Army exercise near El Garrobillo, an officer and a corporal were sent towards another unit camped by the Rambla de Pinilla. They later reported that a red light, sometimes joined by white or greenish-white lights, appeared to follow them between roughly 4am and 6am. They judged it to be silent, very low and close enough to illuminate part of a reservoir wall red.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1978-07-14_avistamiento_en_mazarron_murcia.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Planeta Benitez</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Murcia_fed56b_mazarron_1978_encoun_6b0dda-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Mazarron 1978" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Those details make the encounter memorable: military witnesses, an observation lasting about two hours and apparent movement around houses and hills. Yet the surviving official file is only a later intelligence transcription of part of an Army captain’s report. It contains no individual witness statements, measured bearings, weather data, photographs, radar records or reconstruction of the route. The case therefore remains unresolved in the limited sense that no source was identified, not in the stronger sense that an extraordinary craft was established.

## The night exercise and witness account

The incident took place while an Army unit was conducting five days of exercises in the El Garrobillo area of Mazarrón. On the night of Thursday 13 July, an acting junior officer and a corporal were ordered to carry out a mock raid against a company section camped in the Rambla de Pinilla. Their identities were removed from the publicly released documentation, in line with the anonymisation applied across Spain’s declassified military UFO archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1978-07-14_avistamiento_en_mazarron_murcia.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Planeta Benitez</span></span></span>

The official note divides the reported experience into two broad phases. At about 4am, the soldiers watched a group of lights for approximately 15 minutes from the vicinity of the Rambla de Pinilla. The distance was not established. The phenomenon was said to move from side to side and consisted mainly of a red light that occasionally went out. Two white lights with a slight green tint appeared intermittently, while four additional white lights were reportedly visible much less often and without a fixed formation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1978-07-14_avistamiento_en_mazarron_murcia.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Planeta Benitez</span></span></span>

On their return towards camp, the soldiers said they encountered the red light again near kilometre 13. The report describes it as stationary over the road at an estimated height of eight to ten metres. One white light then appeared, followed by another. When the men walked towards it, the lights seemed to move forward at the same time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1978-07-14_avistamiento_en_mazarron_murcia.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Planeta Benitez</span></span></span>

The most striking part of the story followed. According to the note, the light travelled ahead of the soldiers and appeared to leave the road whenever houses or hills intervened, passing around the obstacle before taking up a position in front of them again. It allegedly remained with them until they reached the company’s bivouac at about 6am. Its perceived height was variously estimated at between four and 30 metres. The witnesses reported no sound and said the red glow illuminated one wall of a nearby reservoir.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1978-07-14_avistamiento_en_mazarron_murcia.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Planeta Benitez</span></span></span>

The term “followed” should nevertheless be treated as a witness interpretation rather than an independently established movement. The file records how the lights appeared from the soldiers’ point of view, but it provides no triangulation, mapped sightlines or second observation position from which their distance and altitude could have been calculated.

## Why the low-altitude claims are difficult to verify

A light seen in darkness has no obvious scale. Unless it passes in front of or behind a known object, or is viewed simultaneously from separated locations, an observer may be unable to tell whether it is a small nearby source or a powerful light several kilometres away. That problem lies at the heart of the Mazarrón report.

The estimates of four, eight, ten or 30 metres appear precise, but the dossier does not say how they were obtained. There is no recorded angular size, distance measurement or description of a visible solid body. The soldiers principally described lights. Even the phrase “over the road” may mean that the light appeared aligned with the road from their position, rather than that its location directly above the road was demonstrated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1978-07-14_avistamiento_en_mazarron_murcia.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Planeta Benitez</span></span></span>

The apparent behaviour around obstacles is similarly open to two readings. Taken literally, it suggests an intelligently controlled object adjusting its course to stay ahead of the men. A more ordinary possibility is that a distant light was repeatedly hidden by houses, ridges or bends in the terrain. As the observers moved, it could seem to disappear, circle around an obstruction and reappear in front of them. Comparable “following light” reports have been produced when observers misjudged the distance of astronomical objects or fixed lights in the landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/143389200/Encuentro_de_clase_militar_en_Mazarron" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF) Encuentro de clase militar en Mazarron</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academia(PDF) Encuentro de clase militar en Mazarron...</span></span></span>

Researchers Vicente-Juan Ballester Olmos and Manuel Borraz examined this possibility in a detailed critique of the case. They did not identify a definite conventional source, but argued that a distant light could account for several otherwise extraordinary features at once: the long duration, silence, apparently constant separation and impression that the phenomenon kept pace with the soldiers. Their conclusion was not that the event had been solved, but that its evidential strength was lower than the dramatic wording of the report suggested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/143389200/Encuentro_de_clase_militar_en_Mazarron" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF) Encuentro de clase militar en Mazarron</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academia(PDF) Encuentro de clase militar en Mazarron...</span></span></span>

The reported red illumination on the reservoir wall is the hardest detail to reconcile with a faint, distant source. If the wall was genuinely lit by the phenomenon, it would imply considerable brightness or proximity. However, the file gives no location of the observers relative to the wall, no estimate of the illuminated area, no account from an independent witness and no indication that the effect was photographed or checked afterwards. A reddish reflection, an existing local light or an impression strengthened in later retelling cannot be excluded.

## The next-night check found a possible landmark

The only field check mentioned in the surviving record occurred the following night. Personnel revisited the area and found nothing abnormal. They did, however, notice the illuminated antenna of a meteorological station. The original observers reportedly insisted that this could not have caused their experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1978-07-14_avistamiento_en_mazarron_murcia.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Planeta Benitez</span></span></span>

This is important because it shows that investigators recognised the possibility of a fixed environmental light. It does not prove that the antenna was responsible. A convincing test would have required the soldiers to retrace their exact route, identify the directions in which the lights were seen at each stage and compare those sightlines with the antenna and any other illuminated structures. The file records none of that work.

Nor does it preserve the witnesses’ reasons for rejecting the station. They might have known its normal appearance, observed the unknown light in a different direction or seen movements incompatible with a fixed source. Equally, their rejection might simply have rested on their certainty that the light had been close. Without their full statements, neither interpretation can be tested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/143389200/Encuentro_de_clase_militar_en_Mazarron" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF) Encuentro de clase militar en Mazarron</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academia(PDF) Encuentro de clase militar en Mazarron...</span></span></span>

Other ordinary possibilities remain similarly underdeveloped. Separate lights in the landscape, vehicle lights on distant tracks, maritime lights towards the coast, aircraft viewed intermittently or several unrelated sources could potentially have contributed to the changing display. Yet the absence of bearings, visibility reports and an accurate minute-by-minute chronology prevents meaningful comparisons. The occasional appearance of four white lights may even describe a different stimulus from the dominant red light rather than components of one object.

## What the surviving dossier cannot establish

The public file is unusual because its official status is stronger than its investigative content. The core document was produced in September 1978 by an Air Staff intelligence office, which stated that it was copying information received from the Army Intelligence Service. In 1993, during a search of Air Force records, the note was forwarded to the command handling UFO declassification. The four-page dossier was finally marked for release in 1998.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1978-07-14_avistamiento_en_mazarron_murcia.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Planeta Benitez</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Planeta Benitez</span></span></span>

That chain provides reasonable evidence that soldiers made a report and that it entered military channels. It does not amount to a contemporary Air Force investigation. The dossier itself explicitly says that, apart from the intelligence document, no other material concerning the event was held.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1978-07-14_avistamiento_en_mazarron_murcia.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Planeta Benitez</span></span></span>

Several essential items are absent:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **No direct testimony.** The public file does not contain separate interviews with the officer, corporal or other soldiers who may have seen the lights.
* **No confirmed witness count.** It is unclear exactly who saw each stage, for how long, and whether all witnesses agreed on the distance, movement and colour.
* **No route reconstruction.** Military grid references are given for several locations, but the direction of observation from each point is not.
* **No environmental record.** There are no surviving weather observations, visibility estimates, aircraft checks, radar returns or inventory of local lights.
* **No physical evidence.** Nothing was reportedly recovered, photographed or found altered during the examination the following night.
* **No contemporaneous civilian corroboration.** Later researchers reported that local UFO groups they contacted knew of no matching observation in the area at the time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/143389200/Encuentro_de_clase_militar_en_Mazarron" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF) Encuentro de clase militar en Mazarron</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academia(PDF) Encuentro de clase militar en Mazarron...</span></span></span>

</div>

The term “declassified” can also be misleading. It describes the document’s security status, not a conclusion that its contents were verified. Spain’s Ministry of Defence began releasing its unusual-aerial-observation records in the 1990s and now provides a digital collection of 80 files covering about 1,900 pages. The files vary greatly: some include interviews and technical checks, while others contain only a handful of pages. Mazarrón belongs firmly to the latter category.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

## Did later reporting strengthen the case?

Later newspaper and television coverage has kept the Mazarrón encounter visible, often presenting it as one of Spain’s most puzzling declassified UFO files. Such reports accurately repeat many of its memorable elements: the two-hour duration, coloured lights, low estimated altitude, silence and apparent pursuit of the soldiers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eldebate.com/espana/defensa/20230731/expediente-ovni-espana-archivos-desclasificados-defensa_131302.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eldebate.com">[El Debate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eldebate.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El DebateFuerzas Armadas: Expediente OVNI en España: los archivos desclasificados de DefensaJuly 31, 2023 — Fuerzas Armadas: Expediente O...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 31, 2023</span></span></span>

What later reporting has not produced is substantial new primary evidence. No publicly available full witness interviews, contemporary photographs, radar records or independent civilian reports have emerged to confirm the more dramatic parts of the story. Most modern accounts ultimately lead back to the same short military note.

The Ballester Olmos and Borraz examination added useful analysis, mapping the stated locations and identifying the perceptual problem posed by a distant light. It also documented how the report was rediscovered and processed during the declassification programme. However, the authors could not settle the case because the missing directions, testimonies and environmental checks made the proposed fixed-light explanation impossible to test conclusively.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/143389200/Encuentro_de_clase_militar_en_Mazarron" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF) Encuentro de clase militar en Mazarron</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academia(PDF) Encuentro de clase militar en Mazarron...</span></span></span>

Later attention has therefore made the document easier to find, but has not materially strengthened the underlying claim. The story remains striking, while the evidence remains thin.

## The fairest assessment

The Mazarrón encounter deserves its prominent place in Murcia’s UFO history because it is an authentic military report describing an unusually long and apparently close experience. The witnesses were engaged in a structured night exercise rather than casually watching the sky, and their account reached intelligence channels within months. Those facts make the incident more substantial than an anonymous anecdote reported decades later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://planetabenitez.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/1978-07-14_avistamiento_en_mazarron_murcia.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: planetabenitez.com">[Planeta Benitez]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">planetabenitez.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Planeta Benitez</span></span></span>

They do not establish that a physical craft followed the soldiers. The report gives no description of a body or structure, no verified manoeuvre measured against known positions and no instrument detection. Its most extraordinary claims—very low altitude, obstacle avoidance and red illumination—depend entirely on a compressed second-hand account.

The most defensible classification is **unresolved but weakly documented**. A conventional light misperceived as nearby is plausible and helps explain the silence, duration and apparent pursuit. The meteorological-station antenna is an obvious candidate, though the available file cannot show that it matched the sightlines. Conversely, because the original investigation was so limited, no conventional explanation can now be demonstrated with confidence.

Mazarrón 1978 is therefore less a case of an extraordinary event surviving rigorous investigation than a case in which rigorous investigation never occurred. Its enduring mystery comes primarily from the loss of the information that would have allowed the soldiers’ dramatic account to be properly tested.
