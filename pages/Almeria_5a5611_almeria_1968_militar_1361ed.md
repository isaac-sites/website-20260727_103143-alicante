## Introduction

Almería’s 1968 military UFO file proves that a civilian reported an unidentified aerial sighting and that the report passed through Spain’s Air Force command structure. It does **not** prove that officials detected an extraordinary craft, that the object performed impossible manoeuvres, or even that it was physically close to the city. The surviving four-page record contains only a brief second-hand account, a check for controlled airport traffic and a retrospective assessment written during declassification in 1993.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-09_avistamiento_en_almeria_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AV</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveFull text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AVI...</span></span></span>

That distinction makes the file important. It is the clearest surviving official document connected specifically with Almería in Spain’s digitised military UFO archive, but it is also a useful example of how an authentic government record can preserve a genuine report without providing enough evidence to solve it. The most defensible verdict is therefore **unresolved but weakly documented**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://verne.elpais.com/verne/2016/10/25/articulo/1477394008_803441.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: verne.elpais.com">[Verne]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">verne.elpais.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on elpais.com.</span></span></span>

## What the witness and air commander reported

The incident was dated 9 December 1968. According to the military correspondence, a civilian living in Almería city telephoned the local air commander at approximately 16:20 and 16:25. He said he had observed an unidentified flying object at those times and previously. It was described only as oscillating, with appearances to the east and south-west of the city. Its distance and altitude were expressly recorded as undetermined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-09_avistamiento_en_almeria_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AV</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveFull text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AVI...</span></span></span>

The wording requires care. The later military summary called the sighting a “luminous phenomenon”, but the original letter relaying the telephone report used the broader description “unidentified flying object”. Neither document supplies a shape, colour, brightness, apparent size or detailed flight path. The available text does not establish whether one light travelled across the sky, whether the witness saw separate appearances in different directions, or whether the directional account became less precise as it passed from witness to commander and then through the chain of command.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-09_avistamiento_en_almeria_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AV</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveFull text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AVI...</span></span></span>

Seven days after the sighting, on 16 December 1968, the deputy inspector general of the Second Air Region wrote to the Chief of the Air Staff in Madrid. His letter repeated the local commander’s information and stated that Almería airport’s control tower had no controlled traffic in the relevant area at the reported times. The matter was forwarded for official awareness rather than accompanied by a developed technical investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-09_avistamiento_en_almeria_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AV</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveFull text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AVI...</span></span></span>

The letter is notably cautious. Its author said he was passing on the information with the same reservations applied to similar UFO reports. That phrasing shows that the military accepted the existence of the notification, not the witness’s interpretation of what had been seen. It also suggests that forwarding such reports was an administrative practice, especially during a period when the Air Ministry was formalising how unusual aerial observations should be communicated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-09_avistamiento_en_almeria_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AV</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveFull text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AVI...</span></span></span>

## What the airport check actually proves

The absence of controlled traffic is the strongest piece of corroborating information in the file, but its meaning is narrower than it first appears. It supports the claim that the tower did not have an aircraft under its control that obviously matched the report at 16:20 or 16:25. It therefore weakens one simple explanation: a locally controlled arrival, departure or other known airport movement in the immediate operational picture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-09_avistamiento_en_almeria_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AV</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveFull text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AVI...</span></span></span>

It does not establish that the airspace was empty. The record does not say that radar detected and tracked the reported object, nor does it contain a systematic search of military flights, distant routes, balloons, meteorological activity or aircraft outside the tower’s control. “No controlled traffic” is not equivalent to “no aircraft or aerial object of any kind”. The file gives no geographical boundary for the area checked and no indication of how far away the witness’s light might have been.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-09_avistamiento_en_almeria_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AV</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveFull text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AVI...</span></span></span>

This matters because distance cannot be judged reliably from the appearance of a solitary light against the sky. An apparently slow or oscillating object might be nearby, but it might also be a distant aircraft, a bright astronomical object viewed through moving cloud or atmospheric distortion, or another conventional light whose range and motion have been misperceived. The file lacks the positional and timing data needed to test any of those possibilities. Modern technical approaches to unidentified aerial observations emphasise range, bearing, calibrated imagery, triangulation and independent sensor confirmation precisely because visual descriptions alone rarely distinguish between competing explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Scientific Investigation of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) Using Multimodal Ground-Based ObservatoriesMay 29, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 29, 2023</span></span></span>

## What the military file does not establish

The most revealing page is not the 1968 letter but the intelligence officer’s assessment prepared at Torrejón on 23 March 1993. It states directly that the report omits basic details including shape, colour, size, altitude, distance and speed. The only concrete data identified by the reviewing officer were the two reported times and the description of an oscillating movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-09_avistamiento_en_almeria_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AV</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveFull text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AVI...</span></span></span>

The review also says that no later investigation was recorded apart from checking the absence of air traffic in the area. It concludes that the shortage of elementary information prevents even a tentative hypothesis about the phenomenon’s explanation. This is a stronger and more useful finding than simply labelling the case “unidentified”: the Air Force reviewer judged that the record was too incomplete to discriminate between an unusual object and an ordinary source seen under uncertain conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-09_avistamiento_en_almeria_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AV</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveFull text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AVI...</span></span></span>

Several evidential gaps follow from the surviving papers:

* **No direct witness statement survives.** The account appears through a commander’s report quoted in a later letter, so the wording is at least one administrative step removed from the original telephone call.
* **No independent witness is documented.** The file names only one civilian source and contains no second account from another location.
* **No measured direction is given.** “East” and “south-west” are broad sectors, not precise bearings.
* **No duration is clear.** The times of two telephone notifications are known, but the length of each observation is not.
* **No radar confirmation appears.** The record mentions an airport traffic check, not an unexplained radar track.
* **No photograph, film or physical trace is included.**
* **No weather or astronomical comparison was recorded.**

## Declassification did not validate the sighting

Spain began reviewing its military UFO holdings for declassification in 1991. Copies were placed in the Air Force’s central library in 1992, and the collection was later digitised by the Ministry of Defence. The broader archive covers reports dating from 1962 to 1995 and contains more than 1,900 pages, although individual files vary greatly in length and evidential quality.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

The Almería assessment was prepared on 23 March 1993, with subsequent declassification and administrative stamps dated in April. These dates refer to the opening of the record to the public, not to a renewed investigation that confirmed the event. The reviewer’s recommendation was based on the absence of material requiring continued secrecy, not on a judgement that the reported object represented advanced technology or an unknown craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/SpanishUFOFiles/1968-12-09_avistamiento_en_almeria_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Internet Archive Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AV</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveFull text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot;Full text of &quot;Spanish UFO Files&quot; Full text of &quot; Spanish UFO Files &quot; See other formats AVI...</span></span></span>

## The file’s place in Almería’s UFO history

The 1968 document matters because official records are scarce in Almería’s UFO history. Later provincial stories often depend on newspaper coverage, accounts collected by enthusiasts or memories recorded years after an incident. This file is different: its central correspondence was produced within days and retained in the military archive. That gives it firmer provenance than many later anecdotes. Better provenance, however, does not mean better observational detail. The file is authentic as a document but poor as a dataset. It verifies that a report was made and officially circulated; it does not verify the witness’s interpretation or preserve enough information for modern investigators to reconstruct the event. That contrast is the file’s main lesson for reading Almería’s wider UFO history.

The object remains unidentified because the evidence was incomplete at the time, not because the surviving pages establish something beyond conventional explanation.
