## Introduction

The incident was real in the limited but important sense that many people, including Spanish naval personnel, observed an unusual aerial display and the Spanish Air Force formally investigated it. Yet the strongest later analysis indicates that the main phenomenon was probably the high-altitude exhaust cloud from two United States Navy [Poseidon missile]({{ 'missile-theory/' | relative_url }}) launches west of the islands. The supposed landed sphere and occupants rest on far narrower and more doubtful testimony.

## Why Las Palmas became a UFO focal point

The province of Las Palmas covers Gran Canaria, Fuerteventura, Lanzarote and La Graciosa. Its position in the eastern Canary Islands gave residents, sailors and aircraft crews broad views over the Atlantic, including the ocean west and south-west of the archipelago. Several major Canary Island sightings were therefore observed from places within the province even when the source of the light may have been hundreds of kilometres offshore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gobiernodecanarias.org/transparencia/temas/institucional-organizativa/instituciones/informacion-sobre-las-islas-canarias/geografia-canarias/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gobiernodecanarias.org">[Gobierno de Canarias]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gobiernodecanarias.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gobierno de Canarias Portal de transparencia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gobierno de CanariasPortal de transparencia - GeografíaMay 27, 2026 — Las Palmas, que engloba las islas de Gran Canaria, Fuerteventura, L...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 27, 2026</span></span></span>

This geographical point matters because witnesses often described lights as rising from an island, hovering over the sea or approaching a particular town. In a dark sky with few fixed reference points, however, a huge illuminated cloud at extreme altitude can look much nearer than it is. Different observers may then place the same distant phenomenon over Fuerteventura, Gran Canaria or the nearby ocean.

Las Palmas also contained important aviation and military infrastructure. Gando Air Base and the air-traffic facilities serving Gran Canaria created channels through which unusual reports could reach the authorities. Naval vessels and aircraft supplied professionally recorded observations, while newspapers in Las Palmas de Gran Canaria gave the incidents extensive coverage. The result was not necessarily a higher rate of genuinely mysterious activity, but an unusually rich combination of witnesses, official paperwork and press attention.

## The night of 22 June 1976

At 21:27 universal time, crew members aboard the Spanish Navy corvette *Atrevida*, sailing roughly parallel to Fuerteventura, saw a white light apparently rise from the island. According to the later Air Force summary, it climbed to an elevation of about 15 to 18 degrees, became bluish and developed a circular halo. The central light divided; one portion climbed rapidly in a spiral-like movement, while the broad halo remained visible until approximately 22:10. The ship’s radar did not detect a corresponding target.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">p. 3 The General Jefe de la Zona Aérea de Canarias agreed with the Juez Informador on 21 July 1976 and recorded that origin and nature co...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1976</span></span></span>

Within minutes, witnesses at several points on Gran Canaria reported a similar display. Accounts collected by investigators described a luminous focus, expanding bluish or transparent forms and changing shapes visible for an unusually long period. Reports also came from other Canary Islands and vessels at sea, demonstrating that the core event was not an invention by a single witness or one community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160069082" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Image: Manuscritos General Avistamiento de fenómenos extraños en Canarias: 22 de Junio de 1976 (1968-1976) - España.... The wide viewing area is one of the case’s strongest pieces of evidence, but not necessarily evidence of a nearby craft. A phenomenon visible across islands separated by large distances is more likely to have occurred high in the atmosphere or far out over the ocean. That interpretation conflicts with early reports that treated the light as something moving at low altitude over northern Gran Canaria.

### The sphere and its alleged occupants

The detail that transformed an unusual light into a famous close encounter came from a doctor, Francisco Julio Padrón, and the taxi driver taking him to visit a patient. Near Las Rosas, in the Gáldar area, they reportedly saw a large transparent or bluish sphere close to the road. Inside it, the doctor described two very tall humanoid figures wearing reddish clothing.

This account is memorable, but it is not supported as strongly as the mass sighting. Most witnesses saw only the distant luminous phenomenon. The occupants were not reported independently by the many observers elsewhere on the islands, and the official investigator distinguished the well-corroborated light display from the much narrower close-range story.

The Air Force inquiry did not accuse the witnesses of deliberate deception. Its reasoning was closer to a psychological explanation: sincere observers could have interpreted an unfamiliar light under conditions of excitement, darkness and mutual influence. The investigator regarded the principal witnesses as serious people who believed their description, while remaining doubtful that the alleged figures represented an objective event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160069082" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Image: Manuscritos General Avistamiento de fenómenos extraños en Canarias: 22 de Junio de 1976 (1968-1976) - España.... That distinction is essential. Witness sincerity answers the question “Were they knowingly lying?” It does not answer “Was their interpretation accurate?” Darkness, uncertain scale and the absence of physical reference points can produce confident but mistaken estimates of size and distance.

## What the official file established

The Spanish Air Force opened a formal inquiry under procedures already in place for reports of unusual aerial phenomena. The surviving file, numbered 760622, contains witness statements, summaries, drawings and assessments. The regional commander ultimately agreed that the origin and nature of the phenomenon could not be determined from the information then available and classified it as an unidentified aerial phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?control=BMDB20160069082" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Image: Manuscritos General Avistamiento de fenómenos extraños en Canarias: 22 de Junio de 1976 (1968-1976) - España.... “Unidentified” in this context did not mean extraterrestrial. It meant that the inquiry had not established an accepted cause. The file recorded several limitations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* no corresponding radar detection was obtained aboard the *Atrevida*;
* the descriptions varied between observers;
* only a small group reported the supposed nearby sphere;
* the most dramatic occupant details lacked broad corroboration;
* investigators did not then possess confirmed information about a distant missile launch.

</div>

The file remained classified until July 1994, when it entered the Spanish Air Force’s public archive. Spain’s wider declassification programme had begun in 1991, with physical copies made available through the Air Force’s central library from 1992. The Ministry of Defence later digitised the collection, allowing the original case material to be consulted rather than known only through UFO books and newspaper retellings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/micrositios/inicio.do" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

Declassification strengthened confidence that a significant aerial display had occurred and that the military had investigated it seriously. At the same time, it weakened some popular embellishments. The official paperwork did not contain evidence of a recovered object, confirmed landing site, radar-tracked spacecraft or government conclusion that an alien vehicle had visited Gran Canaria.

## The Poseidon missile explanation

The strongest conventional explanation is that observers saw exhaust and fuel clouds generated by two Poseidon ballistic missiles launched from a United States Navy submarine far west of the Canaries. At high altitude, gases from a rocket expand into enormous translucent structures. Although the ground below is already dark, the exhaust can remain illuminated by the Sun, producing white, blue or iridescent globes, spirals and halos.

This mechanism fits several otherwise puzzling features of the 1976 reports:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the display was visible across a very wide area;
* the apparent object developed a large circular cloud;
* its colour and shape changed over several minutes;
* parts appeared to separate or climb;
* stars could reportedly be seen through portions of the luminous form;
* the phenomenon persisted far longer than a meteor or ordinary aircraft light.

</div>

Technical reconstructions by Manuel Borraz, Vicente-Juan Ballester Olmos and Ricardo Campo placed the phenomenon at least hundreds of kilometres west of the islands and tens of kilometres above the Earth. Campo’s published summary gives minimum estimates of roughly 762 kilometres west and 46 kilometres altitude during an early stage, increasing as the display developed. These are model-based estimates rather than direct measurements, but they are consistent with a high-altitude missile plume rather than a large object hovering over Gáldar.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://naukas.com/2016/06/22/dos-gigantes-rojos-cabalgando-un-misil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naukas.com">[Naukas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naukas.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil - Naukas...</span></span></span>

Missile-launch records cited by these researchers identify two Poseidon launches on 22 June 1976 from the submarine *USS Von Steuben*. They argue that the timing, direction and appearance align with the Canary observations, allowing for a reported one-hour error in a compiled launch database. The overlapping exhaust from two launches may also explain the display’s unusual complexity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://naukas.com/2016/06/22/dos-gigantes-rojos-cabalgando-un-misil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naukas.com">[Naukas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naukas.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil - Naukas...</span></span></span>

The remaining caution is that the Spanish file itself did not establish this cause in 1976, and much of the correlation work was conducted later. The missile interpretation is therefore best described as highly plausible or probable, not as a conclusion demonstrated by a recovered fragment or a contemporary admission attached to the Air Force report. It explains the mass sighting far better than it explains every detail later associated with the close encounter.

## A wider Canary missile flap

The Gáldar incident was not isolated. Comparable luminous displays were reported around the Canaries on 22 November 1974, 19 November 1976, 24 March 1977 and 5 March 1979. Sceptical researchers have linked this sequence to submarine-launched missile tests in the Atlantic. Repeated descriptions of expanding globes, clouds and coloured wakes strengthen the argument that observers were encountering the same class of artificial high-altitude phenomenon rather than repeated visits by similar unknown craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://divulgacioncientifica.org/modules.php?file=article&amp;name=News&amp;sid=901" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: divulgacioncientifica.org">[Divulgación Científica]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">divulgacioncientifica.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Divulgación Científica ACDC. Universidad de La Laguna</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Divulgación Científica ACDC. Universidad de La Laguna</span></span></span>

The 5 March 1979 event became especially prominent. It was observed across the Canary Islands and from parts of the African coast, and the Spanish Air Force assembled a file running to hundreds of pages. Its great geographical spread again points away from a small object operating locally over one island.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/international/files/es" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufotransparency.com.</span></span></span>

These cases form a recognisable flap period: several dramatic sightings occurred within a few years, newspapers repeatedly revived earlier stories, and witnesses learned the visual language of spheres, unidentified objects and mysterious lights. That does not mean later witnesses copied earlier ones deliberately. It means that media coverage supplied a ready framework through which ambiguous spectacles were interpreted.

Cold War secrecy helped the mystery survive. Missile tests would not necessarily be announced to island residents, and Spanish investigators did not have immediate access to all United States naval activity in the Atlantic. A correct explanation could therefore remain unavailable while the sighting was fresh, leaving an official “unidentified” classification that later writers treated as permanent.

## The 1980 radar and pilot case

A different Las Palmas case occurred on 22 May 1980, when approach radar serving Gran Canaria detected an unidentified echo south of the island. The official summary reported an approximate speed of 600 knots as the target moved outward along the 210-degree radial and disappeared at about 50 nautical miles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1980-05-22-1980-05-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufotransparency.com.</span></span></span>

A NAYSA aircraft approaching Las Palmas from Villa Cisneros reported a bright, fast-moving light in approximately the same area. The crew described something apparently descending towards the sea. This combination of a visual observation and an approach-radar return makes the incident more substantial than a lone report of a light.

It was not, however, a clean multi-radar confirmation. The surveillance radar of Air Control and Warning Squadron Number 8 and the main Canarias Control radar did not obtain a positive contact. Only the approach system registered the echo, while uncertainties remained about the object’s altitude, exact motion and relationship to cloud conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1980-05-22-1980-05-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufotransparency.com.</span></span></span>

The Air Force appointed an investigating officer and retained a 46-page file, which was declassified in 1995. The case remains less famous than Gáldar because it lacks occupants and a spectacular expanding cloud, but it is arguably more interesting from an aviation perspective. Its value lies in the partial agreement between aircrew and radar, balanced against the failure of other systems to confirm the target.

Calling it unresolved is reasonable. Calling it proof of an extraordinary craft is not. A single radar return can result from anomalous propagation, weather, equipment effects or an ordinary target whose identity was not established. The visual report adds weight, but the available record does not provide enough positional and technical data to rule out every conventional explanation.

## How the story changed after declassification

The public reputation of the 1976 incident grew before readers had access to the complete file. Early newspaper stories and UFO literature concentrated on the largest estimated object, the alleged humanoids and suggestions of military concern. Some later articles repeated claims of radar confirmation even though the naval account explicitly recorded no radar detection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://naukas.com/2016/06/22/dos-gigantes-rojos-cabalgando-un-misil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naukas.com">[Naukas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naukas.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil - Naukas...</span></span></span>

Once the documents became available, the case split into three evidential layers:

**Well supported:** a large, unusual luminous display was observed from several islands and at sea.

**Plausibly explained:** the broad halo, changing colours and long duration closely match high-altitude exhaust from submarine-launched missiles illuminated after sunset.

**Weak or disputed:** a giant sphere stood near Gáldar containing two humanoid occupants.

This division is more useful than labelling the whole case either “solved” or “unexplained”. The missile hypothesis can account convincingly for the shared sky display without proving that every witness perceived or remembered it in exactly the same way. Conversely, uncertainty around the doctor’s close-range account does not erase the many independent observations of the distant light.

Later reporting has therefore strengthened the historical case while weakening the extraterrestrial interpretation. There is now better documentation that something striking was seen, but also a much stronger conventional mechanism and a clearer understanding of how sensational details spread through press coverage and UFO culture.

## The balanced verdict

Las Palmas holds an important place in Spanish UFO history mainly because of the documentary richness and cultural impact of the 1976 Gáldar incident. It involved military witnesses, civilians across several islands, a formal Air Force inquiry and a dramatic close-encounter claim that became part of Gran Canaria’s local memory.

The most defensible assessment is that the main aerial spectacle was probably caused by Poseidon missile launches over the Atlantic. Its altitude, duration, enormous apparent scale and expanding luminous structure are all consistent with sunlit rocket exhaust. The evidence does not support treating it as a low-flying spacecraft over Gáldar.

The humanoid story is weaker still. It depends on a very small number of mutually connected witnesses, was doubted within the official inquiry and has no independent physical confirmation. It should be preserved as part of the testimony, but not placed on the same evidential level as the widely observed halo.

The 1980 radar and pilot report remains more genuinely unresolved, although its limited radar confirmation prevents a strong conclusion. Together, the cases show why “unidentified” must be used carefully. In Las Palmas, it can describe an impressive event before its likely cause was recognised, a technically incomplete aviation incident, or a disputed interpretation added to an otherwise real sighting. Those are three different kinds of mystery, and confusing them is what allowed the province’s most famous UFO legend to outgrow the evidence.
