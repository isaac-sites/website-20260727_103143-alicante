## Introduction

Spain’s declassified file on the 22 June 1976 Canary Islands sighting confirms something important but limited: the Spanish Air Force treated the reports seriously, gathered military and civilian testimony, compared accounts and formally classified the event as unidentified. It did not confirm a landing, recover physical material or verify the presence of extraterrestrial visitors.

Known as file 760622, the 107-page dossier is valuable because it preserves the difference between the widely observed luminous display and the much narrower claim that a transparent sphere containing two figures hovered near the ground outside Gáldar, Gran Canaria. Investigators broadly accepted that an unusual light had been seen across several islands. They were far less convinced that the alleged sphere and occupants were physically real.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

Declassification therefore strengthened the historical evidence for a genuine [mass sighting]({{ 'mass-sighting/' | relative_url }}) in [Las Palmas]({{ 'las-palmas/' | relative_url }}), while weakening later suggestions that the authorities possessed proof of a landed craft or alien encounter.

## How Air Force file 760622 was assembled

The investigation followed an established Spanish Air Force procedure rather than an improvised response to newspaper excitement. Air Force rules dating from 1968 provided for the appointment of an investigating officer, known as a *Juez Informador*, to collect statements, examine possible conventional causes and report to the relevant regional command. The Canary Islands inquiry was opened after the 22 June sighting and its findings were forwarded to the head of the Canary Air Zone in July 1976.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

The completed dossier is not a single narrative report. It is a working file containing summaries, questionnaires, handwritten statements, maps, sketches, witness assessments, official correspondence, press material and later administrative records. That structure matters because it allows readers to see how the case developed rather than relying only on the most dramatic retellings.

The inquiry began with one of its strongest sources: the Spanish Navy corvette *Atrevida*. At 21:27 universal time, while sailing close to Fuerteventura, personnel on the bridge saw a bright light that at first resembled an aircraft’s landing lamp. It rose, became bluish and developed a large circular halo. A smaller luminous portion remained below while another component climbed rapidly in an irregular spiral. The halo persisted until about 22:10, but the ship’s radar registered no corresponding target.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

Reports from Gran Canaria followed within minutes. The file summarised observations from Gáldar, Agaete, Boca Barranco, the road between Gáldar and Agaete, and the high ground at Pozo de las Nieves. Witnesses repeatedly described a large transparent or bluish circle, an expanding arc, a bright central source and luminous streaks. Several said that stars remained visible through the glowing area, suggesting that they were looking at an illuminated cloud or halo rather than a solid body blocking the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

Investigators did not treat every statement as equally reliable. The Air Force system ranked witnesses partly according to profession, observational circumstances and corroboration. Military personnel and trained aviation specialists were placed in stronger categories than witnesses whose physical or psychological state might have affected their account. File 760622 records different reliability grades rather than presenting all testimony as one seamless body of proof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

That approach was imperfect and sometimes paternalistic, but it demonstrates that the dossier was an evidential assessment, not an official endorsement of everything reported.

## What investigators accepted and doubted

The file’s most defensible conclusion was that a large aerial light display had occurred. It was reported from Fuerteventura, Gran Canaria and Tenerife by numerous observers whose descriptions agreed on several central features. The *Atrevida* account supplied a recorded time, weather conditions, binocular observation and testimony from naval personnel. A sergeant on security duty at Pozo de las Nieves separately described a circular object of roughly three full-moon diameters rising behind Tenerife and disappearing after about ten minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

The regional commander consequently agreed that the origin and nature had not been determined and applied the administrative description “unidentified aerial phenomenon”. In this context, “unidentified” meant that the investigators had not established a satisfactory cause from the information available to them in 1976. It did not mean that they had identified an intelligently controlled craft, still less an extraterrestrial one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

The absence of radar detection was also treated as a fact, but its significance is limited. A distant illuminated exhaust cloud, high-altitude release or other diffuse optical phenomenon would not necessarily produce the sort of radar return expected from an aircraft. The lack of an echo therefore does not prove that something evaded radar through extraordinary technology; it may instead indicate that there was no nearby solid object to detect.

### The sphere and occupants were a separate claim

The most famous part of the Gáldar story came from a doctor travelling in a taxi to visit a patient. He and the driver reported seeing a sphere about 30 metres across, apparently suspended only 15 to 20 metres away, with two reddish figures inside. This description later became central to books, documentaries and press accounts of the incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

The dossier itself gives strong reasons not to merge this claim automatically with the mass sighting. The alleged close object was described by only a small number of people. A third occupant of the taxi did not see its shape or interior; the vehicle’s roof obstructed his view, and he later lowered his head because he was frightened. He could confirm unusual exterior light and the agitation of the other passengers, but not a sphere or occupants.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

A nearby labourer reported a fire-like patch with two blue streaks and a halo, broadly matching the distant luminous phenomenon rather than a solid craft containing beings. Another woman mentioned something resembling two figures inside a blue globe but explicitly expressed uncertainty. These partial similarities could represent corroboration, but they could equally show different interpretations of the same bright, unfamiliar display.

The investigating officer did not accuse the doctor and driver of lying. On the contrary, he considered them serious and sincere. His distinction was subtler: they had truthfully described what they believed they saw, but that did not establish that the scene existed objectively as described. The final report expressed “reasonable doubt” about the physical authenticity of the close encounter, partly because of contradictions and concerns raised during the medical examination of the principal witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

## The file did not contain the photograph many readers expect

Photographs are often mentioned in accounts of the Canary Islands sightings, but file 760622 contains a significant archival trap. A photograph included near the end of the dossier did not depict the event of 22 June 1976. The investigating officer explicitly explained that it related to an earlier case and had been placed in the later file as supporting or comparative material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

Its history was uncertain. A copy had reportedly been returned by a foreign tourist after appearing in a packet processed by a photographic laboratory. The Civil Guard eventually located the print, but investigators could not establish the photographer, original negative, exact date, time or location with confidence. Laboratory staff said that they had not manipulated the print during processing, although the absence of the negative prevented a complete authenticity assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

This distinction is easily lost when documents are reproduced without their accompanying notes. A photograph’s presence inside an official UFO file does not prove that it shows the event named on the cover. In this instance, the Air Force archive itself warns against that conclusion.

The same caution applies to drawings. The sketches in the dossier are valuable records of what witnesses reported or remembered, but they are not technical photographs of an object. They illustrate testimony rather than independently confirming it.

## Why the official “unidentified” finding was not the final word

The 1976 investigators considered several ordinary possibilities but lacked information about secret military activity far out over the Atlantic. Later researchers have connected the main Canary Islands display with United States Navy Poseidon submarine-launched ballistic missile tests. Such a plume can appear enormous because exhaust gases expand rapidly in the thin upper atmosphere. Sunlight can illuminate the cloud while the islands below are already dark, producing blue, white, orange or reddish colours. Moving rocket stages or deployment vehicles may look like lights separating, spiralling or climbing through the centre of the cloud. As the exhaust disperses, a transparent halo can remain visible for many minutes.

* the phenomenon was visible over a wide geographical area;

The missile interpretation is not an explanation produced by the original Air Force inquiry. It is a later reconstruction using information that investigators may not have had, particularly details of foreign strategic missile trials. It should therefore be described as a strong retrospective explanation rather than as the file’s official conclusion.

It also addresses the mass sighting more successfully than the alleged near-ground sphere. The doctor’s account may represent a dramatic misjudgement of distance and scale, psychological elaboration under unusual conditions or a separate observation. Whatever its cause, the Poseidon evidence does not independently verify two figures inside a local craft.

## How declassification changed the public story

File 760622 was formally declassified on 13 July 1994 and entered the Air Force Central Library later that month. Spain’s Ministry of Defence subsequently made the wider collection of declassified Air Force UFO material accessible through its virtual library. The archive explains that physical copies were deposited with the Air Force library from 1992 before later digitisation allowed public online consultation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

Before that release, selected information had circulated through journalists, investigators and UFO books. The secrecy surrounding the inquiry encouraged suspicions that the military possessed stronger evidence than it had disclosed. One witness even wrote that he believed he was forbidden, under threat of military punishment, from discussing what he had observed. During the 1994 review, an intelligence officer noted that declassification would remove any supposed obligation of silence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

The released paperwork made the investigation look both more serious and less sensational than the legend. It showed that officials had gathered detailed evidence and had not simply dismissed the public. At the same time, it exposed uncertainty, conflicting testimony, doubtful photographs and explicit official reservations about the occupant claim.

Declassification also clarified why the label “unidentified” can be misleading when separated from its administrative context. The commanding general stated that the origin and nature could not be determined, so the event had to remain unidentified. The same archive does not say that an alien craft was established, that occupants were verified or that conventional explanations were impossible for all time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-es-expediente-canarias-1976-06-22-1976-06-22-avistamiento-en-canarias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyExpediente, Avistamiento en Canarias (22 June 1976), Ejército del Aire / Ministerio de Defensa · 1976 · UFO Transparency...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1976</span></span></span>

Indeed, the 1994 classification review concluded that there was no continuing reason to keep the file secret. That decision is difficult to reconcile with claims that it contained confirmed evidence of a recovered craft, an extraterrestrial landing or technology of overriding defence importance.

## What the declassified record establishes

For Las Palmas UFO history, file 760622 remains a landmark primary source because it preserves three distinct levels of evidence that later storytelling often blends together.

First, an extensive luminous phenomenon was genuinely reported on 22 June 1976. The witnesses included civilians, naval personnel and military staff at widely separated locations. The Air Force found enough consistency to recognise a real observational event.

Second, the extraordinary close encounter near Gáldar was not supported at the same level. It depended heavily on two mutually reinforcing witnesses, lacked clear physical traces and was explicitly treated with caution by the investigating officer. Sincerity was accepted; literal accuracy was not.

Third, “unidentified” was the endpoint of a 1976 inquiry, not proof that the phenomenon remains beyond explanation. Later missile-test correlations and the known appearance of sunlit rocket plumes substantially strengthen a conventional explanation for the main display, even though some details of individual testimony remain uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapedia.ai/wiki/the-canary-islands-uap-incident-1976/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapedia.ai">[UAPedia - Unlocking New Realities]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapedia.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Later cross-disciplinary work has associated the display with U</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unlocking New RealitiesThe Canary Islands UAP Incident (1976) - UAPediamatters: The case illustrates how mass sightings, lighti...</span></span></span>

The declassified file therefore matters less as evidence of alien visitation than as a record of how authorities tried to govern uncertainty. It shows officials documenting a confusing mass sighting, ranking witnesses, separating stronger reports from weaker ones and stopping where their available evidence stopped. Its lasting lesson is that an official investigation can confirm that people saw something remarkable without confirming the extraordinary interpretation later attached to it.
